Subject

Build a Network Anomaly Monitor

Operator 120 min 1000 XP
Mission Brief

This operation turns the ESP32 CYD into an intelligent network watchdog that continuously monitors the local WiFi environment, builds a baseline fingerprint database of known devices, and flags anomalies — new devices appearing, established devices disappearing, sudden RSSI spikes suggesting physical movement, deauthentication floods indicating active attacks, and channel changes that could signal interference or jamming. The touchscreen displays a live alert feed color-coded by severity, with historical trend graphs logged to the onboard microSD card.

You will implement periodic WiFi scanning to establish a network baseline, build a device fingerprint database tracking MAC address, typical RSSI range, and first/last seen timestamps, define anomaly detection rules that flag deviations from the baseline, render a severity-coded alert dashboard on the TFT with drill-down capability, and log historical data to microSD for long-term trend analysis.

This is the pattern behind enterprise Wireless Intrusion Prevention Systems (WIPS) — behavioral anomaly detection on the wireless layer, running autonomously on a pocket-sized device.

Agent Requirements
  • ESP32-2432S028R (CYD — Cheap Yellow Display)
  • A microSD card (formatted FAT32) for data logging
  • Arduino IDE or PlatformIO with ESP32 board support
  • Understanding of WiFi fundamentals (SSID/MAC/RSSI)
  • Comfort with data structures (arrays/structs) and time-based logic
Operation Phases
Progress
0 / 5
✓
1
Periodic WiFi Scan Baseline
Implement a scanning loop that runs WiFi.scanNetworks() every 30 seconds and records all visible access points and their metadata. After 5 scan cycles, establish a baseline of "known" networks and devices. Display the baseline device count and average RSSI values on the TFT.
Checkpoint: WiFi Scan Baseline
  • Verify WiFi.scanNetworks() returns a non-zero count of detected access points
  • After 5 scan cycles, confirm the baseline device count is displayed on the TFT screen
  • Check that average RSSI values are in a reasonable range (-30 to -90 dBm)
✓
2
Device Fingerprint Database
Create a struct-based database stored in memory that tracks each device by BSSID: SSID, first seen timestamp, last seen timestamp, min/max/average RSSI, channel, encryption type, and scan-count (how many scans it appeared in). Limit to 64 entries with LRU eviction. Display the database size and newest entry on screen.
✓
3
Anomaly Detection Rules
Implement five anomaly rules: (1) NEW_DEVICE — a BSSID appears that isn't in the baseline, (2) MISSING_DEVICE — a baseline device hasn't been seen in 3+ consecutive scans, (3) RSSI_SPIKE — a device's RSSI changes by more than 15 dBm between scans, (4) DEAUTH_FLOOD — promiscuous mode detects more than 10 deauth frames in 5 seconds, (5) CHANNEL_CHANGE — a known AP switches channels unexpectedly.
Checkpoint: Anomaly Detection Rules
  • Trigger a NEW_DEVICE alert by adding a mobile hotspot and verify the alert fires within one scan cycle
  • Simulate a MISSING_DEVICE by powering off a known device — alert should trigger after 3 consecutive missed scans
  • Verify RSSI_SPIKE detection by moving a device significantly closer/further and confirming the 15 dBm threshold
✓
4
Alert Dashboard on Touchscreen
Render a scrollable alert feed on the TFT with each alert showing: timestamp, rule name, severity (HIGH/MED/LOW), and device details. Color-code by severity: red background for HIGH, yellow for MED, green for LOW. Add a summary bar at the top showing total alerts, alerts in the last hour, and current threat level. Tap an alert to see full details.
✓
5
Historical Trend Graphs & microSD Logging
Initialize the microSD card slot on the CYD and log all alerts and scan summaries as CSV lines with timestamps. Build a trend graph screen that reads the last 24 hours of data and renders: alerts per hour as a bar chart, device count over time as a line graph, and average RSSI trend for the top 5 devices. Add touch navigation between the alert feed and trend screens.
Checkpoint: Historical Trends
  • Verify CSV log file is being written to the microSD card with correct timestamp formatting
  • Confirm the bar chart shows 24 bars (one per hour) and the line graph renders device count over time
  • Touch navigation between alert feed and trend screens works without display glitches or crashes
