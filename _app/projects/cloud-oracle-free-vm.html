Subject

Spin Up a Free Oracle Cloud VM

Recruit 45 min 200 XP
Mission Brief

Your assignment is to create an Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) account, launch a genuinely free compute instance, SSH into it, and understand exactly what keeps it free forever. Every step is click-by-click. No prior cloud experience is required.

Oracle Cloud has two distinct free offerings that operate independently and must not be confused. The 30-day Free Trial gives you promotional credits worth a fixed dollar amount that expires after 30 days or when the credits are exhausted — whichever comes first. The Always Free tier is permanent: it is available for the life of your account and is not tied to the trial period at all. When the trial expires, Always Free resources keep running at zero cost. The mission here is to launch an Always Free instance so the VM is yours indefinitely, not just for 30 days.

The Always Free compute allotment (verified June 2026 against Oracle documentation) covers two shapes. VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro is an AMD x86 micro instance with 1/8 OCPU and 1 GB RAM — you get up to two of these and they are consistently available in every region. VM.Standard.A1.Flex is an Arm-based Ampere instance with a shared pool of 4 OCPUs and 24 GB RAM across all your A1 instances. The A1 Flex is more powerful but is subject to “Out of host capacity” errors in popular regions. This walkthrough uses the AMD E2.1.Micro as the primary path because it is reliable; the A1 Flex is covered as an alternative with its capacity caveat noted.

Agent Requirements
  • A valid email address — one Oracle Cloud account per email address. You will receive a verification link during signup.
  • A phone number for identity verification during account creation.
  • A credit or debit card (Visa or Mastercard with no PIN required) for identity verification only. Oracle places a small temporary hold that is automatically removed. You will not be charged as long as you stay within Always Free limits and do not upgrade your account to Pay As You Go.
  • A terminal with SSH — any modern terminal works. Windows: use PowerShell (SSH is built in on Windows 10 and later). Mac and Linux: the built-in Terminal application. The ssh command is used in Phase 4.
  • No prior cloud experience required. Every console navigation step is described with the exact menu names and button labels as they appear in the OCI Console.

Create Your Oracle Cloud Account
Signing up takes about 10 minutes. The most important decision in this phase is your Home Region — it cannot be changed after account creation. Choose the region closest to you geographically for the lowest latency. Account provisioning is usually instant but can take up to 30 minutes in rare cases.
  1. Open the signup page. In your browser, navigate to:
    https://signup.oraclecloud.com
    Why this URL and not oracle.com/cloud/free? The oracle.com/cloud/free page is a marketing landing page. The signup flow begins directly at signup.oraclecloud.com. If a button on oracle.com/cloud/free links you there, that is the same destination.
  2. Fill in Account Information. Select your country, enter your name, and provide your email address. Complete the CAPTCHA, then click Verify my email. Check your inbox for an email with subject Confirm your Oracle cloud account and click the link inside. The link is valid for 30 minutes.
  3. Set a password. The password must be 8–40 characters and include at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one number, and one special character. It cannot contain your name, email address, or spaces.
  4. Create your Cloud Account Name. This is a short, unique identifier for your tenancy (your isolated slice of OCI). It does not affect cost or capability. Choose something memorable like your name or a project name.
  5. Select your Home Region. A dropdown lists all available OCI regions. Pick the one closest to you. Examples: US East (Ashburn), US West (Phoenix), UK South (London), Germany Central (Frankfurt), Japan East (Tokyo).
    This choice is permanent. Oracle documentation states: “You can’t change your home region after your account is created.” If unsure, contact Oracle Sales before creating the account. Your Always Free resources exist only in the home region.
  6. Accept the Terms of Use and click Continue.
  7. Enter your address and phone number. The phone number is required for identity verification. Provide a number that can receive a call or SMS.
  8. Add payment verification. Click Add payment verification method, select Credit Card, and enter your card details. Click Finish.
    Will I be charged? No. Oracle places a small temporary authorization hold (often $1) to verify the card is real. This hold is automatically removed and does not become a charge. You will only be charged if you manually upgrade to a Pay As You Go account. Always Free resources do not incur charges on a free account.
  9. Click Start my free trial. Oracle provisions your account. When provisioning completes, you are redirected to the OCI Console dashboard. You will also receive a welcome email titled Get Started with Oracle Cloud.
    If provisioning does not complete within a few minutes, wait up to 30 minutes, then check your email for a “Get Started” link. The link takes you directly to the console once your account is ready.
Always Free vs. trial credits — know the difference before you launch anything.
  • Your account starts with 30-day trial credits. These let you try paid resources. They expire at 30 days or when spent, whichever comes first.
  • The Always Free tier exists separately and never expires. It is available for the life of your account regardless of the trial.
  • The mission here is to launch an Always Free-eligible shape so the VM keeps running after the trial credits are gone. If you launch a shape that is not Always Free-eligible, it will stop or be reclaimed when the trial ends.
  • A1 Ampere caution: if you provision more A1 capacity than the Always Free limit (4 OCPUs / 24 GB RAM total) during the trial, Oracle will disable all of your A1 instances when the trial ends and delete them 30 days later unless you upgrade to a paid account. Keep A1 usage within the free limits or be ready to upgrade.
Checkpoint — what you should see
You are logged into the OCI Console at cloud.oracle.com. The top navigation bar shows your tenancy name and your home region in the upper-right corner. The left sidebar includes links for Compute, Networking, and Storage. You have not launched any instances yet.

2
Understand Always-Free Shapes and Generate Your SSH Key
Before launching an instance you need to know which shapes are Always Free-eligible and you need an SSH key pair. The public key goes into the instance at launch; the private key stays on your machine and is how you authenticate when connecting.
The two Always Free compute shapes (June 2026). These limits are per Oracle’s Always Free documentation:

VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (AMD) — 1/8 OCPU, 1 GB RAM, 50 GB default boot volume. Up to 2 instances. Available only in one availability domain in your home region. Reliable availability. Best choice for this walkthrough.

VM.Standard.A1.Flex (Ampere ARM) — Shared pool of 4 OCPUs and 24 GB RAM total across all your A1 instances. Minimum 47 GB boot volume per instance. You can run one instance using the whole pool (4 OCPU / 24 GB) or split it across up to four smaller instances. Subject to capacity shortages in busy regions.

Block storage: 200 GB total across all boot and block volumes in your home region.
  1. Choose your shape now so you are ready for Phase 3. The AMD VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro is the reliable always-free fallback. Use it unless you specifically want the Ampere A1 Flex and are prepared to retry if capacity is unavailable.
    The #1 trap: picking a non-Always-Free shape such as VM.Standard.E3.Flex, VM.Standard3.Flex, or any GPU shape. These consume trial credits and stop or get reclaimed when the credits expire. In the shape picker, you must confirm the label Always Free Eligible appears on the image or shape you select. If that label is absent, stop and pick a different shape.
  2. Generate an SSH key pair on your local machine. Open your terminal and run:
    ssh-keygen -t ed25519 -C "oracle-vm" -f ~/.ssh/oracle-vm
    When prompted for a passphrase, press Enter twice to skip it (acceptable for a personal learning VM; add a passphrase for production systems).
    What this creates: two files in your ~/.ssh/ directory. oracle-vm is your private key — never share it, never paste it anywhere. oracle-vm.pub is your public key — this is what goes into the OCI instance during Phase 3. Anyone can see the public key; only you hold the private key that matches it.
  3. Verify both key files exist:
    ls -la ~/.ssh/oracle-vm ~/.ssh/oracle-vm.pub
    You should see both files listed. The private key (oracle-vm) should have permissions -rw------- (readable only by you). If it does not, fix the permissions:
    chmod 400 ~/.ssh/oracle-vm
    Windows (PowerShell) note: ssh-keygen is built into Windows 10 and later via OpenSSH. Your keys land in C:\Users\YourName\.ssh\. The chmod 400 command is not needed on Windows — the key file permissions are handled differently by the SSH client.
  4. Copy the public key content to your clipboard. You will paste this into the OCI Console in Phase 3:
    cat ~/.ssh/oracle-vm.pub
    The output is a single long line starting with ssh-ed25519. Select the entire line and copy it. On Windows PowerShell:
    Get-Content $env:USERPROFILE\.ssh\oracle-vm.pub
    Alternative: let OCI generate the key for you. The Create Instance form has a Generate a key pair for me option. OCI creates the key pair and prompts you to Save Private Key. Download it immediately — you cannot retrieve the private key later. If you choose this route, skip steps 2–4 above and use the downloaded .key file in Phase 4 instead of ~/.ssh/oracle-vm.
Checkpoint — what you should have
Two files exist at ~/.ssh/oracle-vm (private key) and ~/.ssh/oracle-vm.pub (public key). The public key content is copied to your clipboard. You know your target shape is VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (AMD, Always Free Eligible) or VM.Standard.A1.Flex (Ampere ARM, Always Free Eligible). No instance has been created yet.

3
Create the Compute Instance
This phase walks through every section of the OCI Create Instance form. The form has several collapsible sections; the ones that matter for an Always Free setup are Image and shape, Networking, and Add SSH keys. Everything else can stay at its default value.
  1. Navigate to Compute. In the OCI Console, open the main navigation hamburger menu (top-left, three horizontal lines). Go to Compute → Instances.
  2. Click Create instance (the blue button near the top of the Instances list page).
  3. Name your instance. In the Name field under Basic information, enter a name like my-free-vm. The compartment can stay as your root compartment (your account name).
  4. Select the image. Under Image and shape, click Edit (or the section header to expand it if collapsed). Click Change image. The Browse Images panel opens. Look for the Always Free Eligible label on the image tile. Oracle Linux 8, Ubuntu 22.04, and Ubuntu 24.04 LTS all carry this label. Select your preference:
    • Oracle Linux 8 — default SSH user is opc. Lighter footprint, closer to RHEL.
    • Ubuntu 22.04 — default SSH user is ubuntu. Stable LTS, widely documented.
    • Ubuntu 24.04 LTS — default SSH user is ubuntu. Current LTS release; may appear as the default in the console. Either Ubuntu version works for this walkthrough.
    Click Select image to confirm.
    Always Free Eligible label on images. Per Oracle documentation: “The Linux images labeled ‘Always Free Eligible’ in the Console are compatible with Always Free compute instances and incur no licensing fees.” If an image does not show this label, it may carry a licensing cost even on an Always Free shape.
  5. Select the shape. Still in the Image and shape section, click Change shape. The Browse All Shapes panel opens with tabs for shape series.
    • For AMD E2.1.Micro: click Specialty and previous generation, then select VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro.
    • For Ampere A1 Flex: click Ampere, then select VM.Standard.A1.Flex. Set OCPUs and RAM proportionally (up to 4 OCPU / 24 GB total for Always Free).
    Confirm the shape is labeled Always Free Eligible before proceeding. Click Select shape.
    If you do not see VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro in the list: confirm you are in the correct availability domain. The AMD E2.1.Micro is available in only one availability domain per home region. Use the Availability domain dropdown at the top of the Create Instance form to switch availability domains (AD-1, AD-2, AD-3) until the shape becomes selectable.
  6. Configure networking. Expand the Networking section. If this is your first instance, no VCN exists yet. Click Create new virtual cloud network. Accept the defaults OCI pre-fills (a VCN name like vcn-<date>, subnet name, and CIDR block 10.0.0.0/24). Leave Assign a public IPv4 address set to Yes (this is the address you will SSH to).
    What OCI creates for you: a Virtual Cloud Network (VCN), one public subnet, an Internet Gateway, a Route Table pointing 0.0.0.0/0 at the Internet Gateway, and a Default Security List with an ingress rule already open for SSH (TCP port 22 from any source). You do not need to configure the security list manually for SSH access when using this auto-created VCN path.
  7. Add your SSH public key. Expand the Add SSH keys section. Select Paste public keys. Paste the contents of your oracle-vm.pub file (the long line starting with ssh-ed25519 that you copied in Phase 2).
    Accidentally pasted the private key? The form will show an error: “If you try to upload or paste a private key, an error occurs.” The public key starts with ssh-ed25519 or ssh-rsa. The private key starts with -----BEGIN OPENSSH PRIVATE KEY-----. Make sure you are pasting from oracle-vm.pub, not oracle-vm.
  8. Boot volume. Leave the default boot volume size (50 GB for E2.1.Micro, 47 GB minimum for A1 Flex). Do not increase it beyond your Always Free 200 GB total storage allotment.
  9. Click Create (the blue button at the bottom of the form). OCI begins provisioning. The instance page appears and shows the state as Provisioning (orange indicator). This typically completes within 1–3 minutes.
Ampere A1 Flex: “Out of host capacity” error.
  • This means no physical Ampere servers are currently available in the selected availability domain in your home region. It is not an account problem.
  • Option A: Wait a few hours and try again. Capacity is released as other users terminate instances.
  • Option B (recommended for this walkthrough): Switch to VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (AMD). It is Always Free-eligible and consistently available. You lose the Ampere’s performance but gain reliable provisioning.
Checkpoint — what you should see
The instance details page shows the state as Running (green indicator). The Instance information tab shows the shape name (VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro or VM.Standard.A1.Flex) and the image name you selected. The Primary VNIC information section shows a Public IP address (a dotted-decimal IPv4 address like 141.147.X.X). Copy that IP address — you need it in Phase 4.

4
Open the SSH Port and Connect to Your Instance
When OCI auto-creates a VCN during instance creation, the default security list includes an ingress rule that allows SSH (TCP port 22) from any source. In most cases SSH works immediately after the instance reaches Running state. This phase confirms the security list rule is in place, then walks through the SSH connection command and what to expect on first connect.
  1. Verify the SSH ingress rule. From the instance details page, scroll down to the Primary VNIC information section and click the subnet link (it looks like subnet-<date>). On the subnet page, click Security list in the left sidebar, then click the default security list name (something like Default Security List for vcn-<date>). Under Ingress Rules, confirm a rule exists with:
    • Source: 0.0.0.0/0
    • Protocol: TCP
    • Destination Port Range: 22
    If this rule is missing (it should not be on an auto-created VCN), click Add Ingress Rules. Set Source CIDR to 0.0.0.0/0, IP Protocol to TCP, and Destination Port Range to 22. Click Add Ingress Rules to save. Return to your instance page.
  2. Open your terminal on your local machine (not inside the OCI Console).
  3. Connect via SSH. Replace YOUR_PUBLIC_IP with the IP address you copied from the instance details page. Use the correct username for your chosen image:
    # If you chose Oracle Linux: ssh -i ~/.ssh/oracle-vm opc@YOUR_PUBLIC_IP # If you chose Ubuntu: ssh -i ~/.ssh/oracle-vm ubuntu@YOUR_PUBLIC_IP
    Default SSH usernames (verified from OCI documentation):
    Oracle Linux and RHEL-compatible images → opc
    Ubuntu images → ubuntu
    Using the wrong username is the second most common SSH failure. If you get Permission denied (publickey) immediately, before checking the key, make sure you have the correct username for your image.
  4. Accept the host key fingerprint. On first connection SSH asks:
    The authenticity of host '141.147.X.X (141.147.X.X)' can't be established. ED25519 key fingerprint is SHA256:xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx. Are you sure you want to continue connecting (yes/no/[fingerprint])?
    Type yes and press Enter. SSH adds the host to your ~/.ssh/known_hosts file. This prompt will not appear again for the same IP address.
  5. Confirm you are on the remote VM. Once connected you see a prompt like [opc@my-free-vm ~]$ (Oracle Linux) or ubuntu@my-free-vm:~$ (Ubuntu). Run a quick command to verify:
    uname -a
    The output shows the Linux kernel version and your CPU architecture — x86_64 for the AMD E2.1.Micro, aarch64 for the Ampere A1. Example on Oracle Linux 8:
    Linux my-free-vm 5.15.0-208.160.7.el8uek.x86_64 #2 SMP ... x86_64 x86_64 x86_64 GNU/Linux
    Example on Ubuntu 22.04:
    Linux my-free-vm 5.15.0-1071-oracle #77-Ubuntu SMP ... x86_64 x86_64 x86_64 GNU/Linux
    The uname -a output confirms you are executing commands on the remote Oracle Cloud VM, not your local machine. The hostname in the prompt matches the instance name you set in Phase 3. The exact kernel version numbers will differ from the examples above — Oracle updates images regularly.
SSH gives “Permission denied (publickey)”.
  • Wrong username: Oracle Linux uses opc, Ubuntu uses ubuntu. Confirm the username matches your image choice.
  • Wrong private key: Confirm you are passing -i ~/.ssh/oracle-vm (the private key, not the .pub file).
  • Key permissions too open: Run chmod 400 ~/.ssh/oracle-vm on Mac/Linux. SSH refuses keys readable by others.
  • Wrong public key in the instance: If you pasted the wrong public key during instance creation, you cannot fix this without terminating and re-creating the instance or using the OCI Console’s serial console to add a new key.
SSH hangs or times out (no response at all).
  • The security list is missing the TCP port 22 ingress rule. Return to step 1 above and verify or add the rule.
  • The instance is still in Provisioning state. Wait until the console shows Running (green) before attempting SSH.
  • You are connecting to the wrong IP address. Confirm the IP is the Public IP address on the instance details page, not the Private IP.
Checkpoint — the observable result
Your terminal displays a shell prompt on the remote Oracle Cloud VM: [opc@my-free-vm ~]$ for Oracle Linux or ubuntu@my-free-vm:~$ for Ubuntu. The output of uname -a shows a Linux kernel and the instance hostname. You are now operating a cloud-hosted Linux server that runs 24/7 at no cost within Always Free limits.

5
Put It to Work and Keep It Free
You have a running free VM with an SSH shell. This phase shows a concrete first use (installing and serving a web page with Nginx), explains how to open port 80 in the security list and the OS firewall, and covers the cost safety rules that keep the VM in the Always Free tier indefinitely.
  1. Update the OS package list. Inside your SSH session:
    # Oracle Linux (uses dnf): sudo dnf update -y # Ubuntu (uses apt): sudo apt update && sudo apt upgrade -y
    This applies security patches. On a fresh OCI instance it takes 1–3 minutes. The -y flag (dnf) or && apt upgrade -y (apt) skips confirmation prompts.
  2. Install Nginx.
    # Oracle Linux: sudo dnf install nginx -y sudo systemctl enable --now nginx # Ubuntu: sudo apt install nginx -y
    Nginx starts automatically on Ubuntu. On Oracle Linux the enable --now command starts it and marks it to start at boot.
  3. Open port 80 in the OCI security list. Go back to the OCI Console in your browser. Navigate to the security list you verified in Phase 4. Under Ingress Rules, click Add Ingress Rules and set:
    • Source Type: CIDR
    • Source CIDR: 0.0.0.0/0
    • IP Protocol: TCP
    • Destination Port Range: 80
    Click Add Ingress Rules.
  4. Open port 80 in the OS firewall. OCI security lists control traffic at the network level, but Oracle Linux also runs firewalld by default as a second layer. Ubuntu does not enable ufw by default on OCI instances, so Ubuntu users can skip this step.
    # Oracle Linux only: sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-service=http sudo firewall-cmd --reload
    Why two firewall layers? The OCI security list acts at the virtual network level (before packets reach the VM). The OS firewall (firewalld on Oracle Linux) acts inside the VM. Both must allow port 80 for an HTTP request to reach Nginx. The OCI security list alone is not sufficient when firewalld is active.
  5. Verify Nginx is serving. In your browser, navigate to your VM’s public IP address over plain HTTP:
    http://YOUR_PUBLIC_IP
    You should see the Nginx default welcome page (“Welcome to nginx!” on Ubuntu or “HTTP Server Test Page” on Oracle Linux). This confirms traffic is flowing from the internet through OCI’s network layer, through the OS firewall, and into Nginx on your free VM.
    Note the http:// (not https://). A fresh Nginx install does not have a TLS certificate configured. The browser may warn you about an unencrypted connection — for this test page that is expected. Adding TLS (via Let’s Encrypt and Certbot) is a natural next step once you have a domain name pointed at the VM.
  6. Keep it free: the cost safety checklist.
    Rules that keep your VM free forever:
    • Upgrading to Pay As You Go (PAYG) does NOT charge for your Always Free instances. Oracle documentation states that Always Free resources stay free after upgrading; only resource usage above the Always Free limits incurs charges. You may want to upgrade to avoid the A1 trial-end deletion described below — but doing so is a deliberate choice, not accidental billing.
    • Keep total boot + block volumes at or below 200 GB across all Always Free resources in your home region.
    • Keep A1 Flex usage within 4 OCPUs / 24 GB RAM total across all A1 instances (the Always Free limit). If you exceed this at trial-end, Oracle disables all your A1 instances and deletes them 30 days later.
    • Do not create more than 2 AMD E2.1.Micro instances.
    • Do not attach paid block volumes or object storage buckets beyond the Always Free storage limits (200 GB block volume total; 20 GB object storage on Always Free tier).
    • Run a real workload to prevent idle reclamation. Oracle may reclaim Always Free compute instances that are idle over a 7-day window: 95th-percentile CPU below 20%, network utilization below 20%, and (for A1 shapes only) memory utilization below 20%. Logging in has no effect on this check — what matters is actual utilization. Even a lightweight cron job, a personal web server, or a VPN service is sufficient to stay above the threshold. If an instance is reclaimed it can typically be relaunched.
    • Check your billing/cost dashboard periodically. In the OCI Console: Menu → Billing & Cost Management → Cost Analysis. Always Free usage shows $0 cost. If you see non-zero charges, investigate immediately — you may have inadvertently created a paid resource.
  7. Teardown (optional). If you no longer need the VM and want to remove all resources, go to Compute → Instances, click on the instance name, then click More actions → Terminate. Check Permanently delete the attached boot volume to also remove the boot disk and free the storage. Terminated instances are removed from the console within minutes. The VCN and security list remain; delete them from Networking → Virtual cloud networks if you want a clean slate.
Browser shows “This site can’t be reached” when navigating to the public IP on port 80.
  • Confirm the OCI security list ingress rule for TCP port 80 was saved (step 3 above).
  • If you are on Oracle Linux, confirm sudo firewall-cmd --list-services shows http in the output. If not, repeat step 4.
  • Confirm Nginx is actually running: sudo systemctl status nginx. The output should show Active: active (running).
  • Make sure you are navigating to http:// (not https://). Port 443 is not open by default.
Checkpoint — mission complete
Your browser shows the Nginx welcome page when you navigate to http://YOUR_PUBLIC_IP. Your SSH session is open at the prompt. You have a free, always-on Linux server in Oracle Cloud that will keep running at zero cost as long as you stay within the Always Free limits and maintain enough utilization to avoid the idle-reclamation threshold. From here you can host a personal site, run a Minecraft server within the CPU/RAM limits, use it as a VPN endpoint, or practice Linux administration in a real cloud environment.
