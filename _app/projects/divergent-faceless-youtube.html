Subject

Build a Faceless YouTube Channel

Operator 180 min 1000 XP
Mission Brief

A faceless YouTube channel is a channel where the creator never appears on camera. The video is built entirely from voiceover audio, stock footage or still images, and text overlays. There is no studio, no lighting rig, and no performance. The only thing a viewer sees is the content itself. This format is legitimate and widespread — documentary-style channels, explainer channels, and educational channels have used it for over a decade.

This project walks you through producing one complete, published faceless YouTube video from zero. You will pick a topic, write a script with an LLM, generate a voiceover using a free text-to-speech tool, source royalty-free visuals, assemble the video in a free editor, create a thumbnail, and upload it to YouTube Studio with correct metadata and the required AI-content disclosure. Every phase ends with a concrete file or artifact you can point to.

Honest framing on monetization: YouTube's YPP threshold is 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months. Reaching that takes real content that real viewers choose to watch. YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy explicitly targets AI-generated videos that are template-based, repetitive, or add no original perspective — channels built entirely on mass-produced AI output are subject to channel-wide demonetization or suspension. The goal of this project is to teach you the production pipeline, not to shortcut creative work.

Agent Requirements
  • A Google account — required to create the YouTube channel and access YouTube Studio
  • A computer running Windows, macOS, or Linux — all tools used here run on all three
  • Python 3.8 or newer installed — needed for the edge-tts voiceover tool in Phase 3 (verify with python3 --version)
  • Access to a free-tier LLM: Claude at claude.ai or ChatGPT at chatgpt.com — a browser and an account are enough; no API key required
  • CapCut Desktop (free download at capcut.com) or DaVinci Resolve 19 (free download at blackmagicdesign.com) installed before Phase 5
  • No camera, no microphone, and no prior video editing experience required

Niche and Topic Selection
Pick a specific subject area where you can answer real questions, then validate one concrete video topic before writing a single word of script. Picking a broad niche like "technology" is not enough — you need a lane narrow enough that you can name the five specific questions your ideal viewer is actually searching for.
  1. Choose a niche you can speak to honestly. Faceless channels succeed when the creator has genuine interest or knowledge in the subject. Reliable categories for beginners because they have stable search demand: personal finance basics (budgeting, credit, debt payoff), history explainers (events, biographies, timelines), science and technology explainers, productivity and software tools, and language or study skills. Pick one category you would actually watch videos about.
  2. Run a YouTube search to validate demand. Open YouTube in a browser. Type your niche into the search bar and do not press Enter yet — watch the autocomplete dropdown. Each suggestion is a real query real people are typing. Write down six autocomplete suggestions. These are candidate topics.
    Why autocomplete works: YouTube surfaces autocomplete suggestions based on actual search volume. A suggestion that appears means enough people are searching for it that YouTube considers it worth predicting. A suggestion that does not appear means the audience is too small to bother.
  3. Check competition on your top three candidate topics. Search each one and look at the top five results. Note: How many views does the top video have? When was it published? Does any video under 6 months old have over 50,000 views? That is a signal of healthy demand. Are all results from channels with over 500,000 subscribers? That is a signal that newcomers may struggle to rank. Aim for topics where at least one result under 6 months old has strong views but the top channel is not enormous.
  4. Lock in your first video topic. Pick the single best topic from your list. Write one sentence that states exactly what your video will teach or show: for example, "This video explains how to build a $1,000 emergency fund in 90 days using the envelope method." That sentence is your north star for the script in Phase 2. If you cannot write that sentence, the topic is still too vague.
  5. Create your folder structure on your computer. All your project files go here.
    mkdir -p ~/faceless-yt/scripts mkdir -p ~/faceless-yt/voiceovers mkdir -p ~/faceless-yt/visuals mkdir -p ~/faceless-yt/exports mkdir -p ~/faceless-yt/thumbs
    On Windows use File Explorer to create a folder named faceless-yt with those five subfolders. The path above is for macOS and Linux. Either way, this folder is your production workspace for the rest of the project.
Cannot decide on a niche?
  • Constraint helps: give yourself a hard deadline of 20 minutes for this phase. An imperfect niche you execute on beats a perfect niche you never start.
  • Avoid trending news or current events as your first niche. Those videos have a 48-hour shelf life and require constant new content to stay relevant. Educational "evergreen" topics (how to do X, what is Y, history of Z) keep getting views for months after upload.
  • Avoid niches you know nothing about. LLMs can write a script about anything, but if a viewer asks a question in the comments you cannot answer honestly, that is a problem for the channel long-term.
Checkpoint — artifact: topic-brief.txt
Create ~/faceless-yt/scripts/topic-brief.txt and write three things in it: (1) your chosen niche in one word or phrase, (2) your first video topic as a single sentence starting with "This video explains...", and (3) the three YouTube autocomplete suggestions you validated. If you cannot fill all three fields, go back to step 2. The folder structure from step 5 should exist on your computer before moving on.

Script Writing with an LLM
Use a free LLM (Claude at claude.ai or ChatGPT at chatgpt.com) with a structured prompt to produce a complete 3-to-5 minute script. A 3-minute video at a comfortable narration pace requires roughly 450 words. A 5-minute video requires roughly 750 words. The script you produce here becomes the exact text fed to the voiceover tool in Phase 3.
  1. Open your LLM of choice in a browser tab. Claude at claude.ai and ChatGPT at chatgpt.com both have free tiers that do not require a credit card. Either works for this task.
  2. Paste the following prompt, filling in the bracketed sections with your topic from Phase 1. Copy the entire block as-is:
    Write a YouTube video script for a faceless educational channel. The video topic is: [YOUR ONE-SENTENCE TOPIC FROM PHASE 1]. Requirements: - Total length: 500 to 700 words (approximately 4 minutes at a calm narration pace) - Structure: Hook (first 30 seconds, open with a surprising fact or a direct question), Main Content (3 clear sections, each with a heading in the script), Recap (one sentence per section), Call to Action (ask viewers to subscribe for more videos like this) - Tone: clear, calm, and informative; avoid hype language and superlatives - Format the script as plain prose, not bullet points; it should read naturally when spoken aloud - Mark each section with a label in brackets like [HOOK], [SECTION 1: title], [SECTION 2: title], [SECTION 3: title], [RECAP], [CTA] - Do not include any stage directions, music cues, or B-roll notes; those are handled separately Do not start any sentence with "I" (the channel has no on-screen host).
    Why this prompt structure works: the section labels give you clear cut points for the visual layer in Phase 4. The word count target maps to a real voiceover duration. The prohibition on stage directions keeps the script clean for text-to-speech input in Phase 3.
  3. Review the output critically before accepting it. Read the script aloud to yourself. Does it flow naturally when spoken? Does the hook grab your attention in the first sentence? Is the information in each section actually accurate? Correct any factual errors before saving — you are responsible for the claims in the video, not the LLM.
  4. Revise with a follow-up prompt if needed. If a section is too dense or the hook is weak, ask the LLM to rewrite just that section:
    Rewrite only the [HOOK] section. Make the opening question more specific and surprising. Keep it under 60 words.
  5. Save the final script to your scripts folder. Copy the text and save it as a plain text file named after your topic:
    ~/faceless-yt/scripts/ep01-emergency-fund.txt
    Replace ep01-emergency-fund with a short slug for your actual topic. Use hyphens, no spaces. This file is the master source for Phase 3.
Script sounds robotic or unnatural when you read it aloud?
  • Ask the LLM to shorten sentences: "Rewrite this paragraph so no sentence is longer than 18 words." Shorter sentences read more naturally in TTS audio.
  • Remove all em-dashes and semicolons from the script file before feeding it to edge-tts in Phase 3. Some TTS voices pause awkwardly on these punctuation marks. Replace them with periods or commas.
  • Avoid parenthetical asides in the script. TTS engines read them with the same pacing as regular text, so they sound odd. Put the information inline or cut it.
Checkpoint — artifact: ep01-your-topic.txt
You have a script file saved in ~/faceless-yt/scripts/. The file contains a complete script with clearly labeled sections: [HOOK], three content sections, [RECAP], and [CTA]. The word count is between 450 and 750 words. You have read it aloud once and corrected any section that felt awkward. Do not move to Phase 3 until the script reads smoothly from start to finish.

Voiceover Generation with edge-tts
edge-tts is a free Python package (version 7.2.8, LGPLv3 license) that connects to Microsoft Edge's online text-to-speech service. It produces MP3 audio from your script text with over 100 available voices. It is completely free with no character limits and no account required. The output quality is significantly better than older offline TTS engines. This phase produces your voiceover.mp3 file.
  1. Install edge-tts using pip. Open a terminal (Command Prompt or PowerShell on Windows, Terminal on macOS/Linux):
    pip install edge-tts
    Confirm the install:
    edge-tts --version
    Expected output: 7.2.8 or similar.
  2. List available English voices to pick one.
    edge-tts --list-voices | grep en-US
    A partial list of reliable options:
    en-US-ChristopherNeural (male, authoritative) en-US-JennyNeural (female, conversational) en-US-GuyNeural (male, informational) en-US-AriaNeural (female, natural) en-US-EricNeural (male, calm)
    Choosing a voice: for educational content, en-US-ChristopherNeural and en-US-AriaNeural tend to produce the most natural-sounding pacing. Try two voices on the first paragraph of your script before committing to one for the full file.
  3. Test your chosen voice on the first paragraph of the script. Copy just the [HOOK] section into a file called test.txt and run:
    edge-tts --voice en-US-ChristopherNeural --file ~/faceless-yt/scripts/test.txt --write-media ~/faceless-yt/voiceovers/test.mp3
    Play the output file to check pacing and naturalness. If the voice does not suit your content, try another from the list in step 2.
  4. Generate the full voiceover from your complete script file. Replace the voice name and file paths with your choices:
    edge-tts --voice en-US-ChristopherNeural --file ~/faceless-yt/scripts/ep01-emergency-fund.txt --write-media ~/faceless-yt/voiceovers/ep01-voiceover.mp3
    The command produces an MP3 file in your voiceovers folder. Generation takes a few seconds for a 500-word script.
  5. Listen to the complete voiceover file before moving on. Open the MP3 and listen from start to finish. Note the total duration — a well-paced 500-word script produces roughly 3 to 3.5 minutes of audio. Write the duration down; you will need it when sourcing visuals in Phase 4.
edge-tts command not found after install?
  • On Windows, pip may install the tool in a directory not on your PATH. Try python -m edge_tts instead of edge-tts, or open a new Command Prompt window after the install to pick up the updated PATH.
  • If the audio output is silent or corrupt, your internet connection may have dropped mid-generation. edge-tts requires an active connection because it uses Microsoft Edge's cloud TTS service. Re-run the command on a stable connection.
  • Want a premium voice? ElevenLabs (elevenlabs.io) offers a free tier with 10,000 credits per month. The credit-to-character rate varies by model (roughly 0.5–1 credit per character; the legacy English/multilingual models bill 1:1), so 10,000 credits covers at least ~10,000 characters — comfortably more than one 700-word script (roughly 3,500 characters). After the free tier is exhausted for the month, the Starter plan is $6/month for 30,000 credits. ElevenLabs voice quality is higher than edge-tts; use it as an upgrade once you confirm the pipeline works end-to-end.
Checkpoint — artifact: ep01-voiceover.mp3
You have an MP3 file in ~/faceless-yt/voiceovers/. You have listened to it in full and it matches your script. The audio is clear, the pacing is natural, and you know the exact duration in minutes and seconds. Write the duration in your topic-brief.txt from Phase 1 before moving on.

Visuals and B-Roll Sourcing
A faceless video needs one visual change roughly every 4 to 6 seconds to keep viewers watching. For a 3.5-minute video that is approximately 35 to 50 visual segments. This phase covers how to source those visuals legally and for free using Pexels and Pixabay, and how to organize them so the editing step in Phase 5 is not chaos.
  1. Read your script and mark visual cue points. Go through your script file and every time the topic shifts — a new concept, a new example, a transition — add a note in brackets with a one-word or two-word visual description. For example:
    [VISUAL: coin stack] Most people never build an emergency fund because the starting amount feels impossibly large. [VISUAL: stressed person at desk] When an unexpected expense hits, the only option becomes debt. [VISUAL: calendar 90 days] The 90-day method breaks the goal into three monthly targets of roughly $333 each.
    This annotated script is your shot list. You are not yet sourcing anything — just building the list.
  2. Source video clips from Pexels. Go to pexels.com/videos. The Pexels license permits free use in YouTube videos including commercial use. No attribution is required (though it is appreciated). Search for each visual keyword from your shot list. Download the clip as MP4, medium resolution (720p or 1080p is sufficient). Save each file to ~/faceless-yt/visuals/ with a descriptive name like coin-stack.mp4.
    Pexels license summary: photos and videos are free to use, modify, and distribute for personal and commercial purposes including YouTube. The restriction is that you may not sell unaltered Pexels content as a standalone product or use it on competing stock photo platforms. Using clips in a YouTube video you publish is explicitly permitted.
  3. Fill gaps with Pixabay. Go to pixabay.com/videos. Pixabay's content license is similar to Pexels — free for commercial use including YouTube. If Pexels does not have a clip that matches your visual cue, search Pixabay as a second source. Download and save to the same visuals folder.
  4. For abstract or concept visuals, use still images. Pexels (pexels.com) also hosts photos. A still image held for 4 to 6 seconds with a slow zoom effect (a Ken Burns effect available in most free editors) works well for concepts that are hard to represent with video. Download stills to the same visuals folder as JPG or PNG.
  5. Verify your visual count against your voiceover duration. Count the clips you downloaded. Divide your voiceover duration in seconds by 5. That is the minimum number of clips you need for one visual change every 5 seconds. If you have 3.5 minutes of audio (210 seconds), you need at least 42 clips. Download more if short.
    Quality check: look at each clip before saving. Avoid clips with visible watermarks from other services, clips where a face is prominently featured (viewer attention shifts to the face and away from your narration), and clips with embedded audio that will conflict with your voiceover track in Phase 5.
Cannot find clips that match your topic?
  • Search for the concept rather than the specific thing. If your video is about budgeting and you cannot find a clip of someone using a budget spreadsheet, search "person laptop" or "desk work" — abstract work scenes cut fine over financial narration.
  • Avoid searching for brand names or specific products — those clips rarely exist on free stock sites. Stay with general concepts: people, places, objects, nature, technology.
  • YouTube's built-in Audio Library (studio.youtube.com, Audio Library tab) also has free stock footage in some regions. Check under "Video" if available in your country.
  • Content ID warning: do not use clips from other YouTube videos, TV shows, movies, or news broadcasts even if they appear on a free download site. YouTube's Content ID system will detect the original source and may mute your audio or claim your video's revenue. Only use sources with an explicit free commercial-use license like Pexels and Pixabay.
Checkpoint — artifact: ~/faceless-yt/visuals/ folder
Your visuals folder contains enough clips and images to cover your full voiceover duration at one visual per 5 seconds. Each file has a descriptive name matching the visual cue in your annotated script. Every file came from Pexels or Pixabay. No file has a visible watermark. You can match each file to a specific line or section of your script.

Video Assembly in CapCut Desktop
CapCut Desktop (capcut.com) is free to download and use, and the desktop app historically exports standard videos without a watermark — but ByteDance shifts features between free and paid tiers, so confirm there is no watermark on your test export before committing (Phase 5 includes a check step). If a watermark appears, switch to DaVinci Resolve (free, blackmagicdesign.com) or Shotcut (free, shotcut.org), which never watermark exports. This phase walks through assembling your voiceover and visuals into a complete final.mp4 at 1920×1080 (1080p); with another editor the same logical steps apply — only the button names differ.
  1. Open CapCut Desktop and create a new project. Click New Project. In the resolution settings, select 16:9 and 1080p. This sets the canvas to 1920×1080 pixels, which is the standard resolution for YouTube uploads.
  2. Import your voiceover as the first track. Click Import in the media panel and select your ep01-voiceover.mp3 from the voiceovers folder. Drag it onto the timeline. It will appear as an audio track at the bottom of the timeline. This is your master timeline length — the video ends when this audio ends.
  3. Import all your visual clips and images. Click Import again and select all files from your visuals folder (Ctrl+A or Cmd+A to select all). They appear in the media panel.
  4. Place visuals on the video track above the audio, working through your annotated script. Drag clips onto the video track in order, matching each clip to the corresponding section of narration. Trim each clip by dragging its edges so cuts land on the natural pauses in the voiceover. Aim for a visual change every 4 to 6 seconds. You do not need to be frame-precise — clean cuts on breaths and pauses are enough.
  5. Add background music. YouTube Studio provides a free Audio Library at studio.youtube.com under the Audio Library tab. Download a royalty-free instrumental track (search for "ambient" or "lo-fi" to find calm background music). Import it into CapCut and add it as a second audio track below your voiceover. In the audio settings for the music track, lower its volume to approximately 10 to 15 percent of the voiceover volume so the narration stays dominant.
  6. Add auto-captions. In CapCut, click Text in the top toolbar, then Auto Captions. Select your voiceover audio track as the source and click Generate. CapCut transcribes the audio and places caption segments on a caption track. Review them for accuracy and fix any mis-transcribed words. Captions significantly improve watch time and make your video accessible.
  7. Export the finished video. Click Export in the top-right corner. Settings: Resolution 1080p, Frame rate 30 fps, Format MP4, Quality High. Export to ~/faceless-yt/exports/ep01-final.mp4. Export typically takes 2 to 10 minutes depending on video length and computer speed.
  8. Do a complete watch-through of the exported file. Play ep01-final.mp4 from start to finish in your system video player (not inside CapCut). Verify: audio is clear throughout, visuals match the narration, captions are readable and accurate, and there is no CapCut watermark in the corner. If a watermark appears, confirm you are using CapCut Desktop (not the web version) and re-export.
Audio and video are out of sync in the export?
  • This is almost always caused by the background music track having a different sample rate than the voiceover MP3. In CapCut, right-click the music track and detach the audio, then re-attach it. Alternatively, convert your music file to 44100 Hz WAV using Audacity (free, audacityteam.org) before importing it.
  • If specific clips stutter or freeze on playback inside CapCut but the export is clean, that is a preview rendering issue, not a real problem. Always judge quality from the exported file, not the CapCut preview.
  • YouTube's upload limit is 256 GB or 12 hours, whichever is less. A 4 to 5 minute video at 1080p exports to roughly 200 to 500 MB — far inside both limits, so this is never a concern for this project.
Checkpoint — artifact: ep01-final.mp4
You have a file at ~/faceless-yt/exports/ep01-final.mp4. The file plays completely with synchronized audio and video. Visuals change at least once every 6 seconds. Background music is audible but does not overpower the voiceover. Captions appear on screen. There is no watermark. The video is between 3 and 6 minutes long.

Thumbnail, Title, and SEO Metadata
The thumbnail and title are the two things a viewer sees before deciding whether to click. A video with a weak thumbnail gets ignored regardless of content quality. This phase produces a finished thumbnail at 1280×720 pixels and fills in all the YouTube metadata fields that affect discoverability.
  1. Create the thumbnail in Canva (free at canva.com). Create a free account if you do not have one. Click Create a design, choose Custom size, and enter 1280 width and 720 height (pixels). This is YouTube's recommended thumbnail resolution.
  2. Design the thumbnail with these rules:
    • Use a high-contrast background color that stands out in a list of video thumbnails. Avoid white — it blends into YouTube's light mode background.
    • Add a maximum of 5 words of text. The text must be legible at a very small size (thumbnails appear at about 200 pixels wide on mobile). Use a bold font at 90 points or larger.
    • Use a striking image as the background — sourced from Pexels, using the same license as your video clips. A single strong visual object or scene works better than a collage.
    • Do not use a face unless a face is genuinely relevant to your content. For a faceless channel, a compelling graphic or object works equally well.
  3. Download the thumbnail from Canva. Click Share, then Download, format PNG. Save to ~/faceless-yt/thumbs/ep01-thumb.png. Canva's free tier does not add watermarks to standard designs on a custom canvas.
  4. Write your video title. Use this formula: [Specific Number or Trigger Word] + [Topic] + [Concrete Benefit or Curiosity Gap]. Examples:
    How to Build a $1,000 Emergency Fund in 90 Days The 3-Step Method That Actually Pays Off Debt Why Most People Never Save Money (And How to Fix It)
    Keep the title under 70 characters so it does not truncate in search results. Include the most important keyword from your autocomplete research in the title.
  5. Write a YouTube description. The first two lines appear before the "Show more" cutoff and are the most important for both viewers and search. Use this structure:
    Line 1: Restate the core benefit from your title in a full sentence. Line 2: Include your primary keyword naturally in a sentence. Timestamps: 0:00 Introduction 0:35 [Section 1 title from your script] 1:45 [Section 2 title from your script] 3:00 [Section 3 title from your script] 4:10 Recap and next steps [2-3 sentences about the channel and what type of content you post] #YourNiche #KeywordOne #KeywordTwo
    Timestamps improve watch time by giving viewers navigation points. They also appear in Google search results for your video.
  6. Prepare 8 to 12 tags. In YouTube Studio, the Tags field accepts comma-separated keywords. Use a mix of broad terms and specific phrases:
    emergency fund, how to save money, personal finance for beginners, budgeting tips, save $1000, debt payoff, financial independence, money saving challenge
    Replace these with tags relevant to your actual topic. Tags are a secondary ranking signal; they matter less than the title and description but are worth filling in.
Thumbnail looks blurry or text is hard to read?
  • YouTube compresses thumbnails. Design at 1280×720 (as specified) and use PNG format (not JPEG) for the cleanest export. JPEG compression adds artifacts that are especially visible on text edges.
  • If text is hard to read, add a semi-transparent dark rectangle behind the text in Canva: click Elements, add a square, set it to black at 50% opacity, and position it behind the text layer. This creates a readable contrast backdrop regardless of the background image.
Checkpoint — artifact: ep01-thumb.png + metadata
You have ~/faceless-yt/thumbs/ep01-thumb.png at 1280×720 pixels with no Canva watermark. You have a written title under 70 characters that follows the formula. You have a written description with the first two lines keyword-optimized and a timestamp list. You have 8 to 12 tags written out. All four items — thumbnail, title, description, tags — must be ready before you go to Phase 7.

Upload to YouTube Studio with AI Disclosure
This phase covers the complete YouTube Studio upload flow including the AI-content disclosure toggle, which YouTube requires when a video realistically depicts real people or events with AI. Skipping the disclosure on content that genuinely requires it can result in content removal or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program, so this phase shows you when it applies and the safe default. The phase ends with your video live at a real youtube.com/watch URL.
  1. Create your YouTube channel if you have not already done so. Go to youtube.com, sign in with your Google account, click your profile icon, and click Create a channel. Give the channel a name that reflects your niche. Upload a simple channel icon (at least 800×800 pixels) and a banner image (2560×1440 pixels) — both can be created in Canva on the free tier.
  2. Go to YouTube Studio and start an upload. Navigate to studio.youtube.com. Click Create in the top-right corner, then Upload videos. Click Select files and choose your ep01-final.mp4 from the exports folder. The upload begins immediately in the background while you fill in the metadata.
  3. Fill in the Details tab.
    • Title: paste your title from Phase 6.
    • Description: paste your description from Phase 6.
    • Thumbnail: click Upload thumbnail and select your ep01-thumb.png.
    • Playlist: create a new playlist named after your channel's topic and add this video to it. Playlists are a watch-time signal.
  4. Set the AI-content disclosure. Scroll down in the Details tab to the section labeled Altered content or AI use (the label may vary slightly by region), in the Attributes section. YouTube applies a viewer-facing label when you select Yes. For an AI-generated/AI-voiced video, the safe default is to select Yes.
    What the policy actually says: YouTube's formal disclosure requirement targets content that meaningfully alters or generates photorealistic material that could mislead viewers about something real — making a real person appear to say or do something, or altering footage of a real event. YouTube's own examples state that a plain AI voiceover (such as cloning your own voice for narration) does NOT by itself require disclosure. So an AI TTS voice reading an original script is a grey area, not a clear-cut requirement. Best practice: when in doubt, disclose — the label does not reduce reach or monetization, and it keeps you safely on the right side of the rule.
    What happens if you skip the disclosure?
    • YouTube may manually apply a label to your video without your input.
    • Repeat non-disclosure can result in penalties including content removal or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program.
    • The safest rule: if any audio, image, or video in your production was generated or substantially altered by AI, disclose it. The label does not hurt your video's performance — it simply informs viewers.
  5. Add tags in the Details tab. Click Show more at the bottom of the Details section to reveal the Tags field. Paste your comma-separated tags from Phase 6.
  6. Set audience and age restriction. On the same page, answer the Is this video made for kids? question. For most educational content targeting adults, select No, it's not made for kids. This is important: marking a video as "made for kids" disables comments, notifications, and most engagement features.
  7. Click through Video elements and Checks. Add an End screen in the Video elements tab (YouTube provides a template — add a "Subscribe" element and a "Latest video" element, both placed in the last 20 seconds of your video). The Checks tab scans for copyright issues — confirm no issues are flagged before proceeding. If a copyright claim appears, it is almost certainly from background music. Replace the music track with a track from YouTube's own Audio Library and re-export.
  8. Set visibility and publish. In the Visibility tab, select Public. You can also schedule the publish time to a specific date and time if you prefer. Click Publish (or Schedule). YouTube processes the video and makes it live within a few minutes for most uploads.
  9. Copy and save the video URL. After publishing, YouTube shows you the video URL in the format https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXXXX. Copy this URL and save it to your topic-brief.txt file from Phase 1.
Upload stuck at processing or failed?
  • YouTube processes 1080p video in approximately 5 to 30 minutes after the file finishes uploading. The video is technically live and accessible via its URL even while "processing" — early viewers see a lower-resolution version until processing finishes.
  • If the upload fails, check your file: open ep01-final.mp4 in VLC or another player and confirm it plays completely. A corrupted export will fail the YouTube upload. Re-export from CapCut if the file is damaged.
  • Copyright claim on your background music? Go to YouTube's Audio Library (studio.youtube.com, left sidebar, Audio Library). Every track in that library is pre-cleared for YouTube use with no Content ID claims. Download one, replace the music in your CapCut project, and re-export. This is a one-time fix per track.
A note on monetization expectations.
  • YouTube Partner Program eligibility requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours of public watch time in the last 12 months. There is no shortcut: those numbers come from real viewers choosing to watch your content.
  • YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy explicitly targets AI-generated videos that use generic templates with no original perspective, are mass-produced, or give the impression of scale without genuine creative input. Channels built on this model face channel-wide demonetization, not just individual video removal. (See YouTube's current "reused content" / "inauthentic content" help pages for the live wording.)
  • This project teaches you the production pipeline. The content you put into that pipeline — the research, the angle, the specific insights in the script — is what determines whether the channel builds an audience or does not. A well-produced video on a topic you know something about will always outperform a mass-produced AI template.
Checkpoint — artifact: a live youtube.com/watch URL
Your video is published and accessible at a real youtube.com/watch?v= URL. The thumbnail appears correctly. The title and description are filled in. The AI-content disclosure is set. The End screen elements are in place. Open your video URL in an incognito browser window and watch the first 30 seconds to confirm the voiceover, visuals, and captions all appear correctly for a viewer who is not signed into your account.
