    Section 1: Presentations (10 FEH modules + Vault decks)

    FEH-01: The Hacker Mindset. Opens the offensive curriculum. Covers ethics and legality of hacking, hacker types (white/grey/black hat), the CIA Triad, the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), and the UK Computer Misuse Act (CMA). Walks the 7-phase Cyber Kill Chain (reconnaissance through actions on objectives) and maps it to MITRE ATT&CK tactic structure. Introduces Rules of Engagement, the penetration testing methodology, and career paths in ethical hacking. Sets the tone for the entire course: the hacker mindset is a tool, not an identity, and intent is the line between research and crime.

    FEH-02: Reconnaissance & OSINT. Distinguishes passive from active reconnaissance and covers the OSINT discipline end to end. Google dorking operators with examples, WHOIS lookups, DNS record types and zone transfers, Shodan (IoT search engine), email harvesting, and Maltego. Techniques for metadata analysis (EXIF, document metadata), social media OSINT across LinkedIn, Twitter/X, and GitHub. Teaches how to synthesize disparate signals into a target profile, and discusses defensive countermeasures against reconnaissance.

    FEH-03: Scanning & Enumeration. TCP/IP three-way handshake, TCP flags, port states (open, closed, filtered), and scanning methodology. Heavy Nmap coverage: SYN scan, connect scan, UDP scan, version detection, OS fingerprinting, and the NSE scripting engine. Vulnerability scanners. Banner grabbing, SMB enumeration, SNMP enumeration, LDAP and Active Directory enumeration. Scan evasion via timing controls, fragmentation, and decoy scanning. Null session attacks.

    FEH-04: Vulnerability Assessment. Vulnerability lifecycle, CVE system structure, CVSS v3.1 scoring (metric groups, score ranges) with awareness of CVSS v4.0. NVD, Exploit-DB, and scanner ecosystem (Nessus, OpenVAS, Qualys). Distinguishes false positives from false negatives. Risk formulas, risk matrices, prioritization frameworks, remediation strategies, and the structure of a defensible vulnerability report.

    FEH-05: System Exploitation. Exploitation overview (types, goals, PoC vs. exploit). Password attacks: dictionary, brute force, hybrid, rainbow tables. Hashcat and John the Ripper. Metasploit Framework structure, workflow, and Meterpreter. Linux privilege escalation vectors (SUID, sudo misconfig, cron jobs, kernel exploits, PATH manipulation). Windows privilege escalation (unquoted service paths, DLL hijacking, token impersonation). Post-exploitation including credential harvesting, persistence, covering tracks, log manipulation, and timestomping.

    FEH-06: Web Application Security. Client-server model and a deep dive on the OWASP Top 10 (2021). A01 Broken Access Control covers IDOR, privilege escalation, and forced browsing; A02 Cryptographic Failures; A03 Injection consolidates SQLi (UNION-based, blind, second-order) and XSS (reflected, stored, DOM-based); A05 Security Misconfiguration; A07 Identification and Authentication Failures; A08 Software and Data Integrity Failures; CSRF (now under A01); A10 SSRF. Also covers WAF functionality and bypass techniques, plus bug bounty programs and responsible disclosure.

    FEH-07: Network Attacks & Defense. Passive vs. active and internal vs. external attack classifications. Packet sniffing with Wireshark and the catalog of vulnerable clear-text protocols. ARP poisoning, Man-in-the-Middle (SSL stripping), session hijacking, DNS attacks (cache poisoning, spoofing, tunneling), DHCP attacks (rogue server, starvation), DoS/DDoS (SYN flood, amplification, Layer 7), wireless attacks (evil twin, deauthentication, WPA/WPA2 cracking), and VLAN hopping (switch spoofing, double tagging). Defense section covers IDS/IPS, network segmentation, and Zero Trust Architecture.

    FEH-08: Social Engineering. Psychological foundations of human exploitation: cognitive biases and Cialdini's principles of persuasion. Phishing email types with red flags, vishing, smishing, pretexting (process, scenarios, the long game), physical social engineering (tailgating, piggybacking, smoking area attacks), baiting, and quid pro quo. Case studies of the Twitter Bitcoin Scam (2020), the RSA SecurID Breach (2011), and the Target Data Breach (2013). Defense via security awareness programs and structured red-team SE engagement methodology.

    FEH-09: Cryptography & Steganography. Core terminology, symmetric encryption (AES, block vs. stream ciphers), asymmetric encryption (RSA, Diffie-Hellman), and cryptographic hashing (MD5, SHA family). Digital signatures and PKI (X.509 certificates, certificate chain of trust). TLS/SSL handshake, versions, Perfect Forward Secrecy. Attacks: rainbow tables, side-channel attacks. Steganography via LSB technique, common tools, and disk/file encryption (VeraCrypt, FDE solutions). Coordinates with House Key's cryptography curriculum.

    FEH-10: Malware Analysis Basics. Malware classification and delivery methods (phishing, drive-by download, watering hole, supply chain compromise). Static analysis (file hashing, strings, PE header analysis), dynamic analysis (sandbox environments, execution monitoring), and behavioral analysis (network activity, filesystem modifications, registry analysis). Anti-analysis techniques, Indicators of Compromise (YARA rules, IOC sharing formats), sandbox tools (Cuckoo and cloud-based), reverse engineering introduction, malware families and APT groups, and Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS).

    Vault Presentations. Standalone decks inside the Vault include Botnet Architecture & C2 (centralized, P2P, hybrid C2; infection lifecycle; famous botnets; detection and takedown), Cyber Kill Chain (7-phase framework with ATT&CK mapping), CEH Attack Reference (Kill Chain phases plus MITRE tactics with knowledge check), and Sandbox Setup Guide (REMnux, FlareVM, network isolation, VM anti-detection).
