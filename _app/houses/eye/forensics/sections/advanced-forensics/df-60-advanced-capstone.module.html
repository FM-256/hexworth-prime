Learning Objectives

  • Investigate a multi-source APT compromise across an enterprise network
  • Detect anti-forensics techniques within the evidence
  • Correlate cloud logs, memory dumps, disk images, network captures, email, mobile, and IoT evidence
  • Attribute the attack to a threat actor using MITRE ATT&CK mapping
  • Produce an executive summary and technical investigation report

Scenario: Operation Shadow Gate

Nexus Defense Corp (NDC), a defense contractor handling Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), has detected anomalous outbound traffic from their R&D network to an IP address associated with a nation-state threat actor (APT-41). The anomaly was detected by the SIEM on 2026-03-16 at 08:00 UTC, but CloudTrail logs suggest the compromise began weeks earlier.

Your task is to analyze evidence from seven distinct sources, detect anti-forensics activity, build a comprehensive timeline, attribute the attack, and produce both an executive summary and a technical report.

Scope

Seven evidence sources across cloud, endpoint, network, email, mobile, and IoT domains. This investigation spans 30+ days of attacker activity across 5 systems. Anti-forensics techniques are present -- your analysis must identify and account for them.

Anti-Forensics Detected

During your analysis, identify the following anti-forensics techniques used by the attacker:

TimestompingMalware dropped in C:\Windows\System32\ has $SI timestamps matching legitimate OS files (2020), but $FN timestamps show 2026-02-28 creation. MFT analysis reveals the discrepancy.
Log ClearingSecurity event log on WS-RDEV01 contains only Event ID 1102 dated 2026-03-10. All prior security events were destroyed. System log shows Event ID 104 for additional log channels.
DNS TunnelingC2 communication disguised as legitimate DNS queries. Encoded data in subdomain labels: aGVsbG8.data.c2-domain.xyz. High volume of TXT queries to a single domain.
CloudTrail TamperingThe attacker disabled CloudTrail logging for 4 hours on 2026-03-08 using compromised admin credentials. A "StopLogging" event exists, followed by "StartLogging" -- but the gap cannot be recovered.

Attribution Confidence

ATT&CK mapping alone does not confirm attribution. Multiple threat actors use the same techniques. Attribution confidence increases when you combine TTP analysis with infrastructure analysis (IP registration, domain history, SSL certificates), malware code similarity, targeting patterns, and operational timing (working hours consistent with a specific time zone). In this case, the combination of defense contractor targeting, Cobalt Strike with DNS tunneling, and PDF exploitation matches APT-41 reporting from Mandiant and CrowdStrike at a MODERATE-HIGH confidence level.

Capstone Questions

  1. What was the total dwell time from initial compromise to detection?
  2. How would you quantify the data exfiltration volume from the DNS tunneling evidence?
  3. What regulatory notifications are required given that CUI was compromised? (Hint: DFARS 252.204-7012)
  4. The CloudTrail gap on March 8 means you have no visibility into 4 hours of attacker activity. How do you address this in your report?
  5. What is the significance of the physical badge access on March 12?

Key Answers: (1) 17 days (Feb 28 to Mar 16). (2) Calculate total bytes in DNS TXT query responses to the C2 domain. Each response can carry ~255 bytes of encoded data. Multiply response count by average payload size. (3) DFARS 252.204-7012 requires notification to the DoD CIO within 72 hours of discovery, plus preservation of evidence for 90 days. CMMC incident reporting requirements also apply. (4) Acknowledge the gap explicitly. State that activities during this window cannot be determined from available evidence. Recommend reviewing other log sources (VPC Flow Logs, S3 access logs) that may not have been disabled. (5) Physical access to the secure lab suggests the attacker may have compromised the engineer's badge credentials or the engineer was complicit. It also means physical evidence (documents, hardware) may have been accessed or modified -- expand the investigation scope.

Key Takeaways

  • APT investigations require correlating evidence across cloud, endpoint, network, email, mobile, and physical sources
  • Anti-forensics techniques are expected in APT cases -- your analysis must detect and document them
  • Evidence gaps (like CloudTrail disabled periods) must be acknowledged explicitly in reports
  • Threat attribution relies on TTP analysis mapped to MITRE ATT&CK group profiles
  • Defense contractor compromises trigger mandatory DFARS reporting within 72 hours
  • Physical access evidence (badge logs) can expand investigation scope significantly
  • The executive summary must be actionable for non-technical leadership
