Scenario: Insider Threat Triggered by USB Drives

Your organization suffered a data exfiltration incident where an insider copied sensitive customer data to a personal USB drive. Leadership has asked you to draft a Removable Media Policy to prevent recurrence. Complete the policy builder below.

Policy Document Structure

  • Purpose: Why this policy exists (1-2 sentences)
  • Scope: Who and what systems this policy covers
  • Policy Statements: The mandatory rules (shall/shall not language)
  • Standards: Specific technical requirements that implement the policy
  • Responsibilities: Who enforces and who complies
  • Exceptions: Process for requesting policy exceptions
  • Review Cycle: How often the policy is reviewed and updated

Scenario: Multi-Industry Compliance Determination

For each organization described below, determine which regulations apply by selecting Yes, No, or Partial from each regulatory column. This simulates the compliance scoping exercise performed at the start of any security program assessment.

Organization HIPAA PCI DSS GLBA SOX FERPA
Public hospital accepting insurance and credit card payments, listed on NYSE
Private university that also operates a credit union for students and staff
E-commerce startup (private company, sells through Shopify, uses Stripe for payments)

Answer Key — Regulatory Mapping

  • Public hospital (NYSE): HIPAA: YES (healthcare provider). PCI DSS: YES (takes card payments). GLBA: NO (not a financial institution). SOX: YES (public company). FERPA: NO (not an educational institution).
  • Private university with credit union: HIPAA: Partial (student health center handles PHI). PCI DSS: YES (campus stores accept cards). GLBA: YES (credit union is a financial institution). SOX: NO (private). FERPA: YES (university holds student educational records).
  • E-commerce startup (uses Stripe): HIPAA: NO. PCI DSS: Yes/Partial (even using Stripe, they may still be in scope depending on how payment data flows). GLBA: NO. SOX: NO (private). FERPA: NO. Note: using Stripe/Shopify reduces PCI scope but does not eliminate it entirely.

Control Classification and Security Awareness
Pending

Scenario: Building a Defense-in-Depth Control Set

For each security scenario, classify the controls by type (Technical/Operational/Managerial) and by function (Preventive/Detective/Corrective/Deterrent/Physical/Compensating). Then identify gaps in the control set.

Answer Key — Control Classification

  • A (Warning Banner): Technical (displayed by system) + Managerial (policy-driven). Function: Deterrent
  • B (SIEM brute-force alert): Technical. Function: Detective
  • C (Phishing awareness training): Operational. Function: Corrective (post-incident training) / also Preventive for future incidents
  • D (Biometric + badge + PIN server room): Technical (biometric/badge reader) + Physical (secured room). Function: Preventive + Physical
  • E (Enhanced monitoring instead of MFA): Technical. Function: Compensating
