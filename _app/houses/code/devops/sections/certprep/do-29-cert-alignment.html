1. Why Certify?

Certifications are not magic keys to employment. They are structured proof that you understand a technology domain well enough to pass a proctored, timed exam designed by the vendor or an independent body. They work best when combined with hands-on experience — and the Forge exists to give you that experience.

What Certifications Actually Prove

Baseline Knowledge You understand the core concepts, services, and vocabulary. Hiring managers can skip "do you know what a VPC is?" and go straight to architecture discussions.
Commitment Studying for and passing an exam demonstrates discipline and self-motivation — traits employers value beyond the technical content itself.
Structured Learning Exam objectives force you to cover the full surface area of a technology, including areas you might skip during project-based learning.
Career Leverage Certifications often translate directly to higher salaries. AWS-certified engineers report 20-30% salary premiums. Many government and enterprise contracts require certified staff.
The Forge Advantage

Every module you complete in the Forge maps to real certification exam objectives. This guide shows you exactly which modules cover which domains — so you can study with purpose and know where you stand before you sit for an exam.

2. AWS Certifications

Amazon Web Services offers the most recognized cloud certification program. The path starts broad and narrows into specialty areas. For DevOps engineers, three certifications form the standard progression.

Cloud Practitioner FOUNDATIONAL

High-level understanding of AWS cloud concepts, services, pricing, and security. No hands-on coding required.

90 min | 65 questions | ~$100 | Pass: 700/1000
Solutions Architect Associate ASSOCIATE

Design resilient, cost-optimized architectures. Covers VPC, EC2, S3, RDS, Lambda, IAM, and high availability patterns.

130 min | 65 questions | ~$150 | Pass: 720/1000
DevOps Engineer Professional PROFESSIONAL

CI/CD on AWS, infrastructure as code (CloudFormation, CDK), monitoring, incident response, and operational excellence.

180 min | 75 questions | ~$300 | Pass: 750/1000

4. CKA & CKAD

The Certified Kubernetes Administrator (CKA) and Certified Kubernetes Application Developer (CKAD) are performance-based exams administered by the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF). Unlike multiple-choice exams, you solve real tasks on a live Kubernetes cluster in a browser-based terminal.

CKA ADMINISTRATOR

Cluster setup, networking, storage, security, troubleshooting. You build and fix clusters hands-on during the exam.

120 min | 15-20 tasks | ~$395 | Pass: 66% | Performance-based
CKAD DEVELOPER

Design, build, and configure cloud-native applications on Kubernetes. Focus on pods, deployments, services, and configuration.

120 min | 15-20 tasks | ~$395 | Pass: 66% | Performance-based

9. Study Strategies

Passing a certification exam is not about memorization. It is about building mental models through structured practice, then validating those models under exam conditions. Here are the strategies that consistently produce results.

Spaced Repetition

Review material at increasing intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days. This fights the forgetting curve more effectively than cramming. Tools like Anki or plain flashcards work well for command syntax and service names.

Hands-On Labs

Every hour of hands-on practice is worth three hours of reading. For cloud certs, use free tiers (AWS, Azure, GCP all offer them). For Kubernetes, use minikube, kind, or k3s. For Terraform, provision local Docker containers before touching cloud resources.

Practice Exams

Take practice exams under timed conditions at least one week before your real exam. Do not just check answers — review every question you got wrong AND every question you were uncertain about. The goal is 85%+ on practice exams before scheduling the real one.

Week 1-2 Complete all relevant Forge modules. Read official exam guides. Build a study plan aligned to exam domains.
Week 3-4 Hands-on labs daily. Build real projects using the technology. Start flashcard reviews for terminology and commands.
Week 5-6 Practice exams under timed conditions. Review weak areas. Re-do labs for domains scoring below 80%.
Final Week Light review only. Focus on rest and confidence. Re-read exam objectives one final time. Schedule exam for morning when you are sharpest.
Time Management on Exam Day

Flag difficult questions and move on. Answer every question — there is no penalty for guessing on multiple-choice exams. For performance-based exams (CKA, RHCSA), do the easy tasks first to bank points, then return to complex scenarios.

Suggested Order by Career Stage

Stage Certifications Why This Order
Entry CompTIA Linux+ → GitHub Foundations → Terraform Associate Builds foundational Linux and Git skills before cloud-specific tooling. Terraform is low-cost and high-value as a first cloud cert.
Mid AWS SAA or AZ-104 → GitHub Actions → CKA Cloud architect fundamentals open doors. GitHub Actions validates CI/CD skills. CKA proves container orchestration ability.
Senior AWS DevOps Pro or AZ-400 → CKAD → RHCE Professional/expert certs demonstrate depth. CKAD adds developer perspective. RHCE proves automation mastery with Ansible.
Pick One Cloud

Do not try to certify on both AWS and Azure simultaneously. Choose the platform your employer uses (or the one more common in your job market), certify there, and then expand if needed. The concepts transfer — the second cloud cert is always easier.
