Understanding Microsoft 365 licensing is essential for both the MS-900 exam and real-world administration. This chapter covers how M365 is priced, how to manage subscriptions through the admin center, the support options available, and the Modern Lifecycle Policy that governs feature updates and deprecation.

Microsoft 365 Pricing Models
Per-user, per-month subscription pricing with annual and monthly commitment options.
Cost Optimization
Annual commitment saves ~20% over monthly pricing. Organizations should audit license usage regularly -- unused licenses waste budget. The M365 admin center shows license utilization. Downgrade users who do not need premium features (e.g., move from E5 to E3 if they do not use advanced security).
Mixed Licensing
Organizations commonly mix plans. Knowledge workers get E3/E5, frontline workers get F1/F3, and executives with advanced security needs get E5. Group-based licensing in Entra ID automates license assignment based on security group membership.
Add-On Licensing
Individual features can be added to base plans. Examples: Microsoft Defender for Endpoint P2 added to E3, Teams Phone added to E3, Purview Audit Premium added to E3. Add-ons let you target premium features to specific users without upgrading everyone to E5.

Plan Comparison
Side-by-side feature comparison across Business, Enterprise, and Frontline tiers.
The exam tests scenario-based plan selection. Key differentiators: Business plans cap at 300 users. Only Business Premium and E3+ include Intune. Only E5 includes Defender for Endpoint P2, Phone System, and Power BI Pro. F-series has 2 GB mailboxes and no desktop apps.

Microsoft 365 Admin Center
The centralized management portal for users, licenses, billing, and service health at admin.microsoft.com.
User Management
Create, edit, and delete users. Assign licenses individually or via group-based licensing. Reset passwords, manage MFA, and configure mail forwarding. Bulk operations via CSV import. Deleted users are retained for 30 days in the recycle bin before permanent deletion.
Billing and Subscriptions
View and manage active subscriptions. Purchase additional licenses or add-ons. Set up payment methods (credit card or invoice). View billing statements and cost breakdowns. Configure auto-renew settings. Purchase directly or through a Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) partner.
Usage Reports
Built-in reports show adoption metrics: Teams usage, email activity, OneDrive storage, SharePoint site activity, and Viva Engage engagement. Reports help justify licensing spend and identify underutilized licenses. Data is available for 7, 30, 90, or 180-day periods.

Service Health and Message Center
Monitor service status and stay ahead of upcoming changes through the M365 admin center.
Service Health Dashboard
Real-time status of all M365 services. Shows active incidents, advisories, and historical issues. Provides root cause analysis after incidents are resolved. Configurable email notifications alert admins to service disruptions. Accessible at admin.microsoft.com > Health > Service health.
Message Center
Advance notice of upcoming feature changes, retirement notices, and required admin actions. Posts are categorized by service (Teams, Exchange, SharePoint). Typically 30-90 days advance notice. Set preferences to receive weekly email digests. This is where Microsoft communicates changes before they happen.
Microsoft 365 Roadmap
Public website (microsoft.com/microsoft-365/roadmap) showing planned, in development, and recently launched features. Filterable by product, platform, and status. Useful for planning adoption strategies and preparing users for upcoming changes. Not a commitment -- features may be delayed or cancelled.
Windows Release Health
Dedicated dashboard for Windows update issues. Shows known issues and safeguard holds for each Windows version. Particularly important for organizations managing Windows 11 deployments and feature updates. Accessible from the admin center or the Windows release health website.

Admin Roles
Least-privilege admin roles limit what each administrator can do in the M365 admin center.
Global Administrator
Full access to all admin centers and settings. Can manage everything. Only 2-4 users should have this role. Requires MFA. Use PIM to make it just-in-time rather than permanent.
User Administrator
Create and manage users, reset passwords, manage licenses. Cannot manage billing or global settings. Good for help desk staff who handle user account requests.
Billing Administrator
Manage subscriptions, payment methods, and billing. Cannot manage users or security settings. Appropriate for finance teams who need to manage M365 costs.
Exchange Administrator
Full control over Exchange Online: mailboxes, transport rules, mail flow, and compliance features. Cannot manage users in Entra ID or other M365 services.
SharePoint Administrator
Manage SharePoint Online and OneDrive for Business settings, sites, storage quotas, and sharing policies. Does not have access to Exchange or Teams administration.
Security Administrator
Read/write access to security settings across M365 Defender, Entra ID, and Purview. Can manage security policies, review alerts, and configure threat protection. Cannot manage users or billing.

Microsoft 365 Support Options
From self-service troubleshooting to 24/7 critical issue support -- multiple tiers for every need.
Self-Service
Microsoft Learn documentation, troubleshooting wizards in the admin center, and diagnostic tools. The admin center's "Need help?" panel runs automated diagnostics for common issues before you open a ticket. Microsoft 365 support assistant can resolve many issues automatically.
Included Support
Every M365 subscription includes technical support via the admin center. Submit tickets online or by phone. Severity A (critical business impact -- service down) gets 24/7 response. Severity B (moderate impact) and C (minimal impact) get business hours response.
FastTrack
Free deployment guidance for organizations with 150+ eligible licenses. Microsoft engineers help plan and execute M365 rollouts including Exchange Online migration, Teams deployment, and Intune configuration. Remote assistance only -- FastTrack does not make changes on your behalf.

Modern Lifecycle Policy
Microsoft 365 follows the Modern Lifecycle Policy -- continuous updates with advance deprecation notice.
Continuous Updates
M365 cloud services are updated continuously -- no version numbers, no upgrade cycles. Features roll out gradually through targeted release (early adopters) then standard release (everyone). The admin center controls which users get features first. Updates happen automatically.
Deprecation Notice
Microsoft provides at least 12 months notice before removing features from M365 services. Notices appear in the Message Center. This gives organizations time to migrate workflows, retrain users, and update documentation before features are retired.
Office Apps Servicing
Microsoft 365 Apps for Enterprise require staying on a supported channel and version. Current Channel gets monthly updates. Monthly Enterprise Channel gets features once per month on a predictable date. Semi-Annual Enterprise Channel gets features twice per year (January and July).
End of Support
Perpetual Office versions (Office 2021, 2024) have fixed end-of-support dates. After EOS, no security updates. M365 services may stop supporting connections from unsupported Office versions. This is a key differentiator between subscription M365 and perpetual Office licenses.

Release and Update Channels
Control how quickly new features reach your users -- from early preview to slow-roll stability.
Best practice: put IT admins and change champions on Targeted Release to preview features before they reach all users. Keep the majority of users on Standard Release. For desktop Office apps in highly regulated environments, use Semi-Annual Enterprise Channel for maximum predictability.

Purchasing Channels and Billing
Buy direct from Microsoft, through a CSP partner, or via volume licensing -- each has different management models.
Direct (Web Direct)
Purchase from microsoft.com or the M365 admin center. Self-service billing management. Credit card or invoice payment. Best for small organizations that do not need partner assistance. Pricing is list price with no volume discounts.
Cloud Solution Provider (CSP)
Purchase through a Microsoft partner who manages your billing. Partners can bundle M365 with managed services, support, and custom solutions. Partners may offer volume discounts. Two CSP models: direct (partner bills you) and indirect (partner works through a distributor).
Enterprise Agreement (EA)
Volume licensing for organizations with 500+ users. 3-year commitment with annual true-up. Deepest discounts. Managed through a Microsoft account team or Large Account Reseller. Includes Software Assurance benefits. Best for large enterprises with predictable user counts.
For the MS-900 exam, understand that purchasing channel affects billing management but not feature availability. A Business Standard license purchased through CSP has the same features as one purchased directly from Microsoft. The difference is who manages billing and who provides first-line support.

License Cost Management
Optimize M365 spending by right-sizing licenses, removing unused seats, and leveraging reports.
License Utilization
The admin center shows how many licenses are assigned vs. available. Usage reports reveal which users actively use services. Inactive users consuming E5 licenses should be downgraded or removed. Group-based licensing automates assignment when users join/leave teams.
Right-Sizing
Not every user needs E5. Identify users who only use email (E3 or even Exchange Online P1). Identify frontline workers who need mobile-only access (F1/F3). Reserve E5 for users who need Defender P2, Phone System, or advanced compliance. Mixed licensing saves significant cost.
Subscription Management
Review subscriptions quarterly. Remove licenses for departed employees within the 30-day grace period. Enable self-service purchase controls to prevent unauthorized license purchases. Monitor the billing section for upcoming renewals and price changes.
A 500-person company paying $57/user/month for E5 across all users spends $342,000/year. If 300 of those users only need E3 ($36/user/month), switching them saves $75,600/year. License optimization is a real-world skill and an MS-900 exam topic.

Chapter 3 Summary
Key concepts for the MS-900 exam -- pricing, licensing, and support.
1Pricing: Per-user, per-month. Annual commitment is cheapest (~20% savings over monthly). Enterprise Agreements offer volume discounts for 500+ users with 3-year commitments.
2Plans: Business Basic/Standard/Premium (up to 300 users). E3/E5 (unlimited). F1/F3 (frontline). E5 is the only plan with Defender P2, Phone System, and Power BI Pro included.
3Admin Center (admin.microsoft.com) manages users, licenses, billing, service health, and support tickets. Specialist portals exist for Security, Compliance, Exchange, SharePoint, and Teams.
4Support: Self-service (free) > Community forums (free) > Included support (with subscription, 24/7 for Sev A) > Microsoft Unified (paid, dedicated TAM) > FastTrack (free deployment guidance for 150+ licenses).
5Modern Lifecycle Policy: Continuous cloud updates. 12 months deprecation notice. Targeted Release for early adopters, Standard Release for everyone else. Office desktop apps have separate servicing channels.
6Admin roles: Use least-privilege roles. Global Admin for 2-4 people max. User Admin for help desk. Billing Admin for finance. Service-specific admins (Exchange, SharePoint, Teams) for specialists.
