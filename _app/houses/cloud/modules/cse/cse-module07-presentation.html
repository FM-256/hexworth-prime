Slide 2 of 10 Common Cloud Security Risks

Gartner Prediction: Through 2025, 99% of all cloud security failures will be due to some level of human error.

Top Cloud Security Risks

Unmanaged Attack Surface

Microservices adoption leads to explosion of publicly available workloads, expanding the attack surface exponentially.

Human Error

Misconfigurations, weak passwords, improper access grants, and failure to apply security patches.

Misconfiguration

Complex cloud settings require expertise. Improper configuration creates exploitable vulnerabilities.

Data Breach

Cloud concentrates data, making it a high-value target for attackers seeking sensitive information.

Top Cloud Vulnerabilities

  • Unsecured interfaces and APIs - Exposed endpoints without proper authentication
  • Inadequate change control - Untracked modifications to cloud resources
  • Lack of cloud security architecture - No strategic security design
  • Insecure software development - Missing security in SDLC
  • Unsecured third-party resources - Supply chain vulnerabilities
Slide 3 of 10 Risk Categories & Impact Levels

LOW Risk

  • Limited impact on company and personnel
  • Financial loss is sustainable
  • Short-term loss of processes/procedures
  • Short-term recovery
  • No personal injuries occur

MODERATE Risk

  • Serious impact on company resources
  • Substantial but sustainable financial loss
  • Extended loss of processes
  • Substantial recovery time
  • May involve personal injury

HIGH Risk

  • Severe/catastrophic impact to company
  • Financial loss may cause business failure
  • Very unlikely to recover from damages
  • Potential loss of personnel/life
  • Requires immediate mitigation

Risk Assessment Checklist

  • Understand shared responsibility model with your cloud provider
  • Manage access control of sensitive data
  • Establish malware protection
  • Implement data backup and recovery plan
  • Continuously administer security patches and updates
  • Practice good password hygiene
  • Regularly evaluate your security score
Slide 4 of 10 NIST Risk Management Framework (SP 800-37)

Risk Management Tiers

Tier 1: Organization

Strategic risk management, governance, risk tolerance

Tier 2: Mission/Business Process

Business process alignment, enterprise architecture

Tier 3: Information Systems

Operational security, system-level controls

Frame-Assess-Respond-Monitor Cycle

  • Frame: Risk assumptions, constraints, tolerance, priorities & trade-offs
  • Assess: Threat and vulnerability identification, risk determination
  • Respond: Response identification, evaluation of alternatives, decision, implementation
  • Monitor: Risk monitoring strategy and continuous monitoring execution
Slide 5 of 10 Security Controls & Countermeasures

Defense in Depth Principle

One level of protection is NEVER enough. You must take a complete, layered approach to protecting data.

Key Security Controls

Identity & Access Management

Ensures only authorized users and devices access cloud resources. Includes user accounts, roles, permissions, and authentication.

Data Encryption

Protects confidentiality and integrity using cryptographic algorithms. Applied at rest, in transit, and in use.

Firewall & Network Security

Regulates traffic with rules, policies, and filters based on source, destination, protocol, port, or content.

Security Monitoring & Auditing

Enables detection and response through log collection, analysis, and reporting from cloud resources.

Multi-Factor Authentication

Adds extra security layer requiring two or more forms of identification (password + fingerprint, etc.).

Regular Backups

Ensures data is backed up regularly to prevent data loss from breaches or disasters.

Slide 6 of 10 Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery

Business Continuity Plan (BCP)

Focus: Keep business operational DURING a disaster

  • BCP Team with cross-departmental representatives
  • Business Impact Analysis (BIA)
  • Risk mitigation strategies
  • Continuity strategies (alternate site, telecommuting)
  • Training and testing

Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

Focus: Restore IT infrastructure AFTER a disaster

  • Recovery objectives (RTO/RPO)
  • Hardware/software inventory
  • Personnel roles and responsibilities
  • DR sites (cloud, colocation, remote)
  • Response procedures and DR drills

Key Difference

BCP is prevention-focused and broader in scope, covering all business operations.

DRP is recovery-focused and specific to IT systems and data restoration.

Together, they ensure an organization can both survive and recover from any disruption.

Slide 7 of 10 Threat Modeling & Vulnerability Assessment

Cloud Threat Modeling

Process of identifying and analyzing potential security risks and vulnerabilities in cloud systems.

Benefits

  • Improved Security Awareness: Understand threats and attack vectors, prioritize critical ones
  • Reduced Attack Surface: Identify and eliminate unnecessary or insecure components
  • Enhanced Security Design: Implement encryption, authentication, authorization, logging
  • Increased Security Testing: Define penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, code review

Vulnerability Assessment Process

  1. Identify Assets: Data, applications, and systems requiring protection
  2. Assess Risks: Malware, phishing, DDoS, and other potential threats
  3. Prioritize Risks: Based on likelihood and impact
  4. Implement Controls: Encryption, access control, monitoring
  5. Test and Validate: Verify effectiveness and update regularly

Vulnerability Scanning Tools

Microsoft Defender Vulnerability Management, Qualys Cloud Platform, Tenable, AWS Inspector

Slide 8 of 10 Quantitative vs Qualitative Risk Assessment

Quantitative Risk Formulas

Threat Modeling: Asset (A) + Vulnerability (V) + Threat (T) = Risk (R)

ALE = SLE × ARO

ALE: Annualized Loss Expectancy | SLE: Single Loss Expectancy | ARO: Annualized Rate of Occurrence

Qualitative Analysis

  • Subjective and quick to implement
  • Identifies threats, likelihood, and impacts
  • Uses probability/impact ranking matrix
  • Classifies risks by source or effect
  • Good for initial risk prioritization

Quantitative Analysis

  • Objective with measurable data
  • Provides financial basis for decisions
  • More time-consuming and complex
  • Requires extensive data collection
  • Enables ROI calculations for controls

Slide 9 of 10 Risk Treatment & Response Strategies

MITIGATE

Invest in controls to decrease risk

Example: Implement firewalls, encryption, access controls

AVOID

Don't implement what creates the risk

Example: Don't use technology with known vulnerabilities

ACCEPT

Acknowledge risk without additional controls

Example: Accept natural disaster risk + DR plan

TRANSFER

Have someone else take on the risk

Example: Cyber insurance, cloud services

CRITICAL: NEVER Ignore Risk!

Ignoring risk is NOT a valid response strategy. All identified risks must be documented and addressed through one of the four strategies above.

Residual Risk

After completing risk response, reassess the company's risk levels:

  • Risk is never fully removed or mitigated to zero
  • Goal: Residual risk within company risk tolerance levels
  • If residual risk is acceptable, proceed to operations
  • Continuous monitoring for changes is required
  • New threats and vulnerabilities introduce new risks
Slide 10 of 10 Module Summary

Key Takeaways

  • Cloud Security Risks: Unmanaged attack surface, human error (99% of failures), misconfiguration, data breaches
  • Risk Categories: Low, Moderate, High - based on impact and likelihood
  • NIST RMF (SP 800-37): 7-step lifecycle - Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor
  • Defense in Depth: Layered security controls (IAM, encryption, firewalls, MFA, backups)
  • BCP vs DRP: BCP = maintain operations during disaster, DRP = recover IT after disaster
  • Quantitative Analysis: ALE = SLE × ARO for financial risk calculations
  • Risk Responses: Mitigate, Avoid, Accept, Transfer - NEVER Ignore
  • Residual Risk: Remaining risk after controls, must be within tolerance
