DR Fundamentals
Disaster recovery plans ensure business continuity when infrastructure fails at scale.
What Is DR
The process of restoring IT systems after a catastrophic event: region outage, natural disaster, cyberattack, data corruption. DR is not backup -- it is a complete recovery strategy.
DR vs. HA
High availability prevents downtime through redundancy within a region (multi-AZ). DR recovers from regional or catastrophic failures. HA is proactive; DR is reactive. You need both.
Business Impact Analysis
Identifies critical systems and their RPO/RTO requirements. A payment processing system might need RPO=0, RTO=5min. A marketing website might tolerate RPO=24h, RTO=4h.
Slide 3 of 12
DR Strategy: Backup & Restore
Cheapest DR strategy. Restore from backups in a different region when disaster strikes.
How It Works
Cross-region backup replication runs continuously. On disaster: provision new infrastructure in the DR region, restore data from backups, update DNS. Manual process, slowest recovery.
RPO / RTO
RPO: hours (depends on backup frequency). RTO: hours to days (infrastructure must be provisioned). Suitable for non-critical systems where extended downtime is acceptable.
Cost
Lowest cost. You only pay for backup storage in the DR region during normal operations. Compute costs only during an actual disaster event. No idle infrastructure.
Slide 4 of 12
DR Strategy: Pilot Light
Core infrastructure runs in the DR region at minimum capacity. Scale up on failover.
How It Works
Database replicas and core services run in the DR region at the smallest possible size. On disaster: scale up the infrastructure, start application servers, redirect traffic. Semi-automated.
RPO / RTO
RPO: minutes (continuous replication). RTO: minutes to hours (scaling up takes time). Good for important applications that need faster recovery than backup/restore.
Cost
Low to moderate. Small DB instances and minimal compute run 24/7 in the DR region. Scaling up during a disaster increases cost temporarily. Good balance of cost and recovery speed.
Slide 5 of 12
DR Strategy: Warm Standby
A scaled-down but fully functional copy of the production environment runs in the DR region.
How It Works
The DR region runs a smaller version of production: fewer instances, smaller databases, but everything is deployed and functional. On disaster: scale to full capacity and redirect traffic.
RPO / RTO
RPO: seconds to minutes (continuous replication). RTO: minutes (just scaling and DNS). Suitable for business-critical applications with moderate downtime tolerance.
Cost
Moderate. A running environment 24/7, but at reduced capacity. Typically 20-30% of production cost. Higher than pilot light but significantly faster recovery.
Slide 6 of 12
DR Strategy: Active-Active
Both regions serve production traffic simultaneously. No failover needed -- traffic is always distributed.
How It Works
Both Region A and Region B run full production environments. Global load balancer distributes traffic across both regions. If one region fails, the other absorbs all traffic automatically.
RPO / RTO
RPO: zero (synchronous or near-synchronous replication). RTO: near-zero (automatic rerouting). The gold standard for mission-critical applications like payment processing.
Cost
Highest. You run two full production environments 24/7. Data replication costs, cross-region networking. Worth it only for applications where any downtime causes significant revenue or safety impact.
Slide 7 of 12
Failover Mechanisms
How traffic moves from the failed region to the DR region. Automation is key.
DNS Failover
Route 53, Azure Traffic Manager, Cloud DNS. Health checks monitor endpoints. On failure, DNS records update to point to the DR region. TTL affects propagation speed.
Global Load Balancers
AWS Global Accelerator, Azure Front Door, GCP Global Load Balancer. Route traffic based on health, latency, or geographic proximity. Faster failover than DNS because no TTL wait.
Automated Runbooks
AWS Systems Manager runbooks, Azure Automation runbooks. Pre-defined scripts that execute failover steps automatically: scale resources, promote DB replicas, update routing, notify teams.
Slide 8 of 12
DR Testing
An untested DR plan is a wish list. Test regularly to validate that recovery actually works.
Tabletop Exercises
Walk through the DR plan on paper. Identify gaps, unclear ownership, and missing procedures. Low cost, no production impact. Minimum: quarterly.
Simulation Testing
Actually failover to the DR region in a controlled manner. Verify application functionality, data integrity, and performance. Detect issues that tabletop exercises miss.
Chaos Engineering
Intentionally inject failures into production: kill instances, simulate region outages, corrupt data. Netflix Chaos Monkey is the pioneer. Validates resilience under real conditions.
Slide 9 of 12
Runbooks & Documentation
A runbook is a step-by-step guide for executing a DR failover. No guessing during a crisis.
Runbook Contents
Step-by-step procedure, responsible team members, escalation contacts, expected timelines, rollback procedure. Written for the worst case: the senior engineer is unavailable.
Automation-First
Every runbook step should be a script or automation action. Manual steps introduce human error under stress. AWS SSM, Azure Automation, Terraform apply -- automate everything.
Post-Mortem
After every DR event (real or simulated), conduct a blameless post-mortem. What worked, what failed, what needs improvement. Update the runbook based on findings.
Slide 12 of 12
Key Takeaways
1DR is not backup. DR is a complete strategy for recovering from catastrophic failures. You need both backup AND DR.
2Four DR strategies with increasing cost and decreasing RTO: Backup/Restore, Pilot Light, Warm Standby, Active-Active.
3Failover mechanisms: DNS failover, global load balancers, automated runbooks. Automation is essential -- manual failover is too slow.
4Chaos engineering (Netflix Chaos Monkey) intentionally breaks things to validate resilience. Test in production.
5Runbooks must be automated, tested, and updated. Manual runbook steps fail under the stress of a real disaster.
6DR testing is not optional. Tabletop quarterly, simulation annually, chaos engineering continuously.
