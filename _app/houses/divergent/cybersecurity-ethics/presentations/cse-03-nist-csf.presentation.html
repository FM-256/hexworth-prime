NIST Cybersecurity Framework
The course anchor. Five Functions, four subcategories you will see in every assessment, and the conceptual scaffolding for everything else we discuss.

Slide 2 of 13 · Origin
What the CSF Actually Is
Voluntary. Risk-based. Built by NIST, refined by industry, mandated by no one — and adopted by almost everyone.
Origin
Executive Order 13636 (2013) directed NIST to build a framework for critical infrastructure. Published Feb 2014. v1.1 in 2018, v2.0 in 2024.
What it is not
Not a checklist. Not a control set (those are 800-53). Not a maturity model. Not a regulation. Not a certification.
What it is
A common language for managing cybersecurity risk. Functions, Categories, Subcategories. A way to map what you do to what others do.
Why this course uses it: the syllabus explicitly maps CIS2253 to four CSF subcategories — ID.BE, ID.GV, PR.AT, PR.IP. Every weekly checkpoint comes back to those four. Memorize them now.
Slide 3 of 13 · The Core
Five Functions Across the Lifecycle
v2.0 added a sixth (Govern) at the center. v1.1's five remain the operational verbs.
Identify
Know what you have. Assets, business context, governance, risk, supply chain.
Protect
Apply controls. Access, awareness, data security, processes, maintenance, tech.
Detect
Find what got through. Anomalies, monitoring, detection processes.
Respond
Contain. Plan, communications, analysis, mitigation, improvements.
Recover
Restore. Plan, improvements, communications.
This course only deeply covers Identify and Protect. Detect/Respond/Recover live in your incident-response courses. Why these two? Because Identify and Protect are where the ethical decisions happen before the breach — and the ethical job of the security professional is mostly upstream.
Slide 4 of 13 · Subcategory 1 of 4
ID.BE — Business Environment
"The organization's mission, objectives, stakeholders, and activities are understood and prioritized; this information is used to inform cybersecurity roles, responsibilities, and risk management decisions."
What ID.BE forces
You cannot defend what you cannot describe. ID.BE asks: what is the org for, who depends on it, what activities are essential, what would the world lose if you stopped?
Why it is the ethical foundation
Every later cybersecurity decision — what to protect, how much, at what cost — presupposes an answer to ID.BE. Skip ID.BE and you are securing assets without knowing why they matter.
Subcategory ID.BE-3 ("Priorities for organizational mission, objectives, and activities are established and communicated") is the one most often missing in real organizations. It is also the one that, when missing, makes every later decision arbitrary.
A hospital that has not articulated "patient safety is paramount" cannot weigh a 10-minute outage in EHR access against a SOC2 audit finding. Both are real costs. ID.BE tells you which one wins.
Slide 5 of 13 · Subcategory 2 of 4
ID.GV — Governance
"The policies, procedures, and processes to manage and monitor the organization's regulatory, legal, risk, environmental, and operational requirements are understood and inform the management of cybersecurity risk."
ID.GV-1
Org cybersecurity policy is established & communicated.
ID.GV-2
Cybersecurity roles & responsibilities are coordinated with internal & external partners.
ID.GV-3
Legal & regulatory requirements regarding cybersecurity are understood & managed.
ID.GV-4
Governance & risk management processes address cybersecurity risks.
Why ID.GV matters ethically: a cybersecurity policy that exists only as a PDF on SharePoint is not governance. ID.GV requires that policies be communicated, that roles be coordinated, and that legal requirements be managed — not just listed.
When the security analyst raises an issue and the org has no documented escalation path, ID.GV-2 has failed. The analyst is then forced to choose between silence and going outside the chain. Whistleblowing starts where governance ends.
Slide 6 of 13 · Subcategory 3 of 4
PR.AT — Awareness and Training
"The organization's personnel and partners are provided cybersecurity awareness education and are trained to perform their cybersecurity-related duties consistent with related policies, procedures, and agreements."
PR.AT-1
All users informed & trained.
PR.AT-2
Privileged users understand roles.
PR.AT-3
3rd-party stakeholders understand obligations.
PR.AT-4
Senior execs understand roles.
PR.AT-5
Physical & cyber personnel understand roles.
The ethical edge of PR.AT: awareness programs that flag every employee equally produce alert fatigue. Awareness programs that flag only "suspicious populations" produce discrimination claims. The tradeoff is real. PR.AT does not solve it — it requires you to face it.
Phishing simulations that punish failures damage trust. Phishing simulations that ignore failures provide no signal. The PR.AT design choice between these extremes is a value choice the security professional gets to make.
Slide 7 of 13 · Subcategory 4 of 4
PR.IP — Information Protection Processes
"Security policies, processes, and procedures are maintained and used to manage protection of information systems and assets." 12 subcategories — the largest in CSF.
Configuration baselines
PR.IP-1, PR.IP-3 — baseline configs established, change control applied.
SDLC & data lifecycle
PR.IP-2, PR.IP-6 — SDLC for systems is implemented, data destroyed per policy.
Backups & recovery
PR.IP-4 — backups conducted, maintained, tested. Tested is the word that matters.
Continuous improvement
PR.IP-7, PR.IP-8 — protection processes improved; effectiveness shared.
Response & recovery plans
PR.IP-9, PR.IP-10 — plans are in place and managed and tested.
Vulnerability management
PR.IP-12 — vulnerability management plan is developed and implemented.
PR.IP is where the daily work lives. It is also where the ethical compromises hide: an "approved configuration baseline" that has not been reviewed in three years, a "tested" backup that fails on first attempt, a "vulnerability management plan" that excludes anything older than 18 months.
Slide 8 of 13 · The framework's purpose
CSF Does Not Tell You What to Do
It tells you what to think about. The decisions are yours.
What a control framework does
"Implement multifactor authentication on all administrative interfaces by Q2." Specific. Auditable. Required by regulation.
What CSF does
"PR.AC-7: Users, devices, and other assets are authenticated commensurate with the risk of the transaction." Tells you to think about it. Does not tell you how.
This is why CSF is the right anchor for an ethics course. Ethics is not "follow the rules" — it is "what are the rules for, and which one applies here." CSF asks the same questions in cybersecurity language: what are you trying to protect, why, from whom, and at what cost?
A control framework tells you that 256-bit encryption is the standard. CSF asks whether the data you are encrypting deserves 256-bit encryption — or whether the encryption is a substitute for figuring out whether the data should exist at all.
Slide 9 of 13 · Reference case
Equifax 2017: A Failure Across All Four
147 million records. $1.4 billion in fines and remediation. Mapped onto our four subcategories.
CSF SubcategoryWhat was missingResult
ID.BENo clear articulation that consumer credit data was the org's most critical asset; no ranking of business processes by criticalityPatching priorities driven by IT operations, not by business impact
ID.GVVulnerability disclosure (Apache Struts CVE-2017-5638) reached IT but not the responsible system owners; no documented escalation72-hour patch window from US-CERT silently expired
PR.ATPrivileged users had no specific training on dispute portal architecture; admin certificates expired without actionEncrypted-traffic monitoring failed for 19 months unnoticed
PR.IPVulnerability management plan existed; was not effective. Backup of identity data existed; was not separated from productionSingle point of compromise exposed all 147M records simultaneously
The forensic finding: no single failure caused Equifax. Four CSF subcategory failures — the same four this course is built around — created the conditions where the breach was inevitable. The technical exploit was the trigger; the framework gaps were the cause.
Slide 10 of 13 · Forward connection
CSF in Solarium Recommendations
The 2020 Cyberspace Solarium Commission report referenced CSF more than 40 times. Two recommendations are worth previewing now.
Recommendation 4.4
"Establish and fund a National Cybersecurity Certification and Labeling Authority." The labels would be CSF-aligned. Procurement decisions become CSF-aligned by extension.
Recommendation 5.4
"Pass a National Data Security and Privacy Protection Law." Would extend CSF-style requirements to private-sector data handling at federal level.
Why preview Solarium now: Week 4 covers Solarium in depth. Knowing CSF first means you will read the recommendations as concrete proposals, not vague aspirations. The frameworks are how policy gets translated into engineering reality.
When a Solarium recommendation says "the federal government should require X," it usually means "X mapped to a CSF subcategory." That is how policy becomes procurement, and procurement becomes practice.

Slide 12 of 13 · What this gives you
Why CSF Is the Security Professional's Native Vocabulary
It is shared
Auditors, regulators, vendors, and your CISO all speak it. Saying "we have an ID.GV-2 gap" lands. Saying "no one talks to legal" sounds like a complaint.
It is risk-aware, not control-bound
CSF assumes you make tradeoffs. Control frameworks pretend you do not. The first is closer to reality.
It is morally honest
CSF does not tell you "what is enough." It tells you what to think about. The "enough" question is yours, and it is an ethical one.
The structural argument of this course: ethics in cybersecurity is not a separate domain from technical practice. The CSF subcategories are the ethical decisions, just expressed in defensive language. Every time you face a CSF gap, you face an ethical question about whose interest the gap serves.
