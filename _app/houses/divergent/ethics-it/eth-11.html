Warehouse Dispatch

"Week 3 covers two things: what you are responsible for when code fails, and what technology does to the people who do not write it. Both questions cut to the core of professional responsibility."

Review -- Chapter 7: Ethical Decisions in Software Development
  • Software quality standards must be proportionate to context -- safety-critical systems require stricter standards than low-risk consumer applications.
  • The Therac-25 radiation therapy machine caused patient deaths because hardware safety interlocks were removed and the replacement software controls were not adequately tested. Engineers dismissed early warning signs as operator error.
  • The 2018 Uber autonomous vehicle fatality in Tempe, Arizona resulted from detection suppression implemented to reduce false-positive phantom braking -- the same suppression degraded true-positive response rates.
  • Product liability for software is evolving. Shrink-wrap disclaimers limit legal exposure but do not eliminate professional ethical obligations.
  • Professional codes (ACM, IEEE) require that software engineers act in the public interest. Employment does not eliminate individual professional responsibility.
Review -- Chapter 8: IT Impact on Society
  • The productivity paradox (Solow, 1987) observed that large IT investments in the 1970s-80s did not produce proportional measured productivity gains -- because technology alone does not create value without organizational change.
  • AI and machine learning are displacing routine cognitive work (legal document review, radiology readings, loan processing) at a pace and scale that creates novel ethical obligations for technology developers and employers.
  • Healthcare IT produces measurable benefits (EHR reduces medication errors, telemedicine expands access) but introduces serious risks including high-value ransomware targets, algorithmic bias from non-representative training data, and opaque data monetization.
  • IBM Watson for Oncology was marketed as evidence-based but trained primarily on hypothetical patient scenarios. This was not prominently disclosed. The product recommended "unsafe and incorrect" treatments and was withdrawn from oncology markets.
  • The digital divide excludes citizens from civic and economic participation when essential services move online. Inclusive design is a professional obligation under ACM and IEEE codes.
