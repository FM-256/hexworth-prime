Azure AI Services Overview
Pre-built, API-accessible AI capabilities. No model training required — provision a resource, grab your key, and call the endpoint.
The Core Concept
You are a consumer, not a trainer. Microsoft has already trained the underlying models using massive datasets. You provision the resource, authenticate, and call the REST API. The result comes back as JSON. Your application does not need a GPU or a data scientist.
What All Six Categories Share
Every Azure AI Service follows the same pattern: endpoint URL + API key + REST call = AI result. They all support Azure Monitor logging, private endpoints, managed identity auth, and container deployment. The interface is consistent whether you use Vision or Speech.
Exam Angle
AI-900 tests whether you can match service to scenario. Vision for images. Language for text. Speech for audio. Decision for moderation and anomalies. Document Intelligence for structured extraction from scanned documents. Know which service maps to which problem — that is the exam.
Crawl: "These are ready-to-use APIs — you consume them, not train them." Walk: every call follows the same REST pattern regardless of which AI capability you're invoking. Master that pattern once and it applies to all six categories.

Speech Recognition (Speech-to-Text)
Converting spoken audio into written text. Two modes: real-time (streaming) and batch (file upload). Supports 100+ languages with customizable acoustic models.
Real-Time Mode
Audio is streamed continuously to the service over a WebSocket connection. Partial results arrive as you speak — ideal for live captions, voice assistants, and call center transcription. Latency is sub-second. The SDK handles the socket lifecycle for you.
Batch Mode
Upload an audio file (up to several hours) to Azure Blob Storage and submit a transcription job via the REST API. The service processes it asynchronously and writes the transcript back to blob storage. Cheaper per-minute rate than real-time. Used for recorded meetings, call archives, and podcast indexing.
Custom Speech Models
Standard models struggle with domain vocabulary — "metformin" sounds like "met for min" to a general model. Custom Speech lets you upload domain-specific pronunciation dictionaries and audio samples to fine-tune recognition accuracy. Critical for healthcare, legal, and manufacturing deployments.
Walk through the process: audio capture feeds an acoustic model that converts raw sound into phonemes. The language model then resolves phonemes into probable words using context. The output is a time-stamped transcript with a confidence score per utterance.
Slide 4 of 12
Speech Synthesis (Text-to-Speech)
Converting text to natural-sounding audio. Neural voices use deep learning to produce human-like intonation. SSML gives you fine-grained control over every aspect of the output.
Neural Voices — The Difference
Standard voices concatenate recorded phonemes — you hear the seams. Neural voices were trained end-to-end on hours of recorded speech and generate audio as a continuous waveform. The result has natural intonation, appropriate pausing, and emotional range. Microsoft's "Jenny" and "Guy" are commonly used English neural voices.
SSML — Speech Synthesis Markup Language
SSML is an XML dialect that wraps your text with pronunciation instructions. You can set speaking rate (rate="slow"), pitch (pitch="+10%"), add pauses (<break time="500ms"/>), force specific pronunciation (<phoneme>), and switch voices mid-sentence. Essential for IVR systems, audiobooks, and accessibility tools.
Custom Neural Voice
Organizations can train a neural voice on their own recorded samples to create a proprietary synthetic voice — a "brand voice". Requires Microsoft approval and a speaker's explicit consent. Used by banks, telcos, and media companies to maintain a consistent branded audio identity. This is distinct from standard voice customization.
The visual comparison: Neural voice waveform is a smooth, continuous curve — natural rhythm and intonation. Standard voice waveform shows jagged concatenation artifacts — robotic cadence that listeners immediately notice. For any customer-facing application, neural is the correct choice.

Azure AI Document Intelligence
Extract structured, machine-readable data from unstructured documents. Receipts, invoices, ID cards, tax forms — pre-built models handle each. Custom models handle your proprietary formats.
The Critical Distinction from Basic OCR
Plain OCR returns a flat string of all text on the page — you still have to figure out which string is the vendor name vs the invoice total. Document Intelligence uses AI models trained on thousands of receipts, invoices, and tax forms to understand what the text means. It returns labeled key-value pairs: "InvoiceTotal": "$142.50".
Pre-Built Models
Receipt — merchant name, line items, totals, tax. Invoice — vendor, customer, PO number, due date, line items. ID Document — passport, driver's license fields. Business Card — contact extraction. Tax (W-2) — IRS form fields. Health Insurance Card. All are ready to use with zero training.
Custom Models
Label 5+ sample documents in Document Intelligence Studio to train a custom model on your proprietary form — purchase orders, claims forms, work orders. The labeling UI is drag-and-drop. Once trained, the model extracts your custom fields with the same JSON structure as the pre-built models. No ML expertise required.

Provisioning Azure AI Services
Two approaches: one resource per service (granular control, separate keys) or one multi-service resource (single endpoint for everything). The right choice depends on your architecture.
When to Use Individual Resources
1You need granular billing visibility — see exactly what each service costs per department or project.
2You need separate access control — one team gets Vision access, another gets Language, neither gets the other.
3You need a free tier for experimentation — each service has its own free SKU (F0). Multi-service has no free tier.
4You need service-specific regional compliance — deploy Speech in one region, Language in another.
When to Use the Multi-Service Resource
1You use multiple AI services in one application — one key to manage, one endpoint to configure, one resource to monitor.
2Your team values operational simplicity — fewer secrets to rotate, fewer resources to manage in Azure.
3You are building a prototype or proof of concept quickly — provision once and experiment with any service.
4You need cross-service features — some advanced capabilities only work when services share a resource.
Exam tip: the AI-900 exam frequently tests the trade-off between individual and multi-service provisioning. Remember: individual = granular billing + free tiers + separate keys. Multi-service = simplicity + one key + no free tier.

Authentication and Security
Three ways to authenticate to Azure AI Services endpoints. API keys are simplest. Microsoft Entra ID is most secure. Managed identities eliminate secrets entirely.
API Keys — Simple but Risky
Every Azure AI resource has two keys (primary and secondary). You pass the key in the Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key HTTP header. Keys grant full access — there is no scope restriction. Never hardcode keys in source code. Store them in Azure Key Vault. Rotate them regularly. Use both keys alternately so rotation is zero-downtime.
Microsoft Entra ID (formerly Azure AD)
Assign the Cognitive Services User RBAC role to an Entra ID identity (user, group, or service principal). The client acquires an OAuth 2.0 bearer token and passes it in the Authorization header. Advantages: token expires, access is role-scoped, you get full audit logs in Entra ID, and you can use conditional access policies.
Managed Identities — The Gold Standard
Managed identities are Azure-managed service principals. Enable a system-assigned managed identity on your VM, App Service, or container. Assign it the Cognitive Services User role. Your code calls the local metadata endpoint to get a token — no secret stored anywhere. If the compute is compromised, the credential cannot be extracted because it never existed as a stored value.

Containers for Edge Deployment
Run Azure AI Services inside Docker containers — on-premises, in disconnected environments, on factory floors, in hospitals, or on ships. Cloud intelligence at the edge.
Why Containers for Edge?
Latency: a cloud round-trip adds 50-200ms. For real-time quality inspection on a production line running 200 items/min, that round-trip fails the throughput requirement. The container runs locally — inference in under 10ms.
Data residency: healthcare data (PHI) and financial data may not legally transit to a public cloud region. The container keeps data within the facility.
Disconnected Environments
Ships at sea, military forward-operating bases, and remote mining operations have intermittent or no internet. Azure AI containers run the AI model locally — no connectivity required for inference. The model is baked into the Docker image. The container only needs occasional network access to report usage back to Azure for billing purposes (at minimum once every 24 hours).
Available Container Services
Not all Azure AI Services are available as containers. Currently supported: Speech-to-Text, Text-to-Speech, Language Detection, Key Phrase Extraction, Sentiment Analysis, Named Entity Recognition, Text Analytics for Health, Document Intelligence, Custom Vision inference. Training is always cloud-only. Only the inference endpoint runs in the container.

Monitoring and Logging
Every Azure AI Service emits metrics and diagnostic logs to Azure Monitor. Track usage, catch errors, set alerts, and tune performance — all from one central platform.
Enabling Diagnostic Logging
By default, Azure AI Services emit platform metrics automatically. Diagnostic logs (request/response details, error codes) must be explicitly enabled via Diagnostic Settings on the resource. You choose the destination: Log Analytics Workspace (for KQL queries), Azure Storage (for archival), or Event Hub (for real-time streaming to SIEM systems like Sentinel).
Setting Up Alerts
Azure Monitor alert rules fire when a metric crosses a threshold. Example: if TotalErrors over a 5-minute window exceeds 10, send an email and post to a Teams channel. Alert rules can also trigger Logic Apps, Azure Functions, or runbooks for automated remediation. Alerts are configured on the resource or the resource group level.
Why This Matters for Production
APIs get throttled. Networks have blips. Models return unexpected errors. Without monitoring, you find out from an angry user 3 hours after the fact. With proper alerting and dashboards, you are notified within 5 minutes, you have the exact request IDs in the logs, and you can diagnose whether it was a quota breach, a downstream model issue, or a client error.

Responsible AI with Azure AI Services
Every Azure AI Service ships with a transparency note, content filtering, privacy controls, and usage guidelines. Responsible AI is not an afterthought — it is baked into the product.
Transparency Notes — What They Are
Every Azure AI Service ships a Transparency Note — a public document on Microsoft Learn that explains: what the service does and how it works, what training data was used, what it is not designed for, known failure modes, and recommended use case patterns. For example, the Face API transparency note explicitly states it should not be used for sole-source law enforcement decisions.
Content Safety — Built In
Azure AI Content Safety is a standalone service (and also integrated into Azure OpenAI) that classifies content across four harm categories: Hate, Sexual, Violence, Self-harm. Each category is scored 0-7 (severity). You set thresholds. Content above your threshold is blocked before reaching your application or your users. This runs before, not after, your AI produces output.
Microsoft's Six AI Principles
The exam references these: Fairness (no demographic bias in outcomes), Reliability and Safety (works as expected across conditions), Privacy and Security (data is protected), Inclusiveness (accessible to all), Transparency (explainable behavior), Accountability (humans are responsible for AI decisions). These are not just values — they are design requirements baked into every Azure AI Service.
