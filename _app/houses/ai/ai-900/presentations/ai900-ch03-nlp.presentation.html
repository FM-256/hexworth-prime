What is NLP?
AI that reads, understands, and generates human language — turning unstructured text into structured meaning.
Why NLP is Hard
Human language is ambiguous, contextual, and evolving. "I saw the bank" could mean a riverbank or a financial institution. Sarcasm, idioms, spelling errors, and cultural nuance make language a uniquely challenging AI problem.
What NLP Produces
Structured data from unstructured text: named entities, sentiment scores, key topics, detected language, intent labels, and summarized content. This structured output can feed databases, dashboards, and automated workflows.
Foundation for Generative AI
Large Language Models (GPT, Phi, etc.) are built on NLP fundamentals. Understanding tokenization, embeddings, and semantic parsing is prerequisite knowledge for working with generative AI — making this chapter essential groundwork.
"NLP bridges the gap between the way humans communicate and the structured data computers require."

Text Analytics
Extract meaning from raw text — sentiment, key phrases, named entities, language detection, and PII detection.
Sentiment Analysis
Classifies text as positive, negative, neutral, or mixed. Returns a confidence score for each category (0.0 to 1.0). Sentence-level and document-level scores are both available. Ideal for social media monitoring and support ticket triage.
Key Phrase Extraction
Identifies the main talking points of a text — noun phrases that capture the topics. For "The new Surface Pro is fast and elegant," key phrases would be: "new Surface Pro," "fast," "elegant." Useful for search indexing and document tagging.
Named Entity Recognition
Detects and categorizes named items: people, organizations, locations, products, dates, monetary values, and quantities. The text above yields: Microsoft (ORG), Surface Pro (PRODUCT), October 2025 (DATE). Supports 20+ entity categories.
Slide 4 of 12
Sentiment Analysis
Determine whether a piece of text is positive, negative, neutral, or mixed — at both the document and sentence level.
How It Works
The model tokenizes the text, generates contextual embeddings, and maps them to a sentiment distribution. Unlike rule-based keyword matching, modern transformer-based models understand context — "not bad" is correctly read as positive, not negative.
Mixed Sentiment
A single document can contain multiple sentences with conflicting tones: "The product is great (positive) but delivery took 3 weeks (negative)." Azure AI Language returns a confidence score for each of the four labels simultaneously — you decide the threshold.
Business Applications
Brand monitoring across social media, customer review aggregation, support ticket prioritization (route negative tickets to senior staff first), employee feedback analysis, and political speech analysis. One of the most widely deployed NLP capabilities in production.

Key Phrase Extraction
Identify the main talking points in text — noun phrases that capture topic and intent. No pre-defined categories required.
How Extraction Works
The model identifies noun phrases with high information density — terms that would most effectively summarize the document if all other words were removed. It uses part-of-speech tagging, dependency parsing, and statistical frequency to rank candidate phrases.
Support Ticket Use Case
IT helpdesks receive thousands of tickets daily. Key phrase extraction automatically tags each ticket with its core topics (battery, Windows update, performance). This enables routing by topic, trend analysis over time, and knowledge base auto-population — all without human review.
Azure API Response
The analyze-text endpoint returns a keyPhrases array: ["laptop battery", "Windows update", "battery drains rapidly", "urgent help"]. No training required — works out of the box for 30+ supported languages including English, Spanish, French, and German.

Named Entity Recognition
Identify and classify specific real-world items in text — organizations, people, places, products, dates, and monetary values.
Entity Categories
Azure AI Language NER recognizes 20+ entity categories: Person, Organization, Location, Product, Event, DateTime, Quantity, Percentage, Currency, PhoneNumber, Email, URL, IP Address, Age, and more. Categories map to real-world knowledge domains.
PII Detection
A specialized NER variant called PII (Personally Identifiable Information) Detection specifically identifies sensitive data: social security numbers, passport numbers, credit card numbers, email addresses, phone numbers. Used for data redaction and compliance workflows.
Linked Entities
Linked entity recognition goes further — it identifies entities AND disambiguates them using a knowledge base (Wikipedia). "Microsoft" is not just categorized as ORG; it is linked to the specific Microsoft Corporation Wikipedia article, resolving ambiguity when the same name refers to different things.

Language Understanding
Identify user intent and extract slot entities from natural language — the core of every intelligent conversational application.
Intents vs. Entities
An intent is what the user wants to do (BookFlight, CancelOrder, GetWeather). An entity is a variable that fills in the details (destination=London, date=tomorrow, passengers=2). Together they give you enough information to execute the user's request programmatically.
Conversational Language Understanding (CLU)
Azure's CLU service replaces the older LUIS service. You define intents and entity types, provide example utterances, and CLU trains a model. Once deployed, you send utterances to the REST endpoint and receive structured intent + entity predictions with confidence scores.
Training Requirements
Unlike sentiment analysis or NER (which work zero-shot), CLU requires labeled training data. You define your specific intents (BookFlight, CheckStatus) and entities (destination, date) and provide at least 5-10 example utterances per intent. More examples yield higher accuracy.
Slide 8 of 12
Azure AI Language Service
A unified API surface for all Azure NLP capabilities — one resource, one endpoint, multiple pre-built and custom features.
One Resource, Many Features
Azure AI Language consolidates what were previously separate services (Text Analytics, LUIS, QnA Maker) into a single unified resource. Create one Azure AI Language resource, call one endpoint, and access all features through the analyze-text and analyze-conversations APIs.
Pre-built vs. Custom
Pre-built features (sentiment, NER, language detection, key phrases, PII) work with zero training — call the API and get results. Custom features (CLU for intent recognition, Custom NER, Custom text classification) require you to define labels and provide training examples.
Exam Scope
The AI-900 exam tests your knowledge of what each feature does and when to use it — not the implementation details. Key distinctions: sentiment vs. opinion mining, NER vs. PII detection, key phrase extraction vs. summarization, CLU vs. QnA for different conversational scenarios.

Azure AI Translator
Real-time text and document translation across 100+ languages — powered by neural machine translation (NMT).
Neural Machine Translation
Azure Translator uses deep neural networks (encoder-decoder with attention) rather than rule-based or phrase-based translation. NMT models understand entire sentences as context — producing fluent, natural output rather than literal word-for-word substitution. Supports 100+ languages and 1,000+ language pairs.
Key Capabilities
Text translation (single call, multiple target languages simultaneously), document translation (preserves formatting in Word, PDF, HTML), custom translator (fine-tune on domain-specific terminology), language auto-detection, transliteration (converting script without translation), and dictionary lookup for word definitions.
Real-Time Use Cases
Multilingual customer support portals, international e-commerce product listings, cross-language search, live chat translation, global news aggregation, and enterprise document localization. Combined with Speech services, enables real-time spoken translation for international meetings.

Conversational AI
Chatbots, virtual assistants, and QnA systems that understand user intent and maintain multi-turn dialog context.
Azure Bot Service
A managed PaaS platform for building, hosting, and connecting bots to multiple channels (Teams, Slack, WhatsApp, web chat). Integrates with Azure AI Language for intent recognition, QnA, and CLU. Handles authentication, state management, and channel adapters so you focus on dialog logic.
Custom Question Answering
Formerly "QnA Maker" — now part of Azure AI Language. You provide a knowledge base (FAQ documents, URLs, or manual Q&A pairs) and the service builds a retrieval model. When users ask questions, the bot returns the most relevant answer with a confidence score. No intent training required.
Bot Framework Composer
A low-code graphical dialog designer for building multi-turn conversational flows. Define triggers (intents, events), branches (conditions), and actions (API calls, responses). Generates standard Bot Framework dialogs that can be extended in code. Lowers the barrier for building production bots without writing everything from scratch.

NLP Scenarios
Where NLP creates tangible business value across industries — five real-world deployment patterns you need to recognize for the AI-900 exam.
Customer Support Automation
Classify incoming support tickets by topic and urgency using NER and sentiment. Route negative, high-urgency tickets to senior staff immediately. Auto-suggest knowledge base answers to agents. Deflect common questions with a QnA bot before human escalation.
Document Summarization
Automatically generate abstractive or extractive summaries of long documents — legal contracts, research papers, medical records, earnings reports. Azure AI Language's summarization feature produces multi-sentence summaries and sentence-level rank scores.
Content Moderation
Detect and filter hate speech, personal attacks, profanity, and sensitive information in user-generated content before it is published. NLP-based moderation understands context — "I love this product!" and "I love destroying things!" are processed very differently from keyword matching alone.
AI-900 exam tip: match the scenario to the Azure service — QnA for FAQ bots, CLU for intent-driven apps, Translator for multilingual, sentiment for feedback analysis.

Chapter 3 Summary
Key Takeaways from Chapter 3
1 NLP = AI that reads, understands, and generates human language by converting unstructured text into structured data — entities, intents, sentiment, and topics.
2 Text Analytics provides four pre-built, zero-shot features: sentiment analysis, key phrase extraction, named entity recognition (NER), and language detection. No training data required.
3 Sentiment analysis returns positive/negative/neutral/mixed scores at both document and sentence level. NER detects typed entities (ORG, PERSON, DATE, PRODUCT). PII detection specializes in sensitive data identification.
4 CLU (Conversational Language Understanding) requires training data to recognize custom intents and extract slot entities. Use for action-oriented apps (booking, ordering). Use Custom Question Answering for FAQ-style retrieval.
5 Azure AI Translator supports 100+ languages with neural machine translation. Supports text, document, and custom domain translation. Language detection is automatic.
6 Conversational AI combines Azure Bot Service + CLU + Custom QnA + Bot Framework Composer. Together they power helpdesks, virtual assistants, and knowledge search interfaces.
