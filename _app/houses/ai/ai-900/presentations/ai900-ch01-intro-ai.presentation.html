What is Artificial Intelligence?
Software that mimics human cognitive capabilities using data and math.
Vision
Identifying objects, people, text, and scenes in images and video. Humans do this naturally — AI learns it from millions of labeled examples.
Language
Understanding and generating human language — reading comprehension, translation, sentiment analysis, summarization, and conversation.
Decision & Learning
Making predictions and recommendations from data patterns. Fraud detection, product recommendations, medical diagnosis support — all learned from examples, not hand-coded rules.
"AI is not magic — it's math, data, and engineering."
Slide 3 of 12
Machine Learning Fundamentals
Teaching computers to learn from data instead of explicit programming.
Features — x (Inputs)
The measurable properties used to make a prediction. For predicting house prices: square footage, location, number of bedrooms. For email spam: word frequency, sender domain, link count. Features are your x values.
Labels — y (Known Answers)
The correct answer for each training example — the value you want the model to learn to predict. House price in dollars. Spam = 1 / not spam = 0. During training, the algorithm compares predicted y to actual y and adjusts.
Model — f (The Learned Function)
The mathematical function learned from training data. Given new x values (features it has never seen), the model computes f(x) and outputs a prediction. The formula y = f(x) is the entire concept of supervised ML in one expression.
Slide 4 of 12
Types of Machine Learning
Three branches — defined by what kind of data you train on.
Supervised Learning
Train with examples that have known correct answers (labels).
Regression — predict a number. How much will this house sell for?
Classification — predict a category. Is this email spam or not spam?
Unsupervised Learning
No labels — the algorithm discovers patterns on its own.
Clustering — group similar data points together without being told what the groups are. Used for customer segmentation, anomaly detection, topic modeling.
Deep Learning
Multi-layer neural networks that learn hierarchical representations.
Powers image recognition, speech, and large language models. Requires large datasets and significant compute. The foundation of modern AI.
Slide 5 of 12
Regression — Predicting a Number
Output is a continuous numeric value. The model learns the best-fit function through the data.
When to Use Regression
Any time the answer is a number on a continuous scale: house price, temperature tomorrow, expected website traffic, stock value. If you can measure it with a ruler, regression probably applies.
# Example training record Features: temperature=28, rainfall=2mm Label: ice_cream_sales=215 units # After training — predict with new data Features: temperature=34, rainfall=0mm Prediction: 287 units
Common Regression Algorithms
Linear Regression (straight line), Polynomial Regression (curved line), Decision Tree Regression, Random Forest Regression, Neural Network Regression. Azure ML Designer has all of these as drag-and-drop components.
Measuring Regression Quality
Mean Absolute Error (MAE) — average distance between predicted and actual. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) — penalizes large errors more. R-squared (R2) — proportion of variance explained. Lower MAE/RMSE and higher R2 = better model.
Slide 6 of 12
Classification — Predicting a Category
Output is a class label, not a number. Binary = 2 classes. Multiclass = 3 or more.
Binary Classification Examples
Spam / Not Spam  •  Fraud / Legitimate  •  Disease / Healthy
Churn / Retained  •  Pass / Fail
Output is a probability: 0.87 = 87% chance of spam. Apply a threshold (usually 0.5) to get the final yes/no label.
Multiclass Classification Examples
Penguin species (Adelie, Gentoo, Chinstrap)
Handwritten digit (0-9)  •  Fruit type
Document category  •  Sentiment (positive, neutral, negative)
Output is probabilities for each class — the highest wins.
Slide 7 of 12
Clustering — Finding Groups Without Labels
Unsupervised learning discovers natural groupings in data. You do not tell it what the groups are.
How K-Means Clustering Works
1. Choose K (number of clusters)
2. Place K random centroids in the data space
3. Assign each point to its nearest centroid
4. Move each centroid to the mean of its group
5. Repeat steps 3-4 until centroids stop moving
Real-World Applications
Customer segmentation — group customers by behavior without knowing what segments exist
Anomaly detection — points that don't fit any cluster may be outliers or threats
Document grouping — organize articles by topic automatically

Deep Learning & Neural Networks
Layers of interconnected nodes that progressively extract and transform features.
Why "Deep"?
Depth = number of hidden layers. More layers = more abstract representations. Early layers detect edges and shapes. Later layers detect objects and concepts. "Deep" became synonymous with multi-layer networks in the 2010s when GPU training made them practical.
On the AI-900 Exam
You need to know: neural networks have an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each layer transforms the previous layer's output. Training adjusts the weights on all connections to minimize prediction error. Deep learning powers Azure AI Vision and Azure AI Speech.

Azure AI Services Overview
Pre-built AI capabilities via REST APIs — no training required to get started.
Azure AI Vision
Analyze images and video. Object detection, OCR (read text in images), face analysis, image classification, spatial analysis. Used in retail, manufacturing quality control, and accessibility tools.
Azure AI Language
Text analytics at scale. Sentiment analysis, key phrase extraction, named entity recognition, language detection, question answering, and summarization. Powers chatbots and document processing pipelines.

Speech-to-text, text-to-speech, speech translation, and speaker recognition. Real-time transcription for meetings, voice-controlled applications, and accessibility captioning.

Slide 10 of 12
Generative AI & Large Language Models
LLMs trained on massive text corpora learn to generate coherent, contextually appropriate content.
Prompts
The input you give an LLM. The quality of the prompt directly determines the quality of the output. Prompt engineering — crafting effective inputs — is a real skill tested on AI-900 and used daily in Azure AI deployments.
Tokens
LLMs process text as tokens — roughly 4 characters or 0.75 words each. "Azure AI Fundamentals" = 4 tokens. Pricing, speed, and context limits are all measured in tokens, not words or characters.
Context Window
The maximum amount of text an LLM can consider at once (prompt + response). GPT-4 Turbo has a 128,000-token context window — about 100,000 words. Conversations are limited by this window.
"LLMs don't 'understand' — they predict the most likely next token based on patterns learned from training data."

Microsoft's Responsible AI Principles
Six principles that define how AI should be designed, deployed, and governed.
Fairness
AI systems should treat all people equitably. Avoid encoding biases from training data that could discriminate by race, gender, or age.
Reliability & Safety
Systems perform reliably across conditions. Errors are handled gracefully without causing harm. Critical AI requires rigorous testing and fallback mechanisms.
Privacy & Security
Personal data is protected throughout the AI lifecycle — during training, inference, and storage. GDPR, HIPAA, and Azure compliance frameworks apply.
Inclusiveness
AI must work for everyone — including people with disabilities, different languages, and diverse backgrounds. Accessibility is not an afterthought.
Transparency
Users should understand how AI reaches decisions. Explainability and model cards help. Black-box decisions with no recourse undermine trust and accountability.
Accountability
Humans remain responsible for AI systems. Organizations must have governance frameworks, audit trails, and clear ownership. "The AI decided" is not an acceptable legal or ethical defense.

Chapter 1 Summary
You now have the conceptual foundation for the entire AI-900 exam. Every Azure service, every lab, and every exam question in chapters 2 through 6 builds directly on what is in this chapter. Learn the vocabulary precisely — exams use these words exactly.
Key Takeaways from Chapter 1
1 AI mimics human capabilities — vision, language, decision-making, and learning — using data and mathematics, not magic or sentience.
2 Machine Learning formula: y = f(x). Features (x) go in, a trained model f computes them, a prediction (y) comes out. This is the entire supervised learning concept.
3 Three ML types: Supervised (labeled data → regression + classification), Unsupervised (no labels → clustering), Deep Learning (neural networks for complex patterns).
4 Regression predicts numbers. Classification predicts categories. Binary = 2 classes. Multiclass = 3 or more. Clustering finds groups without any labels.
5 Azure AI Services are pre-trained, ready-to-consume APIs. Vision, Language, Speech, OpenAI Service, and Document Intelligence — no training required to call them.
6 LLMs predict the next token. They do not understand — they generate. Key concepts: prompts, tokens (~4 chars each), and context window. Azure OpenAI provides enterprise-grade access.
7 6 Responsible AI principles: Fairness, Reliability & Safety, Privacy & Security, Inclusiveness, Transparency, Accountability. Memorize these — they appear directly on the exam.
