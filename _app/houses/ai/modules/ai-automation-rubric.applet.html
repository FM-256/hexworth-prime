The Automation Rubric

Not everything should be automated. This framework helps you decide what to automate, when to automate it, and how much precision to demand — before you write a single line of code.

Framework
The Three-Step Process
Before applying any automation rubric, you must complete two prerequisites. Many teams skip to Step 3 and fail.
01
Document the Process
Write down exactly what happens today. Who does what, in what order, using which inputs and outputs. If you cannot describe it, you cannot automate it.
02
Optimize First
Remove waste before automating. Automating a bad process makes the bad process faster. Eliminate unnecessary steps, approvals, and handoffs first.
03
Evaluate with the Rubric
Score the optimized process on four criteria. The total score determines whether automation is worth building and what guardrails to apply.
The #1 automation mistake: skipping Step 2. If your expense report process has 12 unnecessary approval steps, automating it gives you a 12-step approval bot — not a better process.
The Rubric
Four Criteria, Each Scored 1–5
Every criterion is rated on a scale from 1 (low automation value) to 5 (high automation value). Total score drives the recommendation.
Criterion 1: Frequency

How often is this task performed? Daily or hourly tasks (score 5) produce far more ROI from automation than tasks done once a year (score 1). A task done 50x per week that takes 5 minutes each time is a 4+ hour weekly drain — a strong automation target.

Criterion 2: Time Intensity

How long does a single execution take? Multi-hour tasks (score 5) offer more time savings than tasks completed in seconds (score 1). Pair this with frequency: a 4-hour weekly task is a stronger candidate than a 20-minute daily task.

Criterion 3: Structured Data

Are the inputs and outputs structured and predictable? Pure data manipulation (score 5) — CSV in, report out — is ideal for automation. Tasks requiring creative judgment, emotional intelligence, or ambiguous context (score 1) resist automation fundamentally.

Criterion 4: Clear Success Metrics

Can you objectively measure whether the automation succeeded? Binary pass/fail tests (score 5) — the email sent or it did not — make verification trivial. Subjective outcomes (score 1) — "did the customer feel satisfied?" — require human judgment to evaluate and are dangerous to automate without oversight.

Score interpretation: 16–20 = Strong candidate. 11–15 = Good with caveats. 6–10 = Optimize first. 4–5 = Keep human.
Precision
Precision Tiers: Where Should You Start?
Not all tasks require the same accuracy. Matching your precision requirement to your starting point prevents wasted engineering effort.
Low Precision Tier
90% OK
Data entry, scheduling, report formatting, notification routing, status updates. Start here. Human spot-checks catch edge cases without blocking throughput.
High Precision Tier
99%+ Required
Medical records, financial transactions, legal documents, access provisioning. Requires human-in-the-loop review gates, audit trails, and automated testing at each step.
The 80% → 98% Accuracy Journey
First 80% accuracy
Last 18%
Easy — a few days of work 10× the engineering effort
The 80→98 rule: Getting to 80% accuracy is relatively straightforward. Getting from 80% to 98% typically takes 10x the engineering effort. Plan your precision tier before you start building, not after you hit the wall.
