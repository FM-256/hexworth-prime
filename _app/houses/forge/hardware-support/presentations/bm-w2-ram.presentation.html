DDR Generations
Double Data Rate -- transfers data on both the rising and falling edges of the clock signal.
DDR3
Legacy. 240-pin DIMM. 1.5V (DDR3L: 1.35V). Max practical: 16 GB per stick. Found on pre-2014 systems. Not compatible with DDR4 slots (different notch position).
DDR4
Current mainstream. 288-pin DIMM. 1.2V. Max: 64 GB per stick. JEDEC base: 2133-3200 MHz. XMP profiles push to 4800+ MHz. Most installed base today.
DDR5
Latest generation. 288 pins but different notch (not backward compatible). 1.1V. On-die ECC. PMIC on the module. Two 32-bit channels per stick. JEDEC: 4800-6400 MHz.
Not interchangeable! DDR3, DDR4, and DDR5 have different notch positions. They will not physically fit in the wrong slot. A DDR4 board only takes DDR4. Check the motherboard spec sheet.

DIMM vs. SO-DIMM
Full-size DIMMs for desktops, compact SO-DIMMs for laptops and small form factor systems.
DIMM
Full-size Dual Inline Memory Module. DDR4: 288 pins. DDR5: 288 pins (different notch). DDR3: 240 pins. Used in desktop/tower PCs and servers. Latching clips on each end.
SO-DIMM
Small Outline DIMM. Roughly half the length. DDR4 SO-DIMM: 260 pins. DDR5 SO-DIMM: 262 pins. Used in laptops, NUCs, all-in-ones. Inserts at angle, presses flat.
Exam tip: DIMM and SO-DIMM are not interchangeable without an adapter. If a question says "laptop RAM" it means SO-DIMM. Desktop = DIMM. Some Mini-ITX boards use SO-DIMM.

Memory Channels
Multi-channel configurations multiply memory bandwidth. Dual-channel is standard; quad-channel for HEDT and servers.
Single Channel
One stick, one channel. 64-bit bus. Half the bandwidth of dual channel. Acceptable only for basic office tasks.
Dual Channel
Two matching sticks in correct slots. 128-bit effective bus. Doubles bandwidth. For 2 sticks on a 4-slot board: use slots 2 and 4 (A2/B2) -- check manual.
Quad Channel
Four matching sticks, four channels. 256-bit effective bus. Requires HEDT platform (X299, TRX40, Xeon). Diminishing returns for most workloads.
Matching rule: For dual channel, use identical capacity and speed sticks. Mismatched sticks may still work (flex mode) but at the slower module's speed. Always install in the correct color-coded slots.

ECC Memory
Error-Correcting Code RAM detects and corrects single-bit errors in real time. Critical for servers.
ECC (Unbuffered)
Extra chip per rank (9 chips instead of 8). Detects and corrects single-bit errors, detects double-bit errors. Used in workstations and entry servers. Requires ECC-compatible motherboard and CPU.
Registered/Buffered (RDIMM)
Has a register chip between memory controller and DRAM chips. Reduces electrical load, allows more DIMMs per channel. Server/data center only. Higher latency, higher capacity.
DDR5 has on-die ECC built into every module, but this only corrects errors within the DRAM chip itself. It is not the same as system-level ECC (which requires ECC DIMMs + compatible platform). The exam distinguishes these.

RAM Specifications
Reading a RAM spec: DDR4-3200 PC4-25600 CL16 1.35V 16GB 2Rx8 -- here is what each part means.
DDR4Generation. DDR4 = 288 pins, 1.2V standard. Determines which motherboard slot it fits.
3200Speed (MT/s). 3200 megatransfers per second. Effective clock: 1600 MHz x 2 (double data rate).
PC4-25600Bandwidth. 25,600 MB/s peak transfer rate. Calculated: 3200 MT/s x 8 bytes = 25,600 MB/s.
CL16CAS Latency. 16 clock cycles to access data. Lower = faster. CL14 at 3200 is faster than CL18 at 3200.
1.35VVoltage. 1.2V is JEDEC standard for DDR4. 1.35V indicates XMP overclock profile. Higher voltage = more heat.
2Rx8Rank and chip density. 2R = dual rank (two 64-bit addressable areas). x8 = 8-bit wide chips. Affects compatibility with some platforms.

Installing RAM
Physical installation steps and common mistakes to avoid.
1ESD precautions. Ground yourself with a wrist strap or touch the case chassis. Handle DIMMs by the edges only.
2Open retention clips. Push outward on both latches (some boards have one fixed latch). Slot should be clear.
3Align the notch. The notch on the DIMM matches the key in the slot. Only one orientation works. Do not force it.
4Press firmly and evenly. Apply pressure on both ends simultaneously. The retention clips should click closed automatically.
5Verify in BIOS. Boot into UEFI and check that the full capacity and speed are recognized. Enable XMP/EXPO if needed.
Dual channel slot order: On a 4-DIMM board, slots are typically A1, B1, A2, B2. For 2 sticks, populate A2 and B2 first (slots 2 and 4). Installing in A1 and B1 may not enable dual channel on all boards. Always check the manual.

GPU -- Graphics Cards
The most power-hungry and bandwidth-demanding expansion card. Lives in the PCIe x16 slot.
Dedicated GPU
Separate card with its own VRAM (GDDR6/GDDR6X), cooling, and power connectors. PCIe x16 slot. Requires 6-pin, 8-pin, or 12VHPWR power. Used for gaming, CAD, ML, video editing.
Integrated Graphics (iGPU)
Built into the CPU (Intel UHD/Iris, AMD Radeon). Shares system RAM. No extra power needed. Fine for office work, web, video playback. Not for gaming or 3D work.
VRAM
Video RAM dedicated to the GPU. GDDR6: 14-18 Gbps. GDDR6X: 21-24 Gbps. More VRAM = higher resolutions and textures. 8 GB minimum for 1440p gaming.
Power Requirements
Mid-range: 150-200W (1x 8-pin). High-end: 300-450W (2x 8-pin or 12VHPWR). GPU is the biggest power consumer in most systems.
Display Outputs
HDMI 2.1, DisplayPort 1.4/2.0, sometimes USB-C (DP Alt Mode). Multiple outputs for multi-monitor. Always connect monitor to GPU -- not motherboard -- when using a dedicated card.

M.2 Form Factor
A compact slot on the motherboard for SSDs and Wi-Fi cards. Replaces 2.5-inch SATA drives and mSATA.
M.2 NVMe (Key M)
Uses PCIe lanes (x4). Gen 3: 3,500 MB/s. Gen 4: 7,000 MB/s. Gen 5: 14,000 MB/s. The fastest consumer storage. Single notch on the right side of the connector.
M.2 SATA (Key B+M)
Uses SATA protocol through M.2 slot. Max 600 MB/s (SATA III limit). Two notches on connector. Check board support -- not all M.2 slots accept SATA.
M.2 Wi-Fi (Key A+E)
Smaller slot for Wi-Fi/Bluetooth combo cards (Intel AX210, etc.). Uses PCIe x1 + USB. Key notches on left side. Usually pre-populated on boards with built-in Wi-Fi.

Other Expansion Cards
Beyond GPUs -- NICs, sound cards, RAID controllers, capture cards, and USB expansion.
Network Interface Card
PCIe x1 or x4. Adds 10 GbE, Wi-Fi 6E, or specialized network connectivity. Server NICs support SR-IOV, teaming, and hardware offload.
Sound Card
PCIe x1. Dedicated DAC and amplifier for high-quality audio. Reduces CPU load for audio processing. Used by audiophiles and studios.
RAID Controller
PCIe x4 or x8. Hardware RAID with dedicated processor and cache (BBU). RAID 0/1/5/6/10. Enterprise storage arrays. Battery-backed write cache.
Capture Card
PCIe x4. Captures HDMI/SDI video input for streaming or recording. Elgato, AVerMedia, Blackmagic. Hardware encoding reduces CPU load.
USB Expansion
PCIe x1. Adds USB 3.2 or USB-C ports. Common when the motherboard lacks enough USB ports or needs USB-C headers.
Riser Cards
Redirect PCIe slots for different orientations. Used in SFF cases, mining rigs, and 1U servers. Does not add functionality, just redirects the connection.

RAM Troubleshooting
Common RAM failures and how to diagnose them.
1No POST, continuous beeps: RAM not detected. Reseat the DIMMs. Try one stick at a time. Clean contacts with isopropyl alcohol.
2Blue screen (BSOD) / kernel panic: Possible bad memory cell. Run Windows Memory Diagnostic or MemTest86 overnight.
3Only half the RAM showing: Sticks not in dual-channel slots, or one DIMM failed. Check BIOS -- it shows per-slot detection.
4System unstable after XMP: RAM overclock is too aggressive. Reset to JEDEC defaults and re-enable XMP. Try the next lower profile.
5Mixing RAM: Different speeds/timings may cause instability. System runs at the slowest module's speed. Match sticks whenever possible.

Installation Best Practices
Guidelines for installing expansion cards and RAM in any system.
ESD Protection
Always ground yourself. Use an anti-static wrist strap connected to the case chassis. Never touch gold contacts. Work on a non-conductive surface. Do not build on carpet.
Slot Selection
GPU goes in the top PCIe x16 slot (CPU-direct lanes). If two x16 slots exist, the top one is always full bandwidth. Secondary slots may run at x8 or x4.
Driver Installation
GPU: install manufacturer drivers (NVIDIA/AMD/Intel). NIC: Windows usually auto-detects. Sound card: install manufacturer software. Always get drivers from the vendor site.
Thermal Management
M.2 NVMe SSDs need heatsinks (often included with the board). GPUs need adequate case airflow. Do not block intake/exhaust. Thermal throttling degrades performance.

RAM & Expansion Summary
1DDR3/4/5 are not interchangeable -- different notch positions, pin counts, and voltages.
2DIMM for desktops (full-size), SO-DIMM for laptops (half-size). Same DDR generation required.
3Dual channel doubles bandwidth. Install matching sticks in correct color-coded slots (typically A2 + B2 first).
4ECC corrects single-bit errors. Required for servers. DDR5 on-die ECC is different from system-level ECC.
5GPU = PCIe x16, dedicated VRAM, separate power connectors. Always connect monitors to the GPU, not the motherboard.
6M.2 NVMe (Key M) uses PCIe lanes for maximum speed. M.2 SATA (Key B+M) is limited to 600 MB/s. Different keying prevents wrong insertion.
7XMP/EXPO enables RAM speeds above JEDEC defaults. Must be enabled in BIOS. Not stable on all systems.
