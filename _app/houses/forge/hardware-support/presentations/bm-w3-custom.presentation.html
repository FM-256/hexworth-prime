Gaming PC
Priority: GPU performance, fast storage, high-refresh display output. Budget goes to the graphics card first.
GPU Priority
The single most important component. 40-50% of budget. RTX 4060 for 1080p, RTX 4070 Ti for 1440p, RTX 4080+ for 4K. VRAM matters for modern titles.
CPU
Mid-range is fine: i5 or Ryzen 5. Gaming is GPU-bound, not CPU-bound at higher resolutions. 6-8 cores sufficient. Save money here to spend on GPU.
RAM: 16-32 GB DDR5
16 GB minimum, 32 GB for future-proofing. Speed matters for gaming (DDR5-6000 sweet spot for Ryzen). Dual channel required.
Storage: NVMe SSD
1-2 TB NVMe Gen 4 for game library. Games are 50-200 GB each. DirectStorage requires NVMe. HDD for overflow only.
PSU: 750-850W Gold
GPU is the biggest power draw. Fully modular for cable management. Leave headroom for future GPU upgrades.
Case: ATX mid-tower with good airflow. Mesh front panel. 3 intake + 1 exhaust fans minimum. Tempered glass side panel optional. AIO liquid cooler for CPU.
Slide 3 of 14
Workstation
Priority: CPU cores, ECC RAM, large RAM capacity, professional GPU. Built for CAD, video editing, 3D rendering, data science.
CPU Priority
High core count: i7/i9, Ryzen 9, or Threadripper. Rendering and compilation scale with cores. 16-64 cores depending on workload and budget.
RAM: 64-128 GB ECC
Large datasets in memory. ECC for data integrity (required for simulation, engineering). Quad-channel on HEDT. DDR5 for bandwidth.
Professional GPU
NVIDIA RTX A-series or AMD Radeon Pro. ISV-certified drivers for CAD (SolidWorks, AutoCAD). More VRAM, better double-precision. Not gaming cards.
Storage
NVMe Gen 4 for OS + active projects. Secondary NVMe for scratch disk. Large HDD array or NAS for project archives. Consider RAID.
Reliability
ECC RAM prevents silent data corruption. Redundant PSU optional. UPS mandatory. IPMI/BMC for remote management on server boards.
Slide 4 of 14
NAS / Home Server
Network Attached Storage -- priority is drive capacity, redundancy, low power, and quiet operation.
CPU: Low Power
Runs 24/7, efficiency matters. Intel Celeron/i3 or Ryzen 3 sufficient. ECC support preferred. iGPU for Plex transcoding (Intel Quick Sync).
RAM: 8-32 GB ECC
Depends on OS. TrueNAS recommends 1 GB per TB of storage for ZFS. ECC critical for ZFS data integrity. 16 GB is a good starting point.
Storage: Many Bays
4-8+ drive bays. NAS-rated HDDs (WD Red Plus, Seagate IronWolf). RAID 5 or RAID 6 for redundancy. NVMe for cache/metadata.
Case
Hot-swap bays preferred. Compact tower or rack-mount. Good cooling for drive thermals. Quiet fans (runs 24/7 in living spaces).
Network
2.5 GbE minimum. 10 GbE for video editing workflows. Dual NIC for teaming or VLAN separation. UPS mandatory for safe shutdown.
Slide 5 of 14
HTPC -- Home Theater PC
Sits next to the TV. Priority: 4K playback, quiet operation, small form factor, HDMI output.
CPU with iGPU
No discrete GPU needed. Intel with Quick Sync or AMD APU handles 4K decode. Low TDP (35-65W). Fanless or very quiet cooler.
Case: SFF / HTPC
Living room aesthetics. Silverstone ML-series, Fractal Node. Low profile. Fits next to receiver. IR remote support optional.
RAM: 8-16 GB
Minimal needs. 8 GB is fine for Plex/Kodi. 16 GB if also used for light browsing. Speed not critical.
Storage: SSD + HDD
256 GB NVMe for OS. Large HDD (4-8 TB) or NAS mount for media library. Silent SSD for OS eliminates disk noise.
Noise is Key
Total system under 30 dB at idle. Fanless PSU, passive CPU cooler, or AIO with low-RPM fans. No coil whine. Vibration-dampened drives.
Slide 6 of 14
Thin Client / Kiosk
Minimal local hardware. Processing happens on a remote server (VDI). Priority: reliability, low cost, small footprint.
Hardware
Low-power CPU (Celeron, Atom, ARM). 4-8 GB RAM. 32-64 GB flash/eMMC storage. No moving parts. Fanless. Purpose-built (Dell Wyse, HP t-series) or repurposed old PCs.
Software
Runs thin client OS (ThinOS, Linux), or Windows IoT. Boots to VDI client (Citrix, VMware Horizon, RDP). All apps and data on the server.
Advantages
Low cost, easy to manage (centralized), secure (no local data), low power, long lifespan, no local malware risk. Scales by adding server capacity.
Limitations
Requires reliable network. Server outage = all clients down. High-latency tasks (video editing, 3D) do not work well. Dependent on infrastructure.

Thermal Management
Heat is the enemy of performance and longevity. Every custom build needs a cooling strategy.
Air Cooling
Tower cooler on CPU (Noctua, be quiet!). Case fans for airflow (front intake, rear/top exhaust). Positive pressure (more intake than exhaust) reduces dust.
AIO Liquid Cooling
Closed-loop cooler with pump, radiator, fans. 240mm or 360mm. Better thermal performance than most air coolers. Mount radiator as intake (top or front).
Thermal Paste
Fills microscopic gaps between CPU and heatsink. Pre-applied on most stock coolers. Reapply when remounting. Pea-size dot in center. Replace every 3-5 years.
Warning signs: Thermal throttling (CPU/GPU reduces speed to cool down), random shutdowns, system instability under load. Check temperatures with HWMonitor/HWiNFO. CPU over 95C or GPU over 90C = cooling problem.

Component Compatibility
Every part must be compatible with every other part. Here is the compatibility checklist.
1CPU + Motherboard: Socket must match (LGA 1700, AM5). Chipset determines features. Check QVL (Qualified Vendor List).
2Motherboard + RAM: DDR4 or DDR5 (not interchangeable). Check supported speeds and max capacity per slot.
3Motherboard + Case: Form factor (ATX in ATX case, mATX in mATX or ATX case). Check I/O shield and standoff alignment.
4GPU + Case: GPU length clearance (measure from PCIe slot to drive cage). GPU thickness (2-slot, 2.5-slot, 3-slot).
5CPU Cooler + Case: Cooler height clearance. Tower coolers can be 160mm+ tall. AIO radiator must fit in case (top, front, side).
6PSU + Everything: Enough wattage. Correct connectors (especially GPU power). PSU form factor fits the case.

Build Process
The recommended order of assembly for a custom PC build.
1Prepare workspace. Anti-static mat, good lighting, motherboard manual open. Ground yourself.
2Install CPU into motherboard socket. Align triangle, drop in, close retention lever.
3Install RAM into correct dual-channel slots. Press until clips click.
4Install M.2 SSD into M.2 slot. Screw down with standoff.
5Install CPU cooler with thermal paste. Secure mounting hardware. Connect fan header.
6Install motherboard into case on standoffs. Secure with screws. Do not over-tighten.
7Install PSU in case. Route cables. Connect 24-pin, 8-pin CPU, front panel headers.
8Install GPU in top PCIe x16 slot. Secure bracket. Connect PCIe power cables.
9Connect drives, fans, front I/O. Cable manage. Close case. Power on and enter BIOS.

Budget Allocation
Where to spend money depends entirely on the use case. Here are the allocation priorities by build type.
Gaming: GPU First
GPU > CPU > RAM > SSD > PSU > Case. Allocate 40-50% of budget to GPU. Save on case and peripherals if needed.
Workstation: CPU + RAM
CPU > RAM > Pro GPU > Storage > PSU. Core count and RAM capacity drive productivity. ECC RAM is non-negotiable for production work.
NAS: Storage First
Drives > Case (bays) > RAM > CPU > Network. Drives are 60%+ of cost. Buy NAS-rated drives (warranty covers 24/7 operation).
Slide 11 of 14
Virtualization Host
A PC built to run multiple virtual machines simultaneously. Hypervisor workload.
CPU
Many cores, VT-x/AMD-V required. Each VM needs dedicated cores. Ryzen 9 (16 cores) or Threadripper (up to 96 cores). Enable IOMMU for GPU passthrough.
RAM: As Much as Possible
Each VM needs its own RAM allocation. 64 GB minimum for a useful lab. 128-256 GB for serious homelab. ECC preferred.
Storage
Fast NVMe for VM storage (random I/O intensive). Separate OS drive from VM storage. Multiple NVMe SSDs on different controllers to avoid contention.
Network
Multiple NICs for VM traffic separation. 10 GbE for storage network. SR-IOV capable NICs for direct VM-to-NIC mapping. VLANs for isolation.

Exam Scenarios
The A+ exam gives you a use case and asks you to select the best components. Practice these patterns.
Q"Best for video editing?" High core-count CPU, 64+ GB RAM, fast NVMe scratch disk, professional GPU with hardware encoding.
Q"Budget office PC?" i3/Ryzen 3 with iGPU, 8-16 GB RAM, 256 GB SATA SSD, integrated graphics, small case, basic PSU.
Q"Quiet living room media PC?" APU (no discrete GPU), fanless or low-noise cooling, SFF case, NVMe boot + HDD for media, HDMI output.
Q"Maximum uptime server?" ECC RAM, RAID 1 or 10, redundant PSU, UPS, hot-swap drives, IPMI remote management.
Q"Classroom of 30 PCs?" Thin clients + VDI server. Low per-seat cost, centralized management, no local data to secure.

Post-Build Checklist
1BIOS: Update firmware. Enable XMP/EXPO. Set boot order. Enable Secure Boot and TPM. Verify all components detected.
2OS Install: Boot from USB installer. Use GPT partitioning. Install to NVMe SSD. Activate license.
3Drivers: Chipset drivers first, then GPU drivers, then network/audio. Windows Update for remaining. Check Device Manager for warnings.
4Stress Test: Run Prime95 (CPU), FurMark (GPU), MemTest86 (RAM) overnight. Monitor temperatures. If stable for 8 hours, the build is solid.
5Backup: Create a system image before installing software. Set up regular backup schedule (3-2-1 rule).

Custom Builds Summary
1Gaming: GPU first (40%+ budget), mid-range CPU, 16-32 GB RAM, NVMe SSD, 750W+ PSU.
2Workstation: High core-count CPU, 64-128 GB ECC RAM, professional GPU, NVMe + archive storage.
3NAS: Low-power CPU, ECC RAM, many drive bays, NAS-rated HDDs, RAID, quiet case, UPS.
4HTPC: APU with iGPU, SFF case, silent cooling, HDMI output, media storage.
5Thin Client: Minimal hardware, boots to VDI, centrally managed, low cost per seat.
6Compatibility: Always verify socket, DDR gen, form factor, GPU clearance, cooler height, PSU wattage.
7Thermals: Air vs. AIO, positive pressure, thermal paste, monitor temps. Over 95C CPU = problem.
