Power Supplies
PSU Types -- Wattage -- 80 Plus -- Modular -- Connectors -- Voltage Rails -- UPS
The PSU converts AC wall power to DC voltages the PC needs. A failing or undersized PSU causes random shutdowns, instability, and component damage. This deck covers PSU selection, efficiency ratings, connectors, and uninterruptible power supplies.

PSU Basics
The PSU converts 120V/240V AC from the wall outlet to +3.3V, +5V, and +12V DC for components.
+12V Rail
Powers CPU and GPU -- the two biggest consumers. Modern PSUs deliver 90%+ of their wattage on the 12V rail. This is the rail that matters most.
+5V Rail
Powers USB ports, SATA drives, and some motherboard logic. Less demand than 12V on modern systems.
+3.3V Rail
Powers RAM, some chipset functions, and low-voltage logic. Generated from the 12V rail in modern PSUs via DC-DC conversion.
ATX standard: PSUs also provide +5V standby (always-on for Wake-on-LAN, USB charging) and the Power_Good signal that tells the motherboard all voltages are stable.

Wattage Selection
Total wattage must exceed peak system draw. Add up all components and add 20-30% headroom.
CPU65-125W typical (up to 253W for i9/Ryzen 9). Check TDP rating. Actual power can exceed TDP under boost.
GPU75-450W depending on tier. RTX 4060: 115W. RTX 4090: 450W. The biggest variable in wattage calculation.
RAM3-5W per stick. Negligible. DDR5 has on-module PMIC so slightly higher than DDR4.
StorageHDD: 5-10W. SSD: 2-5W. NVMe: 5-8W. Minimal contributors unless you have many drives.
Fans1-5W each. Case fans, CPU cooler fan, AIO pump. 5-20W total for most systems.
Rule of thumb: Budget gaming PC: 550-650W. Mid-range: 750W. High-end (RTX 4080+): 850-1000W. Use online PSU calculators (Seasonic, Corsair, OuterVision) for precise estimates.

80 Plus Efficiency
80 Plus certification measures PSU efficiency -- how much AC power is converted to DC vs. wasted as heat.
Gold is the sweet spot. Most recommended tier for gaming/workstation builds. Bronze is acceptable for budget builds. Platinum/Titanium for servers running 24/7 where electricity costs matter.

Modular vs Non-Modular
How cables attach to the PSU affects cable management, airflow, and build flexibility.
Non-Modular
All cables permanently attached. Cheapest. Unused cables must be stuffed somewhere. Poor airflow in small cases. Adequate for budget builds.
Semi-Modular
24-pin ATX and 8-pin CPU permanently attached (you always need them). All other cables detachable. Good balance of price and flexibility.
Fully Modular
All cables detachable. Only connect what you need. Best cable management. Premium price. Ideal for clean builds and custom cables.
Warning: Modular cables are NOT interchangeable between brands. Corsair cables do not work in EVGA PSUs. The pin-out differs. Using wrong cables can destroy components. Always use cables from the same PSU model.

PSU Connectors
Every connector has a specific purpose. Know them for the exam and for building PCs.
2424-pin ATX: Main motherboard power. Keyed, only fits one way. Provides 3.3V, 5V, 12V, and standby power. Required.
84+4 pin EPS: CPU power. 12V only. Plugs in near the CPU socket (top-left of board). Some boards need two 8-pin connectors.
PCIe6+2 pin PCIe: GPU power. 6-pin: 75W. 8-pin (6+2): 150W. High-end GPUs need 2-3 connectors. Do not daisy-chain on high-end cards.
12V12VHPWR (16-pin): New connector for RTX 40/50 series. 600W through one cable. Proper seating is critical -- bent pins cause melting.
SATASATA power: 15-pin for drives and 2.5" SSDs. Multiple per cable (daisy chain). 3.3V, 5V, 12V.
4pinMolex (4-pin): Legacy. Powers case fans, old drives, RGB controllers. Being phased out. 5V and 12V only.

ATX12V & Form Factors
PSU form factors must match the case. ATX is standard; SFX for compact builds.
ATX (PS/2)
Standard desktop PSU. 150mm wide x 86mm tall x 140-180mm deep. Fits all ATX, mATX, and most full-tower cases. 120mm or 140mm fan.
SFX / SFX-L
Compact PSU for Mini-ITX and SFF cases. 125mm x 63.5mm x 100-130mm. Smaller fan, potentially louder. SFX-L adds length for a larger fan. Max ~850W.
Flex ATX
Very small form factor for 1U servers and ultra-compact cases. 150mm x 81.5mm x 40.5mm. Limited wattage (~300W max). Loud 40mm fan.
Server (Redundant)
Hot-swappable redundant PSUs in servers. Two PSUs share the load; if one fails, the other takes over seamlessly. 80 Plus Platinum or Titanium standard.

Voltage Rail Design
Single rail vs. multi-rail -- how 12V power is distributed and protected.
Single Rail
All 12V power on one rail. Full wattage available to any connector. Simpler. Less chance of tripping OCP on a specific rail. Preferred for high-power GPUs. Protection relies on OPP (over-power protection).
Multi-Rail
12V split into multiple rails (25-40A each). Each rail has its own OCP (over-current protection). Slightly safer -- a short on one rail does not affect others. Common in enterprise and server PSUs.
Modern trend: Most quality consumer PSUs are single-rail. Multi-rail was important when PSUs had weaker protections. Today's OPP, OCP, OVP, UVP, and SCP make single-rail safe for consumers.

UPS -- Uninterruptible Power Supply
Battery backup that provides power during outages and conditions incoming power to protect equipment.
Standby (Offline)
Cheapest. Battery only activates when power fails (5-12ms switchover). Basic surge protection. Fine for home PCs and peripherals. Not for servers.
Line-Interactive
AVR (Automatic Voltage Regulation) adjusts voltage without switching to battery. 2-4ms switchover. Handles brownouts and sags. Best value for small servers and workstations.
Online (Double-Conversion)
Power always runs through battery. Zero switchover time. Cleanest power (full isolation). Most expensive. Required for data centers and critical servers.
VA vs. Watts: UPS capacity is rated in VA (volt-amps) and watts. Watts = VA x power factor (~0.6-0.8). A 1500 VA UPS provides ~900W. Size the UPS for the total load plus 20-30% headroom.

Power Protection
Protecting equipment from power anomalies -- surges, sags, noise, and outages.
Surge Protector
Diverts excess voltage to ground via MOVs (Metal Oxide Varistors). Rated in joules -- higher is better. Replace after a major surge (MOVs degrade). Not the same as a power strip.
Power Conditioner
Filters electrical noise (EMI/RFI) and regulates voltage. Provides cleaner power than a surge protector alone. Used in audio studios and sensitive equipment.
1Surge: Voltage spike (milliseconds). Cause: lightning, utility switching. Protection: surge protector or UPS.
2Sag/Brownout: Voltage drops below normal. Cause: high demand on grid. Protection: line-interactive UPS with AVR.
3Blackout: Complete power loss. Protection: UPS provides battery runtime for safe shutdown.
4Noise: EMI/RFI interference on the power line. Protection: power conditioner, shielded cables, line filter.

PSU Troubleshooting
When the PSU fails, nothing works. These symptoms point to power issues.
1System will not power on at all: Check wall outlet, power cable, PSU switch. Test with a PSU tester or paperclip test (short PS_ON to ground on 24-pin).
2Random shutdowns under load: PSU cannot deliver enough power. Check GPU power connectors. Test with a known-good PSU. Check 12V rail voltage (should be 11.4-12.6V).
3System restarts on its own: Could be overheating, but also PSU OCP/OPP tripping. Check for burning smell. Inspect cables for damage.
4Coil whine or buzzing: Usually harmless (inductors vibrating). Excessive whine may indicate failing capacitors. Replace if accompanied by instability.
5Burning smell: Immediately power off and unplug. Inspect PSU and connectors. A melted 12VHPWR connector is a fire hazard. Do not reuse the PSU.

PSU Installation
Step-by-step PSU installation in an ATX case.
1Position: Most modern cases mount the PSU at the bottom with the fan facing down (draws cool air from under the case). Ensure bottom vent is not blocked.
2Secure: 4 screws through the back panel. Do not over-tighten. The PSU should not rattle.
3Route cables: 24-pin and 8-pin CPU through the back panel cutouts. Use cable management grommets. Route before connecting.
4Connect: 24-pin ATX to motherboard. 8-pin EPS to CPU. PCIe cables to GPU. SATA power to drives. Ensure all clips are fully engaged.
5Test: Flip PSU switch. Press power button. Verify all fans spin. Check BIOS for voltage readings.
