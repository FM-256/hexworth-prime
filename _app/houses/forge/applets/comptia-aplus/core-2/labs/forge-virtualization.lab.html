Virtualization Lab: briefing

What you are doing

You will build a virtual machine three times, on three hypervisors, and find that it is the same job every time. That is the point: a technician who can drive only one product has learned a menu, not virtualization.

  1. Stage 1, Oracle VirtualBox. One guest on your desktop: memory, an installer, an isolated network, a snapshot. You will see what an empty computer does when you boot it.
  2. Stage 2, VMware Workstation. The same machine, every concept renamed. .vdi becomes .vmdk, Guest Additions becomes VMware Tools, and "Internal Network" is gone. Finding what replaced it is the task.
  3. Stage 3, a Type 1 host. The subject stops being one VM and becomes the server: several guests, one finite pool of physical memory, and a host that does not warn when it runs out. It refuses.

Nothing is graded on clicking. Every tick is computed from the machine you actually built, and it goes back out if you break it.

This is a simulation, not the real binary. It models the decisions, the vocabulary and the arithmetic: how much memory you may safely hand a guest, what isolation actually means, why a bare-metal host refuses your fourth VM. Install the real thing from the links above and build the same machine again: everything here transfers, because the constraints are the same constraints.
