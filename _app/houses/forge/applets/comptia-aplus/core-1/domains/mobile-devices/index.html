Domain 1: Mobile Devices

Laptops, Displays, Connectivity & MDM (15% of Exam)

Laptop Hardware Components

Laptops use specialized, compact versions of desktop components.

Replaceable Laptop Components

Battery

Lithium-ion, built-in or removable. Replace when capacity drops.

Keyboard

Ribbon cable connection. Often held by screws or clips.

RAM

SO-DIMM form factor. Access panel or under keyboard.

Storage

2.5" SATA or M.2. Easy upgrade path.

Wireless Card

M.2 E-key or mini PCIe. WiFi + Bluetooth combo.

Screen

LCD panel replacement. Delicate ribbon cables.

Difficulty Varies: Some components (RAM, storage) are easily accessible. Others (keyboard, screen) may require significant disassembly.

Display Types

  • LCD-IPS: Wide viewing angles, accurate colors
  • LCD-TN: Fast response, limited angles
  • LCD-VA: High contrast, good colors
  • OLED: Perfect blacks, no backlight, burn-in risk
OLED displays don't use a backlight - each pixel produces its own light. This enables true black (pixel off) but can suffer from burn-in with static images.

Mobile Connectivity

Wired Connections

USB-C

Reversible, multi-purpose. Data, power, video. Universal.

Lightning

Apple proprietary. iPhone/iPad charging and data.

Micro-USB

Legacy Android. Still common on accessories.

Thunderbolt

40 Gbps. USB-C connector with lightning bolt.

Wireless Technologies

  • WiFi (802.11): a/b/g/n/ac/ax - Local networking
  • Bluetooth: Short range, peripherals, audio
  • NFC: Near Field Communication (~4 cm), payments
  • IR (Infrared): Line of sight, legacy remotes
  • Hotspot/Tethering: Share cellular data

Cellular Networks

  • 4G LTE: ~100 Mbps typical
  • 5G: ~1 Gbps potential, newer devices
  • GSM: SIM-based (AT&T, T-Mobile)
  • CDMA: ESN-based (Verizon legacy)
PRL (Preferred Roaming List) and PRI (Product Release Instructions) updates are carrier-specific. PRL tells phone which towers to use for roaming.

Mobile Device Management (MDM)

MDM allows organizations to manage and secure mobile devices remotely.

MDM Capabilities

  • Remote Wipe: Erase all data if device lost/stolen
  • Remote Lock: Lock device immediately
  • Screen Locks: Enforce PIN/password/biometrics
  • Locator: GPS tracking of device
  • Content Management: Control apps and data
  • Push Updates: Deploy apps and policies
  • Geofencing: Location-based policies

BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)

  • Personal devices used for work
  • Requires clear acceptable use policy
  • MDM can create work "container"
  • Privacy concerns for employees
COPE vs BYOD: COPE (Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled) means company owns the device but allows personal use. BYOD is employee-owned with work access.

Data Synchronization

Types of Data to Sync

  • Contacts and calendar
  • Email and messages
  • Photos and videos
  • Documents and files
  • Application data
  • Authentication credentials

Sync Destinations

Cloud

iCloud, Google, OneDrive. Automatic, cross-device.

Desktop

iTunes/Finder, Android transfer. Local backup.

Automobile

CarPlay, Android Auto. Contacts, music, maps.

Account Types

  • Microsoft 365: Exchange-based, enterprise
  • Google Workspace: Gmail, Drive, Calendar
  • Apple iCloud: Apple device ecosystem
  • Exchange ActiveSync: Corporate email sync
ActiveSync is Microsoft's protocol for syncing mail, calendar, and contacts. It's supported by most email clients and MDM solutions.
