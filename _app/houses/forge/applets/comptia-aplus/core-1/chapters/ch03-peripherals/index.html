Display Technologies

LCD (Liquid Crystal Display)

The most common display technology today. LCD panels use liquid crystals that block or allow light to pass through, creating images. Key characteristics:

  • Low power consumption - Energy efficient compared to older CRT monitors
  • Lightweight - Thin profile makes them portable
  • Requires backlight - Cannot produce light on their own

LCD Panel Technologies

Type Full Name Best For Trade-off
TN Twisted Nematic Gaming (fast response) Poor viewing angles
VA Vertical Alignment Contrast ratio Slower response time
IPS In-Plane Switching Color accuracy, viewing angles More expensive

LCD Backlight Sources

💡

LED Backlight

  • Light Emitting Diodes
  • Better contrast ratio
  • No inverter board needed
  • Thinner displays
  • More energy efficient
🔦

CCFL Backlight

  • Cold Cathode Fluorescent Light
  • Older technology
  • Requires inverter board
  • Thicker displays
  • Higher power consumption

OLED (Organic Light-Emitting Diode)

Each pixel produces its own light - no backlight needed. This enables:

  • True blacks - Pixels can be completely off
  • Infinite contrast ratio - Perfect dark vs bright
  • Thinner designs - No backlight layer needed
  • Better viewing angles - No backlight bleed
Exam Tip: Know the difference: LCD needs a backlight (LED or CCFL), while OLED pixels create their own light. OLED = better blacks, LCD = longer lifespan.

Display Settings & Features

Resolution

The number of pixels displayed horizontally x vertically. Higher resolution = sharper image.

Name Resolution Aspect Ratio
HD (720p) 1280 x 720 16:9
Full HD (1080p) 1920 x 1080 16:9
QHD (1440p) 2560 x 1440 16:9
4K UHD 3840 x 2160 16:9
Native Resolution: The resolution that produces the best image quality on a display. Above or below native resolution is supported but may look blurry or stretched.

Refresh Rate

How many times per second the display updates the image. Measured in Hertz (Hz).

  • 60 Hz - Standard for office work
  • 120-144 Hz - Better for gaming
  • 240+ Hz - Competitive gaming

Multiple Displays

  • Extended desktop - Expands workspace across monitors
  • Duplicate/Mirror - Same image on both displays
  • Primary monitor - Must be designated in display settings

Privacy & Antiglare Filters

Physical screens that attach to displays to limit viewing angles (privacy) or reduce reflections (antiglare).

Projectors

Brightness is measured in lumens. Higher lumens = visible in brighter rooms.

Specialty Input Devices

  • Signature pad - Captures handwritten signatures
  • Barcode/QR scanner - Reads product codes
  • Magnetic stripe reader - Reads credit cards
  • Chip reader - Reads EMV chip cards
  • NFC/tap pay device - Contactless payments

Output Devices

  • Printer - Produces physical documents
  • Monitor - Visual display output

Both Input AND Output

  • Scanner - Can scan and sometimes print
  • KVM switch - Keyboard, Video, Mouse switch - shares peripherals between multiple computers

Video Devices

  • Webcam - Video input for calls/streaming
  • VR headset - Immersive display (computer-connected or smartphone-based)
  • Optical glasses/headsets - AR/Smart glasses

Audio Devices

Motherboards have color-coded audio jacks (3.5mm):

  • Green - Line out (speakers/headphones)
  • Pink - Microphone in
  • Blue - Line in
  • USB headsets - Include their own sound processing

Storage Devices

NAS (Network Attached Storage) - File server connected to the network. Can support RAID for redundancy. Used for video streaming and centralized file storage.

USB Connector Types

🔌

Type-A

Standard rectangular connector. Most common on computers.

📠

Type-B

Square-ish connector. Common on printers.

📱

Micro-USB

Small connector. Older phones, small devices.

🔄

USB-C

Reversible. Modern standard. Can carry data, video, power.

Lightning Connector

Apple's proprietary connector used on iPhones (until iPhone 15). 8-pin, reversible design.

USB Adapters

USB to Ethernet adapter - Adds wired network capability to devices without built-in Ethernet ports (ultrabooks, tablets).

Thunderbolt Technology

Thunderbolt is Intel's high-speed interface that combines data, video, and power into a single connection.

Version Year Speed Connector Features
Thunderbolt 1 2011 10 Gbps Mini DisplayPort -
Thunderbolt 2 2013 20 Gbps Mini DisplayPort 4K video support
Thunderbolt 3 2015 40 Gbps USB-C 10 Gbps Ethernet
Thunderbolt 4 2020 40 Gbps USB-C Two 4K or one 8K display
Key Point: Thunderbolt 3 and 4 use the USB-C connector, but not all USB-C ports support Thunderbolt! Look for the lightning bolt symbol (⚡) to identify Thunderbolt ports.

Video Cables and Connectors

VGA (Video Graphics Array)

  • Analog signal - Older technology
  • 15-pin D-sub connector (DE-15)
  • Blue connector color coding
  • Max cable length: 5-10 meters
  • Still found on projectors and legacy equipment

DVI (Digital Visual Interface)

Transitional technology between analog VGA and modern digital connections.

Type Signal Description
DVI-A Analog only Compatible with VGA
DVI-D Digital only Single or Dual Link
DVI-I Integrated (both) Single or Dual Link
Single vs Dual Link: Dual Link DVI doubles the bandwidth for higher resolutions (up to 2560x1600). Look for extra pins in the center of the connector.

HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface)

  • Digital audio AND video in one cable
  • Max cable length: ~20 meters
  • Connector variants: Standard, Mini-HDMI, Micro-HDMI
  • Most common modern video connection

DisplayPort

  • VESA standard - Open standard (no royalties)
  • Compatible with HDMI via adapters
  • Locking connector mechanism
  • Higher bandwidth than HDMI
  • Common on PC monitors and graphics cards
Exam Tip: HDMI carries audio + video. VGA is analog only. DisplayPort is VESA standard. Know the DVI variants: A=Analog, D=Digital, I=Integrated.

Hard Drive Cables and Connectors

SATA (Serial ATA)

The standard interface for internal storage devices.

Version Speed Common Use
SATA 1.0 1.5 Gbps Legacy
SATA 2.0 3.0 Gbps HDDs
SATA 3.0 6 Gbps SSDs

eSATA (External SATA)

External version of SATA. Same speed as internal SATA but designed for external drives. Does NOT provide power - requires separate power cable.

PATA/IDE (Parallel ATA)

  • Legacy technology - Replaced by SATA
  • 40-pin ribbon cable
  • Speed: 16-133 Mbps
  • Supports master/slave configuration

SCSI (Small Computer System Interface)

Enterprise/server storage interface. Parallel SCSI used wide ribbon cables.

SAS (Serial Attached SCSI)

Modern enterprise storage. Backward compatible with SATA drives.

Version Year Speed
SAS-1 2005 3 Gbps
SAS-2 2009 6 Gbps
SAS-3 2013 12 Gbps
SAS-4 2017 22.5 Gbps
Key Point: SAS drives can connect to SATA ports, but SATA drives CANNOT connect to SAS ports. SAS is used in enterprise/server environments.

Serial and Legacy Connectors

Serial Port (DB-9)

  • 9-pin D-sub connector
  • Sends data one bit at a time
  • Still used for: network equipment (routers, switches), industrial equipment, legacy devices
  • USB-to-Serial adapters available

Molex Connector

4-pin power connector (legacy). Previously used for HDDs, optical drives, fans. Being replaced by SATA power.

Legacy Alert: Serial ports and PATA/IDE are legacy technologies but may still appear on the A+ exam. Know what they look like and their basic purpose.
