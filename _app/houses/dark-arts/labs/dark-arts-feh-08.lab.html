Key phishing indicators: sender domain mismatch, urgency language, misspellings, suspicious URLs (homoglyphs like paypa1 vs paypal), executable attachments, Return-Path header differs from From header.
Legitimate companies never send .exe attachments. Check: does the URL domain match the claimed sender? Does the email address a generic "Dear Customer" instead of your name? Does the Return-Path differ from From?

Exercise 3: Vishing Script Analysis
Read each phone call transcript. Identify the social engineering techniques used. Select all that apply, then submit your analysis.
Call Transcript #1 — IT Support Impersonation
Attacker:
"Hi, this is Mike from the IT Security team. We've flagged your account for suspicious login attempts from Russia. I need to verify your identity to prevent a lockout. Can you confirm your username and current password?"
Victim:
"Oh, okay. It's jsmith... and my password is..."
Call Transcript #2 — Tech Support Scam
Attacker:
"Hello, I'm calling from Microsoft. Our systems have detected a critical virus on your computer. You need to install our remote support tool immediately — otherwise your files will be permanently deleted within the next hour."
Call Transcript #3 — BEC Phone Verification
Attacker (impersonating CFO):
"Hey, it's [CFO name]. I'm in a board meeting — can't talk long. I need you to process a wire transfer for $47,000 to close a deal. This is confidential. Don't discuss with anyone else yet, just get it done. Our legal team is waiting."
Cialdini's 6 principles of influence: Authority (claiming power/expertise), Scarcity (limited time/resource), Social Proof (others are doing it), Reciprocity (doing a favor first), Liking (building rapport), Commitment (getting small yes first).
