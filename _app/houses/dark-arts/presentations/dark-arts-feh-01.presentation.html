What is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the practice of deliberately probing computer systems, networks, and applications to find security vulnerabilities that malicious hackers could exploit. The key difference: authorization.

Core Definition

An ethical hacker uses the same tools, techniques, and methodologies as malicious hackers, but with explicit permission from the system owner to improve security rather than cause harm.

Legal Framework

  • Authorization is mandatory - Written permission before any testing
  • Scope must be defined - What systems, what methods, what timeframe
  • Rules of engagement - Clear boundaries on what's allowed
  • Data protection - Findings must be handled confidentially
  • Responsible disclosure - Report vulnerabilities properly
# The Three Questions Before Any Test:
1. Do I have written authorization?
2. Am I within the defined scope?
3. Am I complying with all legal requirements?

The CIA Triad

Every security control exists to protect one or more pillars of the CIA Triad. Understanding what you're protecting is essential to understanding how to attack it.

CONFIDENTIALITY

Keeping secrets secret. Information accessible only to authorized parties.

Attack Vector: Data exfiltration, eavesdropping, credential theft

✓ INTEGRITY

Ensuring data remains trustworthy and unaltered by unauthorized parties.

Attack Vector: Data tampering, man-in-the-middle, injection attacks

AVAILABILITY

Systems and data accessible when needed by authorized users.

Attack Vector: DDoS, ransomware, resource exhaustion

Hacker Perspective: Every attack targets at least one pillar. Identify which pillar is weakest in your target, and that's where you focus.

Types of Hackers

Not all hackers are the same. Understanding the landscape helps you understand motivations, methods, and the ethical boundaries that define your role.

White Hat Hackers (Ethical Hackers)

Authorized security professionals who hack to improve security. That's you.

Black Hat Hackers

Malicious actors who break into systems without permission for personal gain, disruption, or ideology.

Grey Hat Hackers

Operate in the grey area - may find vulnerabilities without permission but disclose them responsibly. Still illegal in most jurisdictions.

Script Kiddies

Inexperienced individuals using pre-built tools without understanding how they work. Low skill, high noise.

Hacktivists

Hack for political or social causes. Anonymous, LulzSec, etc. Ideologically motivated.

Nation-State Actors

Government-sponsored hackers with significant resources. APT groups, cyber warfare units. Highest sophistication.

The Cyber Kill Chain

Developed by Lockheed Martin, the Kill Chain describes the stages of a cyber attack. Understanding each phase helps both attackers (to execute) and defenders (to disrupt).

01
Reconnaissance

Research target, gather intel

02
Weaponization

Create malicious payload

03
Delivery

Transmit weapon to target

04
Exploitation

Execute code, trigger vuln

05
Installation

Install backdoor/persistence

06
Command & Control

Establish remote control

07
Actions on Objective

Achieve attacker's goal

Key Insight: Defenders only need to break one link in the chain to stop an attack. Attackers must successfully complete every phase. This asymmetry is why defense is possible despite being harder.

MITRE ATT&CK Framework

While the Kill Chain describes phases, MITRE ATT&CK is a knowledge base of real-world adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) based on observed attacks.

What is ATT&CK?

ATT&CK stands for Adversarial Tactics, Techniques, and Common Knowledge. It's a globally accessible framework maintained by MITRE Corporation that documents how threat actors operate throughout the attack lifecycle.

Structure

  • Tactics - The "why" (objectives): Initial Access, Execution, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, Defense Evasion, etc.
  • Techniques - The "how" (methods): Specific ways to achieve tactical goals (e.g., Phishing, Process Injection)
  • Sub-techniques - More specific variations of techniques
  • Procedures - Actual implementations by specific threat groups
# Example ATT&CK Mapping:
Tactic: Initial Access (TA0001)
Technique: Phishing (T1566)
Sub-technique: Spearphishing Attachment (T1566.001)
Procedure: APT28 sends targeted PDF with exploit

Why it matters: ATT&CK provides a common language for describing attacks, allows you to map defenses to specific techniques, and helps threat hunters know what to look for.

Legal Framework

Understanding the law is not optional. Even well-intentioned security research can land you in prison if you cross legal boundaries. Know the rules, follow them strictly.

🇺🇸 Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)

U.S. federal law that makes it illegal to access a computer without authorization. Key phrase: "exceeds authorized access." Even authorized users who access systems beyond their permission can violate CFAA.

Real Consequences

CFAA violations can result in up to 10 years imprisonment (20 for repeat offenders), massive fines, and civil liability. Famous cases: Aaron Swartz, Weev, Andrew Auernheimer.

🇬🇧 Computer Misuse Act (CMA)

UK law with similar intent. Three main offenses: unauthorized access, unauthorized access with intent to commit further offenses, and unauthorized modification of computer material.

Essential Requirements

  • Written Authorization - Always get it in writing, specifying scope, methods, and timeframe
  • Scope Compliance - Never exceed what's authorized, even if you find something interesting
  • Data Protection - Follow GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, or other applicable regulations
  • Responsible Disclosure - Report vulnerabilities through proper channels
  • Bug Bounty Rules - If participating, follow program rules exactly

SLIDE 08/12

Rules of Engagement

Before any penetration test, rules of engagement (RoE) must be clearly defined and agreed upon. This protects both the tester and the client.

Essential RoE Components

  • Scope Definition - IP ranges, domains, applications, physical locations (if applicable)
  • Testing Window - Dates, times, time zones (business hours vs. off-hours)
  • Allowed Methodologies - What techniques are permitted (social engineering? DoS? physical?)
  • Excluded Targets - Systems explicitly out of scope (production databases, third-party services)
  • Contact Information - Emergency contacts if something goes wrong
  • Communication Protocol - How to report critical findings immediately
  • Data Handling - How to handle sensitive data discovered during testing
  • Cleanup Procedures - Removing backdoors, test accounts, uploaded files
# Example Scope Definition:
IN SCOPE:
- 192.168.1.0/24 (DMZ network)
- *.example.com (all subdomains)
- Web application at https://app.example.com

OUT OF SCOPE:
- Production database server (192.168.1.50)
- Third-party payment gateway
- Physical security testing
- DoS/DDoS attacks

Penetration Testing Methodology

A structured approach ensures thorough testing and comprehensive results. The five-phase methodology is standard across the industry.

PHASE 1
RECONNAISSANCE

Gather information about the target. OSINT, passive and active recon, building a complete picture of the attack surface.

PHASE 2
SCANNING

Identify live hosts, open ports, running services, and versions. Map the network, enumerate services.

PHASE 3
EXPLOITATION

Attempt to gain access using identified vulnerabilities. Proof of concept, credential attacks, exploit execution.

PHASE 4
POST-EXPLOITATION

Once inside, escalate privileges, maintain access, pivot to other systems, determine impact and value.

PHASE 5
REPORTING

Document findings, provide remediation recommendations, executive summary, technical details, evidence.

Remember: Each phase builds on the previous. Thorough reconnaissance leads to better scanning. Better scanning reveals more exploitation opportunities. Skip steps and you miss vulnerabilities.

Career Paths in Ethical Hacking

The skills you're learning open doors to multiple lucrative and exciting career paths. Understanding your options helps you focus your learning.

Penetration Tester

Conduct authorized simulated attacks on systems, networks, and applications. Typical salary: $80K-$150K. Certifications: OSCP, CEH, GPEN.

Red Team Operator

Advanced offensive security role simulating real-world adversaries against an organization's entire security program. Typical salary: $100K-$180K. Certifications: OSEP, CRTO, GXPN.

Bug Bounty Hunter

Independent researcher finding vulnerabilities in bug bounty programs. Income varies wildly (top hunters make $500K+). Platforms: HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Synack.

Security Researcher

Discover new vulnerabilities, develop exploits, advance the field. Work for vendors, research firms, or independently. Typical salary: $90K-$200K.

Application Security Engineer

Focus on securing software development lifecycle, code review, security testing. Typical salary: $95K-$160K. Certifications: CSSLP, GWAPT.

Threat Intelligence Analyst

Analyze attacker TTPs, track threat actors, provide actionable intelligence. Typical salary: $75K-$140K. Certifications: GCTI, CTIA.

Ethics & Responsibility

The skills you're learning are powerful. They can protect organizations or destroy them. Your choice to use them ethically is what separates you from criminals.

The Ethical Hacker's Oath

I will use my skills only with proper authorization.
I will respect the privacy and confidentiality of data I encounter.
I will disclose vulnerabilities responsibly and professionally.
I will never cause intentional harm to systems or people.
I will continuously improve my skills and knowledge.
I will mentor others and advance the security community.
I will hold myself to the highest professional standards.

Power Comes With Responsibility

  • You will find vulnerabilities - How you handle them defines your character
  • You will see sensitive data - Treat it with respect and confidentiality
  • You will have opportunities to abuse trust - Don't. Ever.
  • You will make mistakes - Own them, learn from them, disclose them
  • You represent the profession - Your actions affect how security professionals are perceived

The Ultimate Truth

Your reputation is everything in this field. One ethical violation can end your career permanently. No amount of skill can overcome a reputation for dishonesty or malicious intent. Choose integrity, always.
