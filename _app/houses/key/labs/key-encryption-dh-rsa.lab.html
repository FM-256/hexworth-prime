4) Diffie–Hellman Key Exchange (Interactive)

Two parties agree on a public prime p and generator g. They keep private secrets a and b, exchange public values, and derive the same shared key: K = g^{ab} mod p.

Alice public A = g^a mod p
Bob public B = g^b mod p
A = g^a mod p
B = g^b mod p
Shared key K
Alice computes K = B^a mod p; Bob computes K = A^b mod p — both match.

5) RSA (Interactive Mini-Demo)

Choose small primes p and q to build n=p·q and φ=(p−1)(q−1), pick e coprime with φ, compute d ≡ e⁻¹ (mod φ), then encrypt/decrypt m with c=m^e mod n, m=c^d mod n.

For teaching only — small numbers, not secure.

6) Notes & Tips

  • ASCII vs Unicode: this app focuses on plain ASCII for clarity.
  • Uppercase and lowercase differ by one bit in ASCII (the 0x20 bit).
  • Two hex digits = one byte. Example: 0x41 = decimal 65 = 01000001.
  • DH and RSA sections are for demonstration — do not use these small numbers for real security.
