Resistors

Resistors limit current flow. They are the most fundamental passive component. Every LED circuit, every pull-up, every voltage divider uses resistors. Resistance is measured in ohms, and values are encoded as colored bands on the component body.

Capacitors

Capacitors store and release electrical energy. They smooth power supply fluctuations (decoupling), filter noise, and create timing circuits. Capacitance is measured in farads, but practical values are typically in microfarads (uF), nanofarads (nF), or picofarads (pF).

Ceramic Capacitors Small, non-polarized, cheap. Values from 1pF to 1uF. Used for decoupling (100nF across power pins) and noise filtering. No polarity -- can be inserted either way.
Electrolytic Capacitors Larger values: 1uF to 10,000uF. Polarized -- the longer lead is positive, the stripe marks negative. Used for power supply smoothing. Inserting backwards causes failure.
Decoupling Best Practice:

Place a 100nF ceramic capacitor across the power and ground pins of every IC or sensor module. This absorbs high-frequency noise that can cause erratic behavior. On a breadboard, place it as physically close to the chip as possible.

Transistors

Transistors are electronic switches. A small current or voltage at the control pin (base or gate) allows a much larger current to flow between the other two pins. This is how a 3.3V GPIO pin at 8 mA can control a motor drawing 500 mA at 12V.

Logic-Level MOSFETs:

Standard MOSFETs need 5V-10V at the gate to fully turn on. The Pi outputs 3.3V. Use logic-level MOSFETs (like IRLZ44N) that fully conduct at 3.3V gate voltage. Check the datasheet for "Vgs(th)" -- it must be below 3.3V.

Diodes

Diodes allow current to flow in only one direction. The anode is the positive side; the cathode (marked with a band) is the negative side. Current flows from anode to cathode when forward voltage is applied.

Signal Diode (1N4148) Fast switching, low current (200 mA). Used for logic circuits, signal clamping, and protection against reverse voltage on signal lines.
Rectifier Diode (1N4001) Higher current (1A). Used for flyback protection across relay coils and motors. The cathode stripe faces the positive supply rail.
Schottky Diode Lower forward voltage drop (~0.3V vs ~0.7V for standard). Used in power supply protection and efficiency-critical circuits.
Zener Diode Designed to conduct in reverse at a specific voltage. A 3.3V Zener can clamp 5V signals to 3.3V for GPIO protection (with a series resistor).

Reading Datasheets

Every component has a datasheet from the manufacturer. Datasheets are dense, but you only need a few key sections for most Pi projects. Knowing where to look saves hours of guesswork.

Datasheet SectionWhat It Tells YouWhy It Matters
Absolute Maximum RatingsVoltage and current limits that must never be exceededExceeding these destroys the component instantly
Electrical CharacteristicsTypical operating voltage, current, and performanceDesign your circuit around these values
Pin ConfigurationWhich pin is which (pinout diagram)Wiring a pin incorrectly can destroy the component or the Pi
Application CircuitRecommended wiring from the manufacturerStart with the reference design, then modify
Operating TemperatureSafe temperature rangeImportant for outdoor or enclosed Pi projects
# Example: Reading the 2N2222 NPN transistor datasheet # Absolute Maximum Ratings: # Vceo (Collector-Emitter Voltage): 40V max # Ic (Collector Current): 800 mA max # Pd (Power Dissipation): 500 mW max # Electrical Characteristics: # hFE (Current Gain): 100-300 typical # Vbe(sat) (Base-Emitter Saturation): 0.6V typical # Pin Configuration (TO-92 package, flat side facing you): # Left = Emitter, Center = Base, Right = Collector # To switch 200 mA through the collector with hFE=100: # Base current needed = 200 mA / 100 = 2 mA # Base resistor = (3.3V - 0.6V) / 2 mA = 1350 ohm # Use 1K ohm for margin (provides ~2.7 mA)
Finding Datasheets:

Search for the part number on sites like octopart.com, mouser.com, or digikey.com. Every legitimate component has a freely available datasheet from the manufacturer. If a component has no datasheet, do not use it.

Component Packages

Components come in two physical formats. Through-hole components have wire leads that push through holes in a PCB or breadboard. Surface-mount (SMD) components solder directly to pads on the board surface.

FeatureThrough-HoleSMD (Surface-Mount)
Breadboard compatibleYes -- designed for itNo -- requires adapter boards
Hand solderingEasyDifficult (tiny pads)
SizeLargerMuch smaller
Cost at volumeHigher (manual insertion)Lower (machine placement)
Used inPrototypes, learning, hobbyistProduction boards, commercial products

For all PiVerse breadboard projects, use through-hole components. When you see a breakout board (like an accelerometer module), the SMD component is already soldered on; you interact with the through-hole header pins.

Key Takeaways

  • Resistor color bands encode value: Band 1 (tens digit), Band 2 (units digit), Band 3 (multiplier), Band 4 (tolerance)
  • Ceramic capacitors (100nF) decouple power -- place one across every IC's power pins
  • Transistors (NPN/MOSFET) let a low-current GPIO pin switch high-current loads safely
  • Logic-level MOSFETs must have Vgs(th) below 3.3V to work with the Pi
  • Flyback diodes across inductive loads (motors, relays) prevent voltage spikes that damage transistors
  • Read datasheets for absolute maximums, pinouts, and application circuits before wiring anything
