Electronic Components
Resistors, Capacitors, Transistors, Diodes -- the building blocks of every circuit.
Before you can design circuits, you need to understand the components that go into them. Each component has a specific electrical behavior, a schematic symbol, and a datasheet that describes its limits. This chapter covers the four fundamental passive and active components you will use in every Pi project.

Resistors -- Ohm's Law
Resistors limit current flow. V = I x R is the single most important equation in electronics.
What They Do
Resist the flow of electrons. Measured in Ohms. Higher resistance = less current for the same voltage. They convert excess electrical energy into heat.
Color Bands
4-band code: Band 1 (tens), Band 2 (ones), Band 3 (multiplier), Band 4 (tolerance). Brown-Black-Orange-Gold = 10 x 1000 = 10k ohms, 5% tolerance.
Common Values
220R for LEDs on 5V, 330R for LEDs on 3.3V, 1k for signal limiting, 10k for pull-up/down, 4.7k for I2C pull-ups.
Ohm's Law: V = I x R Example: LED on 3.3V GPIO V_supply = 3.3V, V_led = 2.0V, I_target = 5mA R = (3.3 - 2.0) / 0.005 = 260 ohms Nearest standard: 330 ohms (E24 series) Power dissipated: P = I^2 x R = 0.005^2 x 330 = 0.008W A 1/4W resistor handles this easily.

Resistor Configurations
Resistors in series add up. Resistors in parallel divide. Both patterns appear in Pi circuits.
Voltage Divider
Two resistors in series create a voltage divider: V_out = V_in x R2 / (R1 + R2). This is how you step 5V down to 3.3V for GPIO input protection. Use 1k (R1) and 2k (R2) for a 2:1 ratio that yields 3.33V from 5V.

Capacitors -- Energy Storage
Capacitors store and release electrical energy. They smooth power supplies and filter noise.
Ceramic Capacitors
Small, non-polarized, cheap. Values from 1pF to 1uF. Used for noise filtering (decoupling). Place a 100nF ceramic cap near every IC's power pins.
Electrolytic Capacitors
Large values (1uF to 10,000uF). Polarized -- long leg is positive. Used for power supply smoothing. Wrong polarity can cause them to explode.
Capacitance Unit
Measured in Farads (F). Typical values: pF (picofarads), nF (nanofarads), uF (microfarads). 1uF = 1000nF = 1,000,000pF.
Decoupling Capacitors
Every IC (including the Pi's GPIO expanders) needs a 100nF ceramic capacitor between VCC and GND, placed as close to the chip as possible. This absorbs high-frequency noise from digital switching. Without decoupling caps, sensors give noisy readings and communications fail randomly.

Transistors -- Electronic Switches
A transistor lets a small GPIO signal control a large current. The key to driving motors, relays, and LED strips.
NPN Bipolar (2N2222)
Three pins: Base, Collector, Emitter. A small current into the Base (from GPIO through a 1k resistor) allows a large current to flow from Collector to Emitter. Used for switching loads up to ~800mA.
N-Channel MOSFET (IRLZ44N)
Three pins: Gate, Drain, Source. Voltage on the Gate (from GPIO, no resistor needed) controls current from Drain to Source. Logic-level MOSFETs switch with 3.3V. Handles amps, not milliamps.
GPIO driving a motor through an NPN transistor: GPIO17 ---[1k R]--- Base (B) Motor+ --- 5V Motor- --- Collector (C) Emitter (E) --- GND Flyback diode across motor (cathode to Motor+)
Why Not Direct GPIO?
GPIO pins provide 16mA max. A small motor draws 200-500mA. A relay coil draws 70mA. You MUST use a transistor or MOSFET as an intermediary. The GPIO pin controls the transistor; the transistor controls the load.

Diodes -- One-Way Valves
Diodes allow current to flow in one direction only. LEDs are diodes that emit light.
Signal Diodes (1N4148)
Fast switching. Used for logic circuits, clamping, and protection. Forward voltage drop: ~0.7V. Small glass package with a band marking the cathode.
Rectifier Diodes (1N4007)
Handle higher current (1A). Used in power supply circuits and as flyback protection across inductive loads (motors, relays, solenoids).
Schottky Diodes
Lower forward voltage drop (~0.3V). Faster switching. Used for voltage clamping on GPIO pins and in power supply circuits where every millivolt matters.
Flyback Diodes
When you switch off an inductive load (motor, relay), the collapsing magnetic field generates a voltage spike that can destroy your transistor and GPIO pin. A flyback diode across the load absorbs this spike. Always use one with motors and relays. Place it reverse-biased across the load: cathode to positive, anode to negative.

LEDs -- Light Emitting Diodes
LEDs are the most common output component in Pi projects. Understanding their specs prevents burnout.
1Forward Voltage (Vf): Red ~1.8V, Green ~2.2V, Blue ~3.0V, White ~3.2V. This voltage is "used up" by the LED; the resistor drops the rest.
2Forward Current (If): Typical 20mA max, 5mA for comfortable brightness. On 3.3V GPIO, always use a current-limiting resistor.
3Polarity: Long leg = anode (+), short leg = cathode (-). The flat edge on the plastic casing also marks the cathode.
4RGB LEDs: Common cathode (share GND) or common anode (share VCC). Three color channels controlled by three GPIO pins or PWM for color mixing.

Reading Datasheets
Every component has a datasheet. Here is how to extract the information you actually need.
1Absolute Maximum Ratings: The limits that must never be exceeded. Voltage, current, temperature. Exceeding these destroys the component instantly.
2Electrical Characteristics: Typical operating parameters. Forward voltage, on-resistance, gain. Design your circuit around these values, not the maximums.
3Pinout Diagram: Which physical pin connects to which function. Critical for transistors (BCE vs EBC varies by manufacturer) and ICs.
4Application Circuit: Many datasheets include a recommended circuit. Start with this and modify for your needs.
5Thermal Derating: Components perform worse at high temperatures. Check the derating curve if your project runs hot.
Where to Find Datasheets
Search "[part number] datasheet" or use Octopart, DigiKey, or Mouser. Always get the manufacturer's official datasheet, not a third-party summary. For the BCM2711 (Pi 4 SoC), the datasheet is published by Broadcom and covers every GPIO register.

Breadboards and Prototyping
Breadboards let you build circuits without soldering. Understanding the internal connections is essential.
Terminal Strips
The main area has rows of 5 connected holes. Components in the same row on the same side are electrically connected. The center gap separates left from right.
Power Rails
The long strips along the edges (marked + and -) run the full length of the board. Connect Pi 3.3V to + and GND to -. Some boards have a break in the middle.
Jumper Wires
Male-to-male for breadboard connections. Male-to-female for connecting to Pi GPIO header. Female-to-female for connecting two pin headers. Use consistent colors: red=power, black=ground.

Integrated Circuits and Modules
ICs package complex functionality into a single chip. Modules mount ICs on breakout boards for easy breadboard use.
Common ICs for Pi
MCP3008 (ADC), MCP23017 (GPIO expander), PCF8574 (I2C GPIO), SN74HC595 (shift register), L293D (motor driver). Each connects via I2C, SPI, or direct GPIO.
Breakout Modules
Pre-built boards with an IC, required passive components, and header pins. Examples: BME280 sensor module, OLED display module, relay module. Saves you from SMD soldering.
Module Advantages
Modules include decoupling capacitors, voltage regulators, and pull-up resistors already wired correctly. For beginners, modules reduce wiring errors. For prototyping, they save time. For production, you design a custom PCB with the raw IC.

Key Takeaways: Electronic Components
Five essential facts about the building blocks of circuits.
1Ohm's Law governs everything. V = I x R. Calculate the resistor value for every LED. Calculate the current draw for every circuit. If you cannot do the math, do not build the circuit.
2Capacitors filter noise. Place a 100nF ceramic cap near every IC's power pins. Use electrolytics on power supply rails. Polarized capacitors explode if reversed.
3Transistors amplify GPIO. Use NPN (2N2222) or MOSFET (IRLZ44N) to switch loads that draw more than 16mA. GPIO controls the transistor; the transistor controls the load.
4Diodes protect circuits. Always use a flyback diode across inductive loads. Schottky diodes clamp GPIO voltage spikes. LEDs are diodes that need current-limiting resistors.
5Read the datasheet. Absolute Maximum Ratings, Electrical Characteristics, and Pinout are the three sections you need. Never design to the maximum -- leave margin.
