Analog Signals and ADC
Analog vs digital, Nyquist sampling, MCP3008 ADC, signal conditioning.
The Pi has no analog input. Every GPIO pin is digital. To read analog sensors you need an external ADC like the MCP3008. Understanding sampling theory, signal conditioning, and conversion math is essential for accurate measurements.

Analog vs Digital
Continuous vs discrete -- bridging the gap with ADCs.
Analog
Continuous values. Temperature varies smoothly. A potentiometer outputs 0V to 3.3V with infinite resolution.
Digital
Discrete steps. A 10-bit ADC has 1024 levels. 3.3V / 1024 = 3.2mV per step.

Nyquist Theorem
Sample at 2x the highest signal frequency or lose information.
1Rule: Sample rate must be >= 2x the signal frequency. 1kHz signal needs >= 2000 SPS.
2Aliasing: Below Nyquist rate, the digital signal misrepresents the analog original.
3MCP3008: 200k SPS max. Sufficient for temperature/light, not for audio.

The MCP3008
8-channel 10-bit SPI ADC -- the standard for Pi analog input.
from gpiozero import MCP3008 sensor = MCP3008(channel=0) raw = sensor.value # 0.0 to 1.0 voltage = raw * 3.3 # Convert to volts

Signal Conditioning
Prepare raw signals for the ADC: dividers, filters, buffers.
Voltage Divider
Scale 5V to 3.3V with R1=1k, R2=2k. Protects ADC input.
RC Low-Pass Filter
10k + 100nF = 160Hz cutoff. Removes noise from sensor signals.
Op-Amp Buffer
Unity gain buffer isolates sensor from ADC impedance loading.

The simplest analog input: variable voltage mapped to LED brightness.

Reading a Thermistor
Convert resistance to temperature with the Beta equation.
import math adc = MCP3008(0) v = adc.value * 3.3 R = (3.3 - v) / v * 10000 B = 3950; R0 = 10000; T0 = 298.15 temp_k = 1/((1/T0)+((1/B)*math.log(R/R0))) temp_c = temp_k - 273.15

ADC Resolution
10-bit vs 12-bit vs 16-bit and when resolution matters.
110-bit (MCP3008): 1024 steps. 3.2mV resolution at 3.3V ref. Fine for pots, LDRs, basic temp.
212-bit (ADS1015): 4096 steps. 0.8mV resolution. Better for precision sensors.
316-bit (ADS1115): 65536 steps. 0.05mV resolution. Required for strain gauges, thermocouples.
Slide 11 of 12
Calibration Techniques
Map raw ADC values to real-world units accurately.
1Two-point calibration: Measure known values at two points. Linear interpolation between them.
2Offset and gain: actual = (raw - offset) * gain. Determine offset and gain from known references.
3Averaging: Read N samples, compute mean. Reduces noise by sqrt(N). 16 samples = 4x noise reduction.

Key Takeaways: Analog Signals and ADC
Five essential facts from this chapter.
1Pi has no analog input. Use MCP3008 (10-bit, 8-ch, SPI) for analog sensors.
2Nyquist: sample at 2x signal frequency. Below this, aliasing corrupts data.
3Signal conditioning first. Voltage dividers, RC filters, buffers before the ADC.
4gpiozero wraps MCP3008. MCP3008(0).value = 0.0 to 1.0. Multiply by 3.3 for volts.
5Each sensor needs conversion math. Thermistor: Beta equation. LDR: divider ratio. Read the datasheet.
