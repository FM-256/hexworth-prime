When Wires Become Transmission Lines

At low frequencies, a wire is just a wire. But when the signal's wavelength approaches the trace length, the trace becomes a transmission line with characteristic impedance. The rule of thumb: if the trace is longer than 1/10 of the signal's wavelength, impedance matters.

At what point does impedance matter? Signal propagation speed on FR-4: ~15cm/ns (half the speed of light) For a 12MHz clock (RP2040 crystal): wavelength = 15cm/ns / 12MHz = 12.5m Critical length = 12.5m / 10 = 1.25m -- way longer than any trace. No problem. For USB Full-Speed (12Mbps): wavelength = 12.5m Critical length = 1.25m -- still fine for a small board. For USB High-Speed (480Mbps): effective frequency ~240MHz Wavelength = 15cm/ns / 240MHz = 0.625m Critical length = 6.25cm -- THIS matters on your board. For QSPI flash at 133MHz: Wavelength = 1.13m. Critical length = 11.3cm. Keep QSPI traces short and you are fine.

Characteristic Impedance

A trace over a ground plane forms a microstrip transmission line. Its impedance depends on trace width, distance to the ground plane (dielectric thickness), and the dielectric constant of FR-4 (~4.2-4.5). USB requires 90 ohm differential impedance. Single-ended signals typically target 50 ohm.

Parameter Effect on Impedance
Wider trace Lower impedance (more capacitance to ground)
Thinner dielectric (closer to ground) Lower impedance (more capacitance)
Higher dielectric constant Lower impedance
Thicker copper Slightly lower impedance

Input your stackup parameters (dielectric thickness, copper weight, dielectric constant) and it tells you the trace width needed for your target impedance. For a standard JLCPCB 4-layer stackup, ~0.3mm traces give approximately 50 ohm single-ended impedance.

Crosstalk

Crosstalk is unwanted coupling between adjacent traces. A fast-switching signal on one trace induces a voltage on its neighbor through electromagnetic coupling. The closer and longer the parallel run, the worse the crosstalk.

3W Rule Space traces at least 3x the trace width apart (center-to-center) to reduce crosstalk to acceptable levels. A 0.2mm trace needs 0.6mm center-to-center spacing.
Minimize Parallel Runs Two traces running parallel for 5cm couple more than two traces that cross at 90 degrees. Route sensitive signals perpendicular to noisy ones.
Guard Traces A grounded trace between two signal traces acts as a shield. Used for sensitive analog signals (ADC inputs) near noisy digital traces.
Layer Separation Signals on L1 running perpendicular to signals on L4 have minimal coupling. The ground plane between them acts as a shield.

USB Differential Pair Routing

USB uses differential signaling: two traces (D+ and D-) carry complementary signals. The differential impedance must be 90 ohm +/- 10%. The traces must be length-matched to within 0.15mm (150 microns) for USB 2.0.

USB routing rules for RP2040 designs: 1. Impedance: 90 ohm differential (45 ohm single-ended each) Trace width and spacing depend on your stackup JLCPCB 4-layer: ~0.2mm trace, ~0.15mm gap (check calculator) 2. Length matching: D+ and D- within 0.15mm of each other Add serpentine (meander) to the shorter trace to equalize 3. Series resistors: 27 ohm on each line (RP2040 datasheet requirement) Place close to the RP2040 USB pins 4. No vias: Route the entire differential pair on one layer Each via adds ~0.5nH inductance = impedance discontinuity 5. Ground plane continuity: The return current follows the trace on the ground plane below Never route USB over a gap in the ground plane 6. Keep it short: <100mm total length recommended Less routing = fewer opportunities for problems

EMI Basics

Every switching signal generates electromagnetic interference. The RP2040's 125MHz system clock, the USB 12/480MHz signals, and the switching regulator's 500kHz-2MHz oscillation are all EMI sources. A poorly designed board can interfere with nearby equipment or fail regulatory testing (FCC/CE).

Continuous Ground Plane The single most effective EMI reduction technique. Gaps, slots, or splits in the ground plane force return currents to detour, creating loop antennas.
Short, Direct Traces Every trace is an antenna. Shorter traces radiate less. Route high-speed signals as directly as possible with minimal stub length.
Decoupling at the Source Decoupling capacitors at every IC power pin suppress current spikes before they propagate across the board as EMI.
Edge Rate Control Slower edge rates (rise/fall times) reduce high-frequency harmonics. Series resistors on GPIO outputs slow edges and reduce EMI.

Key Takeaways

  • Traces become transmission lines when their length exceeds 1/10 of the signal wavelength -- USB High-Speed always matters
  • Impedance depends on trace width, dielectric thickness, and dielectric constant -- use calculators, not guesswork
  • Crosstalk is reduced by the 3W spacing rule, minimizing parallel runs, and maintaining continuous ground planes
  • USB differential pairs need 90 ohm impedance, length matching within 0.15mm, 27 ohm series resistors, and no vias
  • A continuous ground plane is the single most effective technique for both signal integrity and EMI reduction
  • Trace width for power is determined by current capacity; for signals, by impedance target
