What is a Schematic?
A standardized diagram showing all electrical connections in a circuit, using symbols instead of physical drawings.
Logical, Not Physical
A schematic shows WHAT connects to WHAT. It says nothing about WHERE components sit on the board. Two components next to each other on a schematic might be on opposite sides of the PCB.
Machine-Readable
EDA tools (KiCad, Altium, Eagle) parse schematics into netlists -- a list of every connection. The netlist drives PCB layout, BOM generation, and design rule checks.
Why You Must Learn This
Every datasheet, every reference design, every open-source hardware project communicates through schematics. If you cannot read a schematic, you cannot design, debug, or modify any electronic device. It is the universal language of hardware engineering.

Component Symbols
Every component type has a standardized symbol. Learn the core set and you can read any schematic.
Resistor
Zigzag line (US) or rectangle (IEC). Two terminals. Value in ohms. Limits current flow.
Capacitor
Two parallel lines (non-polarized) or one curved (polarized/electrolytic). Value in farads (usually uF or pF). Stores charge.
Inductor
Coil/loops symbol. Value in henries (usually uH). Stores energy in magnetic field. Used in power supplies and filters.
Diode / LED
Triangle pointing at a bar. Current flows from anode to cathode. LED adds arrows for light emission.
Transistor (MOSFET)
Three terminals: Gate, Drain, Source. Acts as a voltage-controlled switch. N-channel and P-channel variants.
IC (Integrated Circuit)
Rectangle with labeled pins. Pin numbers match the physical package. The RP2040 appears as a large rectangle with 56 pins.

Nets and Connections
A net is a named electrical connection. Every wire on a schematic is part of a net.
Wire = Direct Connection
A line between two pins means they are electrically connected. The line is a net. Where lines cross with a dot, they connect. Where lines cross without a dot, they do NOT connect.
Net Labels
Named labels replace long wires. If two pins have the same net label, they are connected even if no wire is drawn between them. This keeps schematics readable. Example: "UART0_TX" on pin 1 and on pin 45 means those pins are connected.
Junction Dots
A filled circle (junction dot) at a wire crossing means the wires connect. No dot means they do not. This is the most common source of schematic misreading. Always check for the dot. Some tools use a small square instead of a circle -- the meaning is identical.

Power Symbols and Rails
Power and ground have special symbols. They are implicit global nets.
VCC / VBUS / 3V3
Upward-pointing arrow or bar. Represents positive supply voltage. All VCC symbols on all pages connect to the same net.
GND
Downward-pointing lines (rake symbol). Ground reference. All GND symbols connect to the same global ground net.
Multiple Rails
Complex designs have multiple voltage rails: 5V (VBUS), 3.3V (3V3), 1.1V (VREG_VOUT). Each is a separate global net with its own symbol or label.
Pico Power Rails
The Raspberry Pi Pico schematic shows: VBUS (5V from USB), VSYS (system input, 1.8-5.5V), 3V3_EN (enable for regulator), 3V3 (regulated output from RT6150 buck-boost), and 1V1 (RP2040 core voltage from internal LDO). Understanding which rail powers which component is critical for board design.

Reference Designators
Every component on a schematic gets a unique identifier: its reference designator.
R = Resistor
R1, R2, R3... Numbered sequentially. R7 on the schematic is the same R7 silkscreened on the PCB and listed in the BOM.
C = Capacitor
C1, C2... Decoupling caps are often C1-C10. Bulk capacitors use higher numbers. The designator links schematic to physical board.
U = IC
U1 is typically the main MCU. U2 might be the flash chip. U3 the voltage regulator. "U" stands for "unrestricted" (legacy term).
L = Inductor
L1, L2... Used in power supplies (buck/boost converters) and filters.
D = Diode
D1, D2... Includes LEDs, Schottky diodes, Zener diodes, TVS diodes.
J = Connector
J1 for USB connector, J2 for header pins. Q = Transistor, Y = Crystal, SW = Switch, TP = Test Point.

Reading the Pico Schematic: Power Section
The Raspberry Pi Pico schematic is open source. Let us trace the power path.
1 VBUS (5V) enters from the USB connector (J1). A Schottky diode (D1) prevents reverse current if external power is also connected.
2 VSYS rail is the system input. It can come from VBUS via D1 or from an external supply on the VSYS pin. This flexibility lets the Pico run from USB or batteries.
3 RT6150B (U2) buck-boost converter takes VSYS (1.8-5.5V) and outputs regulated 3.3V. The inductor L1 and capacitors C5-C7 are the converter's external components.
4 3V3 rail powers the RP2040, the flash chip, and GPIO. The RP2040's internal LDO generates 1.1V for the digital core from the 3.3V rail.
Trace the Net
When reading any schematic, start at the power input and trace forward. Every component in the power path is there for a reason: the diode for protection, the regulator for voltage conversion, the capacitors for stability, the inductor for energy storage.

Reading the Pico: RP2040 Connections
The RP2040 section of the Pico schematic shows the MCU and all its support components.
Crystal (Y1): 12 MHz
Connected to XIN and XOUT pins. Two 15pF load capacitors (C3, C4) to ground. Provides the reference clock for the PLL that generates the system clock.
Flash (U3): W25Q16JV
2 MB QSPI flash connected to the 6 dedicated QSPI pins: SCLK, SS, SD0-SD3. A 100nF decoupling cap on VCC. Placed physically close to the RP2040.
Decoupling Capacitors
Multiple 100nF caps (C1, C2, etc.) on each VDD pin. One 1uF bulk cap. These filter high-frequency noise from the power supply. Every VDD pin needs its own decoupling cap.
USB Data Lines
DP and DM connected to the USB connector via 27-ohm series resistors. These match the USB impedance. The USB PHY is built into the RP2040.
Slide 9 of 12
Multi-Page Schematics
Complex designs split the schematic across multiple pages. Net labels connect them.
Hierarchical Sheets
Page 1 might show power. Page 2 shows the MCU. Page 3 shows connectors. A net labeled "UART0_TX" on page 2 connects to the same net on page 3. The label is the connection.
Reading Strategy
Start with the block diagram if provided. Then read power first (where does voltage come from?). Then the MCU (what does it connect to?). Then peripherals (sensors, connectors, LEDs).
The Pico Schematic is One Page
The Pico is simple enough to fit on a single schematic sheet. But the RP2040 datasheet's reference design uses multiple pages. When you design your own boards, you will likely use 2-4 pages minimum. Organization matters for readability and review.

Common Schematic Patterns
Patterns you will see on nearly every microcontroller schematic.
Decoupling Network
100nF ceramic cap on every VDD pin, placed as close to the pin as physically possible. One bulk 10uF cap near the power entry. This is non-negotiable -- skip it and the chip will malfunction.
Pull-Up / Pull-Down Resistors
A resistor connecting a signal to VCC (pull-up) or GND (pull-down). Defines the default state of a pin. I2C always needs pull-ups. Reset pins typically have pull-ups.
Voltage Divider
Two resistors in series between voltage and ground. The midpoint outputs a fraction of the input voltage. Used for ADC references, level shifting, and battery monitoring.
ESD Protection
TVS (Transient Voltage Suppressor) diodes on USB, GPIO headers, and any externally-accessible pins. Clamp voltage spikes from static discharge before they reach the IC.

Schematic Review Checklist
Before moving from schematic to PCB layout, verify these items.
1Every power pin has a decoupling cap. Check all VDD, VDDIO, and AVDD pins on every IC.
2All nets are connected. Run an ERC (Electrical Rules Check) in your EDA tool. Unconnected pins should be explicitly marked "no connect" (X symbol).
3Reset circuit exists. Pull-up resistor on the reset pin, optional capacitor for noise filtering, optional button for manual reset.
4Crystal load capacitors match datasheet. Wrong values cause clock drift or failure to oscillate. Check the crystal datasheet for required load capacitance.
5USB series resistors present. 27-ohm resistors on D+ and D- for RP2040 USB connections.
6Boot mode selection works. BOOTSEL button on QSPI_SS with pull-up resistor. Verify it can be held low during power-on.

Key Takeaways: Schematic Reading
You can now read the language of circuits. Next: the physical board itself.
1Schematics are logical, not physical. They show WHAT connects, not WHERE. Net labels link connections across pages.
2Component symbols are standardized. Resistor (zigzag), capacitor (parallel lines), IC (rectangle with pins). Reference designators (R1, C1, U1) link schematic to BOM and PCB.
3Power rails are global nets. VCC, GND, 3V3 symbols connect everywhere they appear. Trace power from input to regulator to load.
4Junction dots matter. Dot = connected. No dot at crossing = not connected. This is the most common misread.
5Always run ERC before layout. Unconnected nets, missing power, floating pins -- the EDA tool catches what your eyes miss.
