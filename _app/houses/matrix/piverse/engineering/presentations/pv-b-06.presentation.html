Gerber Files
Gerber is the universal file format that fab houses use to manufacture your PCB.
One File Per Layer
Top copper (F.Cu.gbr), bottom copper (B.Cu.gbr), top solder mask (F.Mask.gbr), bottom solder mask (B.Mask.gbr), top silkscreen (F.SilkS.gbr), bottom silkscreen, edge cuts, and drill files.
Drill Files
Separate files specify hole positions and sizes. Through-hole drill, NPTH (non-plated), and via drill. Excellon format (.drl). Without drill files, no holes are drilled.
Gerber Viewers
Always inspect Gerbers in a viewer before submitting. KiCad's built-in viewer, gerbv, or the online JLCPCB Gerber viewer. Verify every layer looks correct. Check for missing pads, broken traces, and correct board outline.

Bill of Materials (BOM)
The BOM lists every component that gets soldered to the board.
1Reference designator. R1, C1, U1 -- links back to the schematic and PCB silkscreen.
2Value. 100nF, 10K, RP2040 -- what the component IS.
3Footprint/Package. 0402, 0603, QFN-56 -- the physical size and pad pattern.
4Manufacturer Part Number (MPN). The exact part to order. Example: "GRM155R71C104KA88D" for a specific Murata 100nF cap.
5LCSC Part Number. For JLCPCB assembly, every component needs an LCSC number. Basic parts (resistors, common caps) are cheaper than extended parts.
BOM Optimization
Use the fewest unique part values possible. If you need 10K and 10.2K resistors, consolidate to 10K. Fewer unique parts = simpler BOM = cheaper assembly = fewer placement errors. JLCPCB charges per unique part, not per total part count.
Slide 4 of 12
Pick-and-Place File (CPL)
The Component Placement List tells the assembly machine where to put each component.
Position Data
For each component: reference designator, X coordinate, Y coordinate, rotation angle, and which side (top/bottom). Generated by KiCad's "Fabrication Outputs" menu.
Rotation Corrections
Different EDA tools define 0-degree rotation differently. JLCPCB may need rotation corrections for polarized components (ICs, diodes, LEDs). Their online tool lets you visually verify and correct placement before production.
Fiducial Markers
Small copper circles (fiducials) on the board give the pick-and-place machine reference points for alignment. Place at least two fiducials on opposite corners of the board. Without them, the machine has no way to correct for board rotation or offset during placement.

Solder Paste Stencil
Before components are placed, solder paste is applied to every pad through a stencil.
The Stencil
A thin stainless steel sheet with laser-cut openings matching every pad on your board. Paste is squeegeed across the stencil, depositing precise amounts on each pad. Then the stencil is lifted off.
Paste Volume
Too much paste causes bridges (shorts between adjacent pins). Too little causes cold joints (weak or missing connections). The stencil aperture size controls the volume. For QFN-56, apertures are typically 80% of pad size.
For Hand Assembly
If you are assembling boards yourself (low volume, prototyping), order a stencil with your PCBs. JLCPCB includes a free stencil with PCB orders. Apply paste, place components with tweezers, then reflow in a hot air station or reflow oven. It is surprisingly doable for boards with 50-100 components.

Reflow Soldering
The board passes through an oven with a carefully controlled temperature profile.
1Preheat (150-200C, 60-90s). Gradually heats the board to activate flux and equalize temperature across all components. Prevents thermal shock.
2Soak (200-217C, 60-90s). Holds temperature to ensure all areas of the board reach uniform temperature. Flux cleans oxide from pads.
3Reflow (217-250C, 30-60s). Solder paste melts (liquidus at 217C for SAC305 lead-free). Surface tension pulls components into alignment on their pads (self-centering).
4Cooling (controlled ramp down). Gradual cooling prevents thermal stress cracks in solder joints. Do not quench -- let it cool naturally or use controlled cooling.

JLCPCB Workflow
JLCPCB is the most popular low-cost PCB fabrication and assembly house. Here is the complete order flow.
1Upload Gerbers. ZIP your Gerber and drill files. Upload to JLCPCB. Their system auto-detects board size, layers, and drill count.
2Configure PCB options. Choose layers (2 or 4), thickness (1.6mm standard), color (green is cheapest), surface finish (HASL or lead-free HASL).
3Enable SMT Assembly. Toggle assembly on. Upload BOM (.csv) and CPL (.csv). Select top-side or both-side assembly.
4Component review. JLCPCB shows each component placement on a visual board preview. Verify rotation and position. Fix any misaligned parts.
5Pay and wait. PCB fabrication: 2-5 days. Assembly: 3-7 days. Shipping: 5-20 days depending on method. Total: 10-30 days from order to mailbox.

Design for Manufacturing (DFM)
Rules that make your board cheaper and more reliable to manufacture.
Minimum Feature Sizes
JLCPCB minimums: 0.127mm (5 mil) trace width, 0.127mm spacing, 0.2mm drill, 0.45mm via pad. Stay above these for reliable results. 6 mil trace/space is the sweet spot for cost and reliability.
Panelization
For small boards, combine multiple copies into a panel with V-score or mouse-bite tabs. JLCPCB can panelize for you, or you create your own panel in KiCad. Reduces per-board cost for orders of 10+.
Component Orientation
Align all passive components (resistors, capacitors) in the same direction where possible. This helps the reflow oven heat them evenly and reduces placement machine time. It also makes visual inspection easier.

Surface Finish Options
The surface finish protects exposed copper pads from oxidation until soldering.
HASL (Hot Air Solder Leveling)
Board is dipped in molten solder, then leveled with hot air. Cheapest. Good solderability. But uneven surface -- not ideal for fine-pitch QFN pads. Lead-free HASL is standard.
ENIG (Electroless Nickel / Immersion Gold)
Flat, uniform gold surface over nickel. Excellent for QFN and BGA packages. Good shelf life. More expensive. The gold surface is the industry standard for professional boards.
OSP (Organic Solderability Preservative)
Thin organic coating. Cheapest flat finish. Short shelf life (6 months). Adequate for immediate assembly. Not recommended for boards you plan to store or rework.

Inspection and Testing
After assembly, verify the board works before deploying it.
Visual Inspection
Check for solder bridges (shorts), tombstoned components (one end lifted), missing components, and correct orientation of polarized parts (ICs, diodes, electrolytic caps). A USB microscope helps enormously.
Continuity Testing
Use a multimeter in continuity mode. Check that power rails are not shorted to ground (beep = bad). Check that expected connections exist (power to regulator input, regulator output to MCU VDD).
First Power-On Protocol
Connect a current-limited power supply (set to 100mA max). Monitor current draw. An RP2040 board should draw 20-50mA at first power-on. If it draws the limit immediately, disconnect -- you have a short. Never power a new board from a non-current-limited source.

Common Manufacturing Failures
Problems you will encounter and how to fix them.
XSolder bridges. Excess paste shorts adjacent pins. Fix with solder wick and flux. Prevent with correct stencil aperture sizing.
XTombstoning. Uneven heating lifts one end of a small component (0402, 0603). Caused by thermal imbalance in pad sizes or asymmetric paste application.
XCold joints. Insufficient heat or paste creates a weak, dull-looking joint. Reheat with a soldering iron or hot air.
XWrong component rotation. IC placed 90 or 180 degrees off. Verify pin 1 marker on the IC matches pin 1 on the PCB silkscreen.
XMissing Gerber layers. Forgetting to export the drill file means no holes. Forgetting solder mask means exposed copper everywhere. Always verify Gerbers in a viewer before ordering.

Key Takeaways: Manufacturing and Assembly
Design is half the battle. Getting it manufactured correctly is the other half.
1Gerbers are the universal PCB format. One file per layer plus drill files. Always verify in a Gerber viewer before ordering.
2BOM and CPL files drive assembly. Every component needs an MPN and LCSC number. Fewer unique parts = cheaper.
3Reflow profile: preheat, soak, reflow, cool. 217C is the magic number for SAC305 lead-free solder.
4DFM: respect minimum feature sizes. 6 mil trace/space, 0.3mm via drill, 0.45mm via pad for JLCPCB.
5First power-on with current limiting. Never power a new board without current protection. Check for shorts first.
