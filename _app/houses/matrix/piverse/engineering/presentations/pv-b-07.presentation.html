Project Overview
A single PCB that takes USB power/data, runs RP2040 firmware, and drives a NEMA 17 stepper motor.
RP2040 (Brain)
Generates step/dir pulses via PIO at precise timing. Communicates with the TMC2209 over UART for configuration. USB connection to host computer.
TMC2209 (Driver)
Trinamic stepper driver IC. Silent operation via StealthChop. Up to 2A per coil. Microstepping up to 1/256. UART interface for runtime configuration.
Power System
Two separate power domains: 3.3V logic (from USB via LDO) and 8-24V motor power (from external supply via screw terminal). Complete isolation between logic and motor ground.

TMC2209 Interface
How the RP2040 controls the stepper driver.
Step/Dir/Enable (GPIO)
STEP: Each rising edge moves the motor one microstep. DIR: High = clockwise, Low = counterclockwise. EN: Active low -- pull low to enable motor output. PIO generates STEP pulses for precise timing up to 500 kHz.
UART Configuration
Single-wire UART (TX and RX on the same pin via a 1K resistor). RP2040 UART TX connects through 1K to the TMC2209 PDN_UART pin. This lets you configure microstepping, current limit, StealthChop, and read diagnostics at runtime.
Sense Resistors
Two 0.11-ohm resistors (R_SENSE) set the maximum motor current. The TMC2209 measures voltage across these resistors to regulate current. I_max = 0.325V / R_SENSE = 0.325 / 0.11 = 2.95A peak. For 2A RMS, configure via UART. Use 1% tolerance resistors -- current accuracy depends on resistor accuracy.

Power Domain Separation
Logic power (3.3V) and motor power (8-24V) must be carefully separated.
Why Separate?
Stepper motors generate large current spikes and back-EMF. If motor power noise couples into logic power, the RP2040 crashes or the USB connection drops. Separate ground paths and ground planes prevent this.
Single Ground Point
Logic GND and motor GND connect at exactly one point -- a star ground near the TMC2209. This prevents motor return current from flowing through the logic ground plane. On the PCB, this means a physical split in the ground pour.
Bulk Capacitor
A 100uF electrolytic capacitor on the motor power input absorbs current spikes from the stepper driver's switching action. Without it, the motor power rail rings with every step, radiating EMI and potentially damaging the driver IC. Place it within 10mm of the driver's VM pin.

PCB Layout Strategy
Component placement drives trace routing. Get placement right and routing follows naturally.
1USB connector at board edge. Cable stress goes into the connector mounting pads, not the board. Add silkscreen indicating USB-C orientation.
2RP2040 center-left. Close to USB connector for short D+/D- traces. Crystal and flash within 5-10mm. Decoupling caps surrounding the chip.
3TMC2209 center-right. Near the motor output connector. Sense resistors directly adjacent. Bulk cap nearby. The thermal pad needs a 3x3 via array to the ground plane.
4Motor power at opposite edge from USB. Screw terminal at board edge. Wide traces (40+ mil) from terminal to TMC2209 VM pin. Motor output connector adjacent.

High-Current Trace Design
Motor power traces carry 2+ amps. Standard signal trace widths are insufficient.
Trace Width Calculation
For 2A on an outer layer with 1oz copper and 10C temperature rise: minimum trace width is approximately 40 mil (1mm). For safety, use 50-60 mil or a copper pour. Use Saturn PCB Toolkit to calculate exactly.
Via Current Capacity
A single 0.3mm via carries about 0.5A. Motor power paths need multiple vias in parallel (4-8 vias) to handle 2A+ safely. Place them in a row for even current distribution.
Copper Pour for Motor Power
Instead of individual traces, use a copper pour on both top and bottom layers for the motor power path. This maximizes current capacity and minimizes voltage drop. Connect top and bottom pours with a row of vias along the path.
Slide 8 of 12
Thermal Design
The TMC2209 dissipates up to 1W at full current. Heat management is critical.
Thermal Pad Vias
The TMC2209 QFN exposed pad connects to ground. Place a 4x4 array of 0.3mm thermal vias under the pad, connecting to internal and bottom ground planes. This spreads heat into the board.
Copper Pour Area
Maximize ground copper around the TMC2209 on all layers. A larger copper area means more thermal mass and more surface area for convective cooling. 1W is manageable with good layout but not with minimal copper.
Slide 9 of 12
PIO for Step Generation
Why PIO is the perfect tool for generating stepper motor control signals.
Precise Timing
PIO generates step pulses at exact frequencies from 1 Hz to 500 kHz. No interrupt jitter, no OS scheduling delays. Each step pulse is cycle-accurate. This translates directly to smooth, quiet motor operation.
CPU-Free Operation
While PIO drives the motor, both CPU cores are free for other tasks: processing G-code, running a web interface, managing USB communication. The motor keeps stepping regardless of what the CPU is doing.
Acceleration Profiles
The CPU calculates acceleration ramps (trapezoidal or S-curve) and updates the PIO step frequency at regular intervals. PIO handles the precise pulse generation between updates. This division of labor gives you both mathematical accuracy (CPU) and timing precision (PIO).
Slide 10 of 12
Protection Circuits
Motors are electrically hostile. Protect the driver and the MCU.
TVS on Motor Power
A TVS diode on the motor input clamps voltage spikes from back-EMF when the motor decelerates. Without it, voltage spikes can exceed the TMC2209's 29V absolute maximum.
Flyback Diodes
The TMC2209 has internal flyback diodes, but external Schottky diodes across the motor outputs provide additional protection for the die when the motor is disconnected under load.
USB ESD Protection
A TVS diode array (USBLC6-2SC6 or similar) on the USB D+/D- lines protects the RP2040's USB PHY from electrostatic discharge. This is essential for any user-accessible USB port.

Key Takeaways: Motor Controller
A complete board design integrating everything from Chapters 1-6.
1Separate power domains. Logic (3.3V) and motor (8-24V) with a single ground connection point. This prevents motor noise from crashing the MCU.
2Wide traces for motor current. 40+ mil or copper pours for 2A+ paths. Multiple parallel vias for layer transitions.
3TMC2209 thermal management. 4x4 thermal via array under QFN pad. Maximize ground copper on all layers.
4PIO for step generation. Cycle-accurate timing, zero jitter, CPU-free operation. The perfect tool for motor control.
5Protection everywhere. TVS on motor power, ESD on USB, flyback diodes on outputs. Motors are electrically hostile -- design defensively.
