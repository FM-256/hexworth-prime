RP2040 Power Requirements

The RP2040 needs three power rails: 3.3V for I/O (IOVDD), 3.3V for digital logic (DVDD), and 1.1V for the processor core. The 1.1V rail is generated by the RP2040's internal regulator from the 3.3V supply. Your job as a board designer is to provide clean, stable 3.3V.

Rail Voltage Typical Current Source
IOVDD 3.3V (1.8-3.3V) ~10mA + GPIO load External regulator
DVDD 1.1V ~25mA at 125MHz Internal regulator (from VREG_VIN = 3.3V)
USB_VDD 3.3V ~10mA when USB active External regulator
ADC_AVDD 3.3V <1mA Filtered from 3.3V (LC filter recommended)

LDO vs. Switching Regulators

LDO (Low Dropout Regulator) Linear regulator. Simple: input, output, ground, two capacitors. Low noise. Efficiency = Vout/Vin. A 3.3V LDO from 5V USB is only 66% efficient -- 34% is wasted as heat. Good when input-output voltage difference is small.
Buck Converter (Step-Down) Switching regulator. Uses an inductor and control IC to efficiently convert voltage. 85-95% efficient. Generates switching noise (100kHz-2MHz). Requires more components: inductor, diode, capacitors, feedback resistors.
Boost Converter (Step-Up) Steps voltage up from a lower source. Used when running from batteries (1.8V-3.6V LiPo to 3.3V). Same complexity as buck converters.
Buck-Boost Handles input voltages above AND below the output. The Pi Pico uses an RT6150 buck-boost: accepts 1.8V-5.5V input, outputs 3.3V. Handles both USB (5V) and battery (3.0V) sources.
Pico Power Path: USB VBUS (5V) --[Schottky diode]--> VSYS Battery (1.8-5.5V) --[Schottky diode]--> VSYS | RT6150 Buck-Boost | 3V3 Rail / | \ IOVDD DVDD USB_VDD (via internal 1.1V regulator)

Decoupling Capacitors

Every IC draws current in bursts. When a transistor switches, it demands a spike of current faster than the power supply can respond. Decoupling capacitors are local energy reservoirs -- they store charge near the IC and deliver it instantly during current spikes.

RP2040 decoupling requirements (from datasheet): IOVDD (pins 1, 10, 22, 33, 42, 49): 100nF (0.1uF) ceramic capacitor at EACH pin 6 caps total DVDD (pins 23, 50): 100nF ceramic at each pin 2 caps VREG_VIN (pin 44): 1uF ceramic 1 cap VREG_VOUT (pin 45): 1uF ceramic 1 cap USB_VDD (pin 48): 100nF ceramic 1 cap ADC_AVDD (pin 43): 100nF ceramic + 1uF ceramic 2 caps Total: 13 capacitors minimum for the RP2040 alone Plus bulk capacitance: 1x 10uF on the 3V3 rail
Placement Rule:

Decoupling capacitors must be placed as close to the IC power pins as possible -- ideally within 3mm. The trace from the capacitor to the IC pin should be as short and wide as possible. Every millimeter of extra trace adds inductance, which defeats the purpose of the capacitor.

Thermal Calculations

An LDO converts excess voltage to heat. The power dissipated is P = (Vin - Vout) x Iout. If you are dropping 5V USB to 3.3V at 100mA:

P = (5.0V - 3.3V) x 0.1A = 0.17W (170mW) This is fine for a small SOT-23 package. But at 500mA (full USB current): P = (5.0V - 3.3V) x 0.5A = 0.85W A SOT-23 package has thermal resistance ~150C/W (junction to ambient) Temperature rise = 0.85W x 150C/W = 127.5C At 25C ambient, junction temp = 25 + 127.5 = 152.5C Most ICs max out at 125-150C. This WILL fail. Solutions: 1. Use a buck converter (85-95% efficient, ~0.05W dissipation) 2. Use a larger package (SOT-223: ~60C/W) with thermal pad 3. Add copper pour under the regulator for heat spreading

Capacitor Selection

Type Values ESR Use
MLCC (Ceramic) 100pF - 100uF Very low Decoupling, filtering. Use X5R or X7R dielectric for stability.
Electrolytic 10uF - 10000uF Higher Bulk energy storage on power rails. Polarized -- connect correctly or it explodes.
Tantalum 1uF - 1000uF Low-medium Compact bulk capacitance. Polarized. Can fail short (fire risk if over-voltaged).
Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Derating:

High-capacitance ceramic caps (especially X5R/X7R) lose significant capacitance as applied voltage approaches their rating. A 10uF/6.3V cap may only deliver 6uF at 3.3V. Always check the manufacturer's voltage-capacitance curves and derate accordingly.

Key Takeaways

  • The RP2040 needs 3.3V externally (IOVDD, USB_VDD) and generates 1.1V internally for the core
  • LDOs are simple and low-noise but inefficient at large voltage drops -- use buck converters for efficiency
  • Every IC power pin needs a 100nF ceramic decoupling capacitor placed within 3mm of the pin
  • Thermal dissipation = (Vin - Vout) x Iout -- calculate before selecting a regulator package
  • The Pico uses a buck-boost (RT6150) to handle both USB 5V and battery 1.8-5.5V inputs
  • Ceramic capacitor capacitance drops with applied voltage -- check derating curves for critical values
