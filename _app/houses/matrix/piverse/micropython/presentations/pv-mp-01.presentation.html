The RP2040 Microcontroller
Custom silicon designed by Raspberry Pi Ltd. Dual ARM Cortex-M0+ cores at 133 MHz.
Not an Arduino
The RP2040 is a 32-bit ARM chip, not an 8-bit AVR. Dual cores provide genuine hardware parallelism. The second core is accessible via MicroPython's _thread module.
Programmable I/O
Two PIO blocks with 8 state machines can implement custom hardware protocols in software -- NeoPixel, WS2812, custom serial -- without using CPU time.
Key Difference
The RP2040 runs code from external flash via QSPI, not from internal flash like AVR chips. The 264 KB SRAM across 6 banks allows both cores and DMA to access different banks simultaneously.

Pico Board Layout
40-pin DIP form factor. 51mm x 21mm. Breadboard-compatible.
26 GPIO
GP0-GP28 (not all exposed). Each configurable as input/output with pull-up/pull-down.
Power Pins
VBUS (USB 5V), VSYS (1.8-5.5V input), 3V3 (regulated output), 3V3_EN (regulator enable).
8 Ground
Common ground pins. Always connect GND between Pico and external circuits.
Slide 4 of 12
Pin Types and Functions
Every GPIO pin can serve multiple roles. Only one function is active at a time.
Digital I/O
GP0-GP28. Input or output. Configurable pull-up/pull-down resistors.
ADC
GP26, GP27, GP28 (ADC0-2). 12-bit resolution. GP29/ADC3 reads VSYS/3.
PWM
All GPIO pins. 16 channels (8 slices x 2 outputs). Hardware-driven duty cycle.
I2C
Two controllers (I2C0, I2C1). Mappable to GP0/1, GP4/5, GP8/9, etc.
SPI
Two controllers (SPI0, SPI1). High-speed serial for displays and SD cards.
UART
Two interfaces (UART0, UART1). Serial communication for GPS, Bluetooth, etc.
Pin Multiplexing
A single GPIO pin can be digital I/O, PWM, UART, SPI, or I2C -- but only one at a time. In MicroPython, the function is selected automatically when you create a Pin, PWM, I2C, or SPI object.

The BOOTSEL Button
How firmware gets onto the Pico. Hold it, plug USB, drag and drop.
1 Hold BOOTSEL while plugging the USB cable. The RP2040 enters USB mass storage mode instead of running its current firmware.
2 RPI-RP2 appears as a removable drive on your computer. This is the RP2040's ROM bootloader presenting the flash as a USB disk.
3 Drag the .uf2 file (MicroPython firmware) onto the drive. UF2 (USB Flashing Format) is designed for safe, simple firmware deployment.
4 Auto-reboot. The Pico writes firmware to flash, reboots, and the MicroPython REPL becomes available over USB serial.
Slide 6 of 12
USB Connection Roles
The Micro-USB port on the Pico serves three distinct purposes depending on context.
Power Supply
USB provides 5V on the VBUS pin. The on-board regulator converts this to 3.3V for the RP2040 and all GPIO pins. No external power supply needed for basic projects.
Firmware Flashing
In BOOTSEL mode, the RP2040's ROM bootloader presents flash as a USB mass storage device. Copy .uf2 files to install or update firmware.
Serial Console
During normal operation, USB provides a CDC (Communications Device Class) serial port. This is the REPL -- your interactive MicroPython terminal.
VBUS vs VSYS
VBUS is the raw USB 5V. VSYS is the system power input (1.8V-5.5V) that feeds the voltage regulator. You can power the Pico via VSYS from batteries or external supplies, bypassing USB entirely. Never drive both VBUS and VSYS simultaneously with unregulated sources.
Slide 7 of 12
Memory Architecture
264 KB SRAM in 6 banks. 2 MB external QSPI flash. No internal flash on the RP2040 die.
SRAM -- Runtime Memory
Variables, stack, heap, MicroPython interpreter state. 264 KB across 6 independent banks. About 192 KB available after MicroPython boots.
Flash -- Persistent Storage
Firmware, user scripts (main.py), data files. Survives power cycles. MicroPython creates a small filesystem here for your .py files.
Slide 8 of 12
GPIO Electrical Characteristics
3.3V logic. Not 5V tolerant. Know your limits before connecting anything.
Logic Levels
HIGH = 3.3V, LOW = 0V. This is NOT 5V logic. Connecting a 5V signal directly to a GPIO pin will damage the RP2040 permanently. Use a level shifter or voltage divider for 5V devices.
Current Limits
Each GPIO can source or sink up to 12 mA by default (configurable 2/4/8/12 mA via drive strength registers). Total current across all pins should not exceed 50 mA.
Pull-Up / Pull-Down
Each GPIO has internal pull-up and pull-down resistors (approximately 50K ohm). Configurable in software. Essential for button inputs to avoid floating pins.
Slew Rate
Configurable fast/slow slew rate per pin. Fast slew produces sharper edges but more electromagnetic noise. Slow slew reduces noise for non-timing-critical signals.
Critical Warning
The RP2040 GPIO pins are 3.3V only and NOT 5V tolerant. Applying 5V to any GPIO pin will permanently damage the chip. Always check the voltage levels of sensors and modules before connecting.
Slide 9 of 12
Analog-to-Digital Converter
4-channel, 12-bit ADC. Reads analog voltages and converts them to numbers.
CH0 GP26 (ADC0) -- General-purpose analog input. Connect potentiometers, light sensors, or any 0-3.3V analog signal.
CH1 GP27 (ADC1) -- Second analog input. Same 12-bit resolution as all channels.
CH2 GP28 (ADC2) -- Third analog input. Note: this pin shares the header with other functions.
CH3 GP29 (ADC3) -- Connected internally to VSYS/3 on the Pico board. Reads the system supply voltage. Not on the header.
CH4 Internal temp sensor -- Built-in temperature sensor accessible via ADC channel 4. No external wiring needed.
Resolution
12-bit ADC means 4096 discrete values (0-4095). MicroPython's read_u16() scales this to 0-65535 (16-bit) for compatibility. The actual hardware resolution remains 12 bits.
Slide 10 of 12
Pico vs Pico W
Same RP2040 chip. The W adds wireless connectivity.
Raspberry Pi Pico
RP2040 only. No wireless. Lower cost. On-board LED connected directly to GP25. All 26 GPIO pins fully available. Ideal for wired projects and learning fundamentals.
Raspberry Pi Pico W
RP2040 + Infineon CYW43439 wireless chip. WiFi 802.11n and Bluetooth 5.2 (BLE). On-board LED connected through the wireless chip (not GP25). GPIO 23 reserved for wireless power control.
LED Difference
On Pico, the on-board LED is on GP25: Pin(25, Pin.OUT). On Pico W, the LED is on the wireless chip: Pin("LED", Pin.OUT). Code that works on one may not work on the other without this change.
Firmware Difference
Pico and Pico W use different MicroPython firmware builds. The Pico W build includes the network module and CYW43 driver. Using the wrong firmware means no wireless (Pico firmware on Pico W) or a failed boot (Pico W firmware on Pico).

Debug Pins and SWD
Three pads at the bottom of the board for hardware debugging.
SWCLK
Serial Wire Clock. The clock signal for the SWD debug interface. Used by debug probes to step through code, set breakpoints, and inspect memory.
SWDIO
Serial Wire Data I/O. Bidirectional data line for the SWD protocol. Carries commands from the debug probe and responses from the RP2040.
GND
Ground reference for the debug connection. Must be connected for reliable communication between probe and target.
When You Need SWD
For MicroPython development, you rarely need SWD -- the REPL and Thonny provide sufficient debugging. SWD becomes essential when working with C/C++ SDK, diagnosing hard faults, or recovering a Pico that won't enter BOOTSEL mode.

Key Takeaways: Know Your Pico
Five facts that define the hardware you will program.
1RP2040 = dual ARM Cortex-M0+ at 133 MHz. 32-bit processor, two cores, genuine hardware parallelism. Not an 8-bit AVR.
2264 KB SRAM + 2 MB flash. Six independent SRAM banks. Flash is external via QSPI. About 192 KB free after MicroPython boots.
340 pins, 26 GPIO, 3 ADC, 8 GND. Every GPIO supports PWM. Pin multiplexing lets each pin serve as digital, analog, I2C, SPI, or UART.
4BOOTSEL + USB = firmware flashing. Hold BOOTSEL, plug USB, drag .uf2. No special programmer needed. The ROM bootloader handles everything.
53.3V logic, NOT 5V tolerant. Applying 5V to any GPIO damages the chip permanently. Know your voltage levels before connecting any external component.
