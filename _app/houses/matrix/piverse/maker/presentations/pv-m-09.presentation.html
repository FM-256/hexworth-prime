Audio Capture on the Pi
The Pi has no analog audio input -- USB is the only path.
USB Microphones
The Pi lacks a microphone input or ADC for analog audio. USB microphones present as ALSA sound cards via the USB Audio Class driver. No additional drivers needed. Common choices: Blue Snowball, USB lavalier mics, or USB sound cards with 3.5mm mic input.
I2S MEMS Microphones
Digital microphones (INMP441, SPH0645) connect directly to the Pi's I2S bus via GPIO. Higher quality than cheap USB mics. Require kernel overlay configuration. Multiple mics enable stereo or directional recording.
Sample Rates
Most environmental monitoring uses 44.1kHz or 48kHz sample rate at 16-bit depth. For bird call analysis, 44.1kHz captures frequencies up to 22kHz -- well above the ~10kHz upper range of most bird vocalizations. Higher sample rates waste storage without adding useful data.

ALSA and Audio Architecture
The Linux audio subsystem that manages all sound devices.
L3Application Layer -- Python (sounddevice, pyaudio), arecord CLI, FFmpeg. Your code requests audio samples from the device through these interfaces.
L2ALSA Library -- Advanced Linux Sound Architecture. Manages device enumeration, sample format conversion, buffering, and mixing. The arecord -l command lists all available capture devices.
L1Kernel Driver -- USB Audio Class driver (snd-usb-audio) or I2S driver. Handles DMA transfers between the audio device and system memory. Manages interrupt timing for sample-accurate capture.
# List audio capture devices $ arecord -l # Record 10 seconds of audio to WAV $ arecord -D plughw:1,0 -f S16_LE -r 44100 -d 10 recording.wav # -D: device -f: format -r: sample rate -d: duration

Spectrograms
Converting audio from time-domain to frequency-domain for analysis.
What is a Spectrogram?
A visual representation of sound. X-axis is time, Y-axis is frequency, color intensity is amplitude. Created by applying Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to overlapping windows of audio samples. Each vertical slice is one FFT of a short audio segment.
Mel Spectrogram
A spectrogram with frequency axis warped to the Mel scale, which models human pitch perception. Low frequencies get more resolution, high frequencies get less. Standard input for audio ML models because it better represents perceptually relevant features.
Why Spectrograms for ML?
Audio classification models typically do not process raw waveforms. Instead, audio is converted to a Mel spectrogram image, and the classification problem becomes an image classification problem. A CNN trained on spectrogram images can distinguish bird species, identify chainsaws, or detect gunshots.

Sound Classification with ML
Using pre-trained models to identify environmental sounds.
YAMNet
Google's audio event classifier. Trained on AudioSet (2M+ clips, 521 sound classes). Recognizes birds, vehicles, speech, alarms, rain, thunder. MobileNet v1 backbone on Mel spectrograms. Runs on Pi 5 at real-time speed.
BirdNET
Cornell Lab of Ornithology's bird identifier. Recognizes 6,000+ bird species by their vocalizations. Optimized for ecological monitoring. TFLite version runs on Pi. The gold standard for avian bioacoustics.
Custom Models
Train your own classifier using transfer learning from YAMNet. Record local sounds, label them, fine-tune. Useful for detecting specific events: chainsaw activity, specific frog species, or equipment malfunction sounds.

Wildlife Monitoring Architecture
An autonomous recording station for ecological research.
1Scheduled recording. Cron job triggers recording at dawn and dusk (peak bird activity). Continuous recording is possible but generates ~1.5GB/hour at 44.1kHz 16-bit mono. Storage planning is critical.
2On-device classification. BirdNET or YAMNet analyzes each recording segment. Only segments with confident detections are retained, reducing storage by 80-90%.
3Metadata logging. Each detection logged with timestamp, species, confidence score, GPS coordinates (if available), temperature, and audio filename. SQLite database on the Pi.
4Data sync. When WiFi is available, sync detection logs and flagged audio clips to a central server. rsync over SSH or MQTT messages for lightweight telemetry.
Power Budget
A Pi 5 draws ~5W at idle, ~8W under ML inference load. A 100Wh battery provides ~12-20 hours of operation. Solar panels (20-50W) can sustain indefinite operation in areas with 4+ hours of daily sunlight.

Environmental Sensors via GPIO and I2C
Complementing audio data with physical measurements.
BME280
Temperature, humidity, barometric pressure. I2C interface. Correlate weather conditions with animal activity patterns.
TSL2591
Light intensity sensor. I2C. Measures lux from 0.001 to 88,000. Determines dawn/dusk timing for automated recording schedules.
PM2.5 (SDS011)
Particulate matter sensor. UART serial. Measures air quality. Relevant for environmental health studies and wildfire smoke detection.
GPS (NEO-6M)
UART serial GPS module. Provides location coordinates and precise UTC timestamps. Essential for multi-station deployments where location matters.
PIR Sensor
Passive infrared motion detector. GPIO digital input. Triggers camera capture when an animal moves within detection range. Reduces continuous recording overhead.
DS18B20
Waterproof temperature probe. 1-Wire protocol on GPIO. Measures water or soil temperature. Can chain multiple sensors on one GPIO pin.

Data Pipelines
Moving sensor data from the Pi to analysis and visualization platforms.
MQTT Telemetry
Lightweight publish-subscribe messaging. Pi publishes sensor readings to topics (sensors/station-01/temperature). A central broker (Mosquitto) routes messages to subscribers. Ideal for real-time dashboards. Low bandwidth -- a single reading is ~100 bytes.
InfluxDB Time Series
Purpose-built database for timestamped sensor data. Efficient compression for sequential measurements. Query language optimized for time-range aggregations (hourly averages, daily min/max). Pairs with Grafana for visualization.
Local-First Architecture
Always store data locally on the Pi first (SQLite or CSV). Network connectivity at remote sites is unreliable. The Pi should function autonomously for days or weeks, syncing accumulated data when connectivity is restored.

Weatherproofing and Field Deployment
Protecting Pi hardware in outdoor environments.
1Enclosure. IP67-rated junction boxes with cable glands for power and microphone cables. Drill ventilation holes covered with Gore-Tex membrane patches for pressure equalization without moisture ingress.
2Microphone protection. Mount the USB microphone outside the enclosure under a wind screen (deadcat) and rain shield. Waterproof outdoor microphones (Primo EM172) are purpose-built for ecological recording.
3Power. 12V sealed lead-acid battery or LiFePO4 pack. 12V-to-5V buck converter (not a linear regulator -- efficiency matters). Solar charge controller manages panel-to-battery charging.
4Temperature management. Pi operates from 0C to 85C (BCM2712 spec). Below freezing, lithium batteries lose capacity. Insulate the battery compartment. In extreme heat, shade the enclosure and ensure airflow.

Real-World Monitoring Projects
Practical environmental sensing deployments on Pi hardware.
Bird Population Survey
Network of Pi stations with BirdNET running at dawn chorus. Automated species identification, daily diversity indices, seasonal migration tracking. Data aggregated to central InfluxDB with Grafana dashboards.
Urban Noise Mapping
Grid of Pi nodes measuring SPL (sound pressure level) across a city. Classify noise sources (traffic, construction, aircraft). Generate heat maps of noise pollution over time. Inform urban planning decisions.
Bat Detector
Pi with ultrasonic USB microphone (up to 192kHz sample rate). Record echolocation calls, identify bat species by call frequency and pattern. Time-expansion playback makes ultrasonic calls audible to humans.
Weather Station Network
Pi + BME280 + rain gauge + anemometer + UV sensor. Log readings every 60 seconds. Publish via MQTT. Create local microclimate maps with higher spatial resolution than commercial weather services.

Key Takeaways: Environmental Sensing
The five facts that matter.
1The Pi has no analog audio input. Use USB microphones (plug-and-play via ALSA) or I2S MEMS microphones (GPIO, higher quality, requires configuration).
2Sound classification converts audio to Mel spectrograms and applies image classification CNNs. YAMNet handles 521 general sound classes; BirdNET identifies 6,000+ bird species.
3On-device classification reduces storage by 80-90%. Only retain audio segments with confident detections instead of archiving hours of silence and wind noise.
4Local-first data architecture is mandatory for field deployments. Store to SQLite on the Pi, sync when connectivity is available. Never depend on real-time network access.
5Weatherproofing is as important as the code. IP67 enclosure, Gore-Tex vent, wind-screened microphone, solar power, and temperature management determine whether a deployment lasts days or years.
