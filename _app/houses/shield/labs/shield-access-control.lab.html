Access Models

DACOwner-set

Discretionary — the resource owner grants access at their discretion. Unix file perms & ACLs.

MACLabels

Mandatory — the system enforces clearance vs. classification labels. Bell-LaPadula: no read up, no write down. SELinux.

RBACINCITS 359

Role-Based — permissions attach to roles, users get roles. Supports role hierarchies & separation of duties (static at assignment, dynamic at access). ANSI/INCITS 359-2012.

ABAC800-162

Attribute-Based — decisions from subject / resource / action / environment attributes evaluated at request time. Fine-grained. NIST SP 800-162.

Stage 1 — Build the RBAC model

The old EHR gave everyone broad access. Rebuild it on least privilege: grant each role only the permissions its job genuinely needs. Click a cell to grant; click again to revoke.

Objective: a clinician sees clinical data; support roles do not. Watch for the classic trap — the System Admin runs the platform but should hold no clinical access.

Stage 2 — Separation of duties & ABAC rules

RBAC alone can't express context. Layer a separation-of-duties constraint and the attribute rules that make access fit the situation. Enable the rules that belong — and reject the one that only looks safe.

Objective: enforce need-to-know, shift windows, emergency break-glass, billing location, and sensitive-record clearance — without an over-broad rule that breaks a 24/7 hospital.
Each enabled rule becomes a check the decision engine runs at request time. The stress test in Stage 3 will reveal exactly which rules you got right.

Stage 3 — Stress test the policy

Your configured policy is now a live Policy Decision Point. Real access requests will hit it one by one — the engine evaluates each against your RBAC matrix, SoD constraint and ABAC rules, then compares to the correct decision. In access control, one wrong call is a breach: aim for a perfect run.
