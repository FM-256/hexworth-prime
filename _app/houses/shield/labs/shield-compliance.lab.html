Frameworks

HIPAA Healthcare

Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act. Covers Protected Health Information (PHI) held by covered entities and their business associates. Security Rule mandates administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for electronic PHI.

45 CFR Parts 160 & 164; SY0-701 5.4
PCI-DSS Payments

Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. Applies to any entity that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data (PAN, cardholder name, etc.). 12 requirements across 6 goals. Quarterly external vulnerability scans required.

PCI DSS v4.0; SY0-701 5.4
GDPR EU Privacy

General Data Protection Regulation. Applies to processing of personal data of EU natural persons, regardless of the processor's location. Breach notification to supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware. Fines up to €20M or 4% of global annual revenue.

Regulation (EU) 2016/679; SY0-701 5.4
SOX Financial

Sarbanes-Oxley Act. Applies to publicly-traded US companies (and foreign issuers listed on US exchanges). Sections 302/404 require controls over financial reporting accuracy. IT controls: change management, audit trail, segregation of duties.

Pub.L. 107-204; SY0-701 5.4

Stage 1 — Data Classification

Veridian Regional Hospital operates as a healthcare provider that also accepts payment cards and processes payroll. As GRC analyst, classify each data asset by its regulatory type. The regulation applicability engine uses your classifications to derive the hospital's compliance obligations.

Objective: select the correct data type for each asset. Classification drives every downstream obligation. A misclassification changes which regulations apply.

Stage 2 — Regulation Applicability

The engine reads your Stage 1 classifications and computes which regulations apply and what obligations they create. Review the derived obligation set — this is the ground truth your control program must satisfy in Stage 3.

Objective: understand what the engine derived and why. Obligations shown are the required controls you must assign in Stage 3. Regulations shown as not-applicable are excluded because none of your classified assets triggered them.
Applicability is computed automatically from your Stage 1 classifications. No student input required here — review the derived obligations before proceeding to control mapping.

Stage 3 — Control Mapping

For each applicable regulation, assign the required safeguards from the control catalog. Enable every control that genuinely belongs. The audit engine in Stage 4 checks for gaps (missing required controls) and over-claims (enabling inapplicable or incorrect controls).

Objective: achieve full coverage with no gaps and no wrong-claims. Each applicable regulation has a required set of controls — the audit flags both what is missing and what is incorrectly asserted.

Stage 4 — Compliance Audit

Your control program is now a live compliance posture. The audit engine evaluates every required control across every applicable regulation, checks for gaps, and validates that no incorrect controls are claimed. A program with any gap or over-claim fails certification.
