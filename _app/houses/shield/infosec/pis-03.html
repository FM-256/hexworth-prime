Threat Actors and Vectors

Course Index

Learning Objectives

  • Classify threat actors by type, motivation, and capability level
  • Describe the characteristics of an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) operation
  • Identify the phases of an APT attack lifecycle
  • Classify attack vectors by delivery channel and select appropriate countermeasures
Mission Context

Know your enemy. Every pathogen has an origin. Every breach has a source. Containment operators who cannot identify the type of actor and the vector it used cannot close the exposure point -- and that means the next specimen comes through the same hole.

Threat Actor Taxonomy

SY0-701 Objective 2.1 requires you to classify threat actors by type, motivation, and capability. Different actors require different countermeasures. A nation-state actor with unlimited resources and years of patience is not defended against the same way as a script kiddie running public exploit tools.

Nation-State

Motivation: Espionage, sabotage, geopolitical advantage. Highest sophistication and resources. Capable of zero-day development, supply chain compromise, and multi-year persistent operations. Examples: APT29 (Russia), APT41 (China), Lazarus Group (North Korea). The 2020 SolarWinds attack attributed to Russia compromised 18,000 organizations through a trusted software update.

Criminal

Motivation: Financial gain. Ransomware operators, credit card theft, BEC (Business Email Compromise) fraud, crypto theft. Highly organized -- modern criminal groups operate like businesses with support staff, affiliate programs, and customer service for ransom negotiation. The 2021 Colonial Pipeline ransomware attack (DarkSide group) caused fuel shortages across the US Southeast.

Hacktivist

Motivation: Political or ideological goals. Defacement, DDoS, data leaks to embarrass targets. Anonymous and affiliated groups have targeted governments, corporations, and institutions. Generally less sophisticated than criminal or nation-state actors but capable of significant disruption through volume and coordination.

Insider

Motivation: Financial gain, grievance, espionage, or negligence. The most dangerous actor type because they have legitimate access, know where the data is, and bypass perimeter defenses. Malicious insiders deliberately exfiltrate or destroy data. Negligent insiders cause breaches through carelessness. 60% of data breaches involve an insider component (IBM X-Force).

Script Kiddie

Motivation: Notoriety, curiosity, or mischief. Low sophistication. Uses publicly available exploit frameworks (Metasploit, Shodan scanning) without deep technical understanding. Volume-based threat -- they run automated scans against the internet looking for unpatched systems. Defenses: patch management, default credential removal, and firewall rules.

Operational Context

When a new specimen arrives at intake, the first question is always: who sent it here? Nation-state actors send precision-engineered pathogens. Criminal actors send hostage pathogens designed for rapid monetization. Insiders are the specimens that walk in through the front door with valid credentials.

Advanced Persistent Threats (APT)

APT is a classification of attack methodology. An APT combines three properties: advanced techniques (custom malware, zero-days, living-off-the-land), persistent access (maintained for months or years), and targeted objectives (specific organizations, data, or infrastructure). SY0-701 Objective 2.1 includes APT as a distinct category.

APT Phase Description Example Technique
Initial Access Gain first foothold in the target environment Spear phishing, watering hole attack, supply chain compromise
Persistence Maintain access that survives reboots and credential changes Scheduled tasks, registry run keys, web shells, backdoor accounts
Lateral Movement Move from initial target to high-value systems Pass-the-hash, Kerberoasting, RDP/WMI pivoting
Exfiltration Remove targeted data without triggering detection Slow-drip exfiltration over DNS, HTTPS to C2 server, encrypted archives
Covering Tracks Remove evidence of the intrusion Log deletion, timestomping, rootkit deployment

APT actors are typically discovered through threat hunting, anomaly detection in SIEM, or intelligence sharing -- not through signature-based detection. Dwell time averages 204 days globally (Mandiant M-Trends 2023). During that time, the actor has full access to the environment.

Operational Context

An APT in a containment facility does not break down doors. It finds a door already open and waits. The 2020 SolarWinds breach had a dwell time of months before detection -- the attackers were inside thousands of organizations before a single alarm triggered. Reducing dwell time from months to days requires behavioral detection, not signature scanning.

Supply Chain Attacks

A supply chain attack compromises software, hardware, or services before they reach the target. Rather than attacking the defended perimeter directly, the attacker inserts malicious code or hardware into a trusted component the target will install or use. SY0-701 Objective 2.2 includes supply chain as a distinct attack vector category.

Supply Chain Attack Type Method Real Example
Software Build Compromise Malicious code inserted into trusted software during development or build process SolarWinds (2020): Orion update backdoor affected 18,000 organizations
Third-Party Library Backdoor Malicious contributor inserts backdoor into open-source library XZ Utils (2024): backdoor nearly reached mainstream Linux distributions
MSP/MSSP Compromise Attack on a managed service provider to reach all clients simultaneously Kaseya VSA (2021): REvil ransomware deployed to 1,500 businesses via single MSP platform
Operational Context

The most dangerous specimen delivery method is trusted delivery. Supply chain attacks bypass the perimeter entirely because the threat was already inside when the equipment was delivered. Every trusted software update is a potential vector. Verification, code signing, and software bill of materials (SBOM) audits are the countermeasures.

Attack Vectors

An attack vector is the pathway used by a threat actor to gain unauthorized access or deliver a payload. SY0-701 Objective 2.2 requires you to identify and classify attack vectors across network, software, and human domains.

Direct Access / Physical

Attacker has physical access to a device. USB drives, bootable media, hardware implants. Bypasses all network-based defenses.

Wireless

Attacks over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, or cellular. Rogue access points, evil twin attacks, WPA2 handshake capture for offline cracking.

Email

Phishing, spear phishing, malicious attachments, BEC. Largest single attack vector by volume. 94% of malware delivered via email (Verizon DBIR).

Supply Chain

Compromised software updates, hardware with embedded malware, third-party libraries with backdoors. Trusted delivery mechanism used as weapon.

Removable Media

USB drives with malware, infected hardware devices. Classic vector for air-gapped attacks (Stuxnet was delivered via USB to an air-gapped Iranian facility).

Open Service Ports

Unpatched services exposed to the internet. Shodan scanning identifies vulnerable RDP (3389), SSH (22), and SMB (445) services in minutes.

Cloud / Misconfiguration

Exposed S3 buckets, overly permissive IAM roles, public-facing APIs without authentication. Configuration errors are the leading cause of cloud data breaches.

Social Media

Reconnaissance, impersonation, spear phishing via LinkedIn/Twitter, fake job postings to harvest credentials, watering hole targeting industry communities.

Operational Context

Every exposure point in this facility is a potential vector. The intake process for every specimen includes a full vector analysis: how did it enter, what path did it use, and what would have stopped it at each step. Closing vectors prevents the next specimen from using the same pathway.
