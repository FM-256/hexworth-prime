Operational Briefing

  • Categorize threat actors by type: nation-state, hacktivist, insider, criminal, script kiddie
  • Evaluate threat actors along key attributes: sophistication, resources, motivation, and intent
  • Map the five attack surface types and their reduction strategies
  • Distinguish threat actor motivations and their impact on incident response decisions
  • Identify authoritative threat intelligence sources and what each provides
Mission Context:

"Every specimen in this facility has an origin. Know the adversary before you engage. A containment operator who does not understand who releases pathogens, why they do it, and how they deliver them will misclassify every threat they encounter."

Threat Actors

A threat actor is any entity -- individual, group, or nation -- with the intent and capability to harm an organization's information assets. Security+ SY0-701 Objective 2.1 requires you to categorize threat actors by their attributes: sophistication, resources, motivation, and typical targets.

Nation-State
Motivation: Espionage / Sabotage
Government-sponsored actors with significant resources and advanced capabilities. Operate for strategic geopolitical objectives. May conduct multi-year campaigns (APTs).
Example: Volt Typhoon (China), targeting US critical infrastructure pre-positioning.
Hacktivist
Motivation: Ideology / Publicity
Politically or socially motivated actors. Goals include defacement, data exposure, and DDoS against organizations they oppose. Capabilities vary widely.
Example: Anonymous DDoS campaigns against government sites during political events.
Insider Threat
Motivation: Financial / Revenge / Negligence
Current or former employees, contractors, or partners with authorized access. Most dangerous because they bypass perimeter controls. May be malicious or simply careless.
Example: The 2022 Uber breach began with social engineering a contractor with system access.
Criminal / Organized
Motivation: Financial gain
Profit-driven groups conducting ransomware, fraud, and data theft. Well-resourced, professional operations with customer service, negotiation teams, and affiliate models.
Example: LockBit ransomware group -- RaaS model, over $91M extorted from US organizations.
Script Kiddie
Motivation: Notoriety / Curiosity
Unskilled individuals using publicly available tools and exploits without understanding how they work. Low sophistication but high volume -- automated scanning makes them a consistent background noise.
Example: Automated credential stuffing attacks using breach databases from public sources.
Operational Context:

Every pathogen in the catalog has a source attribution. Nation-state, criminal, hacktivist, or opportunist. Classification affects the containment response -- you do not use the same protocol for all five.

Threat Actor Attributes

Security+ SY0-701 requires understanding threat actors along several dimensions beyond just their category. These attributes determine how you prioritize defenses and respond to incidents.

AttributeWhat It Means for Defense
Internal vs. ExternalInternal actors already bypass perimeter controls. Defense must focus on least privilege, monitoring, and data loss prevention. External actors are stopped at the perimeter first.
Resources / FundingWell-funded actors (nation-states, organized crime) can purchase zero-days, maintain persistent access for years, and outlast investigation timelines. Resource-constrained actors rely on automation and public tools.
SophisticationHighly sophisticated actors develop custom malware and use living-off-the-land techniques that evade signature-based detection. Low-sophistication actors are caught by basic controls.
IntentDestructive intent (sabotage) requires different response than data theft. Ransomware operators want payment, not destruction -- knowing this affects negotiation and recovery decisions.
APT (Advanced Persistent Threat)A classification for long-term, targeted campaigns -- typically nation-state. APTs establish persistent access and conduct slow, careful reconnaissance before acting. Dwell time averages 200+ days before detection.
Operational Context:

The intake protocol classifies each specimen by origin sophistication. A script-kiddie pathogen and a nation-state pathogen require fundamentally different containment responses.

Attack Surfaces

An attack surface is the total set of exposure points where an adversary could attempt to enter or extract data from an environment. Understanding your attack surface is prerequisite to reducing it.

Surface TypeExamplesReduction Strategy
NetworkOpen ports, exposed services, publicly reachable IP addressesFirewall rules, network segmentation, disable unused services
SoftwareUnpatched applications, third-party libraries, browser pluginsPatch management, software inventory, dependency scanning
HumanEmployees, contractors, support staff susceptible to social engineeringSecurity awareness training, phishing simulations, verification procedures
PhysicalUnattended workstations, accessible server rooms, discarded hardwareBadge access, clean desk policy, secure hardware disposal
Supply ChainThird-party software, hardware components, managed service providersVendor risk assessments, software composition analysis, contractual security requirements

Attack surface reduction is one of the highest-leverage security activities because it eliminates entire categories of risk rather than mitigating individual threats. Every service you disable, every account you remove, every port you close is a potential breach vector permanently removed.

Operational Context:

Every entry point in this facility is a potential exposure point. The containment directive is simple: if it does not need to be open, close it. If it does not need to exist, remove it.

Threat Actor Motivations

Motivation drives behavior. Understanding why an adversary attacks determines what they will target, how they will behave during an incident, and what outcomes they are trying to achieve.

MotivationTypical ActorWhat They Want
Financial gainCriminal organizationsRansomware payment, stolen credentials for resale, financial fraud, corporate espionage for competitive advantage
EspionageNation-statesIntellectual property, government secrets, military technology, diplomatic communications
Sabotage / DisruptionNation-states, hacktivistsOperational disruption, infrastructure damage, degrading adversary capability
IdeologyHacktivistsEmbarrassment, publicity for a cause, coercion through data exposure
RevengeDisgruntled insidersSabotage of specific systems, data destruction, reputational damage to employer
Curiosity / ChallengeScript kiddies, hobbyistsBragging rights, technical challenge, vandalism
Operational Context:

Containment operators profile every incoming pathogen by its probable motivation. A ransomware operator wants money -- there is a negotiation protocol. A nation-state actor wants access -- eradication is the only acceptable response.

Threat Intelligence Sources

Threat intelligence is evidence-based knowledge about adversaries and their tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs). Good threat intelligence allows defenders to anticipate attacks rather than only respond to them.

SourceWhat It Provides
MITRE ATT&CKComprehensive matrix of adversary tactics and techniques organized by attack phase. Used for threat modeling, detection engineering, and red team operations.
CVE / NVDCommon Vulnerabilities and Exposures database. Every published vulnerability receives a CVE identifier with severity score (CVSS). Primary source for patch prioritization.
CISA AdvisoriesUS Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency publishes alerts on actively exploited vulnerabilities and threat actor campaigns targeting critical infrastructure.
ISACsInformation Sharing and Analysis Centers -- sector-specific threat sharing communities (FS-ISAC for financial, H-ISAC for healthcare). Members share real incident data.
Commercial FeedsPaid threat intelligence services (CrowdStrike, Mandiant, Recorded Future) providing curated, analyst-verified intelligence with attribution and IOCs.
OSINTOpen-source intelligence from public sources: paste sites, dark web forums, social media, researcher blogs, and incident reports. Free but requires significant analyst effort to process.
Operational Context:

The facility's outbreak intelligence feed aggregates all five source types. You will learn to work with each one during the BSL-1 labs. For now, know they exist and what each provides.

Self-Check

  1. A hospital is targeted by attackers who encrypt all patient records and demand payment. Based on actor type and motivation, what category of threat actor is most likely responsible?
  2. An employee who was recently passed over for promotion is found to have exfiltrated the company's customer database to a personal drive. What type of threat actor is this, and what attribute makes them particularly dangerous?
  3. A government agency wants to monitor a foreign nation's satellite program communications over an extended period without detection. Which threat actor category and which attribute best describes this scenario?
  4. What is the difference between an attack surface and an attack vector? Give one example of each.

If you can answer all four without referring back, proceed to PIS-R3: Network Foundations.
