Incident Report FW-401. A sales manager leaves a company-issued iPhone in an Uber after a client dinner. The phone has no screen lock, no encryption, and no MDM enrollment. Within 24 hours, the finder accesses the corporate email app (auto-logged-in), downloads the entire client contact list, and forwards six months of pricing proposals to a competitor. The MDM remote wipe command fails because the device was never enrolled. Total cost of the incident: two lost contracts worth $1.2 million and a mandatory breach notification to 340 clients.

Mobile Device Management (MDM)
Centralized platform for enrolling, configuring, monitoring, and securing mobile devices across an organization.
What MDM Controls
Enforce screen lock policies, require encryption, push Wi-Fi and VPN configurations, distribute approved apps, block unapproved apps, configure email profiles, and enforce compliance policies. Non-compliant devices can be automatically blocked from corporate resources.
Common MDM Solutions
Microsoft Intune (integrates with Azure AD), VMware Workspace ONE, Jamf (Apple-focused), MobileIron, and Google Workspace endpoint management. Enterprise MDM provides a single pane of glass for managing thousands of devices across iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS.
Enrollment Process
Devices are enrolled via a management profile (iOS) or device administrator/work profile (Android). Once enrolled, the MDM agent communicates with the server to receive policies, report compliance status, and execute remote commands. Unenrolled devices cannot access corporate resources.

Screen Locks: PIN, Biometric, Pattern
The screen lock is the first barrier between an unauthorized person and everything on the device -- email, banking, photos, corporate data.
Biometric Authentication
Fingerprint (Touch ID, in-display sensors) and facial recognition (Face ID, Android face unlock) provide the strongest practical security. Biometrics cannot be shoulder-surfed and are unique per person. A 6-digit PIN or alphanumeric password serves as the fallback when biometrics fail.
Pattern Locks Are Weak
Research shows that most users create simple patterns (starting top-left, using 4-5 nodes). Smudge attacks reveal the pattern from finger oils on the screen. A 2017 study found that 64% of Android pattern locks could be cracked after observing one unlock attempt. Use PIN or biometric instead.
MDM Lock Policy
MDM can enforce minimum PIN length (6+ digits), require alphanumeric passwords, mandate biometric enrollment, set auto-lock timeout (1-5 minutes), and limit failed attempts before device wipe. These policies apply even if the user prefers no lock screen.
Auto-Lock Timeout
Set auto-lock to 1-2 minutes for phones. Unlike workstations, phones are carried everywhere and frequently set down in public. A 5-minute timeout on a phone left on a restaurant table is an eternity. The shorter the timeout, the smaller the window for unauthorized access.

Remote Wipe
When a device is lost or stolen, remote wipe erases all data to prevent unauthorized access. The nuclear option -- but sometimes necessary.
Full Wipe
Factory resets the entire device -- all data, apps, settings, and accounts are erased. The device returns to out-of-box state. Used for company-owned devices or when the device contains highly sensitive data. Irreversible once executed.
Selective Wipe
Removes only corporate data and the MDM profile while leaving personal data intact. Used for BYOD devices where IT manages a work container/profile. The employee keeps their personal photos, apps, and accounts. Only the corporate email, VPN config, and managed apps are removed.
Prerequisites
Remote wipe requires: (1) device enrolled in MDM, (2) device powered on, (3) device connected to the internet. If the thief immediately puts the phone in airplane mode or removes the SIM, the wipe command cannot reach the device. This is why encryption is the primary defense -- remote wipe is the backup.
Find My Device
Apple Find My iPhone and Google Find My Device provide consumer-level remote lock, locate, and wipe. These should be enabled on all devices, even personal ones. Enterprise MDM provides the same capabilities with centralized management and audit logging.

Mobile Encryption
Full-device encryption ensures data at rest is unreadable without the correct authentication. The primary defense when a device is physically compromised.
iOS Encryption
All iOS devices are encrypted by default when a passcode is set. Uses AES-256 with a hardware-bound key in the Secure Enclave. Brute-force is mitigated by progressive delays and optional auto-erase after 10 failed attempts. The encryption key is derived from the user's passcode combined with a device-unique hardware key -- extracting data from the storage chip without the passcode is computationally infeasible.
Android Encryption
File-Based Encryption (FBE) has been mandatory since Android 10. Each file is encrypted with a different key. Before Android 10, Full Disk Encryption (FDE) was used but had to be manually enabled on some devices. MDM policies should verify encryption status -- do not assume it is enabled on older or budget Android devices.
Why Encryption Matters for Mobile
Phones are lost and stolen at vastly higher rates than laptops. A phone without encryption and without a screen lock gives immediate access to email, banking apps, saved passwords, photos, text messages, and location history. Encryption makes the stolen device a paperweight without the passcode.

App Permissions
Every app permission is a data access grant. A flashlight app requesting access to contacts, camera, microphone, and location is not a flashlight app.
Principle of Least Privilege
Grant only the permissions an app needs to function. A weather app needs location. It does not need contacts, camera, or microphone. Review permissions at install time and in Settings > Apps periodically. Revoke permissions that seem excessive for the app's stated purpose.
Location Tracking
Location permission is the most sensitive. Options: "While Using" (best), "Always" (avoid unless GPS navigation), "Never." Background location access allows apps to track movement patterns 24/7 and sell that data to brokers. Over 75,000 Android apps request location permission -- most do not need it.
iOS vs Android Permissions
iOS provides granular per-app permission controls with "Ask Next Time" and "Allow Once" options. Android 11+ added one-time permissions for camera, microphone, and location. Both platforms now auto-revoke permissions for apps not used in 90 days. MDM can restrict which permissions apps are allowed to request.

Sideloading Risks
Installing apps from outside the official app store bypasses all store security reviews and exposes the device to unvetted, potentially malicious code.
What Sideloading Enables
Sideloaded apps can contain trojans, spyware, keyloggers, ransomware, or cryptocurrency miners. They bypass Google Play Protect and App Store review. Common lure: "free" premium apps, modded games, or apps not available in your region. The "savings" of a pirated $5 app can cost thousands in compromised accounts.
Android: Unknown Sources
Android allows sideloading by enabling "Install Unknown Apps" in settings. MDM should disable this setting on all managed devices. On unmanaged devices, this setting should remain off unless the user has a specific, legitimate need and understands the risk.
iOS: Enterprise Certificates
iOS sideloading uses enterprise distribution certificates or third-party signing services. Malware campaigns have abused enterprise certificates to distribute spyware outside the App Store. Apple can revoke enterprise certificates, but the window between distribution and revocation is the attack window.
MDM App Management
MDM can create an approved app catalog, block sideloading, enforce app-level VPN, and automatically deploy required business apps. Managed App Configuration pushes settings to apps without user intervention. This ensures consistent, secure app deployment across all devices.

BYOD Policies
Bring Your Own Device programs let employees use personal devices for work -- but personal and corporate data must be separated and secured.
BYOD Security Challenges
IT has limited control over personal devices. Users may install risky apps, connect to unsecured Wi-Fi, or delay OS updates. The device may be shared with family members. If the device is compromised by personal activity, corporate data is also exposed. BYOD requires clear policies and technical containerization.
Work Profile Containers
Android Work Profile and iOS managed containers create a separate encrypted space for corporate data. Work apps cannot access personal data and vice versa. IT can selectively wipe the work container without touching personal data. Copy/paste between profiles can be restricted to prevent data leakage.
BYOD Policy Requirements
The written BYOD policy should specify: minimum OS version required, screen lock requirements, encryption mandatory, MDM enrollment required, acceptable use rules, IT's right to selective wipe, employee responsibilities for lost devices, and consequences for non-compliance. Users must acknowledge and sign before enrollment.
Alternatives to BYOD
COPE (Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled): company owns the device, user can add personal apps. CYOD (Choose Your Own Device): user picks from approved device list, company owns it. Both give IT more control than BYOD while still allowing personal use. The trade-off is cost versus control.

Jailbreaking and Rooting
Removing the manufacturer's security restrictions gives the user root access -- and gives malware root access too.
What Jailbreaking Does
Removes iOS or Android security restrictions. Allows installation of unsigned apps, modifying system files, and running as root. Disables the app sandbox -- apps can now access each other's data. Disables Secure Boot chain verification. The device becomes fundamentally less secure.
Security Consequences
No more OS updates (jailbreaks often break with updates). No app sandboxing (malware can access banking app data). Disabled signature verification (any code can run). Malware with root access has complete device control: record calls, read encrypted messages, access camera, exfiltrate all data.
MDM Detection
MDM solutions can detect jailbroken/rooted devices using integrity checks (SafetyNet/Play Integrity on Android, jailbreak detection on iOS). Policy: detect jailbreak, immediately block corporate resource access, notify IT. Some organizations will remote-wipe a jailbroken corporate device on detection.
Corporate Policy
Jailbroken or rooted devices must never access corporate resources. The MDM profile should be configured to block enrollment of jailbroken devices and to revoke access if jailbreak is detected post-enrollment. This is non-negotiable for any organization handling sensitive data.

Bluetooth Security
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless protocol with a history of security vulnerabilities. If you are not actively using it, turn it off.
Bluetooth Attack Types
Bluejacking: sending unsolicited messages (annoying, not dangerous). Bluesnarfing: stealing data (contacts, calendar, messages) from a Bluetooth-enabled device without pairing. BlueBorne (CVE-2017-0781): zero-click remote code execution over Bluetooth -- no pairing required, no user interaction needed, wormable across devices.
Discoverable Mode
When Bluetooth is in discoverable mode, any nearby device can detect it and attempt to connect. Set Bluetooth to non-discoverable (hidden) when not actively pairing. Some attacks work even in non-discoverable mode by scanning for known Bluetooth MAC addresses, but discoverability increases exposure significantly.
Best Practices
Turn Bluetooth off when not in use. Reject unexpected pairing requests. Use Bluetooth 5.0+ which includes improved encryption. Remove paired devices you no longer use. In sensitive environments (classified meetings, executive boardrooms), enforce Bluetooth-off policies via MDM or physical enforcement.
BLE Tracking
Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) beacons broadcast unique identifiers that can be used to track physical movement through spaces (retail stores, airports, buildings). BLE tracking data has been sold to advertisers and law enforcement. Privacy-conscious users should disable BLE when not needed.

Lost Device Procedures
A documented, practiced response plan for lost or stolen devices minimizes damage and ensures consistent handling.
Step 1: Immediate Report
Employee reports loss to IT/security immediately -- ideally within 1 hour. Delayed reporting increases the window for data exfiltration. Provide 24/7 reporting channels (help desk, security hotline, self-service portal). Make reporting easy and blame-free.
Step 2: Locate and Lock
Use MDM or Find My Device to attempt location. Send remote lock command immediately. Change the lock screen message to display a contact number for return. Change all passwords for accounts accessible on the device (email, VPN, cloud services).
Step 3: Assess and Decide
Evaluate: Was the device encrypted? Was MDM enrolled? What data was accessible? Was the device recovered or confirmed stolen? If encrypted + locked, risk is lower. If unencrypted or unlocked, treat as a data breach. Selective wipe for BYOD. Full wipe for corporate devices confirmed stolen.
Breach Notification
If the lost device contained PII, PHI, or regulated data and was not encrypted, a data breach notification may be legally required under HIPAA, state breach notification laws, or GDPR. The difference between an encrypted lost device (security incident) and an unencrypted one (reportable breach) can be millions of dollars in fines and notification costs.

Mobile Threat Landscape
Mobile devices face unique threats beyond traditional PC malware -- from rogue Wi-Fi to commercial spyware.
Rogue Wi-Fi / Evil Twin
Attacker sets up a Wi-Fi access point with the same name as a legitimate network ("Starbucks_WiFi"). The device auto-connects. All traffic flows through the attacker, who can intercept credentials, inject malware, or redirect to phishing sites. Use VPN on all public Wi-Fi.
Mobile Spyware
Commercial spyware (NSO Group's Pegasus) can silently compromise a phone with zero-click exploits -- no user interaction required. Capabilities: read all messages (including encrypted), activate camera/microphone, track location, exfiltrate all data. Keep OS updated to patch the vulnerabilities these tools exploit.
SIM Swapping
Attacker convinces the carrier to transfer the victim's phone number to a new SIM card. They now receive all calls and texts -- including SMS-based MFA codes. Used to hijack bank accounts, email, and cryptocurrency. Mitigation: use app-based MFA (not SMS), set a carrier PIN, use SIM lock.

Mobile Security Checklist
Practical steps every user and IT department should implement for mobile device security.
Device Level
Enable biometric + 6-digit PIN. Verify encryption is active. Enable auto-lock (1-2 min). Enable Find My Device / MDM. Set auto-update for OS. Disable Bluetooth when not in use. Do not jailbreak/root. Set carrier PIN to prevent SIM swapping.
App Level
Install only from official stores. Review permissions -- revoke unnecessary access. Enable app-based MFA (not SMS). Use a password manager. Keep apps updated. Remove apps you no longer use. Do not grant "Always" location to apps that do not need it.
Network Level
Use VPN on all public Wi-Fi. Forget networks you no longer use. Disable auto-join for unknown networks. Verify HTTPS on all sensitive sites. Avoid entering credentials on public networks even with VPN if possible.
Enterprise Level
Enroll all devices in MDM. Enforce compliance policies (encryption, lock, OS version). Deploy work profile containers for BYOD. Block jailbroken devices. Require VPN for corporate resource access. Document and practice lost device procedures.
