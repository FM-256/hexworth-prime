Why Physical Security Matters
If an attacker can touch it, they own it. No firewall protects against a stolen hard drive.
Bypass All Digital Controls
Physical access to a server means direct disk access. Encryption keys can be extracted from memory. USB boot attacks bypass OS-level protections entirely.
Equipment Theft
Laptops, drives, and backup tapes contain entire databases. A stolen laptop costs $49,000 on average when you include the value of the data it contains.
Insider Threats
Employees already have physical access. Without proper segmentation, a single disgruntled employee can walk out with the entire customer database on a USB drive.
The Physical-Digital Connection
Every cyberattack that matters eventually requires physical access -- whether that is a server room, a USB port, or a network jack behind a desk. Physical security and cybersecurity are not separate disciplines. They are one discipline viewed from two angles.

Physical Access Controls
Three categories: something you have, something you know, something you are.
Keyed Locks
Traditional pin tumbler locks. Cost-effective but keys can be duplicated. Key management becomes a tracking problem at scale. Bump-resistant locks offer better security.
Badge / Smart Card Systems
RFID or proximity cards. Programmable, revocable, and auditable. Every swipe creates a log entry. Can be combined with PINs for two-factor physical authentication.
Cipher Locks (Keypad)
Mechanical or electronic keypads. No physical key to lose. Codes can be changed regularly. Vulnerable to shoulder surfing and worn-button analysis on older keypads.
Multi-Factor Physical Auth
High-security facilities combine factors: badge swipe + PIN entry + biometric scan. Each additional factor reduces the probability of unauthorized access. A stolen badge alone is useless without the PIN and the fingerprint.
Slide 4 of 14
Biometric Access Controls
Your body is the credential. Biometrics cannot be lost, shared, or forgotten -- but they can be spoofed.
Fingerprint Scanners
Most common biometric. Low cost. Fast enrollment. Can be spoofed with high-quality prints. Capacitive sensors are harder to fool than optical sensors.
Retina / Iris Scanners
Extremely accurate. Nearly impossible to spoof. Expensive hardware. Retina scans use blood vessel patterns; iris scans use color patterns. Both are unique per individual.
Facial Recognition
Non-contact, fast, and improving rapidly. Can be fooled by photos on older systems. 3D mapping + infrared eliminates most spoofing attacks.
Voice / Palm Vein
Voice recognition works for phone-based auth. Palm vein scanning uses infrared to map vein patterns beneath the skin -- nearly impossible to replicate.
FAR vs FRR
False Acceptance Rate (FAR): how often the system lets in the wrong person. False Rejection Rate (FRR): how often it locks out the right person. The Crossover Error Rate (CER) is where FAR = FRR. Lower CER = more accurate biometric system.

Surveillance Systems
Deter, detect, and document. Cameras are both a preventive and detective control.
CCTV (Analog)
Closed-circuit television. Coaxial cable runs to a DVR. Lower resolution than IP cameras. Still widely deployed in older facilities. Not network-accessible by default.
IP Cameras
Network-connected, high resolution, remotely accessible. Support motion detection, night vision, and analytics. Must be secured -- default passwords are a major vulnerability.
Motion Sensors
PIR (passive infrared), microwave, or dual-tech. Trigger alerts and camera recording when movement is detected. Used in hallways, server rooms, and perimeter zones.
Camera Placement Strategy
Cover entry points, server rooms, parking lots, and loading docks. Overlapping fields of view eliminate blind spots. Position cameras where they are visible to deter and out of reach to prevent tampering. Retention policies determine how long footage is stored -- typically 30 to 90 days.

Environmental Controls
Heat, water, fire, and power failures destroy more data centers than hackers do.
HVAC Systems
Data centers require 64-75 F (18-24 C) and 40-60% humidity. Hot aisle / cold aisle layout optimizes airflow. HVAC failure causes thermal shutdown within minutes.
Fire Suppression
Water sprinklers destroy electronics. Server rooms use clean agent systems: FM-200, Novec 1230, or CO2. Pre-action sprinklers only release water after heat + smoke confirmation.
UPS & Generators
UPS provides instant battery backup during power loss. Generators take 10-30 seconds to start. Together they provide continuous uptime. Regular testing is mandatory.
Environmental Monitoring
Temperature, humidity, water leak, and smoke sensors connected to alerting systems. SNMP-enabled sensors integrate with network monitoring. Early warning prevents catastrophic failure.

Secure Areas
Layered physical zones enforce the principle of least privilege in the physical world.
Server Rooms
Badge + biometric entry. No windows. Raised floors for cable management and airflow. Visitor log required. Cameras on entry and inside the room.
Server Cages & Racks
Locked mesh enclosures inside shared data centers. Individual cages for each tenant in colocation facilities. Physical isolation within a shared building.
Mantrap / Airlock
Two interlocking doors -- the second does not open until the first closes and locks. Prevents tailgating. Often includes weight sensors and cameras between the doors.
Defense in Depth -- Physical
Fence -> Gate -> Lobby -> Badge door -> Secure corridor -> Mantrap -> Server room -> Locked cage -> Locked rack. Each layer adds time, logging, and a decision point where an intruder must either authenticate or be detected.

Visitor Management
Everyone who enters must be identified, logged, and escorted.
Sign-In / Sign-Out Logs
Paper or electronic visitor logs. Record name, company, host employee, time in, time out, and areas visited. Logs must be retained per policy -- often 1 year minimum.
Temporary Badges
Issued at reception. Visually distinct from employee badges (different color, "VISITOR" text). Must be returned on exit. Auto-expire after set hours.
Escort Requirements
Visitors must be accompanied by an authorized employee at all times in secure areas. The escort is responsible for the visitor's actions. No unescorted visitor access, ever.
Delivery & Contractor Procedures
Deliveries verified against purchase orders. Contractors pre-registered by sponsoring department. Background checks for recurring contractors. Separate entrance for deliveries when possible.
The Social Engineering Angle
Visitor management exists because attackers impersonate vendors, delivery drivers, and job candidates. A uniform and a clipboard create perceived authority. Training staff to verify every visitor -- regardless of appearance -- is the most important control.
Slide 9 of 14
Tailgating Prevention
Tailgating is following an authorized person through a secured door without authenticating.
Mantraps / Security Vestibules
Two-door system that admits one person at a time. Weight sensors detect if two people entered. The gold standard for anti-tailgating.
Turnstiles & Optical Gates
Physical barriers that allow one person per badge swipe. Optical turnstiles use infrared beams to detect multiple people. Common in lobby environments.
Security Culture & Training
Employees trained to never hold doors. "Badge-in or stay out" policy. Signage at every secured door. Anonymous reporting for tailgating violations.
Slide 10 of 14
Cable Locks & Device Security
The cheapest physical control: make the device harder to walk away with.
Kensington Locks
Steel cable with a T-bar lock that inserts into a standard Kensington Security Slot found on most laptops. Combination or key-operated. Deters opportunistic theft but can be cut with bolt cutters.
Locking Cabinets & Drawers
Sensitive documents, backup drives, and USB devices stored in locked furniture. Key management is critical -- track who has keys and audit regularly.
USB Port Locks
Physical blockers inserted into USB ports to prevent unauthorized device connections. Removable only with a special key. Prevents data exfiltration via USB drives and juice-jacking attacks.
Asset Tags & Inventory
Every piece of equipment gets a unique asset tag. Barcode or RFID for automated scanning. Asset inventory audits detect missing equipment before data is compromised.
Screen Locks & Privacy Screens
Screen locks activate after idle timeout -- 5 minutes maximum in secure environments. Privacy filters restrict viewing angles to prevent shoulder surfing. Both are physical security controls applied at the device level.

Faraday Cages & RF Shielding
When wireless signals are the attack vector, you contain them physically.
What Is a Faraday Cage?
A conductive enclosure that blocks electromagnetic fields. Named after Michael Faraday. Used to prevent wireless signals from entering or leaving a space.
Use Cases
SCIF (Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility) rooms. Forensic device examination (prevent remote wipe). EMI-sensitive equipment protection. TEMPEST compliance.
TEMPEST Standards
NSA/DoD standard for preventing electromagnetic emanation eavesdropping. Computers emit RF that can be intercepted. TEMPEST-rated equipment and shielded rooms prevent this signal leakage.

Perimeter Security
The first barrier between the outside world and your facility.
Fencing
3-4 feet deters casual trespassers. 7 feet with barbed wire deters determined intruders. 8+ feet with razor wire is critical infrastructure standard. Anti-climb and anti-cut designs available.
Exterior Lighting
Illumination deters crime and enables camera effectiveness. Minimum 2 foot-candles at entrances. Motion-activated lights reduce power costs while still alerting to presence.
Bollards & Vehicle Barriers
Reinforced posts that prevent vehicle ramming attacks. K-rated bollards are crash-tested. Retractable bollards allow authorized vehicle access while blocking unauthorized entry.
Warning Signage
"No Trespassing," "Authorized Personnel Only," "Video Surveillance in Use." Legally required in many jurisdictions to prosecute trespassers. Signage is a deterrent control.
Slide 13 of 14
Physical Security Policies
Controls without policies are suggestions. Policies without enforcement are fiction.
Clean Desk Policy
No sensitive documents left on desks after hours. Screens locked when unattended. Whiteboards erased. Printers cleared. Reduces risk of shoulder surfing and after-hours theft.
Key Management Policy
Track all physical keys by serial number. Log issuance and return. Re-key locks when keys are lost or employees depart. Master key access limited to security manager.
Badge Policy
Badges must be visible at all times. Report lost badges within 1 hour. Deactivation happens immediately on termination. Photos on badges updated annually.
Incident Response -- Physical
Procedures for break-in, tailgating detection, lost equipment, and natural disaster. Chain of custody for evidence. Coordination with law enforcement. Post-incident review within 48 hours.
Compliance Frameworks
PCI-DSS Requirement 9: restrict physical access to cardholder data. HIPAA Physical Safeguards: facility access controls, workstation security, device and media controls. SOX Section 404: physical safeguards for financial records. Physical security is not optional -- it is auditable.

Physical Security -- Key Takeaways
If they can touch it, they own it. Every physical control buys time and creates a log entry.
1 Physical access controls use three factors: something you have (badge), something you know (PIN), something you are (biometrics).
2 Biometric accuracy is measured by FAR, FRR, and CER. Lower CER means a more accurate system.
3 Surveillance systems deter, detect, and document. IP cameras must be secured like any other network device.
4 Environmental controls (HVAC, fire suppression, UPS) protect against non-human threats that cause more downtime than most attacks.
5 Mantraps, turnstiles, and security culture are the three defenses against tailgating.
6 Faraday cages block RF signals. TEMPEST standards prevent electromagnetic eavesdropping. SCIFs use both.
7 Perimeter security follows layers: fencing, lighting, bollards, signage. Each layer adds deterrence and detection.
8 Physical security policies (clean desk, key management, badge policy) turn controls into enforceable standards auditable by PCI-DSS, HIPAA, and SOX.
