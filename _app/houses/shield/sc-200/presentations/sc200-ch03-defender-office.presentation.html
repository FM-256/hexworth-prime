Microsoft Defender for Office 365
Safe Attachments  •  Safe Links  •  Anti-Phishing  •  Threat Explorer  •  Attack Simulation
Microsoft Defender for Office 365 protects email and collaboration tools against phishing, malware, and business email compromise. It provides pre-delivery protection (Safe Attachments, Safe Links), post-delivery remediation (ZAP), and investigation tools (Threat Explorer, automated investigation) to secure the email attack surface.

Defender for Office 365 Overview
Defender for Office 365 adds advanced threat protection to Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, OneDrive for Business, and Microsoft Teams. It defends against zero-day malware, phishing, and business email compromise (BEC).
Plan 1 (P1)
Pre-delivery protection: Safe Attachments (sandbox detonation), Safe Links (URL wrapping and time-of-click verification), and anti-phishing policies with impersonation detection. Covers email, SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams.
Plan 2 (P2)
Everything in P1 plus post-breach tools: Threat Explorer (real-time detections), Threat Trackers, Attack Simulation Training, Automated Investigation and Response (AIR) for email, and Campaign Views for coordinated attack analysis.
Exchange Online Protection (EOP)
The baseline layer included with all Exchange Online mailboxes. Handles anti-spam filtering, basic anti-malware, connection filtering, and outbound spam control. Defender for Office 365 sits on top of EOP.
Protection Stack
Email flows through a layered stack: EOP edge protection first (connection filter, anti-malware, transport rules), then Defender for Office 365 (Safe Attachments detonation, Safe Links rewrite, anti-phishing AI). Layered defense, not single-pass.
Key Concept
EOP is always the first layer. Defender for Office 365 P1 adds advanced pre-delivery protection. P2 adds investigation, simulation, and automation tools. The SC-200 exam expects you to know which features belong to which tier.

Safe Attachments
Safe Attachments opens email attachments in a virtual sandbox (detonation chamber) to detect zero-day malware before the message reaches the user mailbox.
Policy Actions
Block -- quarantine the message. Replace -- deliver message but remove malicious attachment. Dynamic Delivery -- deliver message body immediately with a placeholder; replace placeholder with clean attachment after scan completes. Monitor -- deliver and log only.
Scope and Targets
Policies apply to specific users, groups, or domains. Safe Attachments also covers SharePoint Online, OneDrive for Business, and Microsoft Teams when enabled globally. Files uploaded to these services are scanned asynchronously.
Built-in Protection
Microsoft provides a built-in protection preset that applies Safe Attachments to all recipients by default. Custom policies take priority over the built-in preset. The Strict preset applies the most aggressive settings.
Exam Tip
Dynamic Delivery is the recommended action for most organizations. It delivers the message body immediately so users are not delayed, then swaps in the scanned attachment once detonation completes. No other action avoids delivery delay.

Safe Links
Safe Links rewrites URLs in inbound email and verifies them at time of click. If a URL becomes malicious after delivery, Safe Links blocks access when the user clicks it.
1URL Rewriting -- Safe Links rewrites URLs in email messages to route through the Microsoft filtering service. The rewritten URL starts with https://nam01.safelinks.protection.outlook.com. Original URL is preserved in a parameter.
2Time-of-Click Verification -- when the user clicks a rewritten link, Safe Links checks the URL reputation in real time. If the URL has been flagged as malicious since delivery, the user sees a warning page instead of the destination.
3URL Detonation -- for unknown URLs, Safe Links can trigger detonation (URL sandboxing) that follows the link and analyzes the landing page for credential harvesting forms, exploit kits, or malware downloads.
4Click-Through Tracking -- Safe Links logs every click: who clicked, when, which URL, and whether the click was blocked or allowed. This data feeds into Threat Explorer and advanced hunting tables.
Policy Settings
Do not rewrite URLs -- still scans at time of click but preserves the original URL in the message. Do not track user clicks -- disables click logging. Do not let users click through -- blocks users from dismissing the warning page.
Coverage Beyond Email
Safe Links also protects URLs in Microsoft Teams messages, Office desktop apps (Word, Excel, PowerPoint), and Office for the web. URLs in Teams chats are scanned at click time the same way email URLs are scanned.
Key Concept
Safe Links provides protection against weaponized URLs -- URLs that are clean at delivery time but become malicious later. Traditional gateway scanners only check at delivery. Safe Links checks again at the moment of click.

Anti-Phishing Policies
Anti-phishing policies in Defender for Office 365 use AI and machine learning to detect impersonation, spoofing, and business email compromise (BEC) attacks that bypass standard email authentication.
User Impersonation
Protects specific high-value users (CEO, CFO, HR director). Detects display name lookalikes and sender address variants. Up to 350 protected users per policy.
Domain Impersonation
Protects your organization domains and specified external domains. Detects typo-squatting and homoglyph attacks like contoso.com vs c0ntoso.com or contoso-inc.com.
Mailbox Intelligence
AI analyzes each user communication patterns -- who they email frequently, typical senders, expected domains. Flags messages from senders that break established patterns.
Spoof Intelligence
Detects senders spoofing your domain or partner domains. Uses DMARC, DKIM, and SPF results plus Microsoft proprietary signals. Spoof intelligence insight shows detected spoofing sources.
Advanced Thresholds
Phishing email threshold (1-4) controls sensitivity. Level 1 is Standard. Level 4 is Most Aggressive -- catches more phishing but may increase false positives. Set per policy.
Policy Actions
Actions per detection type: move to Junk Email folder, quarantine the message, redirect to a specific address, or delete the message. Quarantine with admin-only release is the strictest option.
Exam Tip
Mailbox intelligence is unique to Defender for Office 365. It uses per-user AI models to learn each user's communication graph. When impersonation protection and mailbox intelligence are enabled together, false positives drop significantly because the AI knows who each user normally corresponds with.

Preset Security Policies
Microsoft provides preset security policies that apply recommended configurations for EOP and Defender for Office 365 in a single click. These are the fastest way to deploy protection.
Standard Preset
Balanced protection suitable for most users. Applies Safe Attachments (Dynamic Delivery), Safe Links (URL rewrite enabled), anti-phishing (impersonation + mailbox intelligence enabled, threshold level 2), and anti-spam/anti-malware baseline settings.
Strict Preset
Aggressive protection for high-value targets (executives, finance). Higher phishing threshold (level 4), stricter quarantine policies, blocks user overrides, enables "do not let users click through" for Safe Links. Higher false-positive rate.
Built-in Protection
Automatically applies Safe Links and Safe Attachments to all recipients not already covered by a custom or preset policy. Lowest priority -- any custom policy overrides it. Cannot be disabled, only overridden.
1Priority order -- Strict preset takes priority over Standard preset, which takes priority over custom policies, which take priority over Built-in Protection. Higher-priority policies are evaluated first.
2Scope -- assign presets to specific users, groups, or domains. Use Standard for the entire organization and Strict for executive accounts and finance teams.
3Quarantine policies -- preset policies include quarantine notification settings. Standard sends quarantine notifications to users. Strict restricts release to admin-only.

Threat Explorer and Real-Time Detections
Threat Explorer is the investigation and hunting tool for email threats. It provides near-real-time visibility into all email flowing through the organization and the actions taken on each message.
Threat Explorer (P2)
Full investigation tool with 30 days of data. Pivot by sender, recipient, subject, delivery action, detection technology, URL, attachment hash, or campaign. Supports bulk remediation -- select messages and take action across all affected mailboxes.
Real-Time Detections (P1)
Subset of Threat Explorer available in P1. Shows recent threats detected by Safe Attachments and Safe Links. Limited to 7 days of data. Does not support bulk remediation or all pivot options.
Views
All Email -- every message. Malware -- messages with malware detections. Phish -- phishing detections. Content Malware -- malicious files in SharePoint/OneDrive/Teams. Each view supports filtering and export.
Email Timeline
Click any message to see its full lifecycle: delivery, detection, ZAP action, user clicks on URLs, admin remediation. Shows every event that happened to the message from arrival through current state.
Key Concept
Threat Explorer supports manual remediation actions: soft delete, hard delete, move to Junk, or move to Inbox. SOC analysts use it to clean up phishing campaigns that bypassed initial detection -- select all affected messages and remediate in bulk.

Zero-Hour Auto Purge (ZAP)
ZAP is the post-delivery protection mechanism. When Microsoft identifies a delivered message as malicious after the fact, ZAP automatically moves it out of the user mailbox retroactively.
ZAP for Malware
Quarantines messages with newly detected malware attachments. Works on unread and read messages. Always enabled -- cannot be disabled. Applies to Exchange Online mailboxes only (not on-premises).
ZAP for Phishing
Quarantines messages reclassified as phishing. Works on unread and read messages. Controlled by the anti-spam policy setting for high-confidence phishing. Default action is quarantine.
ZAP for Spam
Moves messages reclassified as spam to the Junk Email folder. Only works on unread messages -- if the user has already read the message, ZAP for spam does not move it. Controlled by anti-spam policy settings.
Important
ZAP does not work on messages in quarantine or messages where the user has already moved the message to a folder. ZAP for spam only targets unread messages, but ZAP for malware and phishing act on both read and unread messages.

Attack Simulation Training
Attack Simulation Training (P2) lets security teams run realistic phishing simulations against their own users to measure susceptibility and deliver targeted training to users who fail.
Simulation Types
Credential harvest, malware attachment, link in attachment, link to malware, drive-by URL. Each type mimics a real attack technique with customizable email templates and landing pages.
Payloads
Pre-built payloads from Microsoft or custom payloads. Payloads include the phishing email template, the landing page, and the training assignment. Target specific users, groups, or the entire organization.
Training Assignments
Users who interact with the simulation (click, submit credentials) are automatically assigned training modules. Training covers phishing awareness, reporting suspicious messages, and social engineering red flags.
Reporting
Dashboards show click rate, compromise rate, report rate, and training completion. Track improvements over time across multiple simulation campaigns. Compare results by department or role.
Automation
Simulation automations run on a schedule with randomized payloads and sending windows. Continuous simulation without manual campaign setup. Supports multiple concurrent automations.
Predicted Compromise
Machine learning predicts which users are most likely to be compromised based on simulation history, email patterns, and training completion. Prioritize high-risk users for additional training.
Exam Tip
Attack Simulation Training requires Defender for Office 365 Plan 2 or Microsoft 365 E5. Simulated phishing emails bypass Safe Links and Safe Attachments scanning so they reach users realistically. The Report Message button in Outlook is the correct user response.
Slide 10 of 12
Threat Trackers and Campaigns
Threat Trackers provide curated intelligence on active email threats targeting your organization. Campaign Views group related phishing and malware messages into coordinated attack campaigns.
Noteworthy Trackers
Microsoft-curated alerts about trending threats: new phishing kits, emerging malware families, exploit campaigns. Each tracker links to Threat Explorer with pre-built filters so you can immediately see if your organization is affected.
Tracked Queries
Save custom Threat Explorer queries as trackers. They run automatically and notify you when new matches appear. Use these to monitor specific threat indicators -- a sender domain, attachment hash, or URL pattern unique to a threat you are investigating.
Campaign Views
Microsoft automatically groups related malicious messages into campaigns. Each campaign shows: sender infrastructure, targeted recipients, delivery actions, detection technologies used, and timeline. Helps identify coordinated attacks across the organization.
Campaign Details
Campaign Views show the full scope of a coordinated attack: how many messages were sent, how many were blocked vs delivered, which users clicked, and what remediation actions were taken. Campaign detection uses machine learning to correlate messages by infrastructure, content, and timing.

Automated Investigation for Email
Automated Investigation and Response (AIR) for Defender for Office 365 automatically investigates email-based alerts and recommends or executes remediation actions across all affected mailboxes.
1Trigger -- AIR starts when: a user reports a phishing message, ZAP quarantines a delivered message, an admin triggers investigation from Threat Explorer, or a high-confidence phishing alert fires. Each trigger starts the automated investigation playbook.
2Scope Expansion -- AIR identifies all related messages: same sender, same URL, same attachment hash. It checks how many mailboxes received the same threat and whether any users clicked malicious URLs or opened attachments.
3Verdict -- each entity (message, URL, file) receives a verdict: Malicious, Suspicious, or Clean. Verdicts are based on detonation results, reputation signals, and the expanded investigation scope.
4Remediation -- AIR recommends actions: soft delete messages, block sender, block URL. With auto-remediation enabled, approved action types execute automatically. Pending actions appear in the Action Center for analyst review.
5Action Center -- all completed and pending AIR actions are tracked in the unified Action Center at security.microsoft.com. Analysts approve, reject, or undo remediation actions from a single queue.
Key Concept
When a user reports a phishing email using the Report Message button, AIR automatically investigates the reported message and all similar messages across the organization. This turns user reports into automated, organization-wide threat response.

Chapter 3 Summary
Key concepts you must know for the SC-200 exam.
1Safe Attachments -- sandbox detonation for email attachments. Dynamic Delivery is the recommended action (delivers body immediately, swaps in scanned attachment). Also covers SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams.
2Safe Links -- URL rewriting and time-of-click verification. Protects against weaponized URLs that become malicious after delivery. Covers email, Teams, and Office desktop apps.
3Anti-Phishing -- user impersonation, domain impersonation, mailbox intelligence (per-user AI), and spoof intelligence. Up to 350 protected users per policy. Phishing threshold levels 1-4.
4ZAP -- Zero-Hour Auto Purge retroactively removes malicious messages after delivery. ZAP for malware/phishing works on read and unread messages. ZAP for spam works only on unread messages.
5Threat Explorer -- investigation tool with 30 days of data (P2). Real-Time Detections is the P1 subset (7 days). Supports bulk remediation, email timeline, and pivot-based investigation.
6Attack Simulation Training -- run phishing simulations, measure click/compromise rates, assign training to users who fail. Simulation automations run on schedule. Requires P2 or E5 license.
7AIR for Email -- automated investigation triggered by user reports, ZAP actions, or phishing alerts. Expands scope to all related messages across the organization and recommends remediation in the Action Center.
