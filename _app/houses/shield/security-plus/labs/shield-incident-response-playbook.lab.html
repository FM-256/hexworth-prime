Incident Response Playbook

Walk a real ransomware breach through all six IR phases. Assign actions to the right phase, then prove you know the correct sequence.

Scenario: Ransomware on FileServer-01 Monday morning, 07:42. The SOC receives alerts from the SIEM: FileServer-01 is encrypting shares at high speed. A junior analyst confirms ransomware. The organization's IR plan is active. You are the IR lead. Your job is to execute the playbook correctly -- assigning each action to the phase where it actually belongs and rejecting actions that belong to a different phase.
How this works: For each IR phase below, check EVERY action that belongs to that phase. Leave unchecked any action that belongs to a different phase or is simply wrong. A phase passes only when your selection exactly matches the correct set. After all phases, answer the ordering challenge. Submit when ready.
Phase 1 -- Preparation
Actions that MUST exist BEFORE any incident occurs. Select all that belong here.
Phase 2 -- Detection & Analysis
Actions taken to confirm the incident, scope it, and preserve evidence. Select all that belong here.
Phase 3 -- Containment
Actions that LIMIT the spread of the incident without destroying forensic evidence. Select all that belong here.
Phase 4 -- Eradication
Actions that REMOVE the threat entirely. Select all that belong here.
Phase 5 -- Recovery
Actions that RESTORE systems to normal, verified operation. Select all that belong here.
Phase 6 -- Lessons Learned
Actions that happen AFTER the incident is closed. Select all that belong here.
Ordering Challenge
Confirm you know the correct sequence for the three middle phases.

Arrange the three phases below in the correct sequence.
Note: this lab uses a hybrid model aligned to CompTIA SY0-701's IR process, drawing on NIST SP 800-61. They agree on the ordering of these three phases but structure the surrounding steps differently -- NIST groups Containment, Eradication, and Recovery as a single "Containment, Eradication, and Recovery" stage while SY0-701 treats them as three distinct named phases.

Key rule: you must limit the spread before you can safely destroy it; you must destroy it before you can safely rebuild.
