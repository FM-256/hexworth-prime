DSL -- Digital Subscriber Line

Internet Over Phone Lines

How DSL Works

Uses existing telephone copper wiring to carry data. Voice and data travel on different frequencies simultaneously.

Key Limitation

Speed degrades with distance from the Central Office (CO). Beyond ~18,000 feet, performance drops significantly.

DSL Variants

Type Speed Symmetric?
ADSL Up to 24 Mbps down No (faster down)
SDSL Up to 2 Mbps Yes (same up/down)
VDSL Up to 100 Mbps No (faster down)

Asymmetric = download faster than upload. Good for consumers who mostly download. Symmetric = same both ways. Better for businesses hosting services.

Cable & Fiber Optic Internet

High-Speed Broadband

Cable Internet

  • Uses coaxial cable (same as cable TV)
  • DOCSIS standard (Data Over Cable)
  • DOCSIS 3.0: up to 1 Gbps down
  • DOCSIS 3.1: up to 10 Gbps down
  • Shared bandwidth with neighbors

Shared medium: Performance can degrade during peak hours when many users on the same node are active.

Fiber Optic (FTTP)

  • Fiber to the Premises -- direct fiber connection
  • Speeds: 1 Gbps to 10+ Gbps
  • Symmetrical upload/download common
  • Immune to electromagnetic interference
  • Lowest latency of all options
Variant Meaning
FTTP/FTTH Fiber to the Premises/Home
FTTC Fiber to the Curb (copper last mile)
FTTN Fiber to the Node (copper last mile)

Satellite & Cellular

Wireless WAN Technologies

Satellite Internet

  • Available virtually anywhere with sky visibility
  • Geostationary: ~600ms latency (36,000 km orbit)
  • LEO (Starlink): ~20-40ms latency (550 km orbit)
  • Speeds: 12-300+ Mbps depending on service
  • Weather-sensitive -- rain fade, snow

Cellular Data (4G LTE / 5G)

Generation Speed Latency
3G Up to 42 Mbps ~100ms
4G LTE Up to 300 Mbps ~30-50ms
4G LTE-A Up to 1 Gbps ~20-30ms
5G Up to 10+ Gbps ~1-10ms

5G Bands

  • Low-band: Wide coverage, moderate speed
  • Mid-band: Balance of speed and coverage
  • mmWave: Extreme speed, very short range

Legacy & Dedicated Connections

Dial-Up, ISDN, T-Lines, and Metro Ethernet

Legacy Technologies

Dial-Up
Analog modem over phone line. Max 56 Kbps. Ties up the phone line. 56 Kbps
ISDN
Digital over phone lines. BRI: 2x64 Kbps (128 Kbps). PRI: 23x64 Kbps (T1). Largely replaced by DSL. 128 Kbps-1.5 Mbps

These technologies are largely obsolete but still appear on certification exams. Know their speeds and limitations.

Dedicated Lines

T1 Line
24 channels x 64 Kbps = 1.544 Mbps. Point-to-point dedicated circuit. Symmetrical. 1.544 Mbps
T3 (DS3) Line
672 channels = 44.736 Mbps. Equivalent to 28 T1s. Used by large organizations. 44.7 Mbps
Metro Ethernet
Ethernet service delivered by carrier in a metro area. Scalable from 10 Mbps to 10 Gbps. Familiar Ethernet interface. 10 Mbps-10 Gbps

Network Types: LAN & WAN

Local vs Wide Area

LAN -- Local Area Network

  • Covers a single building or campus
  • Typically Ethernet (wired) or Wi-Fi (wireless)
  • High speed: 1 Gbps to 10+ Gbps
  • Low latency (under 1ms)
  • Owned and managed by the organization

WAN -- Wide Area Network

  • Connects geographically separate LANs
  • Can span cities, countries, or continents
  • Typically leased from service providers
  • Lower speed than LAN (varies by service)
  • Higher latency due to distance
  • The internet is the largest WAN
Slide 8 of 14

MAN, PAN, and SAN

Other Network Scopes

MAN

Metropolitan Area Network

  • Covers a city or metro area
  • Larger than LAN, smaller than WAN
  • Often fiber-based (metro Ethernet)
  • Connects multiple buildings in a city
  • Example: city-wide government network

PAN

Personal Area Network

  • Covers a few meters around a person
  • Bluetooth (typical range ~10m)
  • USB connections
  • NFC (Near Field Communication)
  • Example: phone to earbuds, smartwatch

SAN

Storage Area Network

  • Dedicated high-speed network for storage
  • Block-level access (not file-level)
  • Uses Fibre Channel or iSCSI
  • Appears as local disk to servers
  • Enterprise data centers only

Quick Scope Reference

PAN: ~10m → LAN: Building → MAN: City → WAN: Global

WLAN & CAN

Wireless and Campus Networks

WLAN -- Wireless LAN

A LAN that uses Wi-Fi (802.11) instead of cables.

  • Based on IEEE 802.11 standards
  • Access Points (APs) bridge wireless to wired
  • Range: ~30-100m indoors
  • Speeds: up to multi-Gbps (Wi-Fi 6/6E)
Standard Name Freq Speed
802.11n Wi-Fi 4 2.4/5 GHz 600 Mbps
802.11ac Wi-Fi 5 5 GHz 6.9 Gbps
802.11ax Wi-Fi 6 2.4/5/6 GHz 9.6 Gbps

CAN -- Campus Area Network

Interconnects multiple LANs within a limited geographic area like a university campus or corporate campus.

  • Multiple buildings, single organization
  • High-speed backbone (fiber between buildings)
  • Centrally managed switching/routing
  • Larger than LAN, smaller than MAN

Exam Tip

WLAN is a type of LAN, not a separate category. It refers to the wireless implementation of a local area network. CAN is sometimes called a campus network or enterprise network.

Peer-to-Peer vs Client-Server

Network Architecture Models

Peer-to-Peer (P2P)

  • Every device is both client and server
  • No central authority
  • Simple, low cost
  • Poor scalability and security
  • Best for: small offices, home, file sharing

Client-Server

  • Dedicated server provides resources
  • Centralized management and security
  • Scales to thousands of users
  • Higher cost, requires IT staff
  • Best for: enterprise, schools, data centers

Intranet, Extranet, and Internet

Scope of Access

Intranet

  • Private network, internal only
  • Company resources, wikis, portals
  • Requires authentication
  • Not accessible from outside

Extranet

  • Extended intranet for partners
  • Controlled external access
  • Vendor portals, B2B services
  • VPN or dedicated link access

Internet

  • Global public network
  • Accessible to everyone
  • Uses TCP/IP protocol suite
  • The world's largest WAN

VPN -- Virtual Private Network

Secure Tunnels Over Public Networks

What a VPN Does

Creates an encrypted tunnel across an untrusted network (usually the internet), making it appear as though the remote device is on the local network.

VPN Protocols

  • IPSec: Industry standard, works at Layer 3
  • SSL/TLS VPN: Browser-based, uses port 443
  • WireGuard: Modern, lightweight, fast
  • L2TP/IPSec: Layer 2 tunneling + encryption

VPN Types

Type Use Case Connection
Remote Access Individual user to office Client software on laptop/phone
Site-to-Site Office to office Router/firewall at each end
Client-to-Site Remote workers VPN client + concentrator

Split Tunnel vs Full Tunnel

  • Full Tunnel: All traffic goes through VPN (more secure)
  • Split Tunnel: Only corporate traffic goes through VPN (better performance for personal browsing)

Modern Connectivity Trends

SD-WAN, SASE, and Cloud Networking

SD-WAN

Software-Defined WAN -- uses software to manage and optimize WAN connections across multiple transport types.

  • Combines MPLS, broadband, LTE, fiber
  • Intelligent path selection per application
  • Centralized management dashboard
  • Reduces cost vs dedicated MPLS circuits
  • Built-in encryption between sites

Why It Matters

With cloud adoption (Azure, AWS, M365), traditional hub-and-spoke WAN designs force traffic through a central data center -- creating bottlenecks. SD-WAN enables direct cloud access from branch offices.

SASE (Secure Access Service Edge)

Combines SD-WAN with cloud-delivered security (firewall, CASB, ZTNA). Pronounced "sassy." Emerging standard for distributed workforce networking.

Exam Tip: SD-WAN is increasingly featured on Network+ and CCNA exams. Know that it provides application-aware routing, centralized management, and multi-transport support.

Week 4 Summary -- Connections & Network Types

Key Takeaways

Connection Types

  • DSL: phone line, distance-limited
  • Cable: coax, shared bandwidth
  • Fiber: fastest, lowest latency
  • Satellite: high latency, wide coverage
  • Cellular: mobile, 5G is next-gen
  • T1 = 1.544 Mbps, T3 = 44.7 Mbps

Network Scopes

  • PAN: personal (~10m, Bluetooth)
  • LAN: building/campus
  • MAN: city/metro area
  • WAN: global (internet = biggest)
  • SAN: dedicated storage network

Architecture

  • P2P: simple, no server, small scale
  • Client-Server: centralized, scalable
  • Intranet: internal only
  • Extranet: partners/vendors
  • VPN: encrypted tunnel
  • SD-WAN: software-defined WAN
