Frequency Bands: 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz
Different frequencies offer different trade-offs between range, speed, and interference.
Rule of thumb: Lower frequency = longer range but slower and more crowded. Higher frequency = faster but shorter range. Dual-band and tri-band routers support multiple bands simultaneously.

Channels & Interference
Choosing the right channel avoids co-channel interference and maximizes throughput.
Co-channelSame channel used by multiple APs. Devices must take turns transmitting. Reduces throughput by half or more.
Adjacent-channelOverlapping but different channels (e.g., Ch 4 and Ch 6). Causes unpredictable interference and is harder to manage.
Best practiceUse a Wi-Fi analyzer to survey the environment. Pick the least-used non-overlapping channel. On 5 GHz, channels rarely overlap due to the large number available.
Slide 5 of 13
MIMO & Beamforming
Technologies that make modern Wi-Fi fast and reliable.
MIMO — Multiple-Input Multiple-Output
Uses multiple antennas on both the AP and client to send/receive multiple data streams simultaneously. More streams = more throughput. SU-MIMO: One device at a time (Wi-Fi 4/5). MU-MIMO: Multiple devices simultaneously (Wi-Fi 5 downlink, Wi-Fi 6 both directions). Notation like "4x4" means 4 transmit, 4 receive antennas.
Beamforming
Instead of broadcasting signal in all directions equally, beamforming focuses the signal toward the specific location of each connected client. The AP uses phase differences across its antennas to steer the beam. Result: stronger signal, better range, less wasted energy. Standard in Wi-Fi 5 and 6.

Wireless Security Protocols
Encryption protects data in transit over the air. Not all protocols are created equal.
WEP
Broken
RC4 encryption with static keys. Cracked in minutes with aircrack-ng. Never use.
WPA (TKIP)
Deprecated
Improved key rotation over WEP but TKIP has known weaknesses. Avoid on new networks.
WPA2 (AES)
Current Standard
AES-CCMP encryption. Strong. Required minimum for any network. Personal (PSK) or Enterprise (RADIUS).
WPA3
Recommended
SAE handshake. Forward secrecy. Resistant to offline dictionary attacks. Individual data encryption.
Personal vs Enterprise
Personal (PSK): Everyone shares the same Wi-Fi password. Simple for SOHO. Enterprise (802.1X): Each user authenticates with unique credentials via a RADIUS server. Required for corporate networks. Provides individual session keys and accountability.

Wi-Fi 6: OFDMA & BSS Coloring
Two key Wi-Fi 6 technologies that solve the "too many devices" problem.
OFDMA — Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access
Divides a single channel into smaller sub-channels called Resource Units (RUs). Multiple devices can transmit/receive simultaneously on different RUs within the same channel. Dramatically improves efficiency in dense environments (classrooms, stadiums, offices).
BSS Coloring
Each BSS (Basic Service Set, i.e., each AP) gets a "color" (a number 1-63). Devices can ignore transmissions from neighboring APs with a different color instead of waiting for them to finish. Reduces contention and improves throughput in areas with many overlapping networks.

Bluetooth
Short-range wireless for personal area networks (PANs). Not a Wi-Fi replacement.
StandardIEEE 802.15.1. Current version: Bluetooth 5.3. Operates at 2.4 GHz.
RangeClass 2: ~10m (most devices). Class 1: ~100m (industrial). BLE (Low Energy): up to 400m (line of sight).
SpeedBluetooth Classic: 1-3 Mbps. BLE: optimized for low data rate, low power (IoT sensors, wearables).
Use CasesHeadphones, keyboards, mice, smartwatches, fitness trackers, car audio, IoT sensors, file transfer (AirDrop/Nearby Share).
PairingDevices must pair before communicating. Pairing exchanges keys for encrypted communication. Piconet: up to 7 active devices with one master.
Slide 9 of 13
NFC — Near Field Communication
Extremely short range. Tap-to-connect technology.
Frequency13.56 MHz. Range: under 4 cm (intentionally very short for security).
Speed106-424 Kbps. Tiny data transfers — payment tokens, URLs, contact cards.
Use CasesContactless payments (Apple Pay, Google Pay), transit cards, badge access, Bluetooth pairing shortcuts, smart tags.
SecurityShort range is itself a security feature. Payment transactions are tokenized and encrypted. No persistent connection.
Slide 10 of 13
Cellular: 4G LTE & 5G
Wide-area wireless networking provided by carriers. Your phone uses this when Wi-Fi is unavailable.
4G LTE
Long-Term Evolution. Typical speeds: 10-50 Mbps download, 5-20 Mbps upload. Latency: 30-50ms. Widespread coverage. Used for mobile hotspots, IoT devices, backup internet connections. Supports VoLTE (Voice over LTE) for HD voice calls.
5G
Three bands: Low-band (600-900 MHz, wide coverage, moderate speed), Mid-band (2.5-3.7 GHz, balance), mmWave (24-47 GHz, ultra-fast but very short range). Peak speeds: 1-10 Gbps. Latency: under 10ms. Enables real-time applications, massive IoT, and fixed wireless access.
Fixed Wireless Access (FWA): 5G as a home/office internet replacement. A 5G modem on the roof connects to a nearby cell tower, providing broadband without running cable. Increasingly common in rural areas where fiber is unavailable.

Satellite & Infrared
Niche wireless technologies with specific use cases.
Satellite Internet
Traditional (GEO): Geostationary orbit at 35,786 km. High latency (500-700ms). Speeds: 10-25 Mbps. Covers remote areas where no other option exists. Provider: Viasat, HughesNet. LEO (Low Earth Orbit): Starlink operates at 550 km. Latency: 20-40ms. Speeds: 50-200 Mbps. Game-changer for rural connectivity.
Infrared (IR)
Line-of-sight only. Cannot pass through walls. Very short range (a few meters). Used in TV remote controls, some older device-to-device transfers. IrDA standard (now obsolete for networking). Largely replaced by Bluetooth and NFC for device communication.
Comparison: Satellite provides coverage anywhere on Earth but has latency and cost trade-offs. Infrared is line-of-sight only and essentially dead for networking. Both are exam topics but rare in day-to-day IT work.

Wireless Technologies Compared
Range, speed, and use case at a glance.
Wi-Fi (802.11)
Range: 30-100m indoor. Speed: Up to 9.6 Gbps. Use: LAN connectivity, internet access. The standard for all indoor networking.
Bluetooth
Range: 10-100m. Speed: 1-3 Mbps. Use: PAN devices — headphones, keyboards, wearables, IoT sensors.
NFC
Range: Under 4 cm. Speed: 424 Kbps. Use: Contactless payments, badge access, quick pairing.
Cellular (4G/5G)
Range: 1-30+ km. Speed: 10 Mbps to 10 Gbps. Use: Mobile internet, hotspots, IoT, fixed wireless.
Satellite
Range: Global. Speed: 10-200 Mbps. Use: Remote/rural areas, maritime, aviation. LEO is closing the gap.
Infrared
Range: A few meters (line-of-sight). Speed: Up to 4 Mbps. Use: Remote controls. Obsolete for networking.

Wireless Protocols: Solved
Key Takeaways
1802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) and 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) are the current standards. Wi-Fi 6 adds OFDMA and BSS Coloring for dense environments.
22.4 GHz = longer range, more interference, 3 channels. 5 GHz = faster, 24 channels. 6 GHz = fastest, Wi-Fi 6E only.
3Use channels 1, 6, 11 on 2.4 GHz to avoid overlap. Use a Wi-Fi analyzer to survey before deploying.
4WPA2-AES minimum. WPA3 recommended. WEP is broken. Enterprise networks use 802.1X with RADIUS.
5Bluetooth (PAN, 10m), NFC (<4cm, payments), Cellular (WAN, 4G/5G), Satellite (global, high latency unless LEO).
6MIMO = multiple simultaneous streams. Beamforming = focused signal toward clients. Both are standard in Wi-Fi 5+.
Back to the conference room: the AP supports only 2.4 GHz (802.11n) — slow and congested. Solution: deploy a Wi-Fi 6 AP on 5 GHz with MU-MIMO and beamforming. The video calls now run smoothly because each device gets its own data stream via OFDMA.
