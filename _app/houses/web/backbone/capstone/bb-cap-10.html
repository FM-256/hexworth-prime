Scenario: Meridian Financial Group Network Redesign

Meridian Financial Group is a mid-market financial services firm with 2,800 users across four sites. They are migrating from a legacy MPLS-only WAN to a hybrid SD-WAN + MPLS architecture with AWS cloud integration. You are the lead network architect.

HQ — Boston
  • 1,200 users
  • Primary DC collocated
  • Dual 10G fiber (ISP-A + ISP-B)
  • MPLS circuit: 500Mbps
  • Cisco Nexus 9300 fabric
Branch 1 — NYC
  • 800 users
  • Trading floor — latency-critical
  • Dual ISP: 1G fiber + 500M cable
  • MPLS circuit: 200Mbps
  • Cisco Catalyst 9300 campus
Branch 2 — Chicago
  • 600 users
  • Standard office
  • Dual ISP: 500M fiber x2
  • MPLS circuit: 100Mbps
  • Cisco Catalyst 9200
DR Site — Ashburn VA
  • Colocation facility
  • No users — infrastructure only
  • Dual 10G (colocation provider)
  • Cisco Nexus 9300 (DR fabric)
AWS Cloud
  • VPC-PROD-EAST: 10.128.0.0/16
  • VPC-DR: 10.129.0.0/16
  • Direct Connect: 2x 10G DX
  • Transit Gateway for multi-VPC
SaaS/Cloud Apps
  • Microsoft 365 (Exchange, Teams)
  • Salesforce CRM
  • Bloomberg Terminal via private MPLS
  • Internal apps in AWS

Requirements Analysis

Business Requirements

BR-001 Financial regulatory compliance — all inter-site traffic must be encrypted; no split tunneling for trading floor traffic. MUST
BR-002 WAN cost reduction — leverage internet bandwidth for SaaS; reduce MPLS spend by 40% within 18 months. SHOULD
BR-003 Business continuity — RPO 4 hours, RTO 2 hours for critical financial applications. MUST
BR-004 Zero planned downtime for trading floor systems during migration. NYSE hours are 09:30-16:00 ET. MUST

Technical Requirements

TR-001 WAN availability 99.99% for HQ and NYC; 99.9% for Chicago. MUST
TR-002 Trading application latency HQ-NYC must not exceed 5ms RTT under normal conditions. MUST
TR-003 QoS differentiation at minimum four traffic classes: trading/real-time, voice, business, default. MUST
TR-004 Cloud integration AWS Direct Connect for production; SD-WAN IPsec as failover path. MUST
TR-005 VXLAN/EVPN at HQ DC for workload mobility between HQ and DR site. SHOULD
TR-006 IPv6 readiness — dual-stack capable on campus access layer. MAY

HLD Design Decisions

WAN Architecture Decision

Selected: Hybrid SD-WAN (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN) + Retained MPLS for trading/Bloomberg

  • MPLS retained on HQ-NYC path for Bloomberg private circuit and trading traffic (TR-002 latency requirement; TR-001 deterministic SLA)
  • SD-WAN deployed at all sites for internet-bound SaaS (M365, Salesforce) via Cloud OnRamp
  • IPsec over internet provides backup path for all non-Bloomberg traffic
  • MPLS-only alternative rejected: does not address BR-002 cost reduction target
  • SD-WAN-only alternative rejected: cannot guarantee Bloomberg private circuit requirements

Campus Routing Architecture Decision

Selected: IS-IS backbone at HQ/DR DC; OSPF area per campus site; BGP at WAN edge

  • HQ/DR DC: IS-IS with Segment Routing MPLS for TI-LFA (100% FRR coverage)
  • Campus sites: single OSPF area (stub or totally-stub); default route from vEdge SD-WAN
  • WAN edge: eBGP to SD-WAN fabric; eBGP to MPLS provider; eBGP to AWS DX (AS 7224)
  • Route policy: MPLS preferred (local-pref 200) for defined traffic; internet breakout via SD-WAN for SaaS

IP Addressing Schema Design

  • 10.0.0.0/8 supernet; /12 per region
  • HQ: 10.0.0.0/16 (DC) + 10.1.0.0/16 (campus)
  • NYC: 10.2.0.0/16; Chicago: 10.3.0.0/16; DR: 10.4.0.0/16
  • AWS VPCs: 10.128.0.0/12 block
  • Loopbacks: 10.244.X.Y/32 — X=site#, Y=device# (routers from .1, switches from .100)
  • P2P links: /31 from 10.240.0.0/14; VLAN IDs embedded in third octet
  • Trading VLAN 10 (10.X.10.0/24), Voice VLAN 20 (10.X.20.0/24), Data VLAN 30 (10.X.30.0/24)

QoS Policy Framework Design

  • 4-class model: TC1 Trading/Real-Time (DSCP CS6/EF, LLQ, strict priority), TC2 Voice (DSCP EF, CBWFQ 20%), TC3 Business (DSCP AF31-AF43, CBWFQ 30%), TC4 Default (BE, remaining BW)
  • Bloomberg traffic: classify by DSCP CS6 (vendor marks at source); never queue behind any lower class
  • SD-WAN AAR policy: Trading/Voice SLA class max 5ms/1ms latency; AAR triggers on violation
  • M365 Teams: DSCP EF for voice (port 3478); AF41 for video; CS3 for signaling

High Availability Architecture Design

  • HQ Core: dual Nexus 9500 in vPC (active/active); VSS eliminated in favor of vPC for pure L3 spine
  • Distribution: StackWise Virtual pairs; L3 boundary at distribution; HSRP per VLAN
  • WAN edge: dual vEdge routers (active/active SD-WAN, ECMP); NSF/SSO on both
  • AWS DX: two connections at separate DX locations (IAD1 + IAD2) — 99.99% SLA
  • DR: VXLAN/EVPN DCI between HQ and DR Nexus fabrics; BGP EVPN control plane
  • Convergence: BFD 100ms + TI-LFA for sub-50ms DC fabric protection; BFD 300ms on WAN edge

Migration Plan Summary

Wave Scope Duration Risk Rollback Method
Wave 0 — Lab Lab environment: full topology simulation, all cutover procedures rehearsed 4 weeks None (lab) N/A
Wave 1 — Chicago Pilot Chicago SD-WAN deployment; MPLS kept active; Cloud OnRamp for SaaS 2 weeks Low (no trading floor) Shift local-pref back to MPLS path; SD-WAN idle
Wave 2 — HQ Campus HQ SD-WAN; campus IS-IS migration; AWS DX activation 3 weeks Medium (HQ users) IS-IS distance raised; OSPF re-preferred; DX backup VPN active
Wave 3 — NYC Trading NYC SD-WAN (non-Bloomberg); VXLAN/EVPN DCI between HQ-DR 2 weeks High (trading floor) NYC migration scheduled for weekend; 30-min rollback window
Wave 4 — Decommission Remove legacy OSPF configs; decommission old MPLS circuits (except Bloomberg) 4 weeks Low (new infra stable) Circuit kept on 30-day notice period
Bloomberg Circuit Preserved

The Bloomberg private MPLS circuit is never part of the SD-WAN migration. Bloomberg Terminal connectivity requires a dedicated private circuit per Bloomberg contract requirements. This circuit is retained as-is and excluded from all migration waves. Document this explicitly in the migration HLD to prevent scope creep.
