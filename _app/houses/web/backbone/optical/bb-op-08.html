The OTDR: Optical Time-Domain Reflectometer

The OTDR is the fundamental diagnostic tool for fiber optic plant. It works on the same principle as radar: a short powerful light pulse is launched into the fiber, and the instrument measures the backscattered light returning over time. Since the speed of light in fiber is known (approximately 2x10^8 m/s in glass), time translates directly to distance. Reflections and losses at discrete events (connectors, splices, bends, breaks) appear as distinct features on the OTDR trace.

Fresnel Reflection Events Sharp reflective spikes on the trace caused by fiber end-faces (connectors, mechanical splices, fiber breaks). The height of the spike indicates return loss. APC connectors (8-degree angle) appear as small or invisible spikes because the reflection is directed away from the fiber core.
Rayleigh Backscatter The continuously decreasing background signal between events -- caused by tiny density fluctuations in the glass. The slope of this line gives the fiber attenuation coefficient (dB/km). A change in slope indicates a fiber spliced from a different manufacturing lot with slightly different scatter characteristics.
OTDR Trace Interpretation -- Example Annotated
Distance (m)    Event                      dB Level

850m            Fusion splice               -0.08 dB  [small dip, no spike]

2400m           Macro-bend event            -3.2 dB   [sudden step loss -- investigate]
2540m           Far-end reflection          visible   [end of fiber confirmed]

Total measured loss: 0.5 + 0.08 + 0.45 + 3.2 = 4.23 dB
3.2 dB at 2400m is a PROBLEM -- exceeds any splice or connector spec.
This is a tight bend, crush, or partial break. Locate and remediate.

OTDR dead zones limit the ability to resolve closely spaced events. The event dead zone (typically 2-5 meters) is the distance after a reflection before the instrument can detect another event. The attenuation dead zone (typically 5-20 meters) is the distance required before loss measurements are accurate again. These dead zones require a launch cable between the OTDR and the fiber under test to move the near-end connector reflection out of the dead zone.

Optical Power Meter and Light Source

The simplest and most reliable insertion loss measurement uses a stabilized light source at one end and a calibrated optical power meter at the other. This two-ended test provides a direct, accurate loss measurement and is required for fiber certification per TIA-568 and ISO/IEC 11801 standards.

Test MethodDescriptionAccuracyLimitation
Method A (1 reference cord) Calibrate with launch cord connected to meter; measure total with launch cord + cable + receive cord High; includes all connector pairs TIA recommends for most applications
Method B (2 reference cords) Calibrate with two reference cords mated together; measurement excludes one connector pair at each end Medium; excludes some connectors from result May pass specs while end-connector is bad
OTDR (single-ended) Launch from one end, measure reflective events and attenuation slope Medium; cannot resolve events within dead zone; directional loss measurement Must test bidirectionally and average for accurate loss; near-end dead zone

Bidirectional OTDR and Ghost Events

OTDR measurements can show "ghost" events -- false reflections that appear at exactly twice the distance of a real high-reflection event. This is a reflection that bounced off the first connector, traveled back to the launch, reflected off the OTDR connector, and made a second trip. The ghost event has exactly double the distance of the real connector. Always test bidirectionally and average the results for accurate loss per TIA-568-C.1 requirements. Ghost events typically disappear on the reverse trace.

Optical Spectrum Analyzer (OSA)

An OSA is the diagnostic instrument for DWDM systems. It sweeps across an optical wavelength range and measures the power level at each wavelength with high resolution, producing a power vs. wavelength spectrum plot. An OSA can identify individual DWDM channels, measure their power levels, OSNR, channel spacing, and center wavelength accuracy.

Channel Power Measurement Each DWDM channel should be within a specified power range at the output of each amplifier site. Channels outside this range indicate transponder launch power misconfiguration, EDFA gain tilt (where channels at different wavelengths receive different amplification), or filter insertion loss variations.
OSNR Measurement OSA measures the signal power in-band and the ASE noise between channels to calculate per-channel OSNR. The industry standard is OSNR measured in a 0.1 nm (12.5 GHz) reference bandwidth. OSNR below the minimum required for the modulation format causes uncorrectable FEC errors.

Modern OSA instruments achieve wavelength resolution of 0.02 nm or better -- sufficient to resolve individual 50 GHz DWDM channels and verify center wavelength accuracy. Field-deployed OSAs for DWDM installation and troubleshooting are typically smaller, lower-resolution units that trade some accuracy for portability. Lab-grade OSAs used for transceiver characterization achieve 0.002 nm resolution.

BERT and End-to-End Verification

A Bit Error Rate Tester (BERT) verifies the digital performance of a fiber link by sending a known pseudo-random bit sequence (PRBS) at the line rate and comparing the received sequence to the transmitted pattern. Any bit received in error is counted. The Bit Error Rate (BER) is the ratio of errored bits to total transmitted bits.

For a 10G link, a BER of 10^-12 means one error in one trillion bits -- approximately one error every 100 seconds. Most SONET/SDH and OTN standards require pre-FEC BER better than 10^-4 and post-FEC BER of 10^-15 or better. BERT testing is performed after installation to verify the optical link meets its performance specification before service turn-up.

PRBS Patterns and Standards

Common PRBS patterns: PRBS-7 (127 bits), PRBS-15 (32,767 bits), PRBS-23 (8.3 million bits), PRBS-31 (2.15 billion bits). Higher-order PRBS patterns are more representative of real traffic and better stress-test clock recovery and dispersion tolerance. IEEE 802.3 Ethernet compliance testing specifies PRBS-31. OTN performance testing uses PRBS-31 at the full ODU line rate.

Practice Questions

  1. An OTDR trace of a 3 km fiber run shows: 0.4 dB event at 0m (launch connector), 0.3 dB event at 2850m (far-end connector), and a 2.5 dB step loss at 1200m. The spec for this run is 0.5 dB/km attenuation and 0.5 dB max per connector. Calculate the total expected loss at spec, identify which component is out of spec, and describe three possible causes for the anomalous 2.5 dB event at 1200m.
  2. You are using an OSA to commission a new 16-channel DWDM system. Channel 4 shows OSNR of 18 dB while all other channels show 28-30 dB OSNR. List three possible causes for this single-channel OSNR anomaly and describe how you would use the OSA and OTDR together to localize the root cause.
  3. A BERT test on a newly installed 100G coherent link shows a pre-FEC BER of 1.2 x 10^-3. Post-FEC BER is 10^-15. Is this link in spec? What does this BER indicate about the optical link quality, and at what pre-FEC BER threshold would you start investigating link margin issues even if post-FEC is clean?
  4. Explain the launch cord requirement for OTDR testing. Why is a 100-200 meter launch cord necessary, and what minimum length should be used for testing a 10m patch cord in a data center? What failure mode occurs without the launch cord?

Key Takeaways

  • OTDR measures fiber plant by timing backscattered light; events (connectors, splices, bends) appear as features on the trace
  • Ghost events appear at double the distance of high-reflection connectors; eliminated by bidirectional OTDR measurement
  • Power meter/light source is the definitive insertion loss measurement; always the final certification test
  • OSA measures per-channel power and OSNR in DWDM systems; essential for commissioning and troubleshooting
  • OSNR below minimum for modulation format causes FEC errors that compound faster than the FEC can correct
  • BERT injects known bit patterns to measure end-to-end BER; pre-FEC and post-FEC BER both matter
  • Launch cord is mandatory for OTDR testing -- moves near-end reflection outside the dead zone
