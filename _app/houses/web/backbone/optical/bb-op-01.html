How Light Carries Data

Optical fiber transmits data as pulses of light through a glass or plastic core. The core is surrounded by cladding material with a lower refractive index, creating total internal reflection -- the phenomenon that confines the light to the core as it travels the length of the fiber. A protective coating and outer jacket complete the cable structure.

Light sources are either LEDs (lower cost, used in multimode short-reach applications) or laser diodes (used in single-mode long-haul systems). The wavelength of light used determines which fiber type is appropriate and what amplification technology applies. Common wavelengths are 850 nm (multimode short reach), 1310 nm (single-mode medium reach), and 1550 nm (single-mode long-haul and DWDM).

Why Wavelength Matters

Silica glass fiber has minimum attenuation (~0.2 dB/km) at 1550 nm and minimum dispersion at 1310 nm. DWDM systems exploit the 1550 nm low-loss window to carry 80+ wavelengths simultaneously. Erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs) operate at 1550 nm, making them the natural pairing for long-haul DWDM transport.

Single-Mode vs Multimode Fiber

Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) Core diameter: 8-10 microns. Supports only one propagation mode. Eliminates modal dispersion. Used for distances from 10 km to transoceanic with amplification. Required for DWDM. ITU-T G.652, G.654, G.655 standards.
Multimode Fiber (MMF) Core diameter: 50 or 62.5 microns. Multiple propagation modes cause modal dispersion, limiting distance and bandwidth. Used in data center short-reach (up to ~400m at 100G). OM3, OM4, OM5 grades.

Connector Types

Fiber connectors terminate the fiber end for mating to transceivers or patch panels. Connector quality directly determines insertion loss at each mating point. Physical contact (PC), ultra-physical contact (UPC), and angled physical contact (APC) describe the end-face geometry -- APC connectors have an 8-degree angle that reflects any back-reflection away from the fiber core, achieving return loss greater than 60 dB, required for high-coherence systems.

Contamination: Primary Cause of Fiber Failures

The number one cause of optical connectivity failures is contaminated connectors. A single dust particle on a 9-micron SMF core completely blocks transmission. Always inspect with a fiber scope (or digital inspection probe) before mating. Clean with appropriate fiber optic cleaning tools -- dry clean first, wet-dry if contaminated. Never blow on a connector with breath (introduces moisture and oils).

Decibel Math and Loss Budgets

Optical power and loss are measured in decibels (dB) and dBm. Understanding dB math is non-negotiable for optical engineering.

dBm (absolute power) Power relative to 1 milliwatt. 0 dBm = 1 mW. -10 dBm = 0.1 mW. +3 dBm = 2 mW. Transmit power and receive sensitivity are expressed in dBm.
dB (relative loss or gain) A ratio. 3 dB = half the power. 10 dB = 1/10th the power. Losses are additive in dB: 2 connectors at 0.5 dB each = 1 dB total connector loss.

A loss budget calculates whether enough optical power reaches the receiver after accounting for all losses along the path. The formula is straightforward:

Optical Link Loss Budget Calculation
--- Link: IDF-3 to MDF-1 (SMF, 1310nm, 1G SFP) ---

Transmit power (Tx):            -3 dBm
Receiver sensitivity (min Rx):  -23 dBm
Available loss budget:           20 dB

--- Loss contributors ---
Fiber run:      850m x 0.4 dB/km  = 0.34 dB
Connectors:     4 connectors x 0.5 dB = 2.0 dB
Splices:        2 splices x 0.1 dB = 0.2 dB
Patch panels:   2 panels x 0.5 dB = 1.0 dB
                              --------
Total loss:                        3.54 dB

System margin:  20 - 3.54 = 16.46 dB  [PASS -- healthy margin]

--- Recommended minimum margin: 3 dB for aging and future repairs ---

Typical loss values for planning: SMF at 1310 nm is 0.4 dB/km; at 1550 nm it drops to 0.2 dB/km. MMF at 850 nm is 3.5 dB/km. Each connector mating adds 0.3-0.75 dB. Fusion splices add 0.02-0.1 dB. Mechanical splices add 0.1-0.5 dB and should be avoided in permanent plant.

Practice Questions

  1. A 10G SMF link uses a transceiver with Tx power of -1 dBm and minimum receive sensitivity of -21 dBm. The fiber run is 12 km at 0.4 dB/km. There are 6 LC connectors at 0.5 dB each and 3 fusion splices at 0.08 dB each. Calculate the total loss and determine whether the link will work with acceptable margin.
  2. You are deploying 400G QSFP-DD transceivers in a data center. The cable trays are 150 meters long. Should you use OM4 or OS2 fiber? Justify your choice by referencing the appropriate distance spec.
  3. A field technician reports a fiber link has intermittent errors. The OTDR shows 4 dB of loss at a single point 200m from the IDF. Name three likely causes of this localized loss event.
  4. Why must APC connectors (green) never be mated with UPC connectors (blue)? What physical damage occurs and what is the optical consequence?

Key Takeaways

  • SMF (9 um core) eliminates modal dispersion; used for all long-haul and DWDM applications
  • MMF (50 um) is sufficient and cost-effective for data center intra-rack and short inter-switch runs
  • 1310 nm minimizes dispersion; 1550 nm minimizes attenuation -- DWDM lives at 1550 nm
  • LC is the dominant connector for modern SFP/SFP+/QSFP equipment; MPO/MTP for parallel optics
  • APC connectors (green) are required for high-coherence DWDM systems; return loss >60 dB
  • Loss budget: subtract all losses from available power margin; maintain minimum 3 dB system margin
  • Contamination is the leading cause of fiber failures -- always inspect before mating
  • Chromatic dispersion and PMD are managed by DSP in modern coherent optics, not physical compensation
