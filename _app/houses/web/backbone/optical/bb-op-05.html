Why Coherent Was a Revolution

Before coherent optics, DWDM systems used direct detection: the photodetector simply measured the arrival of photons -- on or off, intensity modulated. This is called OOK (On-Off Keying) or NRZ (Non-Return to Zero). The maximum spectral efficiency of direct detection is approximately 1 bit per hertz of optical bandwidth, which limits 10G systems to requiring roughly 10 GHz of optical spectrum per channel.

Coherent detection beats the incoming optical signal against a local oscillator laser at the receiver, converting amplitude and phase information into electrical signals that can be digitally processed. Because phase and both polarizations of light can now carry independent data, a single optical carrier can encode 4 bits per symbol in DP-QPSK (dual polarization quadrature phase-shift keying), 8 bits in DP-16QAM, or 12 bits in DP-64QAM. This is why 100G coherent fits in the same 50 GHz optical channel that carried 10G in the previous generation.

Coherent Detection: The Core Principle

An optical 90-degree hybrid mixer combines the incoming signal with a local oscillator (LO) laser running at the same nominal wavelength. The mixer produces four output signals representing the in-phase (I) and quadrature (Q) components of both X and Y polarizations. These four analog signals are sampled by high-speed ADCs and processed by the coherent DSP ASIC to recover the transmitted data and simultaneously compensate for accumulated chromatic dispersion, PMD, and nonlinear impairments.

Modulation Formats: Capacity vs Reach

Coherent systems select modulation format based on the required capacity and the available OSNR over the optical path. Higher-order modulation packs more bits per symbol but requires proportionally better signal quality.

Modern coherent transponders are adaptive: they can dynamically change modulation format and baud rate based on measured OSNR and reach requirements. A single hardware platform may support DP-QPSK at 100G for long-haul spans and DP-64QAM at 400G for short metro spans -- all with the same physical transceiver, changed by software.

DSP: The Engine of Coherent Performance

The coherent DSP ASIC performs real-time digital signal processing on the received I/Q samples at speeds of 32-64 GBaud (giga-baud). The DSP performs multiple compensation functions simultaneously:

Chromatic Dispersion Compensation CD accumulates at approximately 17 ps/nm/km in standard SMF (G.652). A 1000 km span accumulates 17,000 ps/nm -- enough to completely destroy a direct-detection signal. Coherent DSP applies an inverse transfer function in the frequency domain to fully compensate CD digitally.
PMD Compensation Polarization Mode Dispersion causes the two polarization states to walk off in time. Coherent DSP tracks the PMD state and applies adaptive equalization to separate the X and Y polarization streams. PMD compensation effectiveness scales with DSP processing power.
Nonlinear Compensation (NLC) At high launch powers in DWDM systems, fiber nonlinearities (SPM, XPM, FWM) degrade signal quality. Advanced DSPs implement nonlinear compensation algorithms that can recover 0.5-2 dB of margin by pre-distorting the transmitter or post-compensating at the receiver.
Forward Error Correction (FEC) Soft-decision FEC (SD-FEC) in coherent systems provides 9-12 dB of coding gain by processing soft reliability information from the ADC rather than hard bit decisions. This is a dramatic improvement over the ~6 dB hard-decision RS(255,239) FEC in G.709.

Pluggable Coherent: ZR and ZR+

Historically, coherent optics required large dedicated transponder line cards -- a 1RU or 2RU blade in a specialized DWDM chassis, expensive and power-hungry. The pluggable coherent movement integrates the entire coherent modem into a small form-factor module that plugs directly into a router or switch port.

400ZR Limitation: No EDFA Awareness

400ZR is designed for unamplified dark fiber spans up to approximately 120 km. It does not carry OTN overhead or optical supervisory channel -- it is a simple 400G Ethernet payload. For spans requiring EDFA amplification or existing ROADM infrastructure, OpenZR+ (which adds OTN framing and supports operating in amplified DWDM systems) is the appropriate standard. The performance difference: 400ZR can do 120 km unamplified; OpenZR+ can traverse multiple EDFA spans to reach 2000 km.

Practice Questions

  1. A 1000 km uncompensated SMF span using standard G.652 fiber accumulates chromatic dispersion at 17 ps/nm/km. How much total CD accumulates? Why can a 10G direct-detection system not tolerate this, and how does coherent DSP compensate it without optical DCM modules?
  2. A network operator is planning a DWDM upgrade from 10G DP-OOK to 100G coherent DP-QPSK on the same 50 GHz channel plan. The existing EDFA infrastructure was designed for 10G signals. What margin considerations must the operator evaluate before deploying 100G coherent on the existing amplifier chain?
  3. Compare 400ZR and OpenZR+ for a 400G link between two data centers 85 km apart connected by dark fiber. Which standard is appropriate? Would your answer change if the 85 km span required two in-line EDFA amplifiers due to splices and connector losses?
  4. Why does SD-FEC (soft-decision FEC) outperform HD-FEC (hard-decision FEC) by approximately 3-5 dB of additional coding gain? What additional information does soft-decision processing use that hard-decision processing discards?

Key Takeaways

  • Coherent detection uses a local oscillator laser and 90-degree hybrid to recover both amplitude and phase
  • DP-QPSK: 4 bits/symbol, 100G, long-haul. DP-16QAM: 8 bits/symbol, 200-400G, metro/regional
  • Coherent DSP simultaneously compensates CD, PMD, nonlinear effects, and applies soft-decision FEC
  • Adaptive modulation on a single platform switches between formats based on measured OSNR
  • 400ZR: 400G pluggable for unamplified DCI up to 120 km. OpenZR+: adds OTN, supports amplified DWDM
  • SD-FEC provides ~9-12 dB coding gain vs ~6 dB for legacy HD-FEC (RS(255,239))
  • Pluggable coherent (QSFP-DD, OSFP) eliminates the need for dedicated transponder line cards
