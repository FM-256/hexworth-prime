The Hunting Grounds

Bug Hunting • Platform Reference • 13 Platforms Covered

Every platform has different rules, audiences, payout ceilings, and program inventories. Picking the wrong one wastes weeks of effort. This reference maps the full landscape of bug bounty platforms — from the industry giants to niche specialists — with accurate assessments of who each one serves best and what realistic expectations should look like before you ever submit a single report.

Section 01

Why Platform Choice Matters

Most researchers treat platform selection as an afterthought. They create an account on HackerOne because everyone else does and spend months fighting noise and duplicate reports before wondering why progress feels impossible. Platform choice is a strategic decision, not an administrative one. The platform you pick determines which programs you can access, how fast your reports get triaged, what skills are rewarded, and what payout ceilings look like.

Consider what is actually different between platforms: program inventory, triage quality, researcher community size (which determines competition), payment rails, reputation systems, access tiers, and geographic focus. A researcher who specializes in blockchain vulnerabilities has no business spending most of their time on HackerOne — HackenProof and SlowMist exist precisely for that domain. A beginner who starts on Synack will be rejected. A senior researcher who spends years on Open Bug Bounty is leaving serious money on the table.

ℹ
Platform vs. Self-Hosted Programs
Several major organizations — Google, Apple, Microsoft, Meta, and Shopify — run their own vulnerability reward programs outside any third-party platform. These self-hosted VRPs often pay the highest bounties in the industry (Google's maximum is $151,515 for certain Android vulnerabilities). This module covers third-party platforms only. Self-hosted programs are addressed separately in the Bounty Economics module.

What Platforms Provide

  • • Standardized report submission and tracking
  • • Triage teams that validate reports before client review
  • • Payment processing and tax documentation
  • • Legal safe harbor framework between researcher and company
  • • Reputation and reputation-based access tiers
  • • Leaderboards, community, and learning resources

How to Evaluate a Platform

  • • Number of active programs (especially in your specialty)
  • • Average triage turnaround time on public programs
  • • Payout structure: fixed table vs. negotiated vs. discretionary
  • • Researcher community size = competition density
  • • Access model: open, invite-only, or tiered
  • • Geographic focus and payment method compatibility

Major Platforms

These are the platforms with the largest program catalogs and the most active researcher communities. They form the core of the bug bounty ecosystem and represent the default starting point for most researchers.

Field note: Start here if you are new. The Hacktivity feed alone is worth the account registration — disclosed reports show real vulnerabilities, real payouts, and real report structures. Build reputation on smaller public programs before competing on high-traffic targets. The private program system rewards consistency more than raw technical skill.

The SRT application assesses practical skills across web, network, and mobile attack surfaces. If you pass, the environment rewards serious researchers significantly better than any open platform.
Section 03

European Platforms

GDPR has made European organizations unusually serious about security disclosure. A class of EU-headquartered platforms has emerged to serve this market, offering faster triage, stronger compliance postures, and direct access to European enterprise programs that do not list on US-centric platforms.

Intigriti

Public Managed Triage
Specialty
European Enterprise Programs
Payout Range (Critical)
€2,000 – €20,000
Avg Triage Time
24 – 48 hours
Best For
EU-focused researchers; intermediate+
Strengths
  • + Strongest EU program presence: major Belgian, Dutch, and UK banks and telecoms list exclusively here
  • + 48-hour triage SLA is a platform commitment, not just a guideline
  • + BugBytes newsletter and community events (including live hacking events) build active researcher engagement
  • + GDPR-compliant program structures give European researchers clearer legal standing
Weaknesses
  • - Smaller overall program catalog than HackerOne or Bugcrowd; fewer US-based targets
  • - EUR-denominated payouts create currency risk for non-EU researchers in volatile exchange periods
  • - Community is growing but still smaller, limiting peer knowledge exchange compared to HackerOne
Field note: Intigriti is the right choice if you hunt EU targets. The triage quality is genuinely better than average — reports that would sit unacknowledged for two weeks on HackerOne are often fully triaged within 48 hours here. The BugBytes newsletter is one of the better free resources for staying current on disclosed reports.

Section 04

Specialty and Niche Platforms

A growing subset of platforms targets specific verticals rather than the general web application market. Blockchain security, responsible disclosure without a formal program, and pentest-as-a-service each have dedicated platforms that outperform the generalists for their specific use cases.

Open Bug Bounty
Founded 2014 • Non-commercial, Community-run
Open to All Disclosure Focus
Specialty
Responsible Disclosure (No Program Needed)
Payout Range
$0 (optional rewards by site owner)
Avg Triage Time
Variable / no guarantee
Best For
Beginners; reputation building
Strengths
  • + Allows responsible disclosure for any website even if the organization has no formal bug bounty program
  • + Free to use; no vetting or reputation requirements for submission
  • + Good for building a submission track record early in a research career
  • + Coordinated disclosure process gives you a framework for notifying sites that ignore direct contact
Weaknesses
  • - XSS-focused primarily; not designed for complex vulnerability classes like RCE or SSRF
  • - No guaranteed payment; site owners choose whether and how much to pay
  • - Site owners may never respond; no enforcement mechanism for acknowledgment or remediation
  • - Credibility of submission history here is lower than formal platform history with triaged reports
Field note: Use Open Bug Bounty to practice the mechanics of responsible disclosure and to report XSS findings you encounter on sites without formal programs. Do not count on payment. Do count on the structured practice of writing clear, reproducible reports and coordinating with unresponsive organizations — those skills transfer directly to paid platforms.

Aggregators and Emerging Platforms

A third category of tools aggregates program listings from multiple platforms into a single searchable index. These are not platforms in the traditional sense — they do not handle report submission or payment — but they are operationally useful for discovering programs that match your specific technical skills.

Verify current scope directly on the source platform before testing — aggregated data can be days or weeks stale and an out-of-scope finding is a wasted report.

Section 07

How to Choose Your Platform

Platform selection is not permanent. Most active researchers maintain accounts on two or three platforms simultaneously, but with a clear primary platform where they focus the majority of effort. The following framework maps current skill level and specialization to a recommended starting point and a realistic progression path.

Beginner Researcher
0 – 6 Months of Active Hunting
HackerOne (primary) Open Bug Bounty YesWeHack DOJO Bugcrowd University
Focus on public programs with wide scope and active triage. HackerOne's Hacktivity feed is your primary learning resource — read disclosed reports daily. Use Open Bug Bounty to practice the mechanics of responsible disclosure. Complete YesWeHack DOJO and Bugcrowd University training modules before submitting live reports.
Do not chase payouts yet. Chase accepted reports. Every accepted report, regardless of amount, builds the Signal score that unlocks private programs. A $150 information disclosure finding on a well-trafficked program matters more at this stage than a failed hunt on a high-payout target.
Intermediate Researcher
6 Months – 2 Years; First Private Invites
HackerOne (private programs) Bugcrowd Intigriti YesWeHack
At this stage you should have a positive Signal score and be receiving private program invites. Prioritize private programs over public ones. The quality-to-competition ratio is dramatically better. Add Intigriti if you have any EU-based targets or prefer faster triage. Begin tracking which vulnerability classes you find most often and specialize accordingly.
Geographic specialization is underutilized at this level. EU-based organizations often have more permissive scopes and less crowded programs than comparable US targets. Intigriti and YesWeHack access programs that US-focused researchers ignore.
Advanced Researcher
2+ Years; Consistent Critical Findings
Synack SRT Cobalt HackenProof (if Web3) Zerocopter (if EU)
Apply to Synack. The application is the primary gate. Build a portfolio demonstrating breadth across web, network, and mobile attack surfaces before applying. If your specialty is blockchain or smart contracts, HackenProof and Immunefi offer payout ceilings that no traditional web platform can match. Cobalt is the right fit if structured engagements with deliverables suit your working style better than open-ended hunting.
At this level, platform selection should follow program quality, not platform reputation. A single high-value private program on Zerocopter may be worth more hours than an entire quarter on HackerOne public programs. Evaluate every program on its specific scope, payout table, and competition density — not on the platform hosting it.

✓
The One-Platform Rule
Do not scatter effort across every platform. Pick one primary platform and stay there long enough to build meaningful reputation. Most platform reputation systems are non-transferable. A researcher with strong Signal on HackerOne and a mixed presence across five platforms is worse positioned than a researcher who focused exclusively on one for 12 months. Specialize early, diversify once the track record is established.

Platform Progression Path

  1. Practice (Month 1–2): Complete YesWeHack DOJO and Bugcrowd University. Set up Burp Suite, a VPN, and a dedicated browser profile for bounty work. Read 50 disclosed HackerOne reports from your target vulnerability class.
  2. First Submissions (Month 2–4): Target HackerOne public programs with wide scope and fewer than 50 active researchers. Open Bug Bounty for XSS practice on non-paid targets. Focus on one vulnerability class until you have 3–5 accepted reports.
  3. Reputation Building (Month 4–12): Maintain positive Signal score on HackerOne. Begin receiving private invites. Evaluate whether to add Bugcrowd or Intigriti based on the programs available in your specialization.
  4. Private Access (Year 1–2): Work primarily on private programs. Triage quality and payout consistency improve significantly. Develop a specialization: one vertical (fintech, healthcare, government) and one vulnerability class (authentication, IDOR, injection).
  5. Advanced Platforms (Year 2+): Apply to Synack SRT. Evaluate Cobalt if structured engagements suit your working style. Add HackenProof if blockchain skills have been developed. Consider Zerocopter through community connections.
Discussion Prompts
  • 01 Synack and Cobalt both use vetted researcher models but operate differently: Synack provides bounty-style pay on high-value programs while Cobalt uses structured pentest engagements with deliverables. What are the trade-offs a researcher accepts by choosing the vetted model over an open platform? Consider income consistency, autonomy, legal exposure, and career signaling.
  • 02 European platforms like Intigriti and YesWeHack have grown significantly despite US-centric platforms dominating the market. What structural factors have driven EU platform growth? Consider GDPR enforcement, data residency requirements, language and cultural familiarity, and the regulatory environment for vulnerability disclosure. Will this trend continue?
  • 03 Open Bug Bounty allows reporting vulnerabilities on any website, including those with no formal program and no obligation to respond or remediate. Evaluate the ethical calculus: you find an XSS on a small e-commerce site, report it via Open Bug Bounty, the site owner ignores the report for 90 days, and you disclose it publicly. Did you act responsibly? What obligations do you have to the site's users? What obligations does the site owner have to you?
  • 04 Blockchain and DeFi programs offer dramatically higher critical payouts than traditional web programs, with some exceeding $1,000,000 for protocol-level vulnerabilities. What are the specific skill prerequisites for effective smart contract auditing? How does the research methodology differ from web application testing? Is the higher payout ceiling accessible to a generalist who pivots to blockchain, or does it require years of domain specialization?
