Section 01

The EHE Program & EC-Council Certification Path

Ethical Hacking Essentials (EHE) is an introductory cybersecurity certification developed by EC-Council, the organization behind the globally recognized Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) credential. EHE is designed for students, career changers, and early-career professionals who want a structured, vendor-neutral entry point into the world of offensive and defensive security.

This course follows the official EC-Council EHE curriculum across 12 modules. Upon successful completion, you will be prepared to sit for the EHE certification exam (212-89), earning a credential that demonstrates foundational competency in information security concepts, ethical hacking methodology, and practical tool usage.

EC-Council Certification Roadmap
EHE serves as the entry point into EC-Council's offensive security track. From here, the natural progression is CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker), then CPENT (Certified Penetration Testing Professional), and ultimately LPT Master (Licensed Penetration Tester). Each tier deepens practical skills and increases the rigor of the examination format.

Candidates who pass the EHE exam earn a digital badge issued through Accredible, which can be shared on LinkedIn and included in resumes. The exam consists of 75 multiple-choice questions to be completed in 2 hours, with a passing score of 70%. There are no formal prerequisites, though basic familiarity with networking fundamentals is strongly recommended.

Section 03

What is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the practice of deliberately probing computer systems, networks, or applications to discover security vulnerabilities that a malicious actor could exploit — but doing so with explicit written authorization from the system owner. The goal is to identify weaknesses before adversaries can find and exploit them.

The word "ethical" is not incidental. It marks the essential distinction between a penetration tester and a criminal: the tester operates within defined scope, under a legal agreement, and with the intent to improve security rather than cause harm. Without those three elements, identical technical actions become illegal intrusion.

White Hat Hacking
Authorized, legal testing performed to identify and remediate security weaknesses. Results are reported to the owner and used to harden defenses. Employment or contract agreement defines scope.
Authorized
Black Hat Hacking
Unauthorized intrusion with malicious intent: theft of data, financial fraud, sabotage, or espionage. Violates the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and equivalent laws in virtually every jurisdiction.
Criminal
⚫
Grey Hat Hacking
Unauthorized testing sometimes performed with the intent to notify the victim, often in exchange for payment. Still illegal regardless of intent; no authorization means no legal protection.
Ambiguous / Illegal

Organizations need ethical hackers because software complexity guarantees vulnerabilities. No development team produces perfectly secure code. Third-party testers bring adversarial thinking and attack toolkits that internal developers rarely employ. A successful penetration test uncovers real attack paths before a malicious actor can traverse them.

Critical Distinction
The techniques you will learn in this course are dual-use: every attack method is also a defense mechanism when understood and controlled. You must never apply these techniques against any system without explicit written authorization. Doing so is a federal crime in the United States (CFAA) and carries criminal penalties including imprisonment, regardless of whether damage occurs.

Cybersecurity is one of the fastest-growing fields in technology, with the global shortage of qualified professionals exceeding 3.5 million positions as of 2024. This course positions you for entry-level roles across multiple tracks. Understanding the landscape helps you identify where your strengths and interests align.

Penetration Tester
Simulates adversary attacks against defined targets under contract. Delivers written reports of findings and remediation guidance. Average US salary: $100K–$140K.
Offensive
Security Analyst
Monitors systems for indicators of compromise, analyzes security events, and responds to alerts. Works primarily in defensive, detection-oriented roles.
Defensive
Incident Responder
Investigates active breaches, conducts forensic analysis, and coordinates containment and recovery. High-stakes, time-pressured role at the front line of organizational defense.
Response
SOC Analyst
Monitors Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms, triages alerts, and escalates incidents. Tier 1/2/3 structure provides a clear advancement path from entry level.
Monitoring

🔵

Section 06

Lab Environment Overview

Ethical hacking is a hands-on discipline. Reading about Nmap port scanning is fundamentally different from running scans against a live target and interpreting the results. This course uses a fully isolated virtual lab environment to ensure you develop genuine operational skill alongside conceptual knowledge.

The lab environment consists of a Kali Linux attacker machine and several vulnerable target systems (Metasploitable, DVWA, VulnHub images) running in an isolated network segment with no external internet access. All target systems are intentionally misconfigured and exist solely for educational purposes.

Primary Attack Platform
Kali Linux is the industry-standard penetration testing distribution maintained by Offensive Security. It ships with over 600 pre-installed security tools and is the de facto platform for ethical hacking coursework and professional engagements. Your lab VM runs Kali Linux 2024.x.

The following tools are introduced across the course curriculum:

Nmap Wireshark Metasploit Framework Burp Suite Community Aircrack-ng John the Ripper Hashcat Hydra Nikto Gobuster theHarvester Maltego CE SQLmap BeEF Netcat tcpdump OpenVAS Dirb Enum4linux Snort

Target Systems

  • Metasploitable 2/3: Intentionally vulnerable Linux server
  • DVWA: Damn Vulnerable Web Application (PHP/MySQL)
  • VulnHub images: Module-specific challenge VMs
  • Windows Server 2019: Domain controller lab target
  • OWASP WebGoat: Interactive web vulnerability training

Lab Setup Requirements

  • Hypervisor: VirtualBox 7.x or VMware Workstation 17
  • RAM: Minimum 8 GB (16 GB recommended)
  • Disk: 60 GB free space for VM images
  • CPU: Virtualization-enabled (VT-x/AMD-V)
  • Network: Host-only adapter configured for isolation
Section 07

️ Ethics and Legal Framework

Understanding the legal landscape is not optional background reading — it is the foundational requirement for any ethical hacking practice. Every tool and technique in this course is legal when applied with authorization and illegal without it. The boundary is not technical; it is legal and ethical.

Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)
The primary US federal law governing computer crime. Prohibits unauthorized access to computer systems. Conviction carries up to 20 years imprisonment. "Exceeding authorized access" is also prohibited — scope matters.
US Federal
Responsible Disclosure
When vulnerabilities are discovered outside of formal engagements, responsible disclosure means notifying the vendor privately before public release. Many organizations now run bug bounty programs that formalize this process.
Industry Standard
Rules of Engagement (RoE)
The formal document defining what a penetration tester may and may not do: which IP ranges, which testing methods, blackout windows, escalation procedures, and emergency contacts. Never begin a test without a signed RoE.
Contractual
Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)
Protects client confidentiality. A penetration tester who discovers a critical vulnerability in a financial institution's systems cannot publicly disclose that finding without violating the NDA and potentially applicable law.
Legal Protection
International Considerations
Different jurisdictions have different laws. The UK Computer Misuse Act, the EU Network and Information Security (NIS2) Directive, and GDPR all impose obligations on security professionals operating across borders.
Global
Written Authorization
Verbal permission is legally insufficient. All engagements require written, signed authorization identifying the tester, the client, the scope, the timeframe, and the authorized testing methods. This document is your legal defense.
Non-Negotiable
EC-Council Code of Ethics
EC-Council certified professionals are bound by a formal Code of Ethics that requires: keeping client information confidential, never accessing systems without permission, disclosing all relevant conflicts of interest, reporting illegal activities, and maintaining respect for intellectual property. Violations result in certification revocation.
