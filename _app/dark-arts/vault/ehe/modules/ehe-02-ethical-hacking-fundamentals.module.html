Module 02 • Ethical Hacking Fundamentals

Thinking Like an Adversary

Effective defense requires understanding the attacker's perspective. This module maps the attacker's journey from first reconnaissance to final objective, establishes a shared vocabulary through the Cyber Kill Chain and MITRE ATT&CK, and defines the methodology and professional standards that distinguish ethical hacking from criminal intrusion.

Duration: 75 min
Sections: 7
Exam Weight: Very High
Level: Foundational
Section 01

The Cyber Kill Chain

Developed by Lockheed Martin in 2011, the Cyber Kill Chain models the stages of a targeted cyberattack. It provides defenders with a framework for understanding attacker behavior, identifying detection opportunities, and designing controls that disrupt attacks at multiple stages. Breaking the chain at any point prevents the attacker from achieving their objective.

Click each phase to expand details and see real-world tooling examples. Note that sophisticated attackers may move non-linearly, return to earlier phases after discovery, or combine multiple phases simultaneously.

01
Reconnaissance
► Click to expand

The attacker harvests information about the target without direct interaction (passive) or with limited direct interaction (active). The goal is to build a comprehensive intelligence picture: employee names, email formats, IP ranges, domain structure, technology stack, and physical locations.

Passive: OSINT via LinkedIn, Shodan, WHOIS, DNS records, job postings, social media, Google dorking, certificate transparency logs. No packets touch the target's systems.

Active: DNS zone transfers, email enumeration, web spidering, social engineering calls. Some interaction with target infrastructure occurs.

theHarvester Maltego Shodan Recon-ng WHOIS DNSdumpster LinkedIn OSINT
02
Weaponization 💣
► Click to expand

The attacker pairs a remote access capability (RAT, backdoor, implant) with an exploit into a deliverable payload. This phase occurs entirely on attacker-controlled infrastructure and leaves no trace on the target until delivery.

Common weaponization approaches: malicious Office macros, PDF exploits, trojanized installers, weaponized scripts, exploit frameworks generating custom shellcode.

Defenders cannot directly observe weaponization. Detection relies on identifying patterns associated with known malware families or C2 infrastructure at later stages.

Metasploit msfvenom Cobalt Strike Empire Custom malware
03
Delivery
► Click to expand

Transmission of the weaponized payload to the victim environment. This is the first stage where defenders have a realistic opportunity to detect and block the attack. Email gateways, web proxies, and endpoint controls can intercept delivery.

Delivery vectors: spear phishing email attachments, malicious URLs, USB drops, watering hole attacks (compromising sites visited by targets), supply chain compromise of software updates, direct exploitation of internet-facing services.

Phishing emails GoPhish USB drives Watering hole Drive-by download
04
Exploitation 🔨
► Click to expand

The exploit triggers, leveraging a software vulnerability, configuration weakness, or user action to execute attacker-controlled code on the target system. This is the moment of initial code execution (T1059 in MITRE ATT&CK terminology).

Exploitation types: browser exploits (drive-by), document exploits (macro execution), service exploits (buffer overflow, RCE), credential-based access (no exploit needed), social engineering (user runs payload).

Effective patch management and application sandboxing are the primary defenses at this stage.

Metasploit modules CVE exploits Zero-days Macro execution
05
Installation
► Click to expand

The attacker installs a persistent backdoor or remote access trojan (RAT) to maintain access even if the initial exploit is detected and patched. This stage often involves establishing multiple redundant persistence mechanisms.

Persistence techniques: registry Run keys, scheduled tasks, service installation, DLL hijacking, bootkit/rootkit installation, WMI event subscriptions, startup folder placement.

Behavioral detection tools and endpoint detection & response (EDR) solutions are most effective at identifying installation activity.

Meterpreter Covenant Registry persistence Scheduled tasks
06
Command & Control
► Click to expand

The implanted malware establishes an outbound communication channel to attacker-controlled infrastructure (C2 server). This channel allows the attacker to send commands and receive results from the compromised host.

C2 channels: HTTPS beaconing (blends with normal traffic), DNS tunneling, ICMP covert channels, social media APIs, domain fronting, peer-to-peer (no central server). Traffic is often encrypted and mimics legitimate protocols.

Detection: Network anomaly detection, DNS analytics, SIEM correlation rules looking for beaconing patterns, threat intelligence feeds blocking known C2 infrastructure.

Cobalt Strike Beacon Sliver DNS tunneling HTTPS C2
07
Actions on Objectives
► Click to expand

With persistent access and a C2 channel established, the attacker executes their primary mission. Objectives vary widely by attacker type: data exfiltration, ransomware deployment, intellectual property theft, sabotage, credential harvesting, or establishing infrastructure for further attacks.

Common final objectives: lateral movement to high-value systems, privilege escalation to Domain Admin, data exfiltration to external servers, ransomware deployment across the network, destruction of backups and recovery systems.

At this stage, the business impact of the intrusion materializes. Early detection and containment in prior stages prevents reaching this phase.

Data exfiltration Ransomware Lateral movement Privilege escalation
Defensive Application of the Kill Chain
The Kill Chain's value to defenders is in identifying control gaps. For each phase, ask: what controls do we have here? If an attacker can complete all seven phases undetected, defensive controls are insufficient. Effective security programs aim to detect and respond before phase 7 — ideally at phases 1–3.
Section 02

🍟 Tactics, Techniques & Procedures (TTPs) — MITRE ATT&CK

While the Cyber Kill Chain describes the phases of an attack at a high level, the MITRE ATT&CK framework (Adversarial Tactics, Techniques, and Common Knowledge) provides a granular, evidence-based catalog of specific techniques observed in real-world attacks. It is the most widely adopted threat intelligence framework in the cybersecurity industry.

ATT&CK is organized into Tactics (the attacker's goal at each stage), Techniques (how the tactic is accomplished), and Sub-techniques (specific implementations). As of 2024, the Enterprise matrix contains over 200 techniques and 400 sub-techniques across 14 tactics.

Why ATT&CK Matters
ATT&CK gives defenders and attackers a shared vocabulary. Penetration test reports that cite ATT&CK technique IDs (e.g., T1078 - Valid Accounts, T1055 - Process Injection) allow clients to directly map findings to detection and mitigation guidance. SIEM platforms and EDR tools increasingly map alerts to ATT&CK techniques, enabling faster triage and more precise response.
Section 03

👔 Hacker Classification

Not all hackers operate with the same motivations, skill levels, or legal standing. Understanding hacker classes helps security professionals model threats accurately: a script kiddie running public exploits requires different defenses than a nation-state APT group with a $50 million operational budget and zero-day capabilities.

White Hat
Authorized security professionals testing systems with permission. The ethical hacker. Operates under contract, follows scope, and reports findings.
Authorized
Black Hat
Malicious hackers operating without authorization for personal gain, criminal purposes, or causing deliberate harm. Subject to criminal prosecution.
Criminal
👔
Grey Hat
Unauthorized but without malicious intent. May notify victim organizations. Still illegal regardless of intent. No written authorization means no legal protection.
Unauthorized
Script Kiddie
Low-skill attackers using pre-built tools without understanding the underlying techniques. High volume, low sophistication. Responsible for a large percentage of opportunistic attacks.
Low Skill
🏮
State-Sponsored
Nation-state APT groups with significant resources, custom tooling, and long-term objectives including espionage, IP theft, and infrastructure disruption. Most sophisticated threat category.
APT
✊
Hacktivist
Politically or ideologically motivated hackers targeting organizations whose actions they oppose. Common tactics include DDoS, defacement, and data leaks. Anonymous is a well-known example.
Ideological
💸
Organized Crime
Financially motivated criminal organizations operating ransomware-as-a-service, banking trojans, and fraud operations. Operate with professional HR structures, customer support, and affiliate programs.
Financial
Section 04

Phases of Ethical Hacking

Ethical hacking follows a structured methodology that mirrors the attacker's approach while maintaining documentation, scope adherence, and professional conduct. The five phases below represent the EC-Council's canonical ethical hacking process and form the backbone of formal penetration testing engagements.

Click each phase to expand the full description and associated tooling.

Phase 01
Reconnaissance
►

Reconnaissance (also called footprinting) is the systematic collection of information about the target organization. Passive reconnaissance uses publicly available data sources without interacting with target systems. Active reconnaissance involves direct interaction such as DNS queries, web crawling, or social engineering calls.

Key data gathered: IP ranges, domain names, employee names and email formats, technology stack indicators, physical locations, publicly exposed services, third-party relationships, and any previously disclosed vulnerabilities.

theHarvester Maltego Recon-ng Shodan WHOIS nslookup / dig Google Dorks
Phase 02
Scanning & Enumeration
►

Active scanning probes the target's network and systems to discover live hosts, open ports, running services, operating systems, and software versions. This phase produces the technical map of the attack surface.

Network scanning: ICMP sweeps, TCP SYN scans, UDP scans, service version detection, OS fingerprinting. Enumeration: extracting usernames, share names, group memberships, and service banners from discovered services. Enumeration requires live interaction with services.

Nmap Masscan Nikto enum4linux OpenVAS Nessus smbclient
🔨
Phase 03
Gaining Access
►

Using information gathered in earlier phases, the tester attempts to exploit discovered vulnerabilities to gain unauthorized access to systems. This is the exploitation phase where technical skill, social engineering, or both are applied against the target.

Access methods include: exploiting known CVEs in unpatched software; password attacks (brute force, dictionary, credential stuffing); phishing for credentials or payload delivery; web application attacks (SQL injection, XSS, SSRF); and wireless attacks for network entry.

Metasploit Hydra Hashcat SQLmap Burp Suite ExploitDB John the Ripper
Phase 04
Maintaining Access
►

After gaining initial access, a penetration tester demonstrates the ability to maintain persistent access — simulating what an actual attacker would do to survive detection and reboots. This phase validates the depth of the security gap.

Privilege escalation (local privilege escalation to SYSTEM/root, domain privilege escalation to Domain Admin) is pursued to demonstrate maximum impact. Lateral movement demonstrates the ability to pivot from compromised systems to other network resources.

Meterpreter Netcat PowerShell Empire BloodHound Mimikatz Pass-the-Hash
Phase 05
Covering Tracks
►

Real attackers attempt to remove evidence of their presence to avoid detection and extend the duration of the intrusion. Ethical hackers demonstrate this capability to highlight forensic and detection gaps.

Techniques include: log clearing (Windows Event Log, syslog, bash history), timestomping (modifying file timestamps to confuse forensic timelines), tool removal, and traffic encryption to prevent network forensic reconstruction.

Note: In many engagements, actually clearing logs is not permitted in the Rules of Engagement. Testers instead document the capability without executing destructive actions.

wevtutil (Windows) Auditpol timestomp clearev (Meterpreter)
Section 05

Ethical Hacking Concepts & Engagement Structure

Ethical hacking engagements are formal business arrangements, not informal technical exercises. The professional structure of a penetration testing engagement — the contracts, the scoping, the communication protocols — is as important as the technical methodology. Failures in the business layer create legal exposure and client distrust.

Pre-Engagement Requirements

  • Scope Definition: Specific IP ranges, domains, applications, and physical locations in scope. Explicit list of out-of-scope targets.
  • Written Authorization: Signed letter or contract from system owner authorizing the test. Verbal authorization is legally insufficient.
  • Rules of Engagement (RoE): Permitted testing methods, prohibited techniques (e.g., no DDoS), blackout periods (no testing during payroll processing).
  • NDA Execution: Both parties sign non-disclosure to protect confidential findings and client information.
  • Emergency Contacts: Named contacts for immediate escalation if a critical vulnerability is discovered.

Report Deliverables

  • Executive Summary: Non-technical narrative of findings and business risk for C-suite and board review.
  • Technical Findings: Detailed per-vulnerability documentation with reproduction steps, evidence, CVSS scores, and ATT&CK mapping.
  • Risk Ratings: Critical / High / Medium / Low / Informational with business impact assessment.
  • Remediation Guidance: Specific, actionable recommendations for each finding.
  • Retesting Offer: Follow-up testing to verify that remediations are effective.
Testing Types: Know the Difference
Black Box: No prior knowledge of target systems. Simulates an external attacker. Most realistic but most time-intensive.

White Box: Full knowledge including source code, architecture diagrams, and credentials. Most thorough coverage. Simulates an insider threat or post-breach scenario.

Grey Box: Partial knowledge, such as low-privilege credentials and network diagrams. Balances realism and coverage. Most common in enterprise engagements.

Essential Tools Overview

The tools introduced here appear throughout this course and in professional engagements. This is an introduction to their purpose and primary use cases. Deep-dive usage is covered in the relevant module labs. All tools are pre-installed on your Kali Linux lab environment.

Nmap Scanning
Network Mapper is the industry-standard tool for network discovery and security auditing. Performs host discovery, port scanning, service version detection, OS fingerprinting, and script-based vulnerability scanning via the Nmap Scripting Engine (NSE).
Common Commands
nmap -sV -sC -O target
nmap -sS -p- --min-rate 5000 target
nmap --script vuln target
🔨 Metasploit Framework Exploitation
The world's most widely used exploitation framework. Contains over 2,000 modules covering exploits, payloads, auxiliary functions, and post-exploitation capabilities. Central to professional penetration testing for gaining and maintaining access.

Burp Suite Web App
The standard proxy-based tool for web application security testing. Intercepts and modifies HTTP/HTTPS traffic between browser and server, enabling identification and exploitation of web vulnerabilities including SQLi, XSS, CSRF, IDOR, and authentication flaws.

Wireshark Analysis
The world's leading network protocol analyzer. Captures and interactively browses network traffic. Essential for understanding protocols, analyzing C2 communications, capturing credentials transmitted in cleartext, and performing network forensics.

Password auditing and hash-cracking tool supporting over 100 hash types. Uses dictionary attacks, brute force, and rule-based mangling to recover plaintext passwords from captured hashes. Essential for post-exploitation credential analysis.

Complete suite for wireless network security assessment. Captures WPA/WPA2 handshakes, performs dictionary attacks against captured handshakes, tests for WEP weaknesses, and performs de-authentication attacks to force handshake capture.

Section 07

Key Takeaways

Module 02 establishes the adversarial thinking framework that underlies all subsequent technical modules. Every lab, every exploitation technique, and every defensive recommendation in this course can be mapped back to the Kill Chain, ATT&CK, and ethical hacking methodology covered here.

Break the Chain Early
The Cyber Kill Chain's greatest value to defenders is showing that detection at any stage prevents the attacker from reaching their objective. Defense-in-depth means multiple chain-breaking opportunities.
🍟
ATT&CK Speaks to Everyone
ATT&CK provides a common language between red teams, blue teams, and management. Technique IDs in reports enable direct lookup of mitigations, detection methods, and adversary group usage.
Authorization is Everything
The ethical hacker's technical skills are identical to a criminal hacker's. The difference is written authorization. Scope, RoE, and signed contracts are not bureaucratic overhead; they are legal protection.
👔
Know Your Threat Actor
Threat modeling requires knowing who is likely to attack a given organization. A healthcare system faces different threat actors than a cryptocurrency exchange. Hacker classification informs realistic threat scenarios.
🔨
Tools Serve Methodology
Tools are only as effective as the methodology guiding their use. Nmap output is meaningless without the knowledge to interpret it. Metasploit modules fail without proper target research. Methodology precedes tooling.
Reports Determine Value
A penetration test that produces a technically brilliant exploitation chain but an unreadable report delivers minimal value. The report is the product; the hacking is the research. Writing skill matters.
Final Reminder
Every technique you will learn in the modules ahead is a dual-use capability. Apply them only within your authorized lab environment or under a formal written engagement agreement. Unauthorized application of these techniques — even on systems you own but haven't legally authorized testing against third-party services — carries serious legal consequences. When in doubt, don't.
