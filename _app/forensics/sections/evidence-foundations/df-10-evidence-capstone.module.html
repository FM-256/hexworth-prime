Evidence Capstone Lab: Corporate Data Breach Investigation

This capstone integrates everything from modules DF-01 through DF-09. You will work through a realistic corporate data breach scenario, applying evidence classification, chain of custody procedures, legal analysis, volatility ordering, and investigation planning. Complete each section and use the rubric at the end to evaluate your work.

Capstone Objectives

  • Classify evidence types in a multi-source investigation
  • Document chain of custody for all evidence items
  • Identify applicable federal and state laws
  • Determine the correct order of volatility for collection
  • Write an evidence collection plan following NIST 800-86
  • Draft an expert witness report outline

Scenario: Nexagen Pharmaceuticals Data Breach

Background: Nexagen Pharmaceuticals, a mid-size biotech company, has discovered that proprietary drug formulation data has appeared on a competitor's servers. The company suspects an insider transferred confidential research files to the competitor, StrataChem Inc. Nexagen's CISO contacted your firm to conduct a forensic investigation. The company has obtained a civil court order authorizing examination of the suspect employee's devices and accounts.

Known Facts

  • Suspect: Dr. Sarah Chen, Senior Research Scientist, employed for 7 years
  • Dr. Chen submitted her resignation two weeks ago; her last day is in three days
  • She has a company-issued Dell Latitude laptop (Windows 11, BitLocker encrypted)
  • She has a company-issued iPhone 15 enrolled in MDM
  • She has access to the R&D shared drive on a Windows Server 2022 file server
  • The file server has 90 days of access logs
  • Nexagen uses Microsoft 365 for email (Exchange Online)
  • The compromised files were last modified between January and March 2026
  • IT noticed unusual after-hours VPN connections from Dr. Chen's account in February
  • A USB device was logged connecting to her laptop on February 14 at 23:47

Task 3: Legal Analysis

Answer each question with reference to specific statutes and cases:

  1. Which CFAA subsection(s) might Dr. Chen have violated if she copied proprietary data to a personal USB drive?
  2. Does Nexagen need a warrant to search Dr. Chen's company-issued laptop? Why or why not? What about her personal phone if she also used it for work?
  3. Can Nexagen's IT department review Dr. Chen's Microsoft 365 emails without her consent? Under which ECPA exception?
  4. If Dr. Chen encrypted the exfiltrated files using a personal encryption key, what legal mechanism could compel her to provide the key?
  5. What ethical obligations do you have if your examination reveals that Dr. Chen's supervisor also accessed and shared some of the same files with an unauthorized party?
