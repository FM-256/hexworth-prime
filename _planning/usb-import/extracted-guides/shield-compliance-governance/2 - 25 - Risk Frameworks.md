## Risk Frameworks

### ISO/IEC 27005:2022

ISO/IEC 27005:2022 is an international standard that provides guidelines and principles for information security risk management. It outlines a systematic approach to identifying, assessing, treating, and managing information security risks within an organization. Here's the key components of ISO/IEC 27005:2022:

#### Context Establishment

Before initiating the risk management process, it's essential to establish the context within which the organization operates. This involves defining the scope, objectives, stakeholders, and regulatory requirements relevant to information security risk management.

Context establishment ensures that the risk management process aligns with the organization's strategic goals and objectives while considering internal and external factors that may impact information security.

#### Risk Identification

Risk identification involves systematically identifying threats, vulnerabilities, and potential impacts that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information assets.

Various techniques, such as brainstorming, checklists, and scenario analysis, may be employed to identify potential risks across different areas of the organization, including technology, processes, people, and external factors.

#### Risk Analysis

Risk analysis involves assessing the likelihood and potential impact of identified risks to determine their significance and prioritize them for further evaluation and treatment.

Quantitative and qualitative risk analysis methods may be used to evaluate risks based on factors such as likelihood, impact severity, control effectiveness, and risk tolerance thresholds.

#### Risk Evaluation

Risk evaluation involves comparing the results of the risk analysis against predefined risk criteria and risk acceptance criteria to determine whether the risks are acceptable or require treatment.

Risks that exceed the organization's risk tolerance or acceptance thresholds are considered unacceptable and require treatment, while risks within acceptable levels may be managed through existing controls or accepted without further action.

#### Risk Treatment

Risk treatment involves selecting and implementing appropriate risk mitigation measures to reduce, transfer, or accept the identified risks to an acceptable level.

Risk treatment options may include implementing security controls, transferring risks through insurance or contractual arrangements, avoiding certain activities or investments, or accepting residual risks based on informed decision-making.

#### Communication and Consultation

Throughout the risk management process, effective communication and consultation with stakeholders are crucial to ensure transparency, consensus, and buy-in for risk management decisions and actions.

Stakeholders should be engaged and informed about the outcomes of risk assessments, treatment plans, and any changes to the risk landscape to facilitate collaboration and support for risk management initiatives.

#### Monitoring and Review:

After implementing risk treatment measures, ongoing monitoring and review are necessary to assess the effectiveness of controls, monitor changes in the risk environment, and identify emerging risks or gaps in risk management practices.

Regular reviews help ensure that risk management processes remain aligned with organizational objectives, regulatory requirements, and industry best practices.

#### Recording and Reporting:

Throughout the risk management process, accurate documentation and reporting of risk-related information are essential for accountability, traceability, and compliance purposes.

Records should be maintained to document risk assessments, treatment decisions, control implementations, monitoring activities, and any incidents or changes affecting the risk landscape.

### NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF)

The NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) is a structured and comprehensive approach for managing information security risks within organizations. It provides a systematic process for identifying, assessing, selecting, implementing, and monitoring security controls to protect information systems and data. Here's the key steps of the NIST RMF:

#### Prepare the organization

This initial step involves establishing the foundational elements necessary to support effective risk management activities. It includes defining organizational roles and responsibilities, establishing governance structures, and ensuring senior leadership support for the risk management process.

Preparation also involves developing policies, procedures, and resources to guide risk management activities and ensure alignment with organizational goals and objectives.

#### Categorize information systems

In this step, organizations identify and classify their information systems based on factors such as the type of data they store, process, or transmit, the criticality of their functions, and the potential impact of security incidents.

By categorizing information systems, organizations can prioritize their risk management efforts and tailor security controls to address specific risks and requirements associated with each system.

#### Select NIST SP 800-53 controls

NIST SP 800-53 provides a comprehensive catalog of security controls that organizations can select and tailor to address the specific security requirements of their information systems.

During this step, organizations identify and select appropriate security controls from the NIST SP 800-53 catalog based on the security categorization of their information systems and the identified risks and requirements.

#### Implement controls and document deployment

Once the appropriate security controls have been selected, organizations implement and deploy these controls within their information systems environment.

Implementation activities may include configuring technical controls, developing security policies and procedures, conducting security awareness training, and integrating security measures into system development and acquisition processes.

Documentation of control deployment ensures that security measures are consistently applied and provides a basis for assessing control effectiveness and compliance.

#### Assess controls

In this step, organizations conduct assessments to evaluate the effectiveness of implemented security controls in mitigating identified risks and meeting security requirements.

Assessment activities may include security testing, vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, and review of security documentation and configurations.

The goal of control assessments is to identify weaknesses, deficiencies, or gaps in security controls and determine whether additional measures are needed to address residual risks.

#### Authorize information systems

Based on the results of control assessments, organizations make risk-based decisions regarding the authorization of information systems to operate.

Authorization involves assessing the overall risk posture of information systems, weighing the effectiveness of implemented controls against residual risks, and making informed decisions about whether to accept, mitigate, or transfer remaining risks.

Authorization decisions are documented and communicated to relevant stakeholders to ensure transparency and accountability.

#### Monitor security controls

Continuous monitoring is an ongoing process that involves assessing and documenting the effectiveness of security controls, detecting changes in the security environment, and responding to emerging threats and vulnerabilities.

Monitoring activities include real-time monitoring of security events and incidents, periodic security assessments and audits, review of security logs and reports, and tracking of compliance with security policies and requirements.

By continuously monitoring security controls, organizations can proactively identify and mitigate security issues, maintain situational awareness, and adapt their security posture to evolving threats and changes in the business environment.

### Control Objectives for Information and Related Technology (COBIT)

- Security controls development framework with 5 principles and 7 enablers
- Seven enablers
  - Principles, policies, and frameworks
  - Processes
  - Organizational structures
  - Culture, ethics, and behavior
  - Information
  - Services, infrastructure, and applications
  - People, skills, and competencies

### Sherwood Applied Business Security Architecture (SABSA)

- Security architecture framework
- What, Why, How, Who, Where, and When as they relate to Viewpoints and Layers

### Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)

- Lists requirements and testing procedures as they relate to credit card data

### Federation of European Risk Management Associates (FERMA)

- Lists steps for risk management in a slightly different format
