# Data Lifecycle Phases

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Learn about the phases of a typical data lifecycle and the processes that are involved 

**Description:** In this episode, you will learn about the typical phases involved in a data lifecycle. A data lifecycle is crucial as it delineates the systematic journey of data from its creation to disposal, ensuring efficient management, security, and compliance, ultimately enabling organizations to derive valuable insights and maintain data integrity throughout its entire existence.

## Phases of a Data Lifecycle  

A typical data lifecycle might consist of the following phases:

+ Data creation 
+ Data storage 
+ Data usage
+ Data archival 
+ Data destruction 

Data collection - the amount of data collected varies from company to company as does the methods of collection 

Data location - physical location of data is more important than ever; the geographic location of data influences the relevant data privacy regulations; the physical infrastructure directly effects accessibility

Data maintenance - the ongoing process of running regular checks to identify and correct data that does not follow your corporate standards; data maintenance seeks to ensure that data is accurate, complete, reliable, relevant, timely, and valid

Data retention - keeping data; this is important for a variety of reasons including compliance and regulatory requirements, litigation and legal discovery, business continuity and disaster recovery, and historical analysis and decision making 

Data remanence - refers to the residual representation of data that remains even after attempts have been made to remove or erase it

Data destruction - NIST SP 800-88r1 "Guidelines for Media Sanitization"; technologies and processes used here include:

+ Degausser - used with magnetic media like hard drives, magnetic tapes, and floppy disk drives; a heavy magnetic field used to obliterate the data  
+ Erasing - performing a delete operation against the files or folders; the data can be recovered unless overwrites have occurred 
+ Clearing - also known as overwriting; sensitive data is overwritten with non-sensitive data in order to make the sensitive data unrecoverable 
+ Purging - more intense form of clearing; often times will involve multiple clearing passes combined with another destruction method 
+ Destruction - disintegrator - used with SSDs (degaussing has no effect on these); other options include incineration, crushing, shredding, dissolving 
+ Declassification - any process that purges media or systems so that they may be reused in an unclassified environment; higher security environments do not declassify, they destroy
