# 1-12-1: Test Disaster Recovery Plan

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Identify and explain the importance of disaster recovery testing, given a scenario

**Description:** In this episode, the learner will examine disaster recovery testing activities, industry guidance, and more.


+ Describe Disaster Recovery testing methods
  + Read-through test
    + A preliminary review where team members read through the disaster recovery plan to understand its components and ensure all elements are up-to-date and applicable.
  + Checklist test
    + Involves using a checklist to verify the completeness and accuracy of the disaster recovery plan's elements without enacting any procedures. This test ensures that all necessary resources, contacts, and steps are documented.
  + Table-top exercise
    + A discussion-based session where team members gather to walk through specific disaster scenarios using the disaster recovery plan. The goal is to assess the plan's effectiveness and identify any gaps or improvements needed.
  + Structured walk-through test
    + A detailed review of the disaster recovery plan with all relevant stakeholders. Each step of the plan is examined in detail to validate the response strategies and responsibilities assigned to individuals and departments.
  + Simulation test
    + A test that simulates a specific disaster scenario in a controlled environment to evaluate the organization's readiness and the disaster recovery plan's effectiveness without affecting actual operations.
  + Parallel test
    + Involves running systems in parallel with the primary operational environment. The disaster recovery systems process actual data transactions alongside the live systems to test the former's capability to take over operations if necessary.
  + Full-interruption test
    + The most comprehensive form of disaster recovery testing, where the primary site's operations are fully halted to test the disaster recovery site's ability to take over all operations. This test assesses the recovery site's functionality and the plan's capability to restore operations under real disaster conditions.
+ Communications during disaster recovery testing
  + Stakeholders
    + Keep all relevant stakeholders, including management, IT staff, and business unit leaders, informed about the disaster recovery testing process and outcomes.
  + Test status
    + Regularly update all participants and stakeholders on the status of the disaster recovery test, including any issues encountered and progress made.
  + Regulators
    + Notify and report to regulators as required based on industry standards and regulations, especially if the test involves critical financial, health, or personal data.
+ Describe industry resources for testing DR Plans
  + ISO/IEC (International Organization for Standardization/International Electrotechnical Commission)
  + ISO/IEC 27031: Guidelines for information and communication technology readiness for business continuity.
  + ISO 22301: Security and resilience — Business continuity management systems — Requirements, which include aspects of DR testing.
+ NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)
  + NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1: Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, which includes guidelines on DR testing and exercises.
+ DRI International (Disaster Recovery Institute International)
  + Provides professional practices and standards for DR planners, including DR testing.
+ BCI (Business Continuity Institute)
  + Offers good practice guidelines and standards for business continuity and disaster recovery, including testing procedures.
+ ISACA (Information Systems Audit and Control Association)
  + Provides frameworks like COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies) that include DR planning and testing aspects.
+ FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency)
  + Offers guidelines and resources on emergency management and disaster recovery planning, including testing and exercises.
