# International Laws and Regulations

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Discuss the international laws and regulations that may affect organizations.

**Description:** In this episode, you will learn about the international laws and regulations that may affect an organization's security program.

## International Laws and Regulations

The main points that security professionals need to remember about each of these laws is 1) country of origin, 2) the organization type it affects, and 3) its basic tenet.

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a comprehensive data protection law that came into effect on May 25, 2018, in the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA). It imposes strict rules on those hosting and "processing" the personal data of EU and EAA citizen’s data, anywhere in the world. It applies to all companies that process personal data about individuals in the EU, regardless of where the company is based.

For security professionals, especially those working for organizations that process the personal data of EU citizens, compliance with GDPR is critical. Here’s how GDPR relates to their role:

- Data Protection and Privacy by Design: Security professionals must ensure that data protection and privacy are embedded into the processing systems and operations from the design phase, in compliance with GDPR requirements. This involves adopting a privacy-by-design approach and implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures.
- Data Subject Rights: GDPR strengthens the rights of data subjects, including the right to access, right to be forgotten, and the right to data portability. Security professionals must ensure that systems and processes are in place to facilitate these rights efficiently.
- Data Breach Notification: GDPR requires prompt breach notifications to the relevant supervisory authority and, in certain cases, to the affected data subjects. Security professioals are responsible for developing and implementing incident response plans that include these notification procedures.
- Risk Assessment: GDPR emphasizes the importance of conducting regular risk assessments to identify and mitigate risks to personal data. Security professionals must carry out these assessments and recommend measures to reduce identified risks.
- International Data Transfers: GDPR imposes restrictions on the transfer of personal data outside the EU and EEA. Security professionals need to understand these restrictions and implement measures to ensure compliance when transferring data internationally.
- Record Keeping: GDPR requires organizations to keep detailed records of data processing activities. Security professionals should ensure that their organizations maintain accurate and up-to-date records, including the purposes of processing, data sharing, and retention periods.
- Data Protection Officer (DPO): GDPR requires certain organizations to appoint a DPO. While a security professional may not necessarily be a DPO, they will likely collaborate closely with the DPO to ensure ongoing compliance with GDPR.
- Vendor Management: Under GDPR, organizations are accountable for their vendors’ compliance with the regulation. Security professionals must assess and manage the risks associated with third-party vendors that process personal data on behalf of their organization.
- Training and Awareness: Security professionals should lead or contribute to training programs to raise awareness among employees about GDPR requirements, data protection principles, and the importance of protecting personal data.

### Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules 2011

The Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011, often referred to as the IT Rules 2011, is a set of regulations issued by the Indian government under the Information Technology Act, 2000. These rules define sensitive personal data or information (SPDI), mandate the type of consent required for its collection, outline the purposes for which it can be used, and specify the reasonable security practices and procedures that organizations must follow to protect such data.

For security professionals, particularly those working within or consulting for organizations that operate in India or handle the personal information of Indian residents, understanding and ensuring compliance with the IT Rules 2011 is crucial. Here’s how it relates to their role:

- Identification of SPDI: Security professionals need to identify what constitutes SPDI within their organization's data sets according to the IT Rules 2011. This includes data like passwords, financial information, health conditions, medical records, and sexual orientation.
- Consent Management: The rules require organizations to obtain written consent from individuals before collecting their SPDI. Security professionals must ensure that the mechanisms for obtaining and managing consent are compliant with these requirements and are integrated into the data collection processes.
- Privacy Policies and Disclosure: Organizations are required to publish a privacy policy and make it accessible to individuals providing SPDI. Security professionals should ensure that privacy policies are comprehensive, clearly articulate the purposes of data collection, and are in compliance with the IT Rules 2011.
- Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures: The IT Rules 2011 mandates that organizations implement reasonable security practices and procedures to protect SPDI. Security professionals play a critical role in designing, implementing, and maintaining these security measures, which may involve adhering to established standards like ISO/IEC 27001 or equivalent.
- Data Transfer and Disclosure: Security professionals must ensure that any transfer of SPDI to third parties or outside India complies with the IT Rules 2011, which generally requires ensuring that the recipients of the data adhere to the same level of data protection.
- Grievance Redressal: The rules mandate the appointment of a Grievance Officer and outline the process for addressing grievances related to the processing of SPDI. Security professionals may be involved in setting up the processes and technologies to support the grievance redressal mechanism.
- Training and Awareness: Security professionals should lead or contribute to training programs that educate employees about the importance of SPDI protection, the requirements of the IT Rules 2011, and the organization’s data protection policies and practices.
- Compliance and Audit: Ensuring ongoing compliance with the IT Rules 2011 requires regular audits of security practices and procedures. Security professionals should conduct or oversee these audits to identify compliance gaps and areas for improvement.

### Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)

The Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) is a data protection law adopted by various jurisdictions to govern the collection, use, disclosure, and care of personal data by organizations. While the specifics of the PDPA can vary from one jurisdiction to another (e.g., Singapore's PDPA enacted in 2012, Thailand's PDPA effective since 2021), these laws share common goals of protecting individuals' personal data and ensuring that organizations process this data responsibly and transparently.

For security professionals, understanding and ensuring compliance with the PDPA, regardless of the specific jurisdiction, involves several key considerations:

- Data Protection and Privacy Governance: Security professionals must ensure that their organization’s data governance frameworks are aligned with the PDPA requirements. This includes implementing policies and procedures for data protection, data retention, and data disposal in accordance with the PDPA.
- Consent Management: The PDPA typically requires organizations to obtain consent from individuals before collecting, using, or disclosing their personal data. Security professionals must ensure that systems and processes are in place to manage consents effectively, including providing individuals with the option to withdraw consent.
- Data Inventory and Mapping: Security professionals should conduct data inventory and mapping exercises to identify where personal data is collected, stored, processed, and transmitted within the organization, ensuring that such data is handled in compliance with the PDPA.
- Risk Assessment and Data Protection Measures: Implementing a risk-based approach to data protection is crucial. Security professionals are responsible for conducting risk assessments focused on personal data and implementing appropriate security measures to protect against data breaches and unauthorized access.
- Incident Response and Breach Notification: Security professionals must develop and maintain an incident response plan that includes procedures for managing data breaches. This plan should align with the PDPA’s breach notification requirements, ensuring timely reporting to relevant authorities and affected individuals.
- Data Subject Rights: The PDPA grants individuals certain rights over their personal data, such as the right to access, correct, and request deletion of their data. Security professionals must ensure that mechanisms are in place to facilitate the exercise of these rights.
- Training and Awareness: Security professionals should oversee or contribute to training programs that raise awareness among employees about the importance of personal data protection, the provisions of the PDPA, and the organization’s data protection policies.
- Vendor Management: When outsourcing processing activities to third parties, security professional must ensure that contracts and agreements include provisions requiring vendors to comply with PDPA requirements and protect the personal data entrusted to them.
- Cross-border Data Transfers: For organizations that transfer personal data across borders, security professionals must ensure that such transfers comply with the PDPA’s requirements regarding cross-border data protection.

### Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)

The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) is China's comprehensive data protection regulation that came into effect on November 1, 2021. It lays down strict requirements for the collection, storage, use, processing, transmission, provision, and disclosure of personal information. The law applies to organizations and individuals who process personal information of individuals within the territory of China, as well as those outside of China if they process personal information of individuals in China for the purpose of providing products or services to them or analyzing and evaluating their behavior.

For security professionals, especially those working for multinational corporations that operate in China or handle the personal information of Chinese residents, understanding and ensuring compliance with PIPL is critical. Here's how it relates to their role:

- Data Governance and Compliance: Security professionals need to ensure their organizations' data governance frameworks align with PIPL requirements. This includes implementing principles like data minimization, purpose limitation, and transparency in the processing of personal information.
- Data Protection Measures: Under PIPL, adopting reasonable security measures to protect personal information is mandatory. Security professionals are responsible for designing, implementing, and maintaining appropriate technical and administrative measures to safeguard personal information against unauthorized access, disclosure, and loss.
- Consent Management: PIPL emphasizes the importance of obtaining informed consent for the collection and processing of personal information. Security professionals must ensure that consent mechanisms are clear, voluntary, and provide individuals with the option to withdraw consent.
- Cross-border Data Transfer: PIPL sets specific requirements for the international transfer of personal information, including conducting impact assessments and obtaining regulatory approval or certification. Security professionals must navigate these requirements to ensure compliant cross-border data flows.
- Data Subject Rights: The law grants individuals a range of rights over their personal data, such as access, correction, and deletion. Security professionals must implement processes and technologies that enable individuals to exercise these rights effectively.
- Incident Response and Breach Notification: Security professionals should have an incident response plan that includes procedures for addressing personal information security incidents. This plan must comply with PIPL’s breach notification requirements, ensuring timely communication with regulatory authorities and affected individuals.
- Handling Sensitive Personal Information: PIPL categorizes certain types of personal information as sensitive and imposes stricter handling requirements. Security professionals must identify sensitive personal information within their systems and apply enhanced protection measures.
- Training and Awareness: Security professionals are tasked with developing and conducting training programs to raise awareness among employees about PIPL obligations and the importance of protecting personal information.
- Third-Party Management: For organizations that engage third-party service providers, security professionals must ensure that contracts and agreements mandate compliance with PIPL and safeguard the personal information processed on behalf of the organization.

### Basel II

Basel II is an international banking regulation issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, focusing on risk management aspects of the banking industry. Basel II is based on three main pillars: minimum capital requirements, supervisory review process, and market discipline.

For security professionals, especially those working in or with the banking and financial services sector, understanding Basel II is crucial due to its implications for information security and risk management. Here’s how Basel II relates to the role of a security professional:

- Minimum Capital Requirements: This pillar focuses on the requirement that banks hold a certain amount of financial capital to mitigate risks.
- Supervisory Review Process: This pillar emphasizes the regulatory oversight of banks' internal risk management practices and procedures.
- Market Discipline: This pillar encourages transparency and disclosure to promote market discipline. 

Specific Implications for security professionals:

- Risk Management Framework: Security professionals must ensure that their organization’s risk management framework is comprehensive and aligned with Basel II requirements, particularly in assessing and managing operational and cybersecurity risks that could impact financial stability.
- IT Governance and Controls: The regulation necessitates strict governance and control mechanisms over IT systems and data. Security professionals should oversee the implementation of controls that safeguard against unauthorized access, data breaches, and other security threats.
- Compliance and Reporting: Security professionals are involved in compliance activities, ensuring that the organization adheres to Basel II requirements. This includes participating in the preparation of reports for regulatory bodies that demonstrate the organization's commitment to risk management and security.
- Security Awareness and Training: Given the critical importance of managing operational risks under Basel II, security professionals must also focus on security awareness and training programs that educate employees about their roles in safeguarding the organization’s assets and information.

### Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)

The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) is a Canadian federal law enacted in April 2000. PIPEDA governs how private sector organizations collect, use, and disclose personal information in the course of commercial business. The act applies to personal information collected, used, or disclosed during commercial activities within Canadian provinces or between Canadian provinces and countries other than Canada. PIPEDA is based on ten principles that outline the rights of individuals to control the use of their personal information and the obligations of organizations to protect this information.

For security professionals, particularly those working with or within Canadian organizations or those handling the personal information of Canadian citizens, understanding and ensuring compliance with PIPEDA is crucial for several reasons:

- Information Security and Privacy Controls: PIPEDA requires organizations to implement appropriate security measures to protect personal information against loss, theft, unauthorized access, disclosure, copying, use, or modification. Security profesionals are responsible for designing, implementing, and maintaining these security controls, aligning with PIPEDA's safeguarding requirements.
- Accountability: As one of PIPEDA's principles, accountability necessitates that organizations appoint someone (often a security professional or someone in a similar role) to be responsible for the organization's compliance with the Act. Security professionals may be involved in or directly responsible for overseeing privacy policies and practices, including compliance with PIPEDA.
- Consent: PIPEDA mandates that organizations obtain informed consent for the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information. Security professionals must ensure that IT systems and processes support the requirement for consent and that individuals' choices are respected and implemented correctly in information systems.
- Access and Accuracy: Individuals have the right to access their personal information held by an organization and request corrections to it. Security professionals must ensure systems provide mechanisms for individuals to access their data and request updates, in compliance with PIPEDA.
- Incident Response and Breach Notification: PIPEDA includes requirements for breach notification to both the affected individuals and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada in the case of a breach that poses a real risk of significant harm. Security professionals play a critical role in breach detection, response, and notification processes.
- Openness and Transparency: Organizations must be open about their privacy policies and practices. Security professionals can contribute to this by helping to document and communicate how personal information is protected, including the technical and administrative measures in place.
- Training and Awareness: Security professionals should ensure that staff are trained on the importance of privacy and the organization’s obligations under PIPEDA. This includes understanding how to handle personal information securely and in compliance with the Act.

### Investigatory Powers Act

The Investigatory Powers Act (IPA) was enacted by the UK Parliament in 2016. This comprehensive piece of legislation consolidates and updates the legal framework governing the use of covert surveillance, interception of communications, and the acquisition of communications data by public authorities, including law enforcement and intelligence agencies, in the UK. The IPA provides the legal basis for various investigatory powers, including the collection of bulk communications data, internet connection records, and the interception of communications.

For security professionals, especially those working within or consulting for organizations that operate in the UK or handle the data of UK residents, understanding the implications of the IPA is crucial. Here’s how it relates to their role:

- Data Retention and Access: The IPA requires telecommunications operators to retain Internet Connection Records (ICRs) for up to 12 months. Security professionals need to ensure that systems are capable of complying with these retention requirements and that access to this data is controlled and audited in accordance with the law.
- Lawful Interception: The act allows for the lawful interception of communications under specific conditions. Security professionals must understand these conditions and implement processes and technical measures that enable compliance while ensuring that interception capabilities are not misused.
- Encryption and Backdoors: One of the contentious aspects of the IPA is its implications for encryption. The act includes provisions that could compel service providers to remove electronic protection from data. Security professionals must navigate the ethical and technical challenges posed by these provisions, balancing legal compliance with the commitment to secure customer data.
- Privacy and Security Policies: Security professionals should ensure that privacy and security policies reflect the obligations and powers under the IPA. This includes policies related to data collection, retention, access, and disclosure.
- Compliance with Notices and Warrants: The act provides for the issuance of warrants and notices requiring the assistance of telecommunications providers in conducting surveillance and acquiring data. Security professionals must ensure that their organizations can comply with such legal demands in a manner that is consistent with both the IPA and broader security and privacy principles.
- Security Measures: Despite the investigatory powers granted by the IPA, organizations must still protect personal data against unauthorized access and breaches. Security professionals play a key role in implementing and maintaining these security measures.
- Awareness and Training: Security professionals should lead efforts to educate and train staff on the implications of the IPA for their operations, particularly regarding handling requests from public authorities and understanding the legal framework for surveillance and data collection.
- International Considerations: For multinational organizations, security professionals must understand how the IPA interacts with other jurisdictions' privacy and surveillance laws, ensuring that global operations remain compliant across different legal regimes.
