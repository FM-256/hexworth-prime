## Supply Chain Risk Management

Supply Chain Risk Management (SCRM) is a strategic approach to identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks associated with the acquisition and management of goods, services, and technology from external suppliers, vendors, and partners. It involves evaluating and managing risks throughout the entire supply chain to ensure the security, reliability, and resilience of products and services.

Here's an overview of Supply Chain Risk Management, including hardware, software, and service providers:

- Hardware Providers:
  - Tangible Risk: Risks associated with hardware components include supply chain disruptions, counterfeit parts, malicious implants, and hardware vulnerabilities.
  - Examples: These risks can manifest in various ways, such as compromised firmware or malicious hardware implants inserted during the manufacturing process.
- Software Providers:
  - Tangible Risks: Risks associated with software include vulnerabilities, backdoors, malware, and unauthorized access.
  - Intangible Risks: Risks such as software licensing issues, intellectual property theft, and reliance on outdated or unsupported software versions.
  - Examples: Software vulnerabilities can lead to data breaches, system compromises, and unauthorized access to sensitive information.
- Service Providers:
  - Tangible Risks: Risks associated with service providers include service disruptions, data breaches, unauthorized access, and data loss.
  - Intangible Risks: Risks such as data privacy violations, regulatory non-compliance, and inadequate service levels.
  - Examples: Service providers may inadvertently expose sensitive data through inadequate security measures or suffer from insider threats that compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of services.

To effectively manage supply chain risks, organizations should implement the following practices:

- Risk Assessment: Conduct comprehensive risk assessments to identify and prioritize risks associated with suppliers, vendors, and partners.
- Due Diligence: Perform due diligence when selecting and onboarding suppliers, vendors, and partners, including evaluating their security practices, compliance with regulations, and past security incidents.
- Contractual Agreements: Establish clear contractual agreements that outline security requirements, responsibilities, and obligations for suppliers, vendors, and partners.
- Continuous Monitoring: Implement continuous monitoring mechanisms to detect and respond to security incidents, vulnerabilities, and changes in the supply chain environment.
- Collaboration and Communication: Foster collaboration and communication with suppliers, vendors, and partners to address security concerns, share threat intelligence, and coordinate incident response efforts.

### Risk Mitigations

Risk mitigations are strategies and measures implemented to reduce or eliminate the potential impact of risks associated with the supply chain. Here's an explanation of various risk mitigations that can be implemented as part of supply chain risk management (SCRM) :

- Third-party Assessment and Monitoring:
  - This involves evaluating and continuously monitoring third-party suppliers, vendors, and partners to assess their security posture, compliance with security standards, and adherence to contractual obligations.
  - Organizations conduct regular assessments, audits, and reviews of third parties to identify vulnerabilities, weaknesses, and areas of improvement.
- Minimum Security Requirements:
  - Organizations establish minimum security requirements that suppliers, vendors, and partners must meet to ensure the security and integrity of products, services, and data within the supply chain.
  - These requirements typically include standards for encryption, authentication, access control, data protection, and security testing.
- Service Level Requirements:
  - Organizations define service level requirements that specify the expected performance, availability, reliability, and security of products and services provided by third-party suppliers, vendors, and partners.
  - These requirements help ensure that service providers meet contractual obligations and deliver services that meet the organization's needs and expectations.
- Silicon Root of Trust
  - The silicon root of trust involves establishing a secure foundation for hardware components by embedding cryptographic keys, secure boot mechanisms, and hardware-based security features into silicon chips.
  - This ensures the integrity and authenticity of hardware components throughout the supply chain, preventing tampering, counterfeiting, and unauthorized modifications.
- Physically Unclonable Function (PUF):
  - PUF is a security technology that leverages physical variations in hardware components to generate unique identifiers and cryptographic keys.
  - PUFs enhance the security of hardware devices by providing hardware-based authentication and anti-counterfeiting capabilities.
- Software Bill of Materials (SBOM):
  - SBOM is a list of software components, libraries, dependencies, and licenses used in a software product or system.
  - SBOMs help organizations track and manage software supply chain risks by providing transparency into the software components and their associated vulnerabilities, licensing issues, and security risks.
- Software escrow
  - Software escrow is an agreement between an application development company and the organization for which the application was created that provides the application source code to the organization.
  - Software escrow is stored with an agent that releases the base code.

By implementing these risk mitigations, organizations can strengthen their supply chain resilience, mitigate security risks, and ensure the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of products and services across the supply chain ecosystem.
