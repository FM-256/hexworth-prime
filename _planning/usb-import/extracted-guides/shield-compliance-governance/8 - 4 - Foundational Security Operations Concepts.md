# 1-4-1: Foundational Security Operations Concepts

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Identify and explain operational security concepts, given a scenario.

**Description:** In this episode, the learner will examine foundational operational security techniques. We will explore least privilege, segregation of duties, service-level agreements, and more.


+ Need-to-Know/Least Privilege
  + A practice where individuals have access only to the information and resources necessary for their roles
  + Reduces Risk
    + Unauthorized access \(internal and external\)
    + Abuse
    + Exposure
  + Ensures that access to sensitive information and systems is tightly controlled and aligned with user responsibilities, helping to mitigate the risk of an internal and external breach.
+ Segregation of Duties \(SoD\) and Responsibilities
  + Divides roles and responsibilities 
  + Ensure no single individual has control over all aspects of any critical operation.
  + Can prevent
    + Risk of fraudulent activity 
    + Conflicts of interest
    + Unauthorized access
    + Reducing the Risk of a single person compromising the security infrastructure.
+ Privileged Account Management \(PAM\)
  + Securing and managing accounts with elevated access rights, ensuring they are only used for necessary tasks and monitored for misuse.
  + Enforce strict access controls and monitoring on high-level accounts to prevent unauthorized access and potential security incidents.
+ Privileged Identity Management (PIM)
  + A broader focus \(than PAM\) that includes managing the identities \(not just the accounts\) that have elevated privileges.
+ Job Rotation
  + The practice of regularly transferring employees between different jobs to prevent collusion and reduce the Risk of fraud, as well as enhancing skill sets and knowledge.
  + Can prevent skill stagnation, reducing the Risk of insider threats and ensuring a diverse understanding of different security roles and challenges.
+ Service-Level Agreements \(SLA\)
  + Formal agreements detailing the expected service performance and responsibilities between service providers and their customers
  + Includes availability, serviceability, performance, operation, or other attributes of the service
  + Important to define, negotiate, and monitor SLAs to ensure that security services meet business needs and compliance requirements effectively.
