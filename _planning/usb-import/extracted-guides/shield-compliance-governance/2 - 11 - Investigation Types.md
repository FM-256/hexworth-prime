# Investigation Types

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Discuss administrative, criminal, civil, regulatory, and industry standards investigations.

**Description:** In this episode, you will learn about administrative, criminal, civil, regulatory, and industry standards investigations.

## Investigation Types

### **Administrative (Operational) Investigation**

- Do not result in criminal, civil, or regulatory issues.
- Focus on issues that are internal to the organizations.
- Often related to policy violations or employee misconduct.
- Root cause analysis vital.
- Corrective actions are required to prevent future incidents.
- May involve HR and security departments and can lead to disciplinary action.

### **Criminal Investigation**

- Federal, state, or local laws are violated.
- Law enforcement involvement is vital.
- All evidence must be properly documented.
- Burden of proof lies with the prosecution.
- Judge or jury decides guilt or innocence.
- Can pertain to cybercrimes like hacking, theft of intellectual property, fraud, or child exploitation.
- Security professionals may need to assist by providing digital evidence, ensuring that evidence is collected and handled in a manner admissible in court.

### **Civil Investigation**

- Plaintiff vs defendant.
- Monetary penalties are usually the goal.
- The judgement is based on the preponderance of the evidence.
- Burden of proof lies with plaintiff.
- Usually deal with disputes between individuals or organizations that are not criminal offenses.
- Might involve lawsuits where an organization is accused of failing to protect personal data leading to a data breach.
- Security professionals may be called upon to provide expert witness testimony or to ensure that relevant data is preserved for legal proceedings.

### **Regulatory Investigation** 

- Regulatory body or government agency leads investigation.
- Official notification is sent by the regulatory body to the organization being investigated.
- Full compliance with the investigation is important.
- Failure to comply may mean charges are filed against organization and personnel.
- Can be triggered by non-compliance with standards such as GDPR, HIPAA, or SEC regulations.
- Security professionals must understand how to navigate the requirements of regulatory investigations and may be responsible for interfacing with regulators, providing documentation, and demonstrating compliance.

### **Standards Investigation** 

- Standards organization (like ISO/IEC) leads investigation.
- May be conducted to ensure adherence to industry standards like ISO/IEC 27001 or those set by professional organizations like the Payment Card Industry Security Standards Council (PCI SSC).
- Can vary based on the type of industry, such as healthcare.
- Compliance is necessary to retain good standing.
- Failure to comply may result in loss of accreditation, fines, or sanctions.
- While not legal investigations, they can impact an organization’s ability to operate or its reputation.
- Security professionals may need to ensure that security controls meet or exceed these standards and that they are properly documented and followed.



For security professionals, understanding the nuances of different types of investigations is important because each type may require a distinct approach to how evidence is collected, preserved, and presented. Security professionals must be prepared to:

- Coordinate with legal, human resources, or law enforcement officials as required.
- Understand legal concepts such as the chain of custody for evidence.
- Conduct or facilitate forensic analysis in a manner that adheres to the standards required for the specific type of investigation.
- Provide technical expertise and consultation during the investigation process.
- Develop and implement policies and procedures to prevent incidents that may lead to investigations and to manage investigations effectively if they occur.
