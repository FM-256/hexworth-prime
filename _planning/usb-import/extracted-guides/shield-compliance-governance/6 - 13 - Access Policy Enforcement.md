**Description:** In this episode, you will learn about key components in access policy enforcement in modern networks. These components include Policy Enforcement Points (PEPs), Policy Decision Points (PDPs), Policy Information Points (PIPs), and Policy Administration Points (PAPs). 

## Access Policy Enforcement         

Policy Enforcement Points (PEPs): PEPs are enforcement mechanisms responsible for intercepting access requests and enforcing access control policies based on defined rules. PEPs evaluate the attributes associated with the requesting entity (user or system) and the requested resource to determine whether access should be granted or denied.

Policy Decision Points (PDPs): PDPs are components that evaluate access control policies and make access control decisions based on the attributes provided by the PEPs. PDPs typically utilize a set of rules or policies defined by administrators to determine whether access should be allowed, denied, or further evaluated.

Policy Information Points (PIPs): PIPs are responsible for providing attribute information to the PDPs during the access control decision process. PIPs retrieve attribute values from various sources such as directories, databases, identity management systems, or external services.

Policy Administration Points (PAPs): PAPs are interfaces or tools used by administrators to define, manage, and update access control policies within the system. Administrators can specify rules and conditions based on attributes to enforce specific access control requirements.
