# 1-14-1: Implement and Manage Physical Security

After completing this episode, you should be able to:

+ Identify and explain the importance of physical security techniques, given a scenario

**Description:** In this episode, the learner will examine physical security and various techniques to implement physical security. We will explore industry resources for guidance on physical security as well as various controls such as bollards, lighting, CCTV, gates, guards, fencing, and more.

+ Describe physical security
  + Physical security involves defending tangible assets, structures, and sensitive information from tangible threats or actions that might inflict significant harm or loss.
  + Encompasses measures to shield staff, infrastructure, and critical data against potential physical threats
  + Includes environmental disasters, unlawful entry, and deliberate attacks
  + The strategies employed aim to restrict unauthorized entry to buildings and safeguard individuals and assets from potential injury or destruction.
+ Describe examples of physical security controls
  + Bollards 
    + Sturdy posts installed to control or prevent vehicular access to specific areas.
    + Prevent vehicular access to protected areas, enhancing perimeter security.
  + Lighting
    + Illumination designed to enhance visibility and deter unauthorized access or activities
    + Deters criminal activity by increasing visibility and reducing hiding spots around facilities
  + Motion Sensors
    + Devices that detect movement in a specified area, often used in security systems
    + Detect unauthorized movement, triggering alerts or activating other security measures.
  + Fencing
    + Barriers constructed to secure a perimeter by preventing or controlling access
    + Establishes a physical barrier to control access and secure the perimeter of a property
  + Signs
    + Notices placed to inform, warn, or guide individuals regarding policies, hazards, or directions
    + Warns potential intruders of surveillance and restricted areas, acting as a deterrent
  + Gate
    + Movable barriers at the entrance or within an enclosure used to control access
    + Control points for entry and exit, managing access to a secured area
  + Guards
    + Personnel employed to protect property and individuals by maintaining a secure environment.
    + Provide a human element to security, capable of responding in real-time to incidents
  + CCTV \(Closed-Circuit Television\)
    + A surveillance system using video cameras to displaying on a limited set of monitors
    + Allows for monitoring and recording of activities in and around facilities, aiding in incident response and investigation
  + Access Control Roster
    + A list or database used to manage and monitor the authorization of individuals to enter certain areas.
    + Ensures only authorized personnel can access certain areas, enhancing security through verification
  + Smartcard
    + A card with an embedded microchip used for electronic access control or secure identification
    + Serves as a secure method for personal identification and access control, utilizing encrypted data
  + Biometrics
    + Authentication techniques relying on measurable physical characteristics
    + Offers a high level of verification and access control based on unique personal attributes
  + HVAC \(Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning\)
    + Systems used to regulate indoor temperature, airflow, and air quality.
    + Maintains environmental conditions to protect sensitive equipment
  + Fire Suppression Systems
    + Equipment and methods used to extinguish or prevent the spread of fire in a building or vehicle.
    + Protects against fire damage, ensuring the integrity of the facility and the safety of occupants
      + NIST SP 800-86: Occupant Emergency Plan \(OEP\) - Provides coordinated procedures for minimizing loss of life or injury and protecting property damage in response to a physical threat
+ Describe architectural and structural physical security techniques
  + Raised structures
    + Elevating critical assets or entire buildings to mitigate risks from flooding and unauthorized ground access.
  + Strategically positioned landscaping
    + Using natural or artificial landscape features 
    + Aime to direct or limit access paths and enhance surveillance visibility
  + Security Glass
    + Tinted Glass
      + Offers enhanced privacy and reduces visibility into secure areas from the outside
    + Tempered glass
      + Strengthened through thermal processes to be more resistant to impacts
    + Wired glass
      + Contains embedded metal wire mesh, offering added strength and resistance to breakage.
    + Bulletproof glass
      + Engineered to withstand various levels of ballistic impact, protecting against gunfire
    + Shatterproof
      + Engineered to resist breaking or splintering upon impact, providing an effective barrier against break-ins, vandalism, and accidents, enhancing safety and security
  + Fencing types
    + Chain Link Fencing - Durable and versatile, often used with barbed wire or razor wire for increased security.
    + Palisade Fencing - Steel or iron fencing with pointed tips, offering a high level of security against climbing and cutting.
    + Welded wire Mesh - Offers visibility while maintaining robustness, deterring trespassers with its sturdy construction.
    + Electric fencing - Adds a layer of protection by delivering a non-lethal shock to potential intruders.
    + Anti-climb fencing - Designed with narrow openings and often a curved top to prevent climbing.
    + Expanding or Retractable Fencing - Flexible solutions that can be adjusted as needed for access control.
+ Describe industry resources for guidance on physical security
  + NIST SP 800-53: Physical and Environmental Protection Controls or PE- \<number designation\>.
    + Examples
      + PE-3 - Physical Access control
      + PE-18 - Location of System Components
      + PE-19 - Information Leakage
  + NIST SP 800-37 "Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy." 
    + Covers the process of integrating physical security controls into the risk management framework.
  + ISO/IEC 27001: "Information Security Management Systems - Requirements"
    + Provides the requirements for an information security management system \(ISMS\)
    + Includes aspects of physical and environmental security as part of its control set
  + ISO/IEC 27002: "Information Technology - Security Techniques - Code of Practice for Information Security Controls"
    + Provides best practice guidelines on information security controls
    + Includes those for physical and environmental security under its control objectives and controls
