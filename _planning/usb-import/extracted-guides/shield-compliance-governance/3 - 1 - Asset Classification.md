## Identification and Classification of Assets 

What is an asset? It is anything of value that an organization possesses. Notice that this also includes data. Another common term for data is information. The next episode deals with the identification and classification of data in an organization. 

There are many standards to help us with asset identification and classification. Two popular ones are: 

+ FIPS publication 199 - Standards for Security Categorization of Federal Information and Information Systems 
+ NIST Special Publication 800-60 Volume 1 Revision 1 - Guide for Mapping Types of Information and Information Systems to Security Categories 

A classification scheme is a way to label assets or data; we can then apply controls that are appropriate for these different classifications. 

>**Note:** Asset classifications of hardware often follows the data classification given to the data the hardware works with. For example, if a removable hard disk contains Top Secret data, the drive itself will be labeled as Top Secret. It is not uncommon for a physical label to be placed on the physical asset designating its classification. 

Organizations will also commonly categorize assets. They will do this to help identify the value of assets. They might also do this to identify the potential impact of a security breach or other failure. 

Standards documents encourage us to categorize our assets with confidentiality, integrity, and availability in mind. Categorizations could be based on Potential Impact with categories being Low (limited impact), Moderate (serious adverse effect), or High (catastrophic effect). 

For example, a SCADA system might be categorized as Confidentiality (Low), Integrity (High), Availability (High)

Commonly, there is an asset inventory that gets created in the organization for use by many departments. There is also often an asset configuration repository. A physical equipment lifecycle might also be identified and documented. 

>**Remember:** An organization should be encouraged to review the classifications and categorizations of assets on an ongoing basis. Many companies will do this annually (at least).
