 What is Risk Management? -

 Risk management is…

  Complex
  Treated differently from one organization to another

 The 5 Phases of Risk Management are…

 1. Identification of mission critical functions
 2. Identification of known vulnerabilities
 3. Identification of potential threats
 4. Analysis of business impacts
 5. Identification of risk responses


  MUST KNOW CONCEPTS !!: NIST SP800-30R1 page 12 -

  Risk - The probability (likelihood) that a given threat source will exercise
  a particular vulnerability and the resulting impact should that occur

  Threat -  an event or situation that if it occurred, would prevent the
  organization from operating in its normal manner

  Vulnerability - weakness

  Likelihood - chance something might happen

  Risk - The probability (likelihood) that a given threat source will exercise
  a particular vulnerability and the resulting impact should that occur

  Impact - what a threat will cost (quantitative/qualitative)

  * Countermeasure (control / safeguard) - mechanism applied to minimize risk

  ** Residual Risk - remaining risk(s) after all
  countermeasures/controls/safeguards have been applied


 Risk Assessment Methods – Quantitative Analysis -

  • Based completely on numeric values

  • Goal is to calculate the probable loss for every risk

 FORMULAS TO KNOW:

    ALE = SLE * ARO

    SLE = AV * EF


 EXAMPLE:

    ALE   =   SLE  *  ARO
    $10.00   $1.00    10

    • Countermeasure is less than ALE  --> Do this
    • Countermeasure is equal to ALE   --> Do this
    • Countermeasure is greater than ALE  --> Think before acting


 Additional Quantitative Measures -

  • Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) – lifespan cost

  • Return on Investment (ROI) – cost vs. benefit(s)

  • Mean Time To Recovery (MTTR) - downtime

  • Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) - lifespan

  • Gap Analysis – as is vs. to be


 Risk Assessment Methods – Qualitative Analysis -

 Uses descriptions and words to measure the likelihood and impact of risk.
 (generally scenario-based)

 NOTE: You can also assign numbers between 0 and 9 for exposures and damage
 potential. However, you do not perform calculations on the numbers assigned
 to the risks.

 * Semi-quantitative Analysis - attempts to find a middle ground between the
 previous two risk analysis types to create a hybrid method.
