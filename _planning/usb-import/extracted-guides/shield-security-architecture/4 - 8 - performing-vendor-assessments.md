Performing vendor assessments

 Vocabulary/Concepts to know around vendor assessment -

 Vendor Lock-in (proprietary lock-in) – switching vendors is either impossible
 or would result in substantial complexity & costs

 Vendor Lockout – product is developed in a way that makes it inoperable with
 other products

 Vendor Viability – used to determine if a vendor will be in-business on an
 on-going basis

 Source Code Escrow – a copy of source code is provided to a trusted third party
 in case the vendor ceases to be in business

 Support Availability –type & level of support to be provided by the vendor; it
 is common for support performance & maintenance fees to be defined via a
 Service Level Agreement (SLA)

 Meeting Client Requirements – formal measures taken to validate that vendor's
 delivered service or product offering aligns to established requirements

 Incident Reporting Requirements – contracts should clearly identify the
 requirement for vendors to provide timely notification regarding any security
 incidents

 Geographical Considerations – Data is regulated based on where it moves & lives

 Vendor Policies – Establishing the maturity of vendor security operations &
 defining the minimum set of requirements & expectations via policy; it is
 imperative that vendors adhere to the same policies & procedures in place at
 the organization, & that they are monitored for compliance

 Supply Chain – all of the suppliers, vendors, & partners needed to deliver a
 final product

 Supply Chain Visibility (SCV) – understanding how all vendor hardware, software,
 & services are produced & delivered as well as how they impact an organization's
 operations or finished products

 Third-Party Dependencies – the far broader set of relationships such as vendors,
 suppliers, service providers, credit card processors, utilities, contractors,
 affiliates, trade associations, government agencies, etc…

 Third-Party Assessments – when performed by an independent & trusted authority,
 demonstrates that a vendor has a well-established minimum level of maturity
 &/or capability in place
