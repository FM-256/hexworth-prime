Data considerations

 What are the different types of data? -

 Personally Identifiable Information (PII) - data that can be used to directly
 or indirectly identify an individual

 Sensitive PII - US social security numbers, biometrics, financial records,
 medical records, immigration identifiers, & criminal history; requires stricter
 handling & protection than other types of PII

 Protected Health Information (PHI) - data that can be used to identify an
 individual & includes information about past, present, or future health, as well
 as related payments & data used in the operation of a healthcare business

 Personal Identifiable Financial Information (PIFI) – information about a
 consumer provided to a financial institution including account number,
 credit/debit card number, personal information, & social security number; used
 to obtain access to a financial product or service

 Intellectual Property (IP) – protected by various mechanisms such as copyrights,
 patents, trademarks, & trade secrets


 What is Data Ownership, Classification & Retention? -

 Data Owner - accountable for the protection of the data under their control;
 responsible for ensuring data is appropriately protected & classified


 Data Classification - establishes the necessary controls required to protect
 data

      Public - Disclosure would not cause harm
      Sensitive - Disclosure would cause harm
      Confidential - Disclosure would cause considerable harm


 Data retention - timespan that data must be kept (minimum  & maximum)

       • Requirements are often identified in data classification levels

       • Specific details should be defined in separate data retention policies
       & procedures


 What is Data Destruction? -

 Data destruction - legally compliant means by which data is removed and made
 inaccessible

 Sanitization - means by which information is removed from media

      • Clear
      • Purge
      • Damage (Destroy)
      • Crypto-Erase


 What are the geographic considerations around data? -

 Data sovereignty - laws in force in the country in which data is stored hold
 sway over the data and describe the legal dynamics of data collection to be
 applied

    Subject – User
    Object – Asset (data)


 Attestation of Compliance (AOC) - the set of policies, contracts, & standards
 identified as essential in the agreement between two parties

    Defines how the relationship will be governed
