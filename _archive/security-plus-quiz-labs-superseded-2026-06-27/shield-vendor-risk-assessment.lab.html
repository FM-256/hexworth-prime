Concept Reference -- Read Before You Start
SOC 2 Type I vs. Type II
Type I -- point-in-time design assessment. An auditor confirms that security controls are designed correctly on a single day. Does not tell you whether they actually worked over time.

Type II -- operational effectiveness over a period (typically 6--12 months). An auditor tests whether controls operated effectively throughout that window. Type II is the stronger assurance and the industry standard for sensitive-data vendors.
Right-to-Audit Clause
A contractual provision giving your organization the right to inspect or commission an independent audit of a vendor's security controls. Receiving a SOC 2 report is not the same thing -- a SOC 2 is a vendor-selected audit scope and vendor-selected auditor. A right-to-audit clause lets you define scope and timing. Without it, you rely entirely on what the vendor chooses to show you.
72-Hour Breach Notification (GDPR Art. 33)
GDPR Article 33 requires a data controller to notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach. If your vendor takes longer than 72 hours to notify you, you cannot meet your own regulatory deadline. Vendor SLAs exceeding 72 hours are therefore a direct compliance gap, not just a best-practice miss.
